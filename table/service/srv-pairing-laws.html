The Two Games and the Switches

After this you can For any pairing you can say in one sentence whether it is CONGRUENT (echo the dish) or CONTRAST (oppose it), and which switch is doing the work: salt loves acid and bubbles, fat wants cutting acid or matching richness, food acid demands equal wine acid, chile is inflamed by alcohol and tannin, sweet dishes need sweeter wine, bitter stacks with tannin, umami makes tannin harsh.

  1. Congruent vs. Contrast (The Master Key) Wine & Beverage

    Every pairing on earth runs one of two strategies. CONGRUENT: echo the dish: buttery Chardonnay with lobster in butter, earthy Pinot with mushrooms, jammy Zin with barbecue sauce, oloroso with roasted nuts; flavors reinforce into depth. CONTRAST: oppose it. Champagne's acid through fried chicken's fat, Sauternes against Roquefort's salt, Riesling's sweetness across chili's fire; tension creates lift. Neither is superior; great lists and great sommeliers simply KNOW WHICH GAME each pairing plays and can say so in one sentence at the table. When building a menu's pairings, alternate strategies course to course: all-echo is monotony, all-tension is exhausting; the rhythm is the art.

  2. The Pairing Laws (Salt, Fat, Acid, Heat, Sweet, Bitter) Wine & Beverage

    The working physics, pocket-sized. SALT loves acid and bubbles, softens tannin's grip, and makes sweetness sing (jamón + fino; caviar + Champagne). FAT wants either cutting acid or matching richness, and it is tannin's best friend. ACID in food DEMANDS equal acid in wine (the vinaigrette rule). HEAT (chili) is inflamed by alcohol and tannin, soothed by sugar, cold, and low proof (the Riesling formula; beer and off-dry whites over big reds, always). SWEET dishes need SWEETER wines. BITTER (char, chicory, chocolate) stacks with tannin: sometimes gloriously (char + Syrah), sometimes harshly; fruit and sweetness buffer it. UMAMI (mushrooms, aged cheese, soy) can make tannic wines taste harsher and needs fruit or salt as a bridge. Six switches; every pairing question is some combination of them.

  3. Regional Pairing & the Hard-to-Pair List Wine & Beverage

    'WHAT GROWS TOGETHER GOES TOGETHER' is the lazy-sounding rule with a real mechanism: cuisines and wines co-evolved at the same tables for centuries: Sancerre and crottin, Chianti and ragù, Muscadet and oysters, Rioja and lamb, Fino and fried fish, Barolo and brasato. When lost, pair the PLACE. Then the famous saboteurs and their fixes: ARTICHOKES (cynarin makes wines taste weirdly sweet: go unoaked, herbal whites); ASPARAGUS (Grüner or Sauvignon); EGGS (bubbles: texture and sulfur both handled); VINEGAR-heavy dishes (sherry or match the acid); CHOCOLATE (fortified sweet reds: Banyuls, ruby Port); RAW ONION and hot chili (beer is not surrender; it is the right tool). Knowing the impossible list is half of looking brilliant beside it.
