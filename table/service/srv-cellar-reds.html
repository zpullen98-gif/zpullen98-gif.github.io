The Reds You Will Pour

After this you can Three sentences each on the Bordeaux blend, Pinot Noir, the Rhône school, the Tempranillo/Sangiovese/Nebbiolo food-wine trinity, and the New World crowd-pleasers; and you can steer a table off Cabernet when the plate is chile or delicate fish.

  1. Cabernet Sauvignon & the Bordeaux Blend Wine & Beverage

    The world's most planted noble red: blackcurrant, cedar, graphite, firm tannin, built for oak and age. Its homeland logic: BORDEAUX blends it (Left Bank: Cab-led over gravel, Médoc, Pauillac) with MERLOT (Right Bank–led: plummy, supple, Pomerol, Saint-Émilion) plus Cab Franc and friends, each grape patching the others' weather risks: the blend as insurance policy that became a style. New World icons: Napa (riper, plusher), Coonawarra, Chile. THE pairing is beef, the tannin-fat law's poster child: ribeye, lamb, hard aged cheeses. Menu translation: 'Bordeaux' is not a grape; it is a recipe, and your steak list should never be far from one.

  2. Pinot Noir & Burgundy Wine & Beverage

    The heartbreak grape: thin-skinned, site-obsessed, and at its peak (the Côte d'Or's village-premier-grand cru ladder) the most transparent expression of terroir in red wine: cherry, forest floor, silk tannin, high acid. That structure makes it the SOMMELIER'S SWISS ARMY KNIFE: the red that crosses to fish (salmon, tuna), loves duck, mushrooms, and roast chicken, and rescues 'one bottle for the whole table' negotiations nightly. New World fluency: Willamette (nervy), Sonoma Coast, Central Otago (dark and plush). If the food list leans poultry, pork, mushroom, and fatty fish, Pinot's the by-the-glass workhorse; price it like the friend it is.

  3. Syrah, Grenache & the Rhône School Wine & Beverage

    The Rhône's two voices. SYRAH (northern Rhône: Côte-Rôtie, Hermitage; 'Shiraz' in Australia's louder accent): blackberry, black pepper, olive, smoked meat, the wine that tastes like the grill it belongs next to (lamb, sausages, anything charred: match smoke with savory). GRENACHE (Garnacha in Spain): strawberry-spiced, high-alcohol warmth: the G in GSM blends and the engine of Châteauneuf-du-Pape and Priorat, brilliant with braises, cassoulet-class richness, and herbes-de-Provence anything. The school's pairing signature is SAVORINESS: where Napa fruit flatters, the Rhône's pepper-and-iron shakes hands with the char. Peppercorn-crusted anything: Syrah, close the book.

  4. Tempranillo, Sangiovese & Nebbiolo (The Food-Wine Trinity) Wine & Beverage

    Three Old-World reds built by their cuisines. TEMPRANILLO (Rioja, Ribera del Duero): red fruit and leather under American-oak coconut-vanilla, graded by age (crianza, reserva, gran reserva), lamb and jamón's companion. SANGIOVESE (Chianti Classico, Brunello): sour cherry, herbs, and acid HIGH enough to marry tomato sauce, the eternal answer to red-sauce pairing questions, engineered by centuries of the same dinner table. NEBBIOLO (Barolo, Barbaresco): 'tar and roses', pale color hiding ferocious tannin and acid, demanding fat and years, rewarded by brasato, truffles, and aged cheese. The shared lesson: Europe's greatest food wines have acid FIRST, because they evolved at the table, not the tasting bench.

  5. Malbec, Zinfandel & the New World Crowd-Pleasers Wine & Beverage

    The by-the-glass workhorses of a thousand menus, respected honestly. MALBEC: Cahors' old grape reborn in Mendoza's altitude: plush violet-dark fruit, soft tannin, the steakhouse pour that made Argentina's asado a global pairing cliché because it works. ZINFANDEL (Italy's Primitivo, same grape): brambly, jammy, peppery, high-octane: barbecue's wine, where its sweetness-of-fruit meets the sauce's sugar as an equal (a real pairing principle: match the sauce's sweetness or the wine tastes sour). Both teach the list-builder's truth: 'crowd-pleaser' is not an insult; it is a margin engine that funds the Barolo's shelf space.
