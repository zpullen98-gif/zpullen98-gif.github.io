The Board: Formula and Families

After this you can You can build or explain a board (3-5 cheeses spanning milk, texture and volume, 30-50g a head, always at room temperature an hour out) and place any cheese into fresh / bloomy / washed / semi-hard-to-hard / blue. You know why halloumi holds its shape and gruyère flows.

  1. Cheese Families Dairy, Cheese & Pantry

    A working taxonomy for menus and boards: FRESH (mozzarella, ricotta, chèvre, paneer, queso fresco: no aging, eat now); BLOOMY RIND (brie, camembert: white Penicillium rind ripening inward); WASHED RIND (taleggio, époisses: brine/booze-washed, orange, gloriously stinky, meeker inside than the smell); SEMI-HARD to HARD (gouda, manchego, cheddar, gruyère, and the grating giants parmigiano and pecorino: crystalline crunch = aged tyrosine, a feature); BLUE (roquefort, stilton, gorgonzola: Penicillium roqueforti veins). Melting is science: young, moist, low-acid cheeses flow (gruyère, fontina, young cheddar); aged and acid-set cheeses (halloumi, paneer, feta) hold shape or break. Choose by physics, not affection.

  2. Building the Cheese Board & Cave Basics Cheese Atlas

    The working formula: 3–5 cheeses spanning milk (cow/goat/sheep), texture (fresh→soft→semi→hard→blue), and volume (mild→wild); 30–50g per person alongside other food; ALWAYS at room temperature: one hour out, minimum, since cold mutes fat-borne flavor. Accompaniments argue with purpose: honey with blues, membrillo with sheep, cornichons with Alpine, nothing at all with a perfect Comté. Storage is a mini-affinage program: cheese paper or parchment-then-loose-plastic (never sealed plastic directly: cheese breathes), the vegetable drawer's humidity, cut faces rewrapped fresh. Serve in wedges that give every guest rind-to-core; the point of a wedge is the youngest, mildest bite: board etiquette's one real rule is don't cut off the nose.
