What Is in the Bottle

After this you can For any well or call order you can say what the spirit is made from, roughly what it tastes like, and what it substitutes for, across whiskey, Scotch/Irish/world whisky, gin, vodka, rum, tequila and mezcal, brandy, vermouth, amari, the liqueur shelf, bitters and absinthe.

  1. Bourbon, Rye & American Whiskey Cocktail & Bar

    America's grain laws in a glass. BOURBON: by statute ≥51% corn, NEW charred-oak barrels, no coloring or flavoring: sweet vanilla-caramel from the corn and the char (the barrel is doing half the work; 'straight' means ≥2 years, no additives). RYE: ≥51% rye grain: spicier, drier, black-pepper-and-mint edges; the ORIGINAL whiskey of classic cocktails (a pre-Prohibition Manhattan or Old Fashioned assumed rye's bite, and modern bars have restored it). TENNESSEE: bourbon's rules plus the Lincoln County charcoal mellowing. Cocktail doctrine: bourbon rounds and sweetens a drink; rye sharpens and dries it: stock both and taste the same Manhattan twice to learn the lesson permanently. Bottled-in-Bond (100 proof, one distillery, one season, 4+ years) is the value-hunter's badge on any shelf.

  2. Scotch, Irish & World Whisky Cocktail & Bar

    The malt world beyond the corn belt. SCOTCH: malted barley, used barrels (nuance over vanilla), 3+ years in Scotland: SINGLE MALT (one distillery, pot stills) vs. BLENDED (malt + grain whisky — the volume standard, unfairly snubbed); PEAT is a choice, not a definition: Islay's smoke-iodine (Laphroaig school) vs. unpeated Speyside's orchard-honey (Glenlivet school) are both Scotch, and 'I don't like Scotch' usually means 'I met peat first.' IRISH: triple-distilled smoothness, the pot-still category's creamy spice — the toddy and Irish coffee's engine. JAPANESE: Scottish method, Japanese precision; balance as a style. Cocktail rule: blended Scotch mixes (Penicillin, Rob Roy); prestige single malts mostly shouldn't. Their price buys nuance a shake destroys. Highballs are the exception that proves everything: Japan built a ritual on whisky-and-soda done perfectly.

  3. Gin & the Botanical Family Cocktail & Bar

    Vodka that went to botany school: neutral spirit redistilled with botanicals, JUNIPER by law in the lead (the spice wing's conifer berry, now in charge). The styles ladder: LONDON DRY, the cocktail workhorse (Beefeater, Tanqueray): juniper-forward, bone-dry, the spec assumed by a century of recipes; PLYMOUTH: softer, earthier; OLD TOM: lightly sweetened, the historical bridge (Tom Collins' namesake); GENEVER: the malty Dutch ancestor, closer to light whiskey; NAVY STRENGTH: 57%+, built to punch through juice; contemporary/'New Western' gins dial juniper down for cucumber, rose, citrus (Hendrick's school). Bar doctrine: the Martini, Negroni, and G&T are gin DELIVERY SYSTEMS — when a guest 'hates gin,' pour the style they haven't met; there are more gins than there are palates that hate all of them.

  4. Vodka, Honestly Cocktail & Bar

    The neutral spirit, given its fair hearing: defined by what it lacks (distilled to near-purity, filtered toward flavorlessness), which is precisely its job; vodka carries OTHER flavors without editorializing: the Moscow Mule's ginger, the espresso martini's coffee, the Bloody Mary's entire spice rack, clean infusions (the bar's house-infusion program starts here: horseradish, citrus peel, chile). The honest takes: base ingredient (wheat, potato, corn, grape) matters less than marketing claims and more than zero; texture differences (oily vs. crisp) are real but subtle; premium pricing buys bottle design more than blind-tasting victories. Where it earns respect: the freezer-cold shot with caviar or pickled herring (the garde manger wing nods), the Vesper's supporting role, and every drink where the point is everything except the spirit. A bar needs one good workhorse and one prestige label; the rest is glass.

  5. Rum: Light, Aged, Agricole & Funk Cocktail & Bar

    The most diverse spirit category on the shelf, sugarcane's children, split by raw material and attitude. MOLASSES RUMS: light/white (the daiquiri's clean engine), aged Spanish-style (Cuban/Puerto Rican smoothness), rich English-style (Jamaican and Guyanese weight). RHUM AGRICOLE (Martinique, AOC): fresh cane JUICE, not molasses: grassy, vegetal, funky-bright; the ti' punch tradition. JAMAICAN FUNK: high-ester pot-still rums (Smith & Cross school) tasting of overripe banana and flambéed everything — 'hogo' is the term of art. OVERPROOF: 57–75%, tiki's flame and backbone. CACHAÇA: Brazil's cane spirit, the caipirinha's soul (see the drink wing). Blending doctrine from tiki (next entries): rums COMBINE like no other spirit; a mai tai's split base is two rums arguing productively. Age statements lie more here than anywhere (solera math, sugar additions); taste, don't read.

  6. Tequila & Mezcal Cocktail & Bar

    Agave's two voices, governed by Mexican law. TEQUILA: blue Weber agave, Jalisco region: the ONLY label worth buying says '100% de agave' (mixto's cheaper sugars fund worse mornings); the age ladder: BLANCO (unaged: pure pepper-citrus agave; the margarita and paloma's correct engine), REPOSADO (2–12 months oak: softened, vanilla-edged), AÑEJO (1–3 years: sipping territory), cristalino (añejo filtered clear: fashion). MEZCAL: any of dozens of agaves, traditionally pit-ROASTED (the smoke is the terroir), village-made in Oaxaca: espadín the standard, wild tobalá and tepeztate the collector's shelf; sipped from copitas with orange and worm salt, or subbed 1:1 into any tequila classic for an instantly smokier drink (the mezcal Negroni and mezcal margarita are modern staples). Service note: the lime-and-shot slam is for spring break; agave spirits at this level are sipped like whisky, and the list should price and describe them that way.

  7. Brandy: Cognac, Armagnac, Calvados & Pisco Cocktail & Bar

    Distilled fruit, the original cocktail spirit (pre-phylloxera, the 1800s golden age ran on it). COGNAC: double-distilled grape brandy from its namesake region; the letters decode age minimums (VS (2yr), VSOP (4), XO (10)) and the Sidecar, Sazerac's ancestor, and French 75's original spec all called for it. ARMAGNAC: Gascony's single-distilled country cousin: rustic, richer, vintage-dated, and chronically the value play. CALVADOS: Normandy's apple (and pear) brandy: the trou normand mid-feast shot, and autumn cocktails' secret. PISCO: Peru and Chile's grape brandy, unaged and aromatic, the pisco sour's national engine (both nations claim it; serve both, smile). SPANISH BRANDY (sherry casks) and American apple brandy (Laird's bonded, the Jack Rose) complete the shelf. The revival thesis: brandy cocktails are underpriced classics; a Sidecar done right converts the whiskey table.

  8. Vermouth & the Fortified Aperitif Shelf Cocktail & Bar

    Wine, fortified and botanically infused, the modifier that makes the aristocrat cocktails possible, and the most abused bottle in home bars. The two poles: DRY (French, Dolin school): pale, crisp, herbal, the Martini's partner; SWEET/ROSSO (Italian, Carpano Antica, Punt e Mes): vanilla-spiced, bittersweet, the Manhattan and Negroni's third voice; BLANC/BIANCO splits the difference sweetly. THE LAW EVERY GUEST DESERVES: vermouth is WINE. It oxidizes; refrigerate after opening, kill or cook with it in a month or two (the dusty room-temperature bottle explains every 'I hate vermouth' at the bar). The cousins: QUINQUINAS (Lillet, Cocchi Americano, the Vesper's secret) and AMERICANOS bring quinine's bitter thread; dry sherry moonlights as a vermouth understudy (the wine wing waves). Respect the modifier and every stirred classic improves a full grade.

  9. Amari & the Bitter Liqueurs Cocktail & Bar

    Italy's bitter medicine cabinet, now the modern bar's spine: herbs, roots, barks, and citrus infused into sweetened spirit, arranged by bitterness. The APERITIVO end (lighter, brighter, before dinner): APEROL (orange, gentle: the spritz's engine), CAMPARI (grapefruit-bitter authority: the Negroni's red heart), Cappelletti, Select. The AMARO middle: Nonino (elegant), Averna (caramel-soft), Montenegro (rose-orange), Ramazzotti, sipped neat after dinner as DIGESTIVI, the culture the aperitivo hour bookends. The deep end: FERNET-Branca (menthol-bitter, 'the bartender's handshake'), Malört (Chicago's hazing ritual). CYNAR (artichoke!) bridges categories and solves the wine wing's artichoke pairing problem from inside the glass. Program note: amari are low-cost, high-margin, high-story pours; an after-dinner amaro cart is the beverage program's dessert trolley, and it sells exactly like one.

  10. The Liqueur Shelf: Orange, Maraschino, Chartreuse & Friends Cocktail & Bar

    The sweetened specialists that make classics classic. ORANGE (the most-reached-for family): triple sec/dry curaçao. COINTREAU is the margarita and Sidecar's clean standard; GRAND MARNIER adds cognac's weight (the 'Cadillac' upcharge with an actual reason). MARASCHINO (Luxardo): funky cherry-almond from whole crushed cherries — the Last Word, Aviation, Hemingway daiquiri's secret handshake; nothing like the neon jar. CHARTREUSE: the 130-herb Carthusian monk liqueur, green (herbal fire) and yellow (honeyed), the Last Word's other pillar, chronically allocated, priced like the monks know it. ST-GERMAIN (elderflower: 'bartender's ketchup', affectionately), amaretto (the amaretto sour's redemption arc runs through egg white and bourbon), coffee liqueur (espresso martini economics), crème de violette (the Aviation's sky). Doctrine: liqueurs are SEASONING: quarter- and half-ounces that define drinks; buy the real specs, because the drink is named after what's in it.

  11. Bitters: The Bar's Spice Rack Cocktail & Bar

    Concentrated botanical tinctures dosed in dashes: the salt and pepper of drink-making, and the fastest route from 'mixed drink' to 'cocktail' (the word's original 1806 definition REQUIRED them: spirit, sugar, water, bitters). The trinity: ANGOSTURA (Trinidad's clove-cinnamon universal: the Old Fashioned and Manhattan's signature; also a secret savory weapon: dashes into braises and whipped cream both work), PEYCHAUD'S (New Orleans' lighter, anise-cherry voice: the Sazerac's hometown requirement; the recipe wing's city chapter nods), ORANGE bitters (the pre-Prohibition Martini's missing piece, restored). Beyond: celery (savory gin drinks), mole, tiki 'falernum'-adjacent spice blends, house-made programs (high-proof spirit + the spice wing's inventory = signature bitters at pennies). Two dashes is a spec; four is a choice; a bottle lasts years: the highest flavor-per-dollar ratio in the entire building.

  12. Absinthe & the Rinse Cocktail & Bar

    The green fairy, demythologized: a high-proof anise-wormwood-fennel spirit (the spice wing's licorice line at full volume). The 'hallucinogen' history was bad science and worse politics (thujone levels were never the story; the ban was wine-industry lobbying wearing a lab coat), and it has been legal again for decades. Service traditions: the FOUNTAIN drip, iced water slowly clouding it into the LOUCHE (anise oils blooming opalescent; sugar cube optional, fire theatrical and mostly touristic). The cocktail role that matters nightly: the RINSE: a few drops swirled to coat the glass and dumped, perfuming without dominating: the Sazerac's defining step (rinse, never pour), Corpse Reviver No. 2's whisper, the Chrysanthemum's rare starring turn. Substitute pastis or Herbsaint (New Orleans' own) in a pinch. One bottle lasts a year of rinses and announces a serious bar the moment it appears.
