The Whites You Will Pour

After this you can Three sentences on Chardonnay, Sauvignon Blanc and Riesling (what it is, what it tastes like, what dish it loves) and you know Riesling is the answer to chile heat and Chardonnay's oak level must match the butter level.

  1. Chardonnay: The Shape-Shifter Wine & Beverage

    The world's most planted white and its least fixed identity: Chardonnay is a canvas for winemaking: CHABLIS (no/neutral oak, high acid, oyster-shell) at one pole; golden, barrel-fermented, full-malo California at the other; white Burgundy's Meursault and Puligny threading the needle. Pairing runs the same spectrum: unoaked = raw bar, ceviche, goat cheese; oaked = lobster with butter, roast chicken, cream sauces, and the great congruent pairing, buttery wine, buttery dish. The staff-training script writes itself: 'Do you like Chardonnay?' is a broken question; 'crisp and stony, or rich and golden?' sells the right glass every time.

  2. Sauvignon Blanc & the Aromatic Whites Wine & Beverage

    SAUVIGNON BLANC: grass, gooseberry, grapefruit, and blade-sharp acid; Sancerre and Pouilly-Fumé's mineral school vs. Marlborough's passionfruit megaphone; THE goat-cheese wine (the Loire pairing that defines 'what grows together'), and the herb-friendly white where basil, cilantro, and asparagus (a famous wine-killer) find a friend. The wider aromatic bench every list needs: ALBARIÑO (Rías Baixas: saline, the shellfish specialist), GRÜNER VELTLINER (white pepper, the schnitzel and vegetable-tasting-menu ace), PINOT GRIS/GRIGIO (Alsace rich vs. Italian brisk: same grape, two jobs), GEWÜRZTRAMINER (lychee-rose, low acid: Munster cheese and Alsatian richness). Aromatics are your green-vegetable insurance policy.

  3. Riesling: The Sommelier's Secret Weapon Wine & Beverage

    The most misunderstood great grape: assumed sweet, often bone-dry, always electric with acid: Mosel's featherweight off-dry (the Kabinett-Spätlese-Auslese ripeness ladder), Alsace and Austria's dry power, Clare Valley's lime-and-petrol precision (that kerosene note is TDN, a badge of the grape, not a flaw). Its superpower is the hardest tables: SPICE; low alcohol + touch of sugar + high acid is the exact formula chili heat demands (Thai, Sichuan, Indian: Riesling is the answer sommeliers give before the question ends), plus pork, choucroute, and anything sweet-salty. If your menu carries real heat and your list carries no Riesling, the list is understaffed.
