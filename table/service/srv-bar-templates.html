The Cocktail Templates

After this you can You can answer "what's in it?" for anything a guest orders by name, because you know the seven templates every classic collapses into: sour, old fashioned, the stirred aristocrats, highball/collins, equal-parts negroni, tiki, and the hot drinks.

  1. The Sour Template Cocktail & Bar

    The most productive formula in drinks: SPIRIT + CITRUS + SWEET, the classic spec orbiting 2 : ¾–1 : ¾–1 (ounces). Learn it once and you hold fifty drinks: 2:1:1 with rum and lime is a DAIQUIRI (the bartender's litmus drink, nowhere to hide), with tequila and lime plus orange liqueur splitting the sweet role it's a MARGARITA, with whiskey and lemon a WHISKEY SOUR (egg white optional and encouraged, the dry-shake entry), with gin and lemon topped with soda a TOM COLLINS or FIZZ, with cognac and orange liqueur a SIDECAR, champagne-topped a FRENCH 75. The engineering insight: sour drinks are ACID-BALANCE exercises (the seasoning wing's salt-acid axis, poured). Adjust sweet in eighth-ounces against the day's limes, taste with a straw-thumb sip before straining, EVERY time (citrus varies; specs are starting points; palates are the instrument). Master the template, then hear it everywhere: half the modern craft menu is a sour wearing a costume, and now you can read the seams.

  2. The Old Fashioned Template Cocktail & Bar

    The original definition of 'cocktail' (1806: spirit, sugar, water, bitters) still poured nightly under its own name: SPIRIT + SUGAR + BITTERS + DILUTION: 2oz whiskey (the rye-vs-bourbon fork from the whiskey entry), a quarter-ounce rich demerara syrup (or the muddled cube tradition: slower, grittier, beloved), 2–3 dashes Angostura, stirred over ice, big cube, orange twist EXPRESSED (the garnish entry's oil law). The template's power is substitution: swap the spirit and it travels: aged rum (the rum old fashioned), añejo tequila, apple brandy; swap the sugar: maple, honey; swap the bitters: mole, orange; FAT-WASH the spirit (bacon-fat bourbon: the modern classic Benton's Old Fashioned, the charcuterie wing crashing the bar) and the drink absorbs a kitchen's imagination. The philosophy it teaches: a cocktail is a spirit FRAMED, not masked: the Old Fashioned is the tasting-menu of templates: minimal moves, total exposure, no place for a flawed pour to hide. The fruit-salad muddled version of the mid-century is history's cautionary garnish.

  3. Martini & Manhattan: The Stirred Aristocrats Cocktail & Bar

    The two thrones of the mixing glass: spirit plus vermouth, character by ratio. The MARTINI: gin (classically) + dry vermouth, the ratio as personality test: 2:1 (wet, historical, vermouth-forward), 4–5:1 (the modern dry standard), the 'in-and-out' rinse (barely-there), 50/50 (the low-proof revival's darling); orange bitters restore the pre-Prohibition spec; olive or twist sets the axis (the Gibson's onion, the dirty's brine); vodka is the guest's right and gin is the drink's history. The MANHATTAN: rye (the whiskey entry's original claim) + sweet vermouth + Angostura at 2:1:2-dashes: the PERFECT variant splits the vermouth dry/sweet (a term worth teaching the floor: 'perfect' is a spec, not a compliment), the Rob Roy swaps in Scotch, the black Manhattan swaps vermouth for Averna (the amaro entry cashing in). Shared law: stirred cold and long (the shake-vs-stir entry), served UP in chilled steel-cold glass, and built from refrigerated vermouth: these two drinks are the fastest audit of any bar's discipline: order one, watch, and know everything.

  4. Highballs & the Collins Family Cocktail & Bar

    The tall drink as craft object: SPIRIT + CARBONATION over ice in the highball's narrow chimney (the glassware entry's bubble-protection physics), at ratios near 1:2–1:3. The Japanese HIGHBALL ritual rebuilt the category's dignity: hard clear ice to the brim, whisky poured and stirred to chill FIRST, soda added down the glass wall (never onto ice directly: every rough pour is carbonation spent), one respectful stir, done: whisky-soda as tea ceremony, and the reason Toki-and-soda conquered izakayas worldwide. The family: G&T (Spain's gin-tonica balloon-glass school botanicals it into an event), the COLLINS (the sour template plus soda: the bridge entry between families), the PALOMA (tequila-grapefruit: Mexico's actual favorite tequila drink), the MULE (vodka-ginger beer in copper: the mug is marketing that works), the AMERICANO (Campari-vermouth-soda: the Negroni's gentler parent), and the SPRITZ (next entry). Carbonation doctrine: cold liquids hold bubbles (chill everything), open bottles die fast (small bottles for service), and the highball rewards precision exactly because it looks like it doesn't.

  5. The Negroni & Equal-Parts Architecture Cocktail & Bar

    The 1:1:1 masterpiece: gin, Campari, sweet vermouth in equal ounces: bitter, sweet, botanical in perfect civic balance, stirred over ice with an orange twist, and the gateway through which the aperitivo entry's whole bitter world recruits new citizens (the first Negroni is a shock; the third is a habit; the tenth is a personality). The architecture generalizes into a template family: swap gin for bourbon (BOULEVARDIER: winter's Negroni), for rye (Old Pal, with dry vermouth), for prosecco (SBAGLIATO: the 'mistaken' Negroni, lately famous), for mezcal (the smoke variant the agave entry promised), or go WHITE (Suze or Cocchi Americano builds the bianco). The equal-parts idea extends beyond bitters: the LAST WORD (gin, green Chartreuse, maraschino, lime at ¾ each, the liqueur shelf's résumé) proved four-way equality works and spawned the Paper Plane (bourbon, Aperol, Nonino, lemon, the modern classic built the same way). The lesson for drink development: equal parts is a DESIGN CONSTRAINT that forces ingredient quality. Nothing hides, everything votes, and the template rewards exactly the bottles this module taught you to stock.

  6. Tiki & the Rum Canon Cocktail & Bar

    Mid-century America's baroque rum fantasy. And beneath the mugs and mystery, serious technique: Don the Beachcomber and Trader Vic built drinks on SPLIT BASES (two-plus rums per drink, the rum entry's blending doctrine: Jamaican funk + aged depth + overproof spine, each doing a job no single bottle does), fresh juice at volume (the citrus program at full sprint), spiced syrups (falernum, cinnamon, the orgeat the syrup entry promised), and CRUSHED ice as a spec'd ingredient (the dilution entry's maximum setting, flash-blended or swizzled). The canon: the MAI TAI (rum, lime, orgeat, curaçao: Vic's masterpiece, and nothing like the pineapple slushes wearing its name), the ZOMBIE (the Beachcomber's three-rum, famously-limited-to-two legend, its recipe kept in code from his own bartenders), the JUNGLE BIRD (Campari crashing the luau, tiki's amaro handshake), painkiller, hurricane (the New Orleans wing waves). The lessons that outlast the kitsch: layered base spirits, syrup R&D, and theatrical service are all TRANSFERABLE; tiki is the bar's pastry section: maximum technique wearing maximum whimsy.

  7. Hot Drinks: Toddy, Irish Coffee & the Warm Service Cocktail & Bar

    Winter's category, engineered. The HOT TODDY template: spirit + honey/sugar + lemon + hot water (tea's optional). Whiskey's classic, rum's grog lineage, brandy's sickbed tradition; the law is water HOT but sub-boiling (boiling water flashes off aroma and scalds honey's nuance; the tea wing's temperature discipline applies). IRISH COFFEE, the category's masterpiece, is a spec, not a suggestion: hot strong coffee + Irish whiskey + sugar (it's structural: the cream floats ON dissolved sugar's density) + lightly-whipped cream floated over a spoon (the float entry's signature move; the recipe wing's Buena Vista ritual perfected it at scale: 2,000 a day in glass goblets). The service physics: PREHEAT the glass (a cold glass murders a hot drink in a minute: the chill law's mirror image), preheat with the same water that makes the drink, and mind proof (alcohol's vapor rises aggressively from hot liquid: lower-proof specs drink better warm). Mulled wine, hot buttered rum (batch the butter), and the café brûlot's flaming New Orleans theater complete a shelf that sells itself every time the temperature drops.
