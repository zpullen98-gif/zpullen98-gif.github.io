The Italian Board

After this you can One sentence per cheese on the eleven Italian entries, and you can tell a guest why the crunch in aged Parmigiano is a feature, when Grana Padano is the right substitution and when it is not, and the difference between Gorgonzola dolce and piccante.

  1. Parmigiano-Reggiano Cheese Atlas

    Raw cow's milk, Emilia-Romagna, DOP-protected and made essentially unchanged for 800 years, the 'king of cheeses' and the umami keystone of the Western pantry. Aged 12–36+ months; the crunchy crystals are tyrosine, a badge of age, not salt. 24-month is the all-rounder; 36-month turns deep and brothy. Break it with the almond-shaped knife (never slice: the crag holds aroma), grate it fresh only, and simmer every rind you ever produce in a soup or ragù: the rind is a stock cube the cheese throws in free.

  2. Grana Padano Cheese Atlas

    Parmigiano's larger-production Po Valley cousin: also DOP, also a hard grana (grainy) cow's-milk wheel, but from a wider zone, allowed silage feed, and typically aged shorter. Milder, a touch sweeter, meaningfully cheaper, which is why working Italian kitchens grate Grana daily and save Parmigiano for the table. Knowing when each belongs is a food-cost decision wearing a tasting note: Grana melts into risotto and fillings; Reggiano finishes the plate where its name is spoken.

  3. Pecorino Romano Cheese Atlas

    Sheep's milk ('pecora' = ewe), salty and sharp with a white-pepper bite: Rome's grating cheese and the non-negotiable soul of carbonara, cacio e pepe, and amatriciana, where mellow Parmigiano would whisper when the dish needs a shout. Most is now made in Sardinia to Roman specification. Its salt is loud: season the dish AFTER the pecorino goes in. The broader pecorino family (Toscano, Sardo: younger, nuttier, table cheeses) is a different, gentler conversation.

  4. Mozzarella di Bufala Campana Cheese Atlas

    Water-buffalo milk from Campania, DOP, a pasta filata (stretched-curd) cheese: curds bathed in hot water and pulled into elastic skeins, then balled. Richer, tangier, and creamier than cow's-milk fior di latte, weeping milky whey at the cut. Serve at room temperature, never refrigerator-cold, within days of making; it is a fresh product with a clock. On pizza, its water is the trade-off: fior di latte for the oven, bufala torn on after, the Neapolitan compromise both camps accept.

  5. Burrata Cheese Atlas

    Puglia's 20th-century invention and the menu's modern darling: a mozzarella pouch tied around stracciatella, cream-soaked shreds of the same curd, so the knife releases a slow flood. 'Burrata' means buttered, and it eats that way. It is a vehicle for contrast: something acidic (tomatoes, citrus, agrodolce), something crunchy, good oil, flaky salt. Serve at room temperature the day you buy it; day-three burrata is a fine mozzarella having an identity crisis. Restaurant math note: one ball, halved, plates two appetizers.

  6. Ricotta Cheese Atlas

    'Recooked': traditionally not a cheese at all but a by-product: whey left from cheesemaking reheated until its remaining proteins flock into sweet, fluffy curds. Fresh ricotta (sheep's-whey Roman ricotta is the benchmark) bears no resemblance to the grainy supermarket tub: seek basket ricotta or make it (whole milk + acid, 20 minutes). Drain it overnight for cannoli and fillings: wet ricotta is the assassin of Sicilian pastry. RICOTTA SALATA is the pressed, salted, aged version: snowy, firm, grated over pasta alla Norma.

  7. Mascarpone Cheese Atlas

    Lombardy's cultured cream 'cheese': cream thickened with an acid, 60–75% butterfat, closer to solidified crème fraîche than to anything with rennet. The engine of tiramisù, the enricher of risotto and polenta at the finish, and the pastry kitchen's secret whipped-cream stabilizer (fold a spoonful into cream and it holds for hours). It splits if overbeaten: fold, don't whip hard. Substituting cream cheese changes the dessert's accent from Milan to Philadelphia; sometimes acceptable, never identical.

  8. Gorgonzola (Dolce & Piccante) Cheese Atlas

    Italy's great cow's-milk blue, in two temperaments the menu should name: DOLCE, young, spoonably soft, sweet-creamy with a gentle blue hum, the risotto and pizza blue; PICCANTE (naturale), aged, firmer, crumbly, assertively spicy-blue for the board and the steak. The gnocchi-gorgonzola-walnut triangle and the pear pairing are classics because the sweetness completes the salt. When a recipe just says 'gorgonzola,' the texture it expects tells you which one it meant.

  9. Taleggio Cheese Atlas

    Lombardy's square washed-rind, pungent orange exterior, improbably gentle interior: fruity, beefy, yeasty, melting like a dream. The washed-rind paradox in one cheese: it smells like a locker room and tastes like buttered mushrooms, which makes it the perfect gateway stinker for a cheese program. Melts gloriously into polenta, risotto, and grilled cheese for adults. The rind is edible and opinionated; serve it on and let guests negotiate.

  10. Fontina Val d'Aosta Cheese Atlas

    Alpine raw cow's milk from Italy's mountainous northwest: earthy, nutty, with a faint mushroom must under a brushed rind, and one of the great melting cheeses of the world: the heart of fonduta (Italy's fondue, crowned with white truffle when the gods allow). Its meltability is textbook physics (moist, buttery, moderate acid), which is why 'fontina-style' fills every panini case; the Danish and Swedish versions are milder stand-ins. For a French-onion-soup-level gratin moment with an Italian accent, this is the wheel.

  11. Provolone (Dolce & Piccante) Cheese Atlas

    Southern-born, northern-made pasta filata aged into wheels, torpedoes, and the giant gourds hanging in salumerie. DOLCE (young, gentle) is the sandwich slice; PICCANTE, made with goat or lamb rennet whose enzymes sharpen with age, bites back and belongs on the board and in serious cheesesteak arguments. Provoleta, the Argentine grilling round from this family, crisps outside and pulls inside (see the recipe wing). Smoked provola is the mozzarella family's campfire cousin.
