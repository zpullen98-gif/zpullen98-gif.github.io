Kotleta po-Kyivsky

  • Advanced
  • 1 h 30 m
  • $$ roughly 2 of 4 for cost

rich · herb-fresh · bright · aromatic Leads with richness that coats and comforts, fresh herbs arguing happily on top, opening aromatic and staying there.

Ingredients

  • 4 chicken breasts, wing bone left on if you want the classic silhouette
  • 150g butter mashed with garlic, dill, parsley, lemon, rolled into 4 cold logs, then FROZEN
  • Flour, 3 beaten eggs, fine white breadcrumbs: double-breaded, no gaps
  • Oil + butter for deep frying
  • Mashed potatoes; a warning for your dinner guests

Deep-fry pot

Method

  1. Butterfly and pound the breasts thin between plastic: no tears; a tear is a leak.
  2. Wrap each frozen butter log completely in chicken; tuck like a burrito; chill 30 min. 30 min
  3. Flour, egg, crumbs; then egg and crumbs AGAIN; the double coat is the dam.
  4. Fry at 170°C, 8–10 min to deep gold, finish 5 min in a 180°C oven. Warn the table: the first cut sprays. 10 min

How to tell it is going right

This dish has no standard of its own yet. It is judged on the techniques it exercises: checked at the pan, while there is still something to be done about it.

Chilling to firm: the fridge as binder 48 recipes

  • It went cold before it was handled any further, and it went in spread flat and covered rather than as a warm mass that insulates its own centre.
  • It is cold through rather than cold at the edge: tested at the middle it is as firm as it is at the rim.
  • It holds its shape unsupported; it can be cut, portioned, rolled or lifted without slumping, tearing, or sticking to the hands.
  • The fat in it has gone solid: it feels firm and waxy to the fingers rather than greasy and yielding.
  • It survives the step it was chilled for, going into heat or a coating or a mould still holding its form rather than collapsing the moment it is touched.

Where it goes wrong It was given twenty minutes instead of the hours it needed, so it is firm at the rim and soft at the core: it slumps out of shape as it is portioned, and everything made from it spreads or breaks apart in the pan.

The coat before the fry: dredge, crumb, batter 80 recipes

  • The coat is continuous before it goes anywhere near the fat: held up to the light, no bare patch of food shows through anywhere, seams and thin edges included.
  • It is bonded rather than resting on top: a gentle shake sheds loose dust only, and the coat does not slide off as a sheet when the piece is handled.
  • It bubbles hard and steadily from the moment it enters the fat, and the bubbling visibly slows as it finishes; those bubbles are the surface water leaving, and a quiet entry means the fat is too cool.
  • Out and drained, it is an even gold-brown all over and it is still crisp several minutes later on a rack; a coat that softens within a minute of coming out never lost its water.
  • Broken open, there is no paste of raw flour under the crust and no hollow gap where the coat has lifted away from the food.

Where it goes wrong The fat was below temperature or the pan was crowded, so the coat drank fat instead of sealing against it: it comes out pale and heavy, greasy to the fingers, and slumps off the food as it cools.

From the pass

Kyiv's cutlet, and Ukraine will tell you so. The frozen butter log and the double breading are the two seals that keep the molten herb-butter core in until the knife releases it. Restaurants once served it with a paper frill on the bone as a handle; the geyser at first cut remains the dish's signature and its hazard.
