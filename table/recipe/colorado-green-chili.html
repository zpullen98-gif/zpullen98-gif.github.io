Colorado Green Chili

  • Intermediate
  • 2 h 30 m
  • Serves 4
  • $$

earthy · aromatic · fiery · rich Leads with an earthy, grounding base, aromatics blooming from the first bite, on a rich, rounded finish.

Ingredients

  • 1kg pork shoulder, cubed
  • 3 tbsp oil or lard
  • 1 onion, diced
  • 6 garlic cloves
  • 8 roasted Pueblo or Hatch green chiles, chopped
  • 2 tomatoes, diced
  • 3 tbsp flour
  • 1L chicken stock
  • 1 tsp cumin
  • 1 tsp oregano
  • Salt
  • Flour tortillas or smothered burritos

Allergen screen

Found: gluten

Screened for: gluten, dairy, egg, nuts, peanuts, fish, shellfish, molluscs, soy, sesame, celery, mustard, lupin, alcohol. Not screened: sulphites.

Method

  1. Brown the pork cubes hard in the oil; set aside.
  2. Sweat the onion and garlic, sprinkle in the flour, and cook 2 min to a light roux. 2 min
  3. Add the green chile, tomatoes, cumin, and oregano and cook 5 min. 5 min
  4. Return the pork with the stock and simmer 90 min until the pork is tender and the chili thickened. 90 min
  5. Season and serve as a stew or smothered over burritos and eggs.

How to tell it is going right

This dish has no standard of its own yet. It is judged on the techniques it exercises: checked at the pan, while there is still something to be done about it.

Making a roux 27 recipes

  • Taken to the stage the dish named and no further, judged against that colour (pale, blond, or brown to brick), and it got there slowly enough to have coloured evenly.
  • It is smooth and mobile: a paste with no dry pockets of flour and no lumps, loosening as it warms rather than sitting stiff in the pan.
  • The raw-flour smell has gone and been replaced by the toasted, biscuity smell of its stage, with nothing acrid behind it.
  • No black specks anywhere. One scorched fleck condemns the batch; it cannot be stirred back in and it will be tasted in the finished sauce.
  • Let down into liquid it goes smooth without lumps and thickens to the body the dish wants, leaving no floury taste on the tongue.

Where it goes wrong The heat was too high and the corners of the pan went unstirred, so it scorched at the edge; black specks run through the finished sauce and carry a bitterness that no amount of seasoning covers.

Sweating aromatics: soft, never browned 98 recipes

  • No colour anywhere: tipped under the light the pieces are translucent to pale gold, with no tan edges, no brown tips, and no browned flecks suspended in the fat.
  • The pan is quiet: a low steady hiss, not a crackle. A crackle means the moisture has gone and browning has already started.
  • The largest piece pressed against the side of the pan collapses without resistance and with no crunch left at its centre.
  • The fat is still clear and loose in the pan and the pan floor is clean: nothing caught, nothing browned on to deglaze, because there was never any fond to build.
  • It tastes sweet and mild with no raw allium bite at the back of the throat, and none of the roasted sweetness that belongs to the next technique up the heat scale.

Where it goes wrong The heat was too high and nothing covered the pan, so the pieces took colour before they softened; the finished dish carries a roasty sweetness where it wanted a clean one, and the pieces are still firm at the centre under browned edges.

From the pass

Colorado green chili is thicker and often pork-and-tomato based, distinct from New Mexico's version; a roux gives it body. Pueblo chiles are the Colorado pride; the chili is poured over everything from breakfast burritos to fries across the Front Range.
