Ethiopian · Sauce

Berbere

  • Intermediate
  • 45 min
  • $$ roughly 2 of 4 for cost
  • Vegetarian
  • Vegan

aromatic · warm-spiced · fiery · nutty-toasted Leads with aromatics blooming from the first bite, warm spice humming through the middle, closing toasted and warm.

Ingredients

  • 120 g dried mild red chillies, stems pulled and most seeds shaken out
  • 20 g dried hot red chillies
  • 10 g coriander seed
  • 8 g fenugreek seed
  • 6 g korarima seeds, husks discarded
  • 4 g ajwain
  • 3 g nigella seed
  • 3 g black peppercorn
  • 3 g cassia bark
  • 2 g clove
  • 15 g dried ginger, ground
  • 10 g dried garlic, ground
  • 5 g dried besobela leaf, crumbled
  • 12 g salt

Method

  1. Dry the chillies further in an oven at 60C (140 F) with the door ajar until they snap cleanly rather than bend, about 30 minutes. 30 min
  2. Toast the whole spices separately in a dry pan over low heat until each smells nutty and the coriander begins to pop; pull the fenugreek at the first waft of burnt sugar, because it turns bitter two seconds after it turns fragrant.
  3. Grind the toasted spices to a fine powder, then grind the chillies alone and sieve out the coarse skin.
  4. Fold in the ginger, garlic, besobela and salt, then pass the whole blend through the sieve twice so no pocket of one spice survives.
  5. Seal in a jar away from light and leave it to settle before use; it tastes flat on day one and rounded by day three.

How to tell it is going right

This dish has no standard of its own yet. It is judged on the techniques it exercises: checked at the pan, while there is still something to be done about it.

Dry-pan toasting: spices, seeds and nuts 70 recipes

  • A single layer on dry metal with nothing added: no fat, and nothing else sharing the pan or the tray and competing for the heat.
  • It is kept moving: shaken, stirred, or turned. Anything left still scorches on the face touching the metal while the rest of it is barely warm.
  • It is judged by smell before sight: once it is fragrant there are seconds left, not minutes, and the nose reaches that point well before the colour does.
  • It leaves the pan the instant it is done, onto a cold surface. A hot pan keeps toasting whatever stays in it, and carryover here is the difference between done and ruined.
  • The colour is even and light rather than patchy, with nothing black at the edges: one scorched piece taints the whole quantity it is ground into.

Where it goes wrong It was left alone for a moment while something else was attended to. Dry heat has no margin: the pieces against the metal go from fragrant to acrid inside a minute, and the bitterness cannot be picked back out of a ground mixture.

Straining & passing through a sieve 148 recipes

  • Nothing solid got through: a finger drawn through what came out finds no fibre, seed, skin, curd, or grit.
  • Where the dish wants body it was worked through rather than left to drip, and the solids left in the mesh are spent and dry rather than still holding liquid worth having.
  • It is judged on the tongue rather than by eye: smooth, with no residual grain left behind after the mouthful has gone.
  • The mesh was fine enough for the job and held its shape, and nothing bypassed it by going over the rim.
  • The yield is close to what went in less the spent solids: not half of it abandoned in the sieve because it stopped running and nobody pushed.

Where it goes wrong It was pushed through a mesh too coarse for it, or the sieve was overloaded and slopped at the rim, so the fibres it was meant to remove are in the finished sauce and it reads grainy on the tongue.

From the pass

Berbere is a bulk ingredient, not a seasoning: eight parts chilli flesh to one part aromatic, which is why a wat takes it by the spoonful. That flesh is hygroscopic, so any chilli that still bends holds enough moisture to cake the blend and grow mould within a month. Dry them to a snap first. Fenugreek is the other trap; toast it a shade too far and the bitterness never leaves.
