Ethiopian · Sauce

Berbere

  • Intermediate
  • 45 min
  • Serves 4
  • $$
  • Vegetarian
  • Vegan

aromatic · warm-spiced · fiery · nutty-toasted Leads with aromatics blooming from the first bite, warm spice humming through the middle, closing toasted and warm.

Ingredients

  • 120 g dried mild red chillies, stems pulled and most seeds shaken out
  • 20 g dried hot red chillies
  • 10 g coriander seed
  • 8 g fenugreek seed
  • 6 g korarima seeds, husks discarded
  • 4 g ajwain
  • 3 g nigella seed
  • 3 g black peppercorn
  • 3 g cassia bark
  • 2 g clove
  • 15 g dried ginger, ground
  • 10 g dried garlic, ground
  • 5 g dried besobela leaf, crumbled
  • 12 g salt

Allergen screen

None found among the 14 screened.

Screened for: gluten, dairy, egg, nuts, peanuts, fish, shellfish, molluscs, soy, sesame, celery, mustard, lupin, alcohol. Not screened: sulphites.

Method

  1. Dry the chillies further in an oven at 60C (140 F) with the door ajar until they snap cleanly rather than bend, about 30 minutes. 30 min
  2. Toast the whole spices separately in a dry pan over low heat until each smells nutty and the coriander begins to pop; pull the fenugreek at the first waft of burnt sugar, because it turns bitter two seconds after it turns fragrant.
  3. Grind the toasted spices to a fine powder, then grind the chillies alone and sieve out the coarse skin.
  4. Fold in the ginger, garlic, besobela and salt, then pass the whole blend through the sieve twice so no pocket of one spice survives.
  5. Seal in a jar away from light and leave it to settle before use; it tastes flat on day one and rounded by day three.

From the pass

Berbere is a bulk ingredient, not a seasoning: eight parts chilli flesh to one part aromatic, which is why a wat takes it by the spoonful. That flesh is hygroscopic, so any chilli that still bends holds enough moisture to cake the blend and grow mould within a month. Dry them to a snap first. Fenugreek is the other trap; toast it a shade too far and the bitterness never leaves.
