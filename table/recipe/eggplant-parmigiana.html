Eggplant Parmigiana

  • Intermediate
  • 80 min
  • Serves 4
  • $$
  • Vegetarian

umami-deep · herb-fresh · aromatic · rich Leads with savory depth stacked from the pantry’s bass notes, fresh herbs arguing happily on top, on a rich, rounded finish.

Ingredients

  • 2 large eggplants, sliced 1cm
  • 800g crushed tomatoes
  • 2 garlic cloves, basil
  • 250g mozzarella, drained
  • 80g Parmigiano
  • Olive oil

Equipment

Thermometer · Deep-fry pot

The skills inside

Allergen screen

Found: dairy

Screened for: gluten, dairy, egg, nuts, peanuts, fish, shellfish, molluscs, soy, sesame, celery, mustard, lupin, alcohol. Not screened: sulphites.

Method

  1. Salt eggplant 30 min; pat dry. Fry or roast at 220°C until deeply golden. 30 min
  2. Simmer a quick garlic-basil tomato sauce, 15 min. 15 min
  3. Layer sauce, eggplant, both cheeses; repeat, ending with Parmigiano.
  4. Bake 190°C for 30 min; rest 15 min before cutting. 30 min

How to tell it is going right

This dish has no standard of its own yet. It is judged on the techniques it exercises: checked at the pan, while there is still something to be done about it.

Salting to draw the water out 15 recipes

  • Salted evenly over every surface rather than tipped on in one place and tossed once.
  • Laid out so the water has somewhere to go: a colander, a rack, or a sieve over a bowl, never a flat dish where it sits back in what it gave up.
  • Given real time, and there is visible liquid to show for it: a pool underneath, a drip, or a wrung handful.
  • That liquid is removed. It is squeezed, blotted or poured away, and the food is dry to the touch before it goes anywhere near heat.
  • The dish is seasoned knowing salt has already gone on, tasted before any more is added.

Where it goes wrong It went into the dish still wet, and was salted again out of habit. The water it was carrying dilutes and steams whatever it meets, so the result is watery and over-seasoned at once, and those are two faults that cannot be corrected in the same direction.

Roasting in a hot oven 21 recipes

  • The oven was fully up to heat before anything went in, given time beyond the indicator light rather than judged by it.
  • Every piece has air around it. Nothing touches its neighbour and nothing is packed to the edge of the tray, because crowded food steams itself in its own moisture and never browns.
  • Colour is even across the whole tray, the tray having been turned at least once: no pale row at the back and no dark row at the front.
  • It came out BEFORE the target temperature, not at it. Carryover keeps driving the inside up by several degrees after it leaves the oven, and that rise is part of the cooking rather than an error in it.
  • The temperature was read at the coldest part rather than the easiest one to reach, and read again after the rest.

Where it goes wrong The tray was crowded, or the oven was not truly up to heat. The food releases steam faster than a cool box can carry it away, so it sits pale and slack — and the extra minutes spent chasing colour take the inside well past where it should have stopped.

From the pass

Resting is the difference between clean layers and a delicious landslide: the molten mozzarella and sauce need fifteen minutes to set from liquid back to structure, exactly like a lasagne. Salting the eggplant matters less for bitterness (modern varieties have had it bred out) than for collapsing the sponge: unsalted slices drink oil by the ladleful. Naples, Sicily and Parma all claim the dish; the safest position is to claim a second helping and stay out of it.
