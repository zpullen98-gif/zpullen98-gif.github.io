New Mexico Carne Adovada

  • Intermediate
  • 3 h
  • Serves 4
  • $

aromatic · bright · earthy · fiery Leads with aromatics blooming from the first bite, acid holding everything up to the light, on a slow, honest burn.

Ingredients

  • 1.2kg pork shoulder, in large cubes
  • 10 dried New Mexico red chiles, stemmed and seeded
  • 4 garlic cloves
  • 1 tbsp cumin
  • 1 tbsp Mexican oregano
  • 1 tsp salt
  • 500ml chicken stock
  • 1 tbsp cider vinegar

Equipment

Dutch oven

Method

  1. Toast the dried chiles briefly, soak in hot water 20 min, then blend with the garlic, cumin, oregano, salt, and some soaking water into a smooth red sauce. 20 min
  2. Toss the pork cubes in the red chile sauce and marinate at least 4 hours, ideally overnight. 240 min
  3. Turn everything into a Dutch oven with the stock.
  4. Braise at 160C for 2.5-3 hours until the pork is falling apart and the sauce is deep and thick. 180 min
  5. Stir in the vinegar and season. Serve with rice, beans, or tortillas.

How to tell it is going right

This dish has no standard of its own yet. It is judged on the techniques it exercises: checked at the pan, while there is still something to be done about it.

Dry-pan toasting: spices, seeds and nuts 26 recipes

  • A single layer on dry metal with nothing added: no fat, and nothing else sharing the pan or the tray and competing for the heat.
  • It is kept moving: shaken, stirred, or turned. Anything left still scorches on the face touching the metal while the rest of it is barely warm.
  • It is judged by smell before sight: once it is fragrant there are seconds left, not minutes, and the nose reaches that point well before the colour does.
  • It leaves the pan the instant it is done, onto a cold surface. A hot pan keeps toasting whatever stays in it, and carryover here is the difference between done and ruined.
  • The colour is even and light rather than patchy, with nothing black at the edges: one scorched piece taints the whole quantity it is ground into.

Where it goes wrong It was left alone for a moment while something else was attended to. Dry heat has no margin: the pieces against the metal go from fragrant to acrid inside a minute, and the bitterness cannot be picked back out of a ground mixture.

Braising 29 recipes

  • The pieces went into the liquid already deep brown on their faces, and the pot floor gave up its fond to that liquid rather than keeping it.
  • The liquid comes partway up the pieces and stays there for the whole cook (not submerged, not run dry), and it never boils: the surface trembles and no more.
  • It went long enough for the collagen to convert: a fork twists in it with almost no resistance, and the piece still lifts out whole rather than falling into shreds.
  • The liquid has become a sauce: it has body, it coats the back of a spoon, and it tastes concentrated rather than thin and washed out.
  • The meat is moist and seasoned through to its centre, not dry fibres sitting inside a good sauce.

Where it goes wrong The oven or the burner ran too hot and the liquid boiled, so the fibres tightened and squeezed their moisture out: it shreds dry into a thin greasy liquid, and more time makes it worse rather than better.

From the pass

Pork marinated and braised in pure red chile: no tomatoes, just chile, garlic, and time. Use whole dried New Mexico chiles, not powder; the long marinade lets the chile penetrate the meat, which is the whole point of adovada.
