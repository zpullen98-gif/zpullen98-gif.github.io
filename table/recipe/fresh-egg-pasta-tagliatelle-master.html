Fresh Egg Pasta (Tagliatelle Master)

  • Intermediate
  • 1 h 30 m
  • $$ roughly 2 of 4 for cost
  • Vegetarian

umami-deep · rich Leads with savory depth stacked from the pantry’s bass notes, richness that coats and comforts.

Ingredients Method Check the result Pairings Your notes Videos

Ingredients

  • 400g 00 flour (or all-purpose, honestly fine), the Emilian ratio: 100g flour per egg
  • 4 whole eggs, the best you can buy; the yolks are the color and the character
  • Semolina for dusting; nothing else: no oil, no water, no salt in the dough (salt the water later)
  • A rolling pin or pasta machine; a bench scraper
  • 30 minutes of kneading-and-resting patience; ragù or butter and parmesan waiting

Method

  1. Flour in a mound, well in the center, eggs in the well; beat with a fork, pulling in flour gradually until a shaggy mass forms; knead 8–10 min until smooth as an earlobe and springing back from a thumb press. 10 min
  2. REST 30 min wrapped: unrested dough fights the pin and wins. 30 min
  3. Roll thin enough to read a newspaper through (the same Emilian test as strudel, chapters away); flour the sheet, roll it into a loose flat cigar, and cut 8mm ribbons; shake out the nests.
  4. Boil in aggressively salted water 60–90 seconds: fresh pasta is done almost on arrival. Sauce it in the pan with a splash of pasta water; never rinse.

How to tell it is going right

This dish has no standard of its own yet. It is judged on the techniques it exercises: checked at the pan, while there is still something to be done about it.

Keeping the cooking liquid 60 recipes

  • It was actually kept: the liquid went into a container rather than down the drain, and the volume saved is enough to be worth using.
  • It is clean: strained of solids and grit, and not clouded with the debris of whatever broke up in it.
  • It tastes of what it cooked and tastes concentrated rather than washed out, and it is not so salty or so bitter that the only honest use is the bin.
  • It was cooled quickly and stored cold, not left standing warm on the stove to be dealt with later.
  • Used, it does the job the dish gives it (carrying flavour into a sauce, loosening the dish, or setting when chilled) rather than merely adding water back.

Where it goes wrong The pot was left to cool slowly on the stove and only strained the next day, so it soured overnight: what should have been the best ingredient in the dish goes in the bin, and the recipe gets water instead.

Salted water & the float test 188 recipes

  • The water was salted before anything went in, and tasting it confirms it: seasoned on the tongue, with the salt dissolved rather than sitting on the pot floor.
  • There is enough water, hot enough, that the pot returns to its boil quickly after the food goes in and never stops moving.
  • Where the dish uses the float as its signal, the signal is obeyed: the pieces rise and they come out then, not several minutes later once something else was finished.
  • Nothing is stuck to the bottom of the pot or welded to itself, and the water has not thickened into a cloudy sludge.
  • Drained on a bite rather than on the clock: resistance at the centre, with no chalky raw core.

Where it goes wrong The pot was too small and the water unsalted, so it never recovered its boil: the food sat in slack water, went gummy at the surface, and comes out bland in a way that no sauce afterwards can fix.

From the pass

The sfogline of Bologna, the rolling-pin masters, judge a sheet by holding it to the window: light through the dough, no thin spots. One rule carries the whole craft: 100 grams of flour, one egg, repeat to scale. Fresh tagliatelle exists for ragù; the rough, porous surface of hand-rolled pasta grips sauce the way extruded dried pasta never will.
