Pennsylvania · Breakfast

Scrapple

  • Advanced
  • 4 h
  • Serves 4
  • $$

rich · earthy · gently hot · warm-spiced Leads with richness that coats and comforts, an earthy, grounding base, fading in warm spice.

Ingredients

  • 1kg pork shoulder and 500g pork liver
  • 2 bay leaves
  • 1 onion, halved
  • 2L water
  • 250g cornmeal
  • 100g buckwheat flour
  • 2 tsp salt
  • 1 tsp black pepper
  • 1 tsp sage
  • 1/2 tsp thyme
  • Pinch of cayenne and nutmeg
  • Butter for frying

The skills inside

Sautéing & the shallow fry

Allergen screen

Found: gluten · dairy

Screened for: gluten, dairy, egg, nuts, peanuts, fish, shellfish, molluscs, soy, sesame, celery, mustard, lupin, alcohol. Not screened: sulphites.

Method

  1. Simmer the pork and liver with the onion and bay 2 hours until falling apart. Strain, keeping 1.5L of the broth, and chop or grind the meat fine. 120 min
  2. Bring the broth back to a simmer and rain in the cornmeal and buckwheat, whisking hard to avoid lumps.
  3. Cook 30 min, stirring often, until very thick. Stir in the meat and all the seasonings: taste and season boldly. 30 min
  4. Pour into loaf pans, cool, and chill overnight until solid.
  5. Slice 1cm thick and fry in butter over medium heat 5-6 min a side without moving it, until a deep crust forms. 6 min

How to tell it is going right

This dish has no standard of its own yet. It is judged on the technique it exercises: checked at the pan, while there is still something to be done about it.

Sautéing & the shallow fry 34 recipes

  • Everything sits in a single layer with visible space between the pieces: nothing stacked, nothing touching, or the pan is too small for the quantity.
  • The pieces move freely and keep moving, sliding across the pan rather than sticking and tearing when the pan is worked.
  • Light even browning arrived at quickly: colour on most faces, not the deep crust of a sear and not grey.
  • A thin film of fat with the food glossed by it: no pool left in the pan at the end, and nothing greasy on the fingers or the plate.
  • Cooked through but still firm, having spent only a few minutes in the pan: anything that has gone soft has been in there too long.

Where it goes wrong Too much went in at once, the pan cooled, and the food released its water: what was meant to brown in three minutes stews for ten and comes out grey, slack and wet.

From the pass

Pennsylvania Dutch thrift at its most honest. The one rule at the stove: leave it alone in the pan. Scrapple needs an unbroken crust to hold together, and flipping early guarantees mush.
