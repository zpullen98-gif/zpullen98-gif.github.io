Tennessee · Dessert

Chess Pie

  • Intermediate
  • 75 min
  • $$ roughly 2 of 4 for cost
  • Vegetarian

rich · bright · floral · sweet-edged Leads with richness that coats and comforts, acid holding everything up to the light, trailing gentle sweetness.

Ingredients

  • 1 blind-baked 23cm pie shell (all-butter, edges crimped high, the filling rises then settles)
  • 200g sugar + 60g brown sugar
  • 4 eggs; 120g melted butter; 60ml buttermilk
  • 1 tbsp fine cornmeal + 1 tsp flour, the signature grit; 1 tbsp vinegar + 1 tsp vanilla
  • Nothing fancy; the whole pie costs a dollar in ingredients, which is the point

Method

  1. Whisk sugars, eggs, butter, buttermilk smooth; whisk in cornmeal, flour, vinegar, vanilla.
  2. Pour into the warm blind-baked shell.
  3. Bake 170°C, 40–45 min: the top forms its famous crackled sugar skin; the center should still shimmy slightly. 45 min
  4. Cool COMPLETELY: hours; chess pie sets on the counter, not in the oven. Slice thin; it is sweeter than it looks and it looks sweet.

How to tell it is going right

This dish has no standard of its own yet. It is judged on the technique it exercises: checked at the pan, while there is still something to be done about it.

Pie crust & blind baking 29 recipes

  • The shell goes into the oven cold and the oven is fully up to heat, so the structure sets before the fat has time to melt out of it.
  • Lined and weighted right into the corners rather than heaped in the middle, so the sides are held against the tin as the fat softens.
  • Docked where it is not meant to puff, and the base stays flat rather than lifting into a dome under its own steam.
  • The weights come out and the base gets dry heat of its own before anything wet arrives. Pale and damp is not baked, and a filling will not finish it.
  • The edges are set and coloured and the shell releases from the tin rather than gripping it.

Where it goes wrong It went in warm, or the weights only filled the middle. The sides slump down the tin before the pastry sets, so the case is shallower than it should be, the filling overflows it, and the base is still raw underneath: the soggy bottom, arrived at three ways at once.

From the pass

The name has three folk etymologies: 'chest pie' (it kept in the pie chest), 'jes' pie' (just pie, as the cook allegedly said), or the English 'cheese pie' lineage, and Tennessee tells all three, usually at once. The cornmeal top-skin and the vinegar's quiet counterweight are the whole recipe: pantry-bare ingredients, crackled crust, transcendent result. The buttermilk version leans tangy; the lemon version is another church entirely.
