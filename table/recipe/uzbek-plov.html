Uzbek Plov

  • Advanced
  • 2 h
  • Serves 4
  • $$

aromatic · earthy Leads with aromatics blooming from the first bite, an earthy, grounding base.

Ingredients

  • 600g lamb shoulder, large chunks, some fat; 100g lamb tail fat if you can find the real thing
  • 600g medium-grain rice (devzira if the bazaar smiles), soaked in warm salted water
  • 4 yellow carrots (or orange), cut in thick matchsticks BY HAND, never grated
  • 3 onions; 2 whole heads of garlic, unpeeled; 2 tbsp cumin seeds, 1 tbsp barberries
  • A kazan or heavy dutch oven; this dish has a vessel opinion

Equipment

Dutch oven · Grater / zester

The skills inside

Allergen screen

Screened for: gluten, dairy, egg, nuts, peanuts, fish, shellfish, molluscs, soy, sesame, celery, mustard, lupin, alcohol. Not screened: sulphites.

Method

  1. Render the fat; brown lamb hard; onions to deep gold; carrots in, folded not stirred, 10 min. 10 min
  2. Water to cover, whole garlic heads and spices in: this is the zirvak: simmer 40 min; it must taste over-seasoned; the rice will drink the excess. 40 min
  3. Drain rice; lay it OVER the zirvak: never stir from this moment; add water two knuckles above the rice.
  4. High heat until the water vanishes; pierce holes to the bottom with a spoon handle; lid on tight; lowest flame 25 min. Fluff layers together only at serving. 25 min

How to tell it is going right

This dish has no standard of its own yet. It is judged on the techniques it exercises: checked at the pan, while there is still something to be done about it.

Salted water & the float test 38 recipes

  • The water was salted before anything went in, and tasting it confirms it: seasoned on the tongue, with the salt dissolved rather than sitting on the pot floor.
  • There is enough water, hot enough, that the pot returns to its boil quickly after the food goes in and never stops moving.
  • Where the dish uses the float as its signal, the signal is obeyed: the pieces rise and they come out then, not several minutes later once something else was finished.
  • Nothing is stuck to the bottom of the pot or welded to itself, and the water has not thickened into a cloudy sludge.
  • Drained on a bite rather than on the clock: resistance at the centre, with no chalky raw core.

Where it goes wrong The pot was too small and the water unsalted, so it never recovered its boil: the food sat in slack water, went gummy at the surface, and comes out bland in a way that no sauce afterwards can fix.

Rendering fat: and cooking in what runs out 45 recipes

  • It started cold or low and came up slowly: fat that met a hot pan seizes and scorches before the inside can melt, and there is no recovering it.
  • The fat in the pan is clear and clean-smelling, pale to golden, with no dark sediment on the floor and nothing acrid in the steam.
  • What is left behind is crisp and deep gold with no soft pale fat still clinging to it and no black burnt edges.
  • There is a real volume of it: enough to film the pan floor and be poured off and kept, not a dry smear that never ran.
  • Whatever cooks in it browns in it and takes its flavour, rather than sitting in a greasy pool that never gets hot enough to colour anything.

Where it goes wrong The heat was raised to hurry it along, so the outside seized and browned before the interior fat could melt; very little runs out, and the solids come out burnt on the outside and rubbery within.

From the pass

Plov is the Uzbek national ceremony: wedding plov is cooked by masters (oshpaz) for a thousand guests from a single kazan. The zirvak is the soul and the no-stir law is absolute: the rice steams above the meat like a congregation above a sermon. Hand-cut carrots survive; grated ones vanish and take the dish's honor with them.
