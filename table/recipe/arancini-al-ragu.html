Arancini al Ragù

  • Advanced
  • 2 h
  • Serves 4
  • $$$$

rich · bright · floral · umami-deep Leads with richness that coats and comforts, acid holding everything up to the light, finishing long and savory.

Ingredients

  • Risotto base, made and CHILLED overnight: 400g arborio cooked with saffron, butter and parmesan: yesterday's risotto is the origin story and the best version
  • Heart: 200g thick beef ragù with peas, cold and scoopable; cubes of mozzarella or caciocavallo
  • Coating: flour paste (flour + water, crepe-thin) or flour-egg, then fine breadcrumbs
  • Oil for deep frying at 170°C
  • Lemon wedges; a paper cone if you are doing it properly

Equipment

Deep-fry pot

Method

  1. Wet your hands; flatten a palm of cold saffron rice; pocket a spoon of ragù and a cheese cube; close into a tight ball or the Catania cone shape.
  2. Chill the formed arancini 30 min: cold centers survive the fryer. 30 min
  3. Coat: through the flour slurry, then rolled firmly in breadcrumbs.
  4. Fry at 170°C, 4–5 min to deep orange-gold. Rest 3 min: the name means 'little oranges' and the center is molten citrus-colored lava. Lemon over; eat standing. 5 min

From the pass

Sicily's portable risotto: round in Palermo, pointed like Etna in Catania, and the east-west 'arancino vs arancina' gender war is fought annually with genuine passion. The overnight-chilled rice is structural: fresh risotto collapses; day-old rice, its starch set, holds the sphere. Bar counters across the island sell them at ten in the morning, and no one has ever needed a reason.
