Dan Dan Noodles

  • Intermediate
  • 30 min
  • Serves 4
  • $

nutty-toasted · fiery · bright · sweet-edged Leads with toasted, nutty warmth throughout, real fire that builds and stays, trailing gentle sweetness.

Ingredients

  • 400g fresh wheat noodles
  • 200g ground pork fried crisp with 1 tbsp sweet bean or hoisin
  • Per bowl: 1 tbsp sesame paste or tahini, 1 tbsp chili oil, 2 tsp soy, 1 tsp black vinegar, ½ tsp sugar, ground Sichuan pepper
  • Hot noodle water to loosen
  • Scallions, crushed peanuts, wilted greens

Allergen screen

Found: gluten · nuts · peanuts · soy · sesame

Screened for: gluten, dairy, egg, nuts, peanuts, fish, shellfish, molluscs, soy, sesame, celery, mustard, lupin, alcohol. Not screened: sulphites.

Method

  1. Build the sauce in each serving bowl.
  2. Fry the pork topping until crispy and dark.
  3. Cook noodles; loosen each sauce bowl with 3 tbsp noodle water.
  4. Noodles in, pork on top: everyone mixes their own.

From the pass

The dish is a sauce ratio, not a recipe: sesame paste, chili oil, soy, black vinegar, sugar and Sichuan pepper built in the bottom of each bowl, loosened with noodle water, learned once and riffed on forever. The crisp pork is a topping, not a sauce; its crunch against the slick noodles is the point. Named for the dan-dan shoulder pole Chengdu street vendors carried (noodles swinging from one end, sauce from the other), it was fast food a century before the term existed.
