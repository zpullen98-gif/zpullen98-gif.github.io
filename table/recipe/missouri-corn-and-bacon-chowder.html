Missouri Corn and Bacon Chowder

  • Easy
  • 45 min
  • $$ roughly 2 of 4 for cost

rich · herb-fresh Leads with richness that coats and comforts, fresh herbs arguing happily on top.

Ingredients

  • 6 ears corn, kernels and scraped cobs
  • 150g bacon, diced
  • 1 onion, diced
  • 500g potatoes, cubed
  • 750ml milk
  • 250ml cream
  • 2 tbsp butter
  • 1 tbsp flour
  • Salt, pepper, and chives

Method

  1. Simmer the stripped cobs in the milk 20 min to draw out their sweetness, then discard them. 20 min
  2. Render the bacon; lift out the bits and sweat the onion in the fat.
  3. Sprinkle in the flour, cook 1 min, add the potatoes and the corn milk, and simmer 15 min. 1 min
  4. Add the corn kernels and cream and cook 5 min more. 5 min
  5. Season, finish with butter, and scatter bacon and chives over each bowl.

How to tell it is going right

This dish has no standard of its own yet. It is judged on the techniques it exercises: checked at the pan, while there is still something to be done about it.

Rendering fat: and cooking in what runs out 73 recipes

  • It started cold or low and came up slowly: fat that met a hot pan seizes and scorches before the inside can melt, and there is no recovering it.
  • The fat in the pan is clear and clean-smelling, pale to golden, with no dark sediment on the floor and nothing acrid in the steam.
  • What is left behind is crisp and deep gold with no soft pale fat still clinging to it and no black burnt edges.
  • There is a real volume of it: enough to film the pan floor and be poured off and kept, not a dry smear that never ran.
  • Whatever cooks in it browns in it and takes its flavour, rather than sitting in a greasy pool that never gets hot enough to colour anything.

Where it goes wrong The heat was raised to hurry it along, so the outside seized and browned before the interior fat could melt; very little runs out, and the solids come out burnt on the outside and rubbery within.

Sweating aromatics: soft, never browned 198 recipes

  • No colour anywhere: tipped under the light the pieces are translucent to pale gold, with no tan edges, no brown tips, and no browned flecks suspended in the fat.
  • The pan is quiet: a low steady hiss, not a crackle. A crackle means the moisture has gone and browning has already started.
  • The largest piece pressed against the side of the pan collapses without resistance and with no crunch left at its centre.
  • The fat is still clear and loose in the pan and the pan floor is clean: nothing caught, nothing browned on to deglaze, because there was never any fond to build.
  • It tastes sweet and mild with no raw allium bite at the back of the throat, and none of the roasted sweetness that belongs to the next technique up the heat scale.

Where it goes wrong The heat was too high and nothing covered the pan, so the pieces took colour before they softened; the finished dish carries a roasty sweetness where it wanted a clean one, and the pieces are still firm at the centre under browned edges.

From the pass

A farm-country chowder that puts the state's corn front and center. Steeping the cobs in the milk first is the free step that doubles the corn flavor: the cob holds as much sweetness as the kernel.
