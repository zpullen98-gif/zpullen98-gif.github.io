The Rice Bowl · Main

Dolsot Bibimbap

  • Advanced
  • 45 min
  • $$$ roughly 3 of 4 for cost

nutty-toasted · aromatic · gently hot · smoky Leads with toasted, nutty warmth throughout, aromatics blooming from the first bite, on a drift of smoke.

Ingredients

  • 700 g cooked short grain rice, cooled to just warm
  • 200 g beef sirloin, sliced 3 mm thick
  • 20 ml soy sauce
  • 8 g sugar
  • 20 g garlic, minced
  • 45 ml toasted sesame oil, plus 40 ml for the bowls
  • 150 g spinach
  • 150 g mung bean sprouts
  • 100 g carrot, in fine matchsticks
  • 100 g courgette, in fine matchsticks
  • 100 g shiitake, sliced
  • 4 eggs
  • 60 g gochujang, loosened with 20 ml water and 10 g sugar
  • 10 g toasted sesame seeds
  • 8 g salt

Method

  1. Cook and dress each vegetable on its own: spinach blanched 30 seconds and wrung out dry, sprouts boiled 3 minutes, carrot, courgette and shiitake fried separately until just limp. Season each with a little garlic, sesame oil and salt and keep them apart. 3 min
  2. Marinate the beef 20 minutes in the soy, sugar, garlic and sesame oil, then sear it in a dry hot pan in one layer until the edges catch and darken. 20 min
  3. Set the stone bowls on a medium flame and heat them 5 minutes, until a flicked drop of water skitters and disappears on contact, then swirl 10 ml of sesame oil around the inside of each. 5 min
  4. Press the warm rice firmly against the base and walls in an even layer and leave it completely alone for 3 to 4 minutes, listening for the sizzle to tighten into a fine crackle. 4 min
  5. Lay the vegetables and beef over the top in wedges, slide on a fried egg, and carry the bowl to the table still ticking. The diner mixes in the gochujang, which lifts the crust off the stone in shards.

How to tell it is going right

This dish has no standard of its own yet. It is judged on the techniques it exercises: checked at the pan, while there is still something to be done about it.

Marinating: acid, salt and time 70 recipes

  • Contact is total: every surface is wetted, and the pieces are not welded into a clump with dry faces where they pressed together.
  • The stated time was kept and kept cold, read against a clock, because this is the one technique where longer is a different result rather than a safer one.
  • It worked at the surface and no further: cut a piece and the marinade’s colour stops a few millimetres in. A protein gone opaque and chalky to the centre before any heat reached it has been cooked by the acid.
  • The texture is still springy and whole, not mushy, mealy, or fraying at the surface when it is lifted.
  • Where the next step browns it, the surface was patted dry first: a wet surface cannot brown, and the marinade’s sugars will scorch before the food colours.

Where it goes wrong It was left well beyond the stated time on the assumption that more is better, so acid and salt worked through the thin edges: those cook to cotton and read grainy while the thick centre tastes of nothing at all.

Blanching & shocking 76 recipes

  • A large volume at a hard rolling boil, and enough of it that adding the food barely interrupts it; a pot that goes quiet has started stewing rather than blanching.
  • Timed rather than watched, and short. This is an interruption in the cooking of something, not the cooking of it.
  • The ice bath was made and standing beside the pot BEFORE anything went into the water, not started once the timer went off.
  • It went straight into the ice water, fully submerged, and stayed until cold at the centre: a piece still warm inside is still cooking, and the arrest has not happened yet.
  • Drained and dried afterwards rather than left sitting in water, so it does not carry that water into whatever it meets next.

Where it goes wrong The ice bath was not ready, so the food sat draining in a colander while its own residual heat carried on cooking it. By the time it cooled it was a stage past where it was pulled, and exact arrest is the entire reason for blanching rather than simply boiling.

From the pass

The crust, nurungji, is dextrinised starch and Maillard browning against the stone, and it needs a dry surface to start. Use rice that has stood and given off its steam, not wet rice straight from the cooker, and once it is pressed in do not touch it. A bowl that is not properly hot glues the layer on instead of browning it and the crust tears. Oil the stone and load it at once, or the sesame oil smokes and the bowl tastes bitter.
