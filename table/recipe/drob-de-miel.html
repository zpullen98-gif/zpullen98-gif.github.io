Romanian & Bulgarian · Starter

Drob de Miel

  • Advanced
  • 60 min
  • $$$ roughly 3 of 4 for cost

herb-fresh · rich Leads with fresh herbs arguing happily on top, richness that coats and comforts.

Ingredients

  • 800 g lamb pluck (liver, heart, lungs and kidneys), trimmed; where lung cannot be bought, make the weight up with more heart and liver, keeping liver under half the total or the loaf turns pasty
  • 1 piece caul fat, about 200 g, soaked in cold water
  • 200 g spring onions, finely sliced
  • 40 g dill, chopped
  • 30 g flat-leaf parsley, chopped
  • 20 g lovage leaves, chopped
  • 4 eggs, beaten
  • 4 eggs, hard boiled and peeled
  • 60 g stale white bread, soaked in 100 ml milk and squeezed out
  • 50 g butter
  • 12 g fine salt
  • 4 g black pepper, ground

Equipment

Sheet pan

Method

  1. Simmer the heart and lungs in salted water for 30 minutes, then drain and cool them; leave the liver and kidneys raw, since boiling sets them chalky. 30 min
  2. Chop all the offal to the coarseness of gravel rather than a paste, and mix it with the spring onions softened in the butter, the herbs, bread, beaten egg, salt and pepper.
  3. Line a 900 g (2 lb) loaf tin with the unfolded caul and stand it on a baking tray, since the caul drips as it renders; spread in half the mixture, lay the boiled eggs down the centre end to end, cover with the rest and fold the caul over the top.
  4. Bake at 180C (350F) for 50 minutes to 60 minutes, until the centre reads 71C (160F) and the caul has melted down to a thin brown skin. 50 min
  5. Cool it in the tin for 1 hour to 1 hour 30 minutes, no longer, then refrigerate until cold right through and slice it straight from the fridge; a warm loaf falls apart on the knife and a dense offal loaf left to cool on the counter sits for hours at the temperature bacteria like best. 60 min

How to tell it is going right

This dish has no standard of its own yet. It is judged on the technique it exercises: checked at the pan, while there is still something to be done about it.

Salted water & the float test 188 recipes

  • The water was salted before anything went in, and tasting it confirms it: seasoned on the tongue, with the salt dissolved rather than sitting on the pot floor.
  • There is enough water, hot enough, that the pot returns to its boil quickly after the food goes in and never stops moving.
  • Where the dish uses the float as its signal, the signal is obeyed: the pieces rise and they come out then, not several minutes later once something else was finished.
  • Nothing is stuck to the bottom of the pot or welded to itself, and the water has not thickened into a cloudy sludge.
  • Drained on a bite rather than on the clock: resistance at the centre, with no chalky raw core.

Where it goes wrong The pot was too small and the water unsalted, so it never recovered its boil: the food sat in slack water, went gummy at the surface, and comes out bland in a way that no sauce afterwards can fix.

From the pass

Caul is the lace of fat around the stomach and it does two jobs in sequence: it holds the loaf together while the egg and muscle proteins set, then it renders away and bastes the crust on its way out. Unfold it cold and wet, because warm caul tears. Take the centre to 71C (160F), the rule for anything minced. Liver goes in raw: its proteins set low, and liver boiled first ends up grainy in the finished slice.
