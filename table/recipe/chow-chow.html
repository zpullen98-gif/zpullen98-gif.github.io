Pennsylvania · Side

Chow Chow

  • Intermediate
  • 1 h 30 m
  • $$ roughly 2 of 4 for cost
  • Vegetarian
  • Vegan

bright · earthy Leads with acid holding everything up to the light, an earthy, grounding base.

Ingredients

  • 300g green beans, cut in pieces
  • 300g cauliflower florets
  • 2 carrots, diced
  • 200g corn kernels
  • 200g lima beans
  • 1 red pepper, diced
  • 1 onion, diced
  • 500ml cider vinegar
  • 250ml water
  • 300g sugar
  • 1 tbsp mustard seed
  • 1 tsp celery seed
  • 1 tsp turmeric
  • 2 tsp salt

Method

  1. Blanch each vegetable separately until just tender: they cook at different rates and mixing them raw gives you mush and gravel in one jar.
  2. Bring the vinegar, water, sugar, mustard seed, celery seed, turmeric, and salt to a boil.
  3. Add all the vegetables and simmer 10 min. 10 min
  4. Pack into sterilized jars with the hot brine to cover.
  5. Seal and rest at least a week before eating; it keeps for months.

How to tell it is going right

This dish has no standard of its own yet. It is judged on the techniques it exercises: checked at the pan, while there is still something to be done about it.

Blanching & shocking 76 recipes

  • A large volume at a hard rolling boil, and enough of it that adding the food barely interrupts it; a pot that goes quiet has started stewing rather than blanching.
  • Timed rather than watched, and short. This is an interruption in the cooking of something, not the cooking of it.
  • The ice bath was made and standing beside the pot BEFORE anything went into the water, not started once the timer went off.
  • It went straight into the ice water, fully submerged, and stayed until cold at the centre: a piece still warm inside is still cooking, and the arrest has not happened yet.
  • Drained and dried afterwards rather than left sitting in water, so it does not carry that water into whatever it meets next.

Where it goes wrong The ice bath was not ready, so the food sat draining in a colander while its own residual heat carried on cooking it. By the time it cooled it was a stage past where it was pulled, and exact arrest is the entire reason for blanching rather than simply boiling.

Brining & curing 88 recipes

  • Fully committed either way: in a wet brine nothing floats clear of the liquid, and in a dry cure every face carries salt, seams and cavities included.
  • The time was measured against the weight and the thickness rather than the recipe’s convenience, and it stayed cold for all of it.
  • The change is visible and can be felt: the flesh is firmer than raw and its colour has deepened or gone more translucent, rather than still being slack and pale.
  • Rinsed and dried as the recipe asks, and anything meant to crisp afterwards has a surface dry and tacky to the touch before it goes near heat.
  • Cooked and tasted, it is seasoned right through to the centre rather than merely salty at the surface, and not so salty that the dish cannot be eaten.

Where it goes wrong It sat in the brine well past its time because a few extra hours seemed harmless; the surface is over-salted and the texture has gone from firm to spongy and wet, and no cooking takes salt back out.

From the pass

The Pennsylvania Dutch answer to a full garden and a coming winter. Blanching separately is the tedious step everyone wants to skip and the one that separates a good chow chow from a jar of pickle mud.
