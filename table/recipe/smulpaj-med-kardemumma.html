Scandinavian · Dessert

Smulpaj med Kardemumma

  • Easy
  • 50 min
  • Serves 4
  • $$
  • Vegetarian

rich · warm-spiced · floral · bright Leads with richness that coats and comforts, warm spice humming through the middle, with a clean, bright snap.

Ingredients

  • 700g apples or 500g bilberries/blueberries, or rhubarb in spring
  • 2 tbsp sugar + 1 tbsp cornstarch tossed with the fruit; squeeze of lemon
  • Crumb: 150g flour + 100g rolled oats + 120g cold butter + 90g sugar
  • 1 tsp ground cardamom, the Nordic signature, plus a pinch of salt
  • Vaniljsås (thin vanilla custard) or vanilla ice cream, mandatory

Equipment

Deep-fry pot

Allergen screen

Found: gluten · dairy · egg

Screened for: gluten, dairy, egg, nuts, peanuts, fish, shellfish, molluscs, soy, sesame, celery, mustard, lupin, alcohol. Not screened: sulphites.

Method

  1. Toss fruit with sugar, starch, lemon straight into the dish: no pre-cooking, no bottom crust.
  2. Rub the crumb ingredients to rubble with visible butter pebbles.
  3. Blanket the fruit loosely: do not press; air is what crisps.
  4. Bake 190°C, 35–40 min until the juices bubble through the crust like lava at the borders. Serve warm, drowned in vanilla sauce. 40 min

How to tell it is going right

This dish has no standard of its own yet. It is judged on the techniques it exercises: checked at the pan, while there is still something to be done about it.

Rubbing fat into flour 22 recipes

  • The fat is cold when it starts and still firm when the rubbing stops, and the bowl has not warmed under the hands.
  • Stopped early, while it still looks like coarse crumbs with pieces of fat visible in it, not worked on to a smooth even sand, which means the pieces have gone.
  • Nothing is greasy or glossy and nothing has smeared into a paste against the side of the bowl.
  • Liquid added last and only enough to bring it together: pressed, it just holds, with no wet patch and nothing sticking to the hand.
  • Rested cold before it is rolled or shaped, so the gluten relaxes and the fat firms again after being handled.

Where it goes wrong The fat was too warm, or it was rubbed until the mixture looked uniform. Once the fat has melted into the flour there are no solid pieces left to steam apart in the oven, so the bake is sandy or tough instead of flaky, and nothing done later puts those layers back.

Tempering a custard 33 recipes

  • The hot liquid went into the eggs a little at a time with constant whisking, and only then did everything go back to the pot.
  • The heat under it stayed gentle throughout and it was never allowed to boil.
  • Stirred continuously and right into the corners of the pan, where it thickens first and sets unnoticed.
  • Judged by behaviour: it thickens enough to coat the back of a spoon and hold a clean line when a finger is drawn through it.
  • Taken off before it looks finished, and strained. Residual heat carries it the rest of the way, and a custard held on the heat until it looks right arrives past right.

Where it goes wrong The hot liquid went in too fast, or the pot went back over full heat to save time. The yolks set into threads before they could thicken smoothly, and a scrambled custard does not come back; straining hides a little of it and nothing hides the rest.

From the pass

Sweden skipped the pastry-crust struggle entirely: smulpaj is all topping, no rolling pin, and cardamom instead of cinnamon is what makes it taste like a Stockholm kitchen. The bubbling-through-the-edges is not mess; it is the doneness indicator.
