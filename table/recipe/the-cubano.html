The Cubano

  • Intermediate
  • 60 min
  • Serves 4
  • $$

rich · herb-fresh Leads with richness that coats and comforts, fresh herbs arguing happily on top.

Ingredients

  • 1 loaf Cuban bread (soft, lard-enriched, thin-crusted, a sturdy soft baguette approximates)
  • 300g mojo roast pork, sliced (leftover pernil is the sandwich's reason for existing)
  • 200g good sliced ham; 150g Swiss cheese
  • Dill pickle slices, a full deck; yellow mustard: plain, ballpark yellow; this is canon
  • Butter for the press; a plancha, panini press, or a skillet plus a foil-wrapped brick

Allergen screen

Found: gluten · dairy · mustard

Screened for: gluten, dairy, egg, nuts, peanuts, fish, shellfish, molluscs, soy, sesame, celery, mustard, lupin, alcohol. Not screened: sulphites.

Method

  1. Split the bread; mustard on both faces, no mayo; Tampa and Miami agree on almost nothing but they agree on this.
  2. Layer: ham, mojo pork, Swiss, pickles; close.
  3. Butter the outside; press under weight in a medium skillet, flipping once, 4–5 min a side. 5 min
  4. It is done when flattened to half height, the crust is gold glass, and the cheese has committed. Cut on a hard diagonal: the cut is traditional and non-negotiable.

How to tell it is going right

This dish has no standard of its own yet. It is judged on the technique it exercises: checked at the pan, while there is still something to be done about it.

Griddle & plancha work 45 recipes

  • Full contact with the metal: the food lies flat, with no dome in the middle and no curled edge lifting it away, so browning reaches the entire face rather than a ring of it.
  • Contact is immediate and audible, and the food stays where it is put rather than sliding around in a slick of its own released liquid.
  • The cooked face is an unbroken sheet of even colour: the whole surface browned, not a pattern of marks with pale ground between them, and not a dark centre with pale corners.
  • The metal around the food is dry: no spreading wet patch steaming out from under it, nothing weeping onto the surface and boiling there.
  • Turned once or twice and no more, and lifted while the crust is still brown: a piece flipped repeatedly never holds heat long enough to build one.

Where it goes wrong The surface was crowded and its temperature crashed, so everything gave up its water at once and stewed on the metal: pale, damp, grey at the edges, and stuck fast where the crust never got the chance to form.

From the pass

Born in the cigar-factory lunchrooms of Tampa's Ybor City and perfected along Miami's Calle Ocho: the two cities still litigate custody (Tampa adds salami, Miami calls that a scandal). The press is the technique: compression fuses meat, cheese, pickle and mustard into a single laminated material. A cold unpressed cubano is just a ham sandwich with ambitions.
