Washington Razor Clam Fritters

  • Intermediate
  • 45 min
  • Serves 4
  • $$

briny · bright · herb-fresh Leads with a briny, maritime salinity, acid holding everything up to the light, lifted green at the very end.

Ingredients

  • 500g razor clams, cleaned and chopped
  • 150g flour
  • 1 tsp baking powder
  • 1 egg
  • 120ml milk
  • 2 scallions, sliced
  • 1 tsp Old Bay
  • 1/2 tsp black pepper
  • Neutral oil for frying
  • Lemon and tartar sauce

Equipment

Deep-fry pot

The skills inside

Deep frying

Allergen screen

Found: gluten · dairy · egg · shellfish · molluscs

Screened for: gluten, dairy, egg, nuts, peanuts, fish, shellfish, molluscs, soy, sesame, celery, mustard, lupin, alcohol. Not screened: sulphites.

Method

  1. Clean the razor clams thoroughly, removing the dark parts, and chop them coarsely.
  2. Whisk the flour, baking powder, egg, and milk into a thick batter.
  3. Fold in the clams, scallions, Old Bay, and pepper.
  4. Drop heaped spoonfuls into 180C oil and fry 3 min, turning, until deep gold. 3 min
  5. Drain, salt, and serve with lemon and tartar.

How to tell it is going right

This dish has no standard of its own yet. It is judged on the technique it exercises: checked at the pan, while there is still something to be done about it.

Deep frying 22 recipes

  • The oil was at temperature before anything went in, measured rather than guessed, and the vessel is no more than two-thirds full.
  • Everything went in dry. Surfaces are patted down and any coating is not dripping, because water hitting hot oil erupts and is the one accident this technique actually has.
  • The oil recovers between batches. It is back at temperature before the next load goes in rather than falling further with each one.
  • A steady vigorous bubbling that slows as it finishes: the bubbles are the food’s own water leaving, so a piece that has gone quiet has stopped drying out and is done.
  • Seasoned or finished in the first seconds out of the oil, while the surface is still hot and will hold what lands on it. Anything applied to a cooled crust sits on top and falls off.

Where it goes wrong Too much went in at once and the oil temperature collapsed. The crust forms slowly while the food sits in warm fat absorbing it rather than shedding steam, and it comes out heavy and greasy instead of crisp, which no amount of draining afterwards will fix.

From the pass

Razor clam digs on the Washington coast are a licensed, tide-timed ritual followed by thousands. Chop rather than mince the clams so you keep some texture, and do not overmix once they go in: they toughen with handling.
