West African · Soup

Egusi Soup

  • Intermediate
  • 70 min
  • $$ roughly 2 of 4 for cost

silky · fiery · sweet-edged Leads with a silken, spoon-slow texture, real fire that builds and stays, trailing gentle sweetness.

Ingredients

  • 200g ground egusi (melon seeds)
  • 500g assorted meat/stockfish/tripe, or beef chunks
  • 3 tbsp palm oil
  • Blended pepper mix: tomato, red bell, scotch bonnet, onion
  • 2 big handfuls spinach or bitterleaf; crayfish powder, stock

Method

  1. Simmer meats seasoned until tender; keep the stock.
  2. Fry pepper mix in palm oil; mix egusi with a little water into a paste.
  3. Add egusi in clumps; let them set 10 min before breaking into curds. 10 min
  4. Loosen with stock, return meat, wilt in the greens. Serve with pounded yam or fufu.

How to tell it is going right

This dish has no standard of its own yet. It is judged on the technique it exercises: checked at the pan, while there is still something to be done about it.

Keeping the cooking liquid 60 recipes

  • It was actually kept: the liquid went into a container rather than down the drain, and the volume saved is enough to be worth using.
  • It is clean: strained of solids and grit, and not clouded with the debris of whatever broke up in it.
  • It tastes of what it cooked and tastes concentrated rather than washed out, and it is not so salty or so bitter that the only honest use is the bin.
  • It was cooled quickly and stored cold, not left standing warm on the stove to be dealt with later.
  • Used, it does the job the dish gives it (carrying flavour into a sauce, loosening the dish, or setting when chilled) rather than merely adding water back.

Where it goes wrong The pot was left to cool slowly on the stove and only strained the next day, so it soured overnight: what should have been the best ingredient in the dish goes in the bin, and the recipe gets water instead.

From the pass

Let the egusi lumps steam-set before stirring: those curds are the texture people fight over, and stirring too soon collapses them into porridge instead of soup. The ground melon seeds are the protein engine of a dish that predates every imported thickener; palm oil gives the color and the bitter leaf (or spinach standing in) the counterweight. Serve with fufu or pounded yam doing the work of cutlery: the soup is built for scooping, not spooning.
