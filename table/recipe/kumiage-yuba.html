Kumiage Yuba

  • Intermediate
  • 40 min
  • $ roughly 1 of 4 for cost
  • Vegetarian
  • Vegan

umami-deep · fiery · silky Leads with savory depth stacked from the pantry’s bass notes, real fire that builds and stays, melting away silken.

Ingredients

  • 1.2 L unsweetened soymilk, at least 10 percent solids and nothing added
  • 10g kombu
  • 10g wasabi, freshly grated
  • 20ml usukuchi shoyu
  • 2g flaky salt

Equipment

Grater / zester

Method

  1. Lay the kombu in a wide shallow pan and pour the soymilk over it to a depth of 3 to 4cm. Surface area makes yuba, not volume.
  2. Hold the milk at 75 to 80C (167 to 176F). The surface dulls, then wrinkles at the edge when you breathe across it: that is the film, five to eight minutes.
  3. For kumiage yuba, slide a flat ladle under the film and lift it away loose, with a little milk beneath it, into a chilled bowl.
  4. For hikiage yuba, run a bamboo skewer along the far edge, lift the whole sheet in one unbroken movement and hang it a minute to drain.
  5. Keep going for as long as the pot allows; each sheet comes thinner and sweeter than the last as the protein is used up. Serve with wasabi, a few drops of shoyu, or salt alone.

How to tell it is going right

This dish has no standard of its own yet. It is judged on the technique it exercises: checked at the pan, while there is still something to be done about it.

  • The surface is read rather than assumed: bubbles break slowly in one or two places, or the surface merely shivers, and it does not reach a rolling boil at any point.
  • It was held there: the pot was watched and the heat adjusted rather than set once and left, and there is no tide mark up the pot wall from a boil-over.
  • The liquid stayed as clear as it should be: no clouding from agitation, and no fat churned into an emulsion through it.
  • Whatever is in the liquid is still intact: nothing shredded, broken up, or worried apart by movement.
  • The level is roughly where the elapsed time should have left it; a large drop is the evidence that it ran hotter than this while nobody was looking.

Where it goes wrong It was brought to a boil and then left on the same heat, so it churned for the whole cook: the liquid clouded, the fat emulsified into it, and the solids broke apart into the broth.

From the pass

The skin is soy protein and fat setting at the air interface as surface water evaporates, so it needs a still surface, 75 to 80C (167 to 176F) and milk of at least 10 percent solids. Boil it and the churn stops any film forming: you get grey scum and a scorched base. Thin milk with added emulsifiers never skins at all.
