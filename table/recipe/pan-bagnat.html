Pan Bagnat

  • Easy
  • 30 min
  • $$$ roughly 3 of 4 for cost

briny · bright · herb-fresh Leads with a briny, maritime salinity, acid holding everything up to the light, lifted green at the very end.

Ingredients

  • 4 round crusty rolls or 1 country loaf: sturdy crumb; this bread has a job to do
  • 2 cans best olive-oil-packed tuna; 4 boiled eggs, quartered
  • Tomatoes, sliced and salted; cucumber, radishes, green pepper strips, red onion shaved
  • Niçoise olives, capers, basil leaves; NO mayonnaise: Nice has bylaws about this, nearly literally
  • Good olive oil and red wine vinegar, applied like weather

Method

  1. Split the rolls; pull out a little crumb to make room; douse both faces with oil and a whisper of vinegar.
  2. Layer: tomatoes against the bread (their juice is the point), tuna, egg, vegetables, olives, capers, basil; oil again over the top.
  3. Close; wrap TIGHT in paper or film; weight under a plate and something heavy.
  4. Rest 1 hour minimum, up to 4: pan bagnat means 'bathed bread'; the soak is the recipe. Unwrap, halve, eat outdoors, ideally within sight of water. 60 min

How to tell it is going right

This dish has no standard of its own yet. It is judged on the technique it exercises: checked at the pan, while there is still something to be done about it.

The overnight rest: letting a dish marry 29 recipes

  • Cooled quickly before it went in (spread out, decanted, or stood in cold water) rather than carried to the fridge hot in the pot it was cooked in.
  • Protected from the rest of the fridge once cold: covered, or given space where nothing strong is sitting beside it. A rest this long is long enough to take on whatever it shares the shelf with, and some dishes want the open air.
  • Given the whole time. This is the one technique whose entire content is waiting, and cutting it short leaves the dish exactly as it was when it went in.
  • Brought back deliberately: reheated gently, or brought up to the temperature it is meant to be eaten at, rather than served straight out of the cold because it is late.
  • Tasted and corrected AFTER the rest. Seasoning and brightness both flatten in the cold, so the dish is finished at the end of the wait rather than at the start of it.

Where it goes wrong It went into the fridge hot and covered. It held its own heat for hours in the range where food spoils, and the lid sweated and dripped back into it all night: what comes out is duller and wetter than what went in, which is the exact opposite of the point.

From the pass

This is the salade niçoise (three shelves over, in the French chapter) engineered for the beach, and the pressing rest is the entire dish: the tomato juice and oil migrate into the crumb until sandwich and dressing become one object. A pan bagnat eaten fresh-made is an error of impatience; Nice's guardians of the recipe (a real association) permit no mayonnaise, no cooked vegetables, and no shortcuts on the bath.
