California French Dip

  • Intermediate
  • 3 h 30 m
  • $$$ roughly 3 of 4 for cost

clean · simple A quiet dish that lets its main ingredient speak: season well and stand aside.

Ingredients

  • 2kg beef top round or sirloin roast
  • 3 tbsp oil
  • 2 tbsp salt
  • 1 tbsp black pepper
  • 4 crusty French rolls
  • 1L beef stock
  • 2 onions, sliced
  • 2 bay leaves
  • Hot mustard and horseradish to serve

Thermometer

Method

  1. Season the roast hard and brown all over in the oil, then roast at 160C to 52C internal for rare, about 90 min. Rest, then chill for slicing. 90 min
  2. Simmer the pan drippings with the stock, onions, and bay 30 min to make a rich jus, then strain. 30 min
  3. Slice the cold beef as thin as you possibly can.
  4. Warm the slices briefly in the hot jus. Never boil them.
  5. Pile into split rolls and either dip the whole sandwich in jus or serve the jus alongside.

How to tell it is going right

This dish has no standard of its own yet. It is judged on the techniques it exercises: checked at the pan, while there is still something to be done about it.

Chilling to firm: the fridge as binder 48 recipes

  • It went cold before it was handled any further, and it went in spread flat and covered rather than as a warm mass that insulates its own centre.
  • It is cold through rather than cold at the edge: tested at the middle it is as firm as it is at the rim.
  • It holds its shape unsupported; it can be cut, portioned, rolled or lifted without slumping, tearing, or sticking to the hands.
  • The fat in it has gone solid: it feels firm and waxy to the fingers rather than greasy and yielding.
  • It survives the step it was chilled for, going into heat or a coating or a mould still holding its form rather than collapsing the moment it is touched.

Where it goes wrong It was given twenty minutes instead of the hours it needed, so it is firm at the rim and soft at the core: it slumps out of shape as it is portioned, and everything made from it spreads or breaks apart in the pan.

Roasting in a hot oven 56 recipes

  • The oven was fully up to heat before anything went in, given time beyond the indicator light rather than judged by it.
  • Every piece has air around it. Nothing touches its neighbour and nothing is packed to the edge of the tray, because crowded food steams itself in its own moisture and never browns.
  • Colour is even across the whole tray, the tray having been turned at least once: no pale row at the back and no dark row at the front.
  • It came out BEFORE the target temperature, not at it. Carryover keeps driving the inside up by several degrees after it leaves the oven, and that rise is part of the cooking rather than an error in it.
  • The temperature was read at the coldest part rather than the easiest one to reach, and read again after the rest.

Where it goes wrong The tray was crowded, or the oven was not truly up to heat. The food releases steam faster than a cool box can carry it away, so it sits pale and slack, and the extra minutes spent chasing colour take the inside well past where it should have stopped.

From the pass

Two Los Angeles restaurants, Philippe's and Cole's, have argued over the invention since 1908. Chill the roast before slicing (warm beef tears rather than shaving thin) and only warm the meat in the jus, since boiling turns it grey.
