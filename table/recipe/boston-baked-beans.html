Boston Baked Beans

  • Intermediate
  • 7 h 30 m
  • Serves 4
  • $

sweet-edged Leads with a sweet edge rounding the corners.

Ingredients

  • 500g dried navy beans, soaked overnight
  • 200g salt pork, scored
  • 1 onion, whole
  • 180ml molasses
  • 3 tbsp brown sugar
  • 1 tbsp dry mustard
  • 1 tsp salt
  • 1/2 tsp black pepper

The skills inside

Soaking dried goods back to life

Allergen screen

Found: mustard

Screened for: gluten, dairy, egg, nuts, peanuts, fish, shellfish, molluscs, soy, sesame, celery, mustard, lupin, alcohol. Not screened: sulphites.

Method

  1. Simmer the drained beans in fresh water 30 min until the skins curl back when you blow on them. Drain and save the liquid. 30 min
  2. Set the onion in the base of a bean pot, add beans, and bury the salt pork with the rind standing proud.
  3. Whisk molasses, sugar, mustard, salt, and pepper into 600ml of the bean liquid and pour in.
  4. Cover and bake at 130C for 6-7 hours, topping up with bean liquid every hour so they never dry out. 420 min
  5. Uncover the last hour to darken the top and crisp the pork rind.

How to tell it is going right

This dish has no standard of its own yet. It is judged on the technique it exercises: checked at the pan, while there is still something to be done about it.

  • Fully submerged with room to swell: the level is well above everything at the start and still above it at the end.
  • Given the full time, and judged by what is in the bowl rather than by the hours on the clock.
  • Tested at the CENTRE of the largest piece, cut or pressed. Anything with a hard dry core will still have one after cooking, because the outside softens first and stops the water getting further in.
  • The soaking water was dealt with on purpose: kept because it holds flavour, or thrown away because it holds what should not be eaten, but never simply left undecided.
  • Drained before it is used, so the dish does not silently gain all the water that came with it.

Where it goes wrong Too little water, or too little time. The pieces swell until they are packed tight against each other and the ones in the middle never fully take up water; they will not soften later in the dish either, so the finished plate has hard pieces scattered through it.

From the pass

These beans named the city (Boston, the Bean Town) and molasses came up from the Caribbean trade that Boston's ships ran, its slight bitterness keeping six hours of sugar from cloying. Navy beans, salt pork, an onion, and an oven you're not in a hurry to reclaim. Never salt early; salt toughens bean skins and adds an hour. Saturday beans with brown bread was the Puritan rhythm (cooked before the Sabbath, eaten without labor), and the pot still keeps that schedule happily.
