Chimichurri Verde

  • Easy
  • 15 min
  • $$ roughly 2 of 4 for cost
  • Vegetarian
  • Vegan

bright · herb-fresh · aromatic · fiery Leads with acid holding everything up to the light, fresh herbs arguing happily on top, on a slow, honest burn.

Ingredients

  • 60 g flat-leaf parsley leaves
  • 20 g garlic, about 6 cloves
  • 6 g dried oregano
  • 4 g coarse chilli flakes
  • 8 g flaky salt
  • 60 g warm water
  • 80 g red wine vinegar
  • 160 g olive oil
  • 1 g cracked black pepper

Method

  1. Dissolve the salt in the warm water, then stir in the vinegar, oregano and chilli flakes and leave 10 minutes for the dried herb to swell. 10 min
  2. Chop the parsley by hand to a coarse mince, stopping while the pieces still read as leaf, and mince the garlic fine.
  3. Stir parsley, garlic and pepper into the vinegar mixture.
  4. Pour the oil over and stir once. Do not whisk; it should sit in two layers.
  5. Rest 2 hours at room temperature before serving, and stir up from the bottom each time you spoon it out. 120 min

How to tell it is going right

This dish has no standard of its own yet. It is judged on the techniques it exercises: checked at the pan, while there is still something to be done about it.

Soaking dried goods back to life 69 recipes

  • Fully submerged with room to swell: the level is well above everything at the start and still above it at the end.
  • Given the full time, and judged by what is in the bowl rather than by the hours on the clock.
  • Tested at the CENTRE of the largest piece, cut or pressed. Anything with a hard dry core will still have one after cooking, because the outside softens first and stops the water getting further in.
  • The soaking water was dealt with on purpose: kept because it holds flavour, or thrown away because it holds what should not be eaten, but never simply left undecided.
  • Drained before it is used, so the dish does not silently gain all the water that came with it.

Where it goes wrong Too little water, or too little time. The pieces swell until they are packed tight against each other and the ones in the middle never fully take up water; they will not soften later in the dish either, so the finished plate has hard pieces scattered through it.

Knife cuts: dice, julienne, bias 150 recipes

  • Take any three pieces from anywhere in the pile: they match closely enough in every dimension that you would have to measure them to argue otherwise.
  • The cut faces are flat and clean rather than ragged or crushed; leaves and herbs are cut through, not bruised, and the board carries no dark smear where they were.
  • The stated size is the actual size, held against a rule or a known reference rather than called about right.
  • Everything in the pile is the shape that was asked for: no wedges, no half pieces, and no offcuts swept in to avoid waste.
  • They cook evenly, which is the entire point: at the end of cooking nothing is still raw and nothing has collapsed.

Where it goes wrong The knife was dull, or the vegetable was never squared off first, so the pieces vary: the small ones are mush by the time the large ones are cooked, and the dish has two textures where it wanted one.

From the pass

This is a broken vinaigrette on purpose: there is no emulsion, the oil sits on top, and you stir before every spoonful. Dried oregano is correct here, and it needs its ten minutes in the vinegar or it stays like hay in the teeth. The processor is the failure: it ruptures the parsley cells, the acid then strips magnesium from the chlorophyll, and within the hour you have khaki sludge. Chop by hand, use within two days.
