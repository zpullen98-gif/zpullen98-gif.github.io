Sicilian · Main

Involtini di Pesce Spada

  • Intermediate
  • 40 min
  • $$ roughly 2 of 4 for cost

bright · briny · herb-fresh · charred Leads with acid holding everything up to the light, a briny, maritime salinity, with a bitter-edged char at the rim.

Ingredients

  • 600g swordfish loin in one piece, very cold
  • 100g dry breadcrumbs
  • 60g caciocavallo, grated
  • 80g onion, finely diced
  • 30g pine nuts
  • 30g sultanas, soaked and drained
  • 20g salted capers, rinsed
  • 20 fresh bay leaves
  • 6 tbsp extra virgin olive oil
  • juice of 1 lemon
  • 2 tbsp warm water
  • 1 tsp dried Sicilian oregano
  • a handful of flat parsley, chopped
  • salt and black pepper

Equipment

Grill · Grater / zester

Method

  1. Cut the chilled loin into 3mm slices, lay each between two sheets of film and beat gently with a flat mallet until it is translucent at the edges and about 2mm thick.
  2. Soften the onion in 2 tbsp of the oil until it collapses without colouring, then stir in the breadcrumbs, caciocavallo, pine nuts, sultanas, capers and parsley until the mix clumps in the hand.
  3. Put a teaspoon of filling on each slice, roll it tight and thread the rolls onto skewers alternating with bay leaves so no two rolls touch.
  4. Grill over fierce heat for about 90 seconds a side, until the surface is marked and opaque and the rolls still spring back under a finger.
  5. Whisk the lemon juice with the warm water, beat in the remaining oil and the oregano until it thickens and turns pale, and spoon this salmoriglio over the skewers while they are hot.

How to tell it is going right

This dish has no standard of its own yet. It is judged on the techniques it exercises: checked at the pan, while there is still something to be done about it.

Soaking dried goods back to life 69 recipes

  • Fully submerged with room to swell: the level is well above everything at the start and still above it at the end.
  • Given the full time, and judged by what is in the bowl rather than by the hours on the clock.
  • Tested at the CENTRE of the largest piece, cut or pressed. Anything with a hard dry core will still have one after cooking, because the outside softens first and stops the water getting further in.
  • The soaking water was dealt with on purpose: kept because it holds flavour, or thrown away because it holds what should not be eaten, but never simply left undecided.
  • Drained before it is used, so the dish does not silently gain all the water that came with it.

Where it goes wrong Too little water, or too little time. The pieces swell until they are packed tight against each other and the ones in the middle never fully take up water; they will not soften later in the dish either, so the finished plate has hard pieces scattered through it.

Grilling over live coals 77 recipes

  • The fire was ready before the food was: coals evenly covered in grey ash with no flames licking through and no raw smoke coming off, and a hot zone and a cooler one both available.
  • The grate was hot and clean when the food landed, and the food released cleanly from it when the time came to turn rather than tearing away.
  • Marks are browned, not burnt: deep colour where the metal touched, with no carbon flaking off and no soot transferred onto the food.
  • The interior reaches its target with a thin gradient, not charred outside and cold within, and not driven through by a flare-up.
  • What comes off the fire smells of fire and food only: no raw wood smoke, no fuel, nothing acrid at the back of the nose.

Where it goes wrong It went on over live flames instead of settled coals, so the outside charred bitter in the flare-ups while the middle stayed cold, and everything on the plate tastes of soot.

From the pass

Swordfish is lean and almost free of collagen, so there is no gelatine to hide overcooking: it goes from silky to cotton wool inside a minute. Chill the loin hard, cut 3mm and beat it thinner, then grill fierce and brief. Whisk the salmoriglio with warm water first; without that emulsion the lemon and oil separate and slide straight off the fish.
