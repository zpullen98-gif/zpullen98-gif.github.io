Rømmegrøt

  • Intermediate
  • 30 min
  • $$ roughly 2 of 4 for cost
  • Vegetarian

rich · warm-spiced · silky Leads with richness that coats and comforts, warm spice humming through the middle, melting away silken.

Ingredients

  • 1 litre cultured sour cream, at least 35 percent fat
  • 150 g plain flour
  • 700 ml whole milk, warmed
  • 6 g fine salt
  • 60 g caster sugar mixed with 5 g ground cinnamon, to serve

Method

  1. Bring the sour cream to a bare simmer in a heavy pan and hold it there, stirring, for 10 minutes, until it loosens and the surface begins to gleam. 10 min
  2. Sift in half the flour and beat hard, keeping the pan at a simmer, until clear yellow fat rises and pools; spoon it into a warm jug as it appears and keep going until no more comes, which should give you 150 to 250 ml.
  3. Beat in the rest of the flour, cook it 3 minutes, then let the porridge down with warm milk a ladle at a time until it just falls from the spoon; salt it. 3 min
  4. Cook 5 minutes more, until the raw flour taste has gone and the gloss comes back. 5 min
  5. Serve in warm bowls with a well of the reserved butter in each and cinnamon sugar scattered over.

How to tell it is going right

This dish has no standard of its own yet. It is judged on the technique it exercises: checked at the pan, while there is still something to be done about it.

The bare simmer: holding liquid below the boil 183 recipes

  • The surface is read rather than assumed: bubbles break slowly in one or two places, or the surface merely shivers, and it does not reach a rolling boil at any point.
  • It was held there: the pot was watched and the heat adjusted rather than set once and left, and there is no tide mark up the pot wall from a boil-over.
  • The liquid stayed as clear as it should be: no clouding from agitation, and no fat churned into an emulsion through it.
  • Whatever is in the liquid is still intact: nothing shredded, broken up, or worried apart by movement.
  • The level is roughly where the elapsed time should have left it; a large drop is the evidence that it ran hotter than this while nobody was looking.

Where it goes wrong It was brought to a boil and then left on the same heat, so it churned for the whole cook: the liquid clouded, the fat emulsified into it, and the solids broke apart into the broth.

From the pass

The dish is a controlled break. Flour starch gelatinises and takes the water, forcing butterfat out of the cultured cream so you can skim it, and that skimmed butter poured back on top is the whole point of eating it. It needs real cultured cream at 35 percent fat or more; low fat soured cream carries gums that hold the emulsion together, so no butter renders and you get wallpaper paste. Hold a bare simmer, since a boil curdles it grainy.
