Tamil & the South · Soup

Milagu Jeera Rasam

  • Easy
  • 25 min
  • Serves 4
  • $$
  • Vegetarian

earthy · aromatic · rich · bright Leads with an earthy, grounding base, aromatics blooming from the first bite, with a clean, bright snap.

Ingredients

  • 25 g tamarind, squeezed in 400 ml water
  • 2 ripe tomatoes (about 150 g), crushed by hand
  • 400 ml thin water skimmed from the top of cooked toor dal
  • 1 tbsp black peppercorns (milagu)
  • 1 tbsp cumin seeds (jeeragam)
  • 3 cloves garlic, unpeeled
  • 3 sprigs curry leaves
  • 1/2 tsp turmeric
  • 15 g ghee
  • 1 tsp mustard seeds
  • 1 dried red chilli
  • 1/4 tsp asafoetida
  • A handful of coriander leaves
  • Salt

Equipment

Mortar & pestle

The skills inside

Skimming: foam, fat and a clear broth

Allergen screen

Found: dairy · mustard

Screened for: gluten, dairy, egg, nuts, peanuts, fish, shellfish, molluscs, soy, sesame, celery, mustard, lupin, alcohol. Not screened: sulphites.

Method

  1. Pound the pepper, cumin, garlic and one sprig of curry leaves coarsely in a stone mortar, until the mix smells sharp and you can still see broken pepper.
  2. Boil the tamarind water with the turmeric, salt and crushed tomatoes for 6 to 8 minutes, until the raw sourness is gone and the tomato skins curl away from the flesh. 8 min
  3. Stir in the pounded masala and the dal water, and bring it only to the point where a pale froth climbs the sides of the pot. Pull it off there. It must not roll.
  4. Heat the ghee, pop the mustard, add the dried chilli and the remaining curry leaves until they crackle stiff, pour it over the rasam and cover for two minutes before scattering the coriander.

How to tell it is going right

This dish has no standard of its own yet. It is judged on the technique it exercises: checked at the pan, while there is still something to be done about it.

Skimming: foam, fat and a clear broth 35 recipes

  • It is held below a rolling boil for the whole time. Agitation drives fat and fine protein into the liquid as an emulsion, and nothing skimmed afterwards will take them back out.
  • The grey foam that rises in the first minutes is lifted off and discarded rather than stirred back in.
  • Fat is taken from the surface where it gathers, the pot moved off the centre of the heat if that is what it takes to collect it in one place.
  • The liquid is visibly clearer at the end than it was at the start: it is possible to see down into it.
  • Nothing lifted off goes back in. What was skimmed is discarded, not tipped back once the ladle is full.

Where it goes wrong The pot was allowed to boil, usually while something else was being attended to. Fat and protein emulsify into the liquid under agitation and cannot be lifted off afterwards, so the finished broth stays cloudy and greasy however patiently it is skimmed from then on.

From the pass

Rasam is a decoction, not a soup you reduce. Pull it the moment the surface lifts into a pale froth, around 95C (203F). The heat of pepper is piperine and stays where it is, but the smell is volatile oil, cuminaldehyde from the jeeragam and pinene from the milagu, and a rolling boil carries it out of the pot in under a minute. Boiled rasam is hot and hollow. Pound coarse and add it late.
