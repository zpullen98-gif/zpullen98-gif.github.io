Whitebait Fritters

  • Intermediate
  • 15 min
  • $$ roughly 2 of 4 for cost

rich · bright Leads with richness that coats and comforts, acid holding everything up to the light.

Ingredients

  • 400 g fresh whitebait (inanga)
  • 3 eggs
  • 5 g salt
  • white pepper
  • 30 g butter
  • 1 lemon
  • white bread and butter, to serve

Method

  1. Tip the whitebait into a sieve and let it drain until no more water runs; do not rinse it again, the fish bruise and go milky.
  2. Beat the eggs only until yolk and white are one and no further; whipped air collapses in the pan and leaves a hollow.
  3. Fold the fish through with the salt and pepper until it looks like fish held in a film of egg, not batter with fish in it.
  4. Get the butter foaming in a heavy pan over high heat, ladle in a thin round and cook until the edges turn opaque and the underside is pale gold, about 90 seconds.
  5. Flip once, count thirty seconds and lift it while the centre is only just set; lemon at the table.

How to tell it is going right

This dish has no standard of its own yet. It is judged on the technique it exercises: checked at the pan, while there is still something to be done about it.

Sautéing & the shallow fry 80 recipes

  • Everything sits in a single layer with visible space between the pieces: nothing stacked, nothing touching, or the pan is too small for the quantity.
  • The pieces move freely and keep moving, sliding across the pan rather than sticking and tearing when the pan is worked.
  • Light even browning arrived at quickly: colour on most faces, not the deep crust of a sear and not grey.
  • A thin film of fat with the food glossed by it: no pool left in the pan at the end, and nothing greasy on the fingers or the plate.
  • Cooked through but still firm, having spent only a few minutes in the pan: anything that has gone soft has been in there too long.

Where it goes wrong Too much went in at once, the pan cooled, and the food released its water: what was meant to brown in three minutes stews for ten and comes out grey, slack and wet.

From the pass

Nothing binds this but egg, which sets between 62 and 70C (144 to 158F); there is no gluten network, so the pan must be hot and the fritter must leave early. Drain the inanga properly first, because water in the mix steams the egg apart. Past about two minutes total the tiny fish wring out their own water, the fritter weeps and the texture goes rubbery. Flour is the usual cheat and it makes a pancake.
