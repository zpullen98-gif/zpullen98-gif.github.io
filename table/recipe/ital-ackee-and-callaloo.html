Jamaican · Breakfast

Ital Ackee and Callaloo

  • Intermediate
  • 25 min
  • $$ roughly 2 of 4 for cost
  • Vegetarian
  • Vegan

fiery · herb-fresh · silky Leads with real fire that builds and stays, fresh herbs arguing happily on top, melting away silken.

Ingredients

  • 540 g tinned ackee, drained, or 30 fresh arils cleaned and parboiled
  • 300 g callaloo (amaranth) leaves and stems
  • 45 ml coconut oil
  • 1 onion, 120 g, sliced
  • 4 scallions, 60 g, chopped
  • 6 sprigs thyme
  • 2 tomatoes, 180 g, chopped
  • 8 g pimento berries, crushed
  • 1 whole scotch bonnet, stem on and unpunctured
  • 1 tsp cracked black pepper

The skills inside

Sweating aromatics: soft, never browned

Method

  1. Strip the callaloo leaves from the stems, peel the fibrous skin from the thick stems with the back of a knife, and chop the stems small. They cook first.
  2. Warm the coconut oil, sweat the onion, scallion, thyme and pimento until translucent, then add the stems, cover, and give them 3 minutes. 3 min
  3. Add the leaves and tomatoes with a splash of water and cover; in about 4 minutes they collapse into their own liquid and turn dark green. Sit the whole scotch bonnet on top. 4 min
  4. Tip in the ackee, cover, and shake the pan rather than stir it; 3 minutes, only long enough to heat through. 3 min
  5. Ital cooking takes no salt, so finish with black pepper only, lift out the pepper whole, and serve with roast breadfruit or boiled food.

How to tell it is going right

This dish has no standard of its own yet. It is judged on the technique it exercises: checked at the pan, while there is still something to be done about it.

  • No colour anywhere: tipped under the light the pieces are translucent to pale gold, with no tan edges, no brown tips, and no browned flecks suspended in the fat.
  • The pan is quiet: a low steady hiss, not a crackle. A crackle means the moisture has gone and browning has already started.
  • The largest piece pressed against the side of the pan collapses without resistance and with no crunch left at its centre.
  • The fat is still clear and loose in the pan and the pan floor is clean: nothing caught, nothing browned on to deglaze, because there was never any fond to build.
  • It tastes sweet and mild with no raw allium bite at the back of the throat, and none of the roasted sweetness that belongs to the next technique up the heat scale.

Where it goes wrong The heat was too high and nothing covered the pan, so the pieces took colour before they softened; the finished dish carries a roasty sweetness where it wanted a clean one, and the pieces are still firm at the centre under browned edges.

From the pass

Ackee is food only after the pod yawns open by itself on the tree. Unripe arils carry hypoglycin A and a pod forced open early keeps it, so discard the black seed and every scrap of the pink membrane. After that, treat the aril like set custard rather than vegetable: it is curd held in a thin skin, and a stirring spoon turns the whole pan to yellow paste. Shake it instead.
