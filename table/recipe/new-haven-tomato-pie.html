New Haven Tomato Pie

  • Intermediate
  • 45 min
  • Serves 4
  • $
  • Vegetarian

umami-deep · briny · aromatic · fiery Leads with savory depth stacked from the pantry’s bass notes, a briny, maritime salinity, on a slow, honest burn.

Ingredients

  • For the dough: 500g bread flour, 340ml water, 10g salt, 2g instant yeast
  • 400g crushed San Marzano tomatoes
  • 2 garlic cloves, grated
  • 1 tbsp olive oil
  • 1 tsp oregano
  • 100g pecorino romano, grated
  • Salt and chili flakes

Equipment

Grater / zester

The skills inside

FermentationShaping by hand

Allergen screen

Found: gluten · dairy

Screened for: gluten, dairy, egg, nuts, peanuts, fish, shellfish, molluscs, soy, sesame, celery, mustard, lupin, alcohol. Not screened: sulphites.

Method

  1. Mix and cold-ferment the dough 24-48 hours ahead: the active work below is 45 minutes. 600 min
  2. Heat the oven with a stone or steel at maximum for an hour.
  3. Season the crushed tomatoes with garlic, oil, oregano, salt, and chili: they go on raw, never cooked first.
  4. Stretch the dough thin and oblong, spread the tomato to the edges, and dust with pecorino only.
  5. Bake 6-8 min until charred at the rim. Finish with olive oil. 8 min

How to tell it is going right

This dish has no standard of its own yet. It is judged on the techniques it exercises: checked at the pan, while there is still something to be done about it.

Fermentation 50 recipes

  • Held somewhere with a steady temperature it can actually work at, chosen on purpose rather than wherever there happened to be room.
  • There is visible activity before it is called started: bubbles, rise, or movement in the liquid. A ferment showing nothing after its first hours is not slow, it is not going.
  • It smells clean: sour, yeasty, or alcoholic, with nothing putrid, and with nothing fuzzy or coloured growing where air meets it.
  • Everything is under the liquid or under a cover, as the method requires: nothing solid left sitting dry in the air to spoil while the rest works.
  • Judged by taste and behaviour and then STOPPED: chilled, baked, or refrigerated at the point wanted, rather than left running because the stated hours had not elapsed.

Where it goes wrong It was run on the clock in a kitchen colder or warmer than the recipe assumed. Fermentation is a rate and not a duration, so the same number of hours gives an under-developed ferment in a cold room and a slack, over-soured one in a warm one, and only tasting it would have said which.

Shaping by hand 71 recipes

  • The pieces match: weighed, or judged against each other rather than by dividing the mass by eye, so that no piece is half again the size of its neighbour.
  • Surfaces are smooth and unbroken with no cracks, tears, or dry patches, and the seams are sealed and sitting underneath.
  • The shape is defined and it holds while the tray waits: it has not slumped, spread, or relaxed back out of its form.
  • Nothing is trapped that should not be: no air pocket where filling should be, no filling breaking through the surface, and no excess flour greying the outside.
  • They finish together in the heat: no piece done while another is still raw, which is the only test that the first mark was met.

Where it goes wrong The dough was worked past its point or shaped without a rest, so it fought back: the pieces spring out of shape on the tray and bake tight, uneven, and torn along the surface.

From the pass

The plain pie of New Haven: tomato, pecorino, oil, and char; mozzarella is an extra you must ask for by name, and the purists never do, because the plain pie is the test of the tomatoes and the oven and nothing else. Crushed tomatoes with body, a hard grating cheese for salt, and the coal-fired char doing the seasoning. This is the apizza baseline against which the clam pie is the celebrated exception. Thin, oblong, cut into unequal strips as tradition demands.
