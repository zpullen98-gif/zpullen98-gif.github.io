Singapore Noodles

  • Intermediate
  • 35 min
  • Serves 4
  • $$

smoky · herb-fresh · earthy · umami-deep Leads with wood-smoke running underneath, fresh herbs arguing happily on top, finishing long and savory.

Ingredients

  • 250g thin rice vermicelli, soaked in warm water 10 min until pliable, drained WELL and left to dry-fluff — wet noodles clump and steam
  • 150g shrimp; 150g char siu or ham, slivered; 2 eggs
  • 1 onion + 1 red pepper, slivered; 2 handfuls bean sprouts; 3 scallions in lengths
  • THE SIGNATURE: 1.5 tbsp good curry powder + ½ tsp turmeric, bloomed in oil, not dusted on dry
  • Sauce: 1 tbsp light soy + 1 tsp fish sauce + ½ tsp sugar; white pepper

Equipment

Wok

Allergen screen

Found: gluten · egg · fish · shellfish · soy

Screened for: gluten, dairy, egg, nuts, peanuts, fish, shellfish, molluscs, soy, sesame, celery, mustard, lupin, alcohol. Not screened: sulphites.

Method

  1. Scramble the eggs first in the hot wok; out. Sear shrimp and char siu; out.
  2. Fry onion and pepper 1 min; then bloom the curry powder and turmeric in a little fresh oil in the center 15 seconds until fragrant: raw curry powder tastes dusty; bloomed, it tastes golden. 1 min
  3. Noodles in; toss with tongs, lifting and dropping, adding the sauce around the rim; everything else back in with the sprouts and scallions.
  4. Toss high and fast 90 seconds: every strand evenly yellow, nothing clumped, sprouts still snapping. White pepper; serve immediately.

How to tell it is going right

This dish has no standard of its own yet. It is judged on the techniques it exercises: checked at the pan, while there is still something to be done about it.

Soaking dried goods back to life 22 recipes

  • Fully submerged with room to swell: the level is well above everything at the start and still above it at the end.
  • Given the full time, and judged by what is in the bowl rather than by the hours on the clock.
  • Tested at the CENTRE of the largest piece, cut or pressed. Anything with a hard dry core will still have one after cooking, because the outside softens first and stops the water getting further in.
  • The soaking water was dealt with on purpose: kept because it holds flavour, or thrown away because it holds what should not be eaten, but never simply left undecided.
  • Drained before it is used, so the dish does not silently gain all the water that came with it.

Where it goes wrong Too little water, or too little time. The pieces swell until they are packed tight against each other and the ones in the middle never fully take up water; they will not soften later in the dish either, so the finished plate has hard pieces scattered through it.

Steaming: basket, leaf and lid 47 recipes

  • Steam was running before the food went in: the water at a rolling boil and vapour coming steadily through the basket, rather than the whole thing warming up together.
  • Nothing touches the water at any point: the food sits clear of it throughout, and the level neither drops enough to boil dry nor rises enough to wet it.
  • The lid stayed on. Every lift costs the chamber its heat, and the number of lifts should be as close to none as the dish allows.
  • Lifted out, the surface is glossy and plump with its colour heightened rather than dulled — greens brighter than they went in, not olive.
  • Cooked through with the shape intact: it yields at the centre without having slumped or split, and there is no more than a spoonful of clean condensate beneath it.

Where it goes wrong The lid was loose or dripping and the condensate fell back onto the food, so it arrives waterlogged and pale with its seasoning washed down into the bottom of the dish.

From the pass

The great joke of the dish: it is unknown in Singapore, a Cantonese invention from Hong Kong's cha chaan tengs, named for exotic flair, and now beloved from London takeaways to New York. The two techniques that matter: dry noodles (soaked, drained, rested) and bloomed curry powder: the fifteen-second oil bloom is what turns the spice from chalk to gold. Tongs beat spatulas here; vermicelli wants to be lifted, not shoved.
