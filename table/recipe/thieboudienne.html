Thieboudienne

  • Advanced
  • 2 h 30 m
  • Serves 4
  • $$

umami-deep · bright · herb-fresh · aromatic Leads with savory depth stacked from the pantry’s bass notes, acid holding everything up to the light, opening aromatic and staying there.

Ingredients

  • 4 thick fish steaks (grouper/snapper); stuffing: parsley, garlic, chili pounded to a paste (rof)
  • 400g broken rice
  • 4 tbsp tomato paste, 1 onion, dried fish or shrimp (optional funk)
  • Vegetables: cassava, carrot, cabbage, aubergine, okra
  • Tamarind or lime

Equipment

Mortar & pestle

The skills inside

Reducing a sauce

Allergen screen

Screened for: gluten, dairy, egg, nuts, peanuts, fish, shellfish, molluscs, soy, sesame, celery, mustard, lupin, alcohol. Not screened: sulphites.

Method

  1. Slit fish; pack with rof paste.
  2. Fry tomato paste and onion in oil; add water, vegetables, fish; simmer: fish out at 15 min, veg when tender. 15 min
  3. Cook rice IN that red broth until it drinks it all.
  4. Platter: red rice, fish, vegetables arranged like a market stall; scrape the crust from the pot for the elders.

How to tell it is going right

This dish has no standard of its own yet. It is judged on the technique it exercises: checked at the pan, while there is still something to be done about it.

Reducing a sauce 33 recipes

  • A steady simmer with the surface moving, never a rolling boil that throws it up the sides and scorches what is left on the base.
  • Judged by behaviour rather than the clock: it coats the back of a spoon and holds a clean line when a finger is drawn through it.
  • The drop in volume is visible against the side of the pan: there is a line to see, not a guess about whether anything has happened.
  • Seasoned at the end and not the beginning, because salt concentrates at exactly the rate everything else does.
  • Nothing is catching underneath: a spoon drawn across the base comes up clean, with no browned film building where the heat is fiercest.

Where it goes wrong It was seasoned at the start and then reduced by half. The salt concentrated along with everything else, so the sauce reaches perfect body and is too salty to serve. And the only remedy is to dilute it and reduce it all over again.

From the pass

Senegal's national dish and jollof's acknowledged ancestor, the one-pot fish-and-rice that traveled the coast and became a continent's argument. The crusty rice at the pot's bottom (xoon) is served with ceremony; burn it a little on purpose, because the household's best eater gets first claim. The stuffed fish, herbs and garlic pounded into slits, is the technique that marks a serious cook. UNESCO listed the dish itself as cultural heritage, which surprises nobody in Dakar.
