Iraqi · Main

Margat Bamia

  • Intermediate
  • 35 min
  • $$$ roughly 3 of 4 for cost

bright · warm-spiced · umami-deep · aromatic Leads with acid holding everything up to the light, warm spice humming through the middle, opening aromatic and staying there.

Ingredients

  • 600 g lamb shoulder, cut into 3 cm cubes
  • 700 g small okra pods, none longer than a finger
  • 2 onions, chopped
  • 10 cloves garlic, peeled and left whole
  • 60 g tomato paste
  • 400 g tomatoes, chopped
  • 50 g tamarind paste, loosened in 200 ml hot water
  • 2 dried limes, pierced
  • 2 tsp baharat
  • 60 ml sunflower oil
  • 2 tsp salt
  • 1 litre water

Equipment

Sheet pan

Method

  1. Pare the stem cone off each okra pod at a slant, taking the cap without cutting into the seed chamber, and keep the pods whole and bone dry.
  2. Brown the lamb hard in the oil in a wide pot, lift it out, then soften the onion and whole garlic in the fat until the cloves are gold on every side.
  3. Return the lamb with the tomato paste, baharat, chopped tomato, dried limes, salt and the water, and simmer covered until the meat gives under the edge of a spoon, about 90 minutes. 90 min
  4. Meanwhile spread the okra on a tray and roast at 200C (400F) until the pods look dull, feel dry and no longer squeak against each other, about 15 minutes. 15 min
  5. Stir the okra and the tamarind water into the pot and simmer uncovered until the sauce clings to the pods and the okra is tender but still holds its shape, 20 minutes more. 20 min

How to tell it is going right

This dish has no standard of its own yet. It is judged on the techniques it exercises: checked at the pan, while there is still something to be done about it.

Roasting in a hot oven 56 recipes

  • The oven was fully up to heat before anything went in, given time beyond the indicator light rather than judged by it.
  • Every piece has air around it. Nothing touches its neighbour and nothing is packed to the edge of the tray, because crowded food steams itself in its own moisture and never browns.
  • Colour is even across the whole tray, the tray having been turned at least once: no pale row at the back and no dark row at the front.
  • It came out BEFORE the target temperature, not at it. Carryover keeps driving the inside up by several degrees after it leaves the oven, and that rise is part of the cooking rather than an error in it.
  • The temperature was read at the coldest part rather than the easiest one to reach, and read again after the rest.

Where it goes wrong The tray was crowded, or the oven was not truly up to heat. The food releases steam faster than a cool box can carry it away, so it sits pale and slack, and the extra minutes spent chasing colour take the inside well past where it should have stopped.

Sweating aromatics: soft, never browned 198 recipes

  • No colour anywhere: tipped under the light the pieces are translucent to pale gold, with no tan edges, no brown tips, and no browned flecks suspended in the fat.
  • The pan is quiet: a low steady hiss, not a crackle. A crackle means the moisture has gone and browning has already started.
  • The largest piece pressed against the side of the pan collapses without resistance and with no crunch left at its centre.
  • The fat is still clear and loose in the pan and the pan floor is clean: nothing caught, nothing browned on to deglaze, because there was never any fond to build.
  • It tastes sweet and mild with no raw allium bite at the back of the throat, and none of the roasted sweetness that belongs to the next technique up the heat scale.

Where it goes wrong The heat was too high and nothing covered the pan, so the pieces took colour before they softened; the finished dish carries a roasty sweetness where it wanted a clean one, and the pieces are still firm at the centre under browned edges.

From the pass

Okra's mucilage sits inside the seed chamber, so whole pods keep it there: pare the cone off at a slant and never slice into the body. Drying the pods in a hot oven sets the outer cells before they meet liquid, and the tamarind's acid breaks up the long sugar chains that make the slime rope. Cut them into rounds and stew them from the start and you are serving lamb in glue.
