Scallion Pancakes

  • Intermediate
  • 50 min
  • $ roughly 1 of 4 for cost
  • Vegetarian
  • Vegan

herb-fresh · bright Leads with fresh herbs arguing happily on top, acid holding everything up to the light.

Ingredients

  • 300g flour
  • 180ml just-boiled water
  • Neutral oil + 2 tbsp flour (paste)
  • Big bunch scallions, sliced
  • Salt; dip: soy + vinegar

Method

  1. Make a hot-water dough; rest 30 min: it becomes stretchy and forgiving. 30 min
  2. Roll thin, brush with oil-flour paste, blanket with scallions and salt.
  3. Roll into a snake, coil into a snail, flatten again. This builds the layers.
  4. Pan-fry in shallow oil, pressing and rotating, until shattering-crisp.

How to tell it is going right

This dish has no standard of its own yet. It is judged on the techniques it exercises: checked at the pan, while there is still something to be done about it.

Resting dough: the pause that does the work 71 recipes

  • It actually rested, covered, for the time stated, not cut short because the rest of the schedule was tight.
  • It rolls and shapes without fighting: it stays where it is put, and it does not spring back or shrink on the bench between passes.
  • The surface is smooth and supple rather than dry, cracked, or skinned over.
  • It is at the temperature the next step needs: cold where the fat must stay solid, cool and relaxed where it must be rolled thin.
  • Baked, it holds the dimensions it was shaped to rather than shrinking back from the edges of the tin or the rim of the dish.

Where it goes wrong The rest was skipped or shortened, so the gluten was still tight: the dough fought the pin and sprang back thick and uneven, then shrank down the sides of the tin in the oven.

Sautéing & the shallow fry 80 recipes

  • Everything sits in a single layer with visible space between the pieces: nothing stacked, nothing touching, or the pan is too small for the quantity.
  • The pieces move freely and keep moving, sliding across the pan rather than sticking and tearing when the pan is worked.
  • Light even browning arrived at quickly: colour on most faces, not the deep crust of a sear and not grey.
  • A thin film of fat with the food glossed by it: no pool left in the pan at the end, and nothing greasy on the fingers or the plate.
  • Cooked through but still firm, having spent only a few minutes in the pan: anything that has gone soft has been in there too long.

Where it goes wrong Too much went in at once, the pan cooled, and the food released its water: what was meant to brown in three minutes stews for ten and comes out grey, slack and wet.

From the pass

Hot water kills gluten's bounce: that's why these roll thin without fighting back, where a cold-water dough would snap back like elastic and tear. The lamination is the other half: oil paste, roll, coil, flatten, so the pancake fries into distinct shattering layers instead of one dense disc. Press and rotate as it fries: the spin crisps the surface evenly, and it is the street-stall tell of someone who has made ten thousand of them.
