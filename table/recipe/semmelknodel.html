Austrian · Side

Semmelknödel

  • Intermediate
  • 45 min
  • $$ roughly 2 of 4 for cost
  • Vegetarian

rich · herb-fresh · warm-spiced · nutty-toasted Leads with richness that coats and comforts, fresh herbs arguing happily on top, closing toasted and warm.

Ingredients

  • 300g stale white rolls (Semmeln), cubed: 2 days old is perfect
  • 250ml warm milk; 2 eggs
  • 1 onion, minced, sweated in butter; parsley
  • Nutmeg, salt; 2 tbsp flour only if the dough betrays you
  • Brown butter for serving, or a gulasch to sit beside

Equipment

Sheet pan

Method

  1. Pour warm milk over the bread cubes; rest 15 min. 15 min
  2. Add eggs, onion, parsley, nutmeg; squeeze together: cohesive, still lumpy with bread.
  3. With wet hands, form tight orange-sized spheres; test-boil ONE.
  4. Simmer (never boil) in salted water 15 min. Slice leftovers and fry in butter tomorrow, arguably the better dish. 15 min

How to tell it is going right

This dish has no standard of its own yet. It is judged on the techniques it exercises: checked at the pan, while there is still something to be done about it.

Sautéing & the shallow fry 80 recipes

  • Everything sits in a single layer with visible space between the pieces: nothing stacked, nothing touching, or the pan is too small for the quantity.
  • The pieces move freely and keep moving, sliding across the pan rather than sticking and tearing when the pan is worked.
  • Light even browning arrived at quickly: colour on most faces, not the deep crust of a sear and not grey.
  • A thin film of fat with the food glossed by it: no pool left in the pan at the end, and nothing greasy on the fingers or the plate.
  • Cooked through but still firm, having spent only a few minutes in the pan: anything that has gone soft has been in there too long.

Where it goes wrong Too much went in at once, the pan cooled, and the food released its water: what was meant to brown in three minutes stews for ten and comes out grey, slack and wet.

Shaping by hand 85 recipes

  • The pieces match: weighed, or judged against each other rather than by dividing the mass by eye, so that no piece is half again the size of its neighbour.
  • Surfaces are smooth and unbroken with no cracks, tears, or dry patches, and the seams are sealed and sitting underneath.
  • The shape is defined and it holds while the tray waits: it has not slumped, spread, or relaxed back out of its form.
  • Nothing is trapped that should not be: no air pocket where filling should be, no filling breaking through the surface, and no excess flour greying the outside.
  • They finish together in the heat: no piece done while another is still raw, which is the only test that the first mark was met.

Where it goes wrong The dough was worked past its point or shaped without a rest, so it fought back: the pieces spring out of shape on the tray and bake tight, uneven, and torn along the surface.

From the pass

The single test dumpling is Alpine law: if it dissolves, knead in flour; if it bounces, add milk. And know that fried leftover knödel with egg (Knödel mit Ei) is the secret dish Austrians actually crave.
