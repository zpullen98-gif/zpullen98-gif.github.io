Chermoula Roast Fish

  • Easy
  • 35 min
  • Serves 4
  • $

herb-fresh · gently hot · bright · earthy Leads with fresh herbs arguing happily on top, a warm current of chile, settling low and earthy.

Ingredients

  • 4 white fish fillets or 1 whole fish
  • Chermoula: big bunch cilantro + parsley, 4 garlic cloves, 2 tsp cumin, 1 tsp paprika, pinch cayenne, lemon juice, olive oil, blended
  • Potatoes, tomatoes, peppers, sliced
  • Olives, preserved lemon optional

The skills inside

Marinating: acid, salt and time

Allergen screen

Found: fish

Screened for: gluten, dairy, egg, nuts, peanuts, fish, shellfish, molluscs, soy, sesame, celery, mustard, lupin, alcohol. Not screened: sulphites.

Method

  1. Blend the chermoula; use a third to marinate the fish 20 min. 20 min
  2. Layer sliced vegetables in a dish; toss with another third; roast 20 min at 210°C. 20 min
  3. Fish on top, remaining chermoula over; back in for 12–15 min. 15 min
  4. Serve from the dish with bread.

How to tell it is going right

This dish has no standard of its own yet. It is judged on the technique it exercises: checked at the pan, while there is still something to be done about it.

  • Contact is total: every surface is wetted, and the pieces are not welded into a clump with dry faces where they pressed together.
  • The stated time was kept and kept cold, read against a clock, because this is the one technique where longer is a different result rather than a safer one.
  • It worked at the surface and no further: cut a piece and the marinade’s colour stops a few millimetres in. A protein gone opaque and chalky to the centre before any heat reached it has been cooked by the acid.
  • The texture is still springy and whole, not mushy, mealy, or fraying at the surface when it is lifted.
  • Where the next step browns it, the surface was patted dry first: a wet surface cannot brown, and the marinade’s sugars will scorch before the food colours.

Where it goes wrong It was left well beyond the stated time on the assumption that more is better, so acid and salt worked through the thin edges: those cook to cotton and read grainy while the thick centre tastes of nothing at all.

From the pass

Chermoula is Morocco's pesto: marinade, sauce and dressing in one, and you make double, always, because everything it touches improves. The three-way split is the method: a third marinates the fish, a third seasons the vegetables, a third arrives fresh at the table, so the same sauce shows up raw and cooked in one dish. Cumin and cilantro carry it; the cayenne is a suggestion, not a dare. The roasting vegetables build a rack for the fish and a side dish simultaneously.
