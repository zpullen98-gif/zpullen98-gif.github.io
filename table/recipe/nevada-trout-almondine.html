Nevada Trout Almondine

  • Easy
  • 30 min
  • $$ roughly 2 of 4 for cost

nutty-toasted · rich · bright · herb-fresh Leads with toasted, nutty warmth throughout, richness that coats and comforts, lifted green at the very end.

Ingredients

  • 4 whole trout, cleaned, or 8 fillets
  • 100g flour, seasoned
  • 85g butter
  • 100g sliced almonds
  • 2 tbsp lemon juice
  • 2 tbsp parsley
  • Salt and pepper
  • Lemon wedges

Method

  1. Pat the trout dry and dredge lightly in seasoned flour.
  2. Fry in half the butter over medium-high heat 3-4 min a side until golden and the fish flakes. Keep warm. 4 min
  3. Wipe the pan, add the rest of the butter, and toast the almonds until golden.
  4. Add the lemon and parsley and swirl into a pan sauce.
  5. Pour the almond butter over the trout and serve with lemon.

How to tell it is going right

This dish has no standard of its own yet. It is judged on the techniques it exercises: checked at the pan, while there is still something to be done about it.

Deglazing & pan sauces 49 recipes

  • The pan still holds its fond. Nothing has been rinsed, wiped, or soaked, and the browned layer is intact on the floor.
  • Excess fat was poured off first and the browned layer left behind; the fat is not the flavour and a sauce carrying all of it will not come together.
  • The liquid went into a still-hot pan and the floor came clean: scraped while it bubbles, the base goes from stuck brown to bare metal and the sauce darkens as it takes it up.
  • The fond was brown and not black. A burnt floor dissolves into bitterness that cannot be seasoned out, and is better washed away than deglazed.
  • Reduced first and seasoned afterwards, tasted at the consistency it will be served at rather than at the start.

Where it goes wrong The pan was left to stand and go cold before anybody came back to it. Cold fond will not dissolve, so the scraping lifts nothing, and the sauce arrives thin and pale with the flavour it was supposed to carry still welded to the metal.

The coat before the fry: dredge, crumb, batter 80 recipes

  • The coat is continuous before it goes anywhere near the fat: held up to the light, no bare patch of food shows through anywhere, seams and thin edges included.
  • It is bonded rather than resting on top: a gentle shake sheds loose dust only, and the coat does not slide off as a sheet when the piece is handled.
  • It bubbles hard and steadily from the moment it enters the fat, and the bubbling visibly slows as it finishes; those bubbles are the surface water leaving, and a quiet entry means the fat is too cool.
  • Out and drained, it is an even gold-brown all over and it is still crisp several minutes later on a rack; a coat that softens within a minute of coming out never lost its water.
  • Broken open, there is no paste of raw flour under the crust and no hollow gap where the coat has lifted away from the food.

Where it goes wrong The fat was below temperature or the pan was crowded, so the coat drank fat instead of sealing against it: it comes out pale and heavy, greasy to the fingers, and slumps off the food as it cools.

From the pass

Trout from the Sierra Nevada streams and Lake Tahoe, dressed in the classic almondine style. Toast the almonds until genuinely golden and watch the butter: brown butter is the goal, but a second too long turns it black and bitter.
