Northern Chinese · Main

Shanxi Dao Xiao Mian

  • Advanced
  • 45 min
  • Serves 4
  • $$
  • Vegetarian

aromatic · bright · fiery · warm-spiced Leads with aromatics blooming from the first bite, acid holding everything up to the light, fading in warm spice.

Ingredients

  • 500 g plain wheat flour, about 11 percent protein
  • 225 g water at 20C (68F)
  • 5 g fine salt
  • 600 g ripe tomatoes, scored, peeled and roughly chopped
  • 4 eggs (about 220 g), beaten with 2 g salt
  • 45 g neutral oil
  • 15 g ginger, minced
  • 4 spring onions, whites sliced and greens reserved
  • 4 garlic cloves, sliced
  • 10 g sugar
  • 6 g salt, for the sauce
  • 40 g Shanxi laochencu (aged black vinegar)
  • 200 g green bok choy, cut in lengths
  • 20 g chilli oil, to finish

Allergen screen

Found: gluten · egg

Screened for: gluten, dairy, egg, nuts, peanuts, fish, shellfish, molluscs, soy, sesame, celery, mustard, lupin, alcohol. Not screened: sulphites.

Method

  1. Rub the salt and water into the flour and knead hard for 10 minutes; this dough fights back. Stop when it is dense and smooth and barely takes a fingerprint, like modelling clay. Wrap and rest 40 minutes, then shape into a fat loaf and rest 20 minutes more. 10 min
  2. Pour the eggs into hot oil, let them puff and set at the edges, break them into large curds and lift them out while still glossy.
  3. Fry ginger, garlic and spring onion whites for 30 seconds, add the tomatoes, sugar and salt and cook down 10 minutes, until they collapse to a jam and the oil separates and runs orange at the edge. Return the egg, kill the heat and stir in the vinegar. 10 min
  4. Hold the loaf on your forearm over a pan at a hard rolling boil and shave with a curved blade in long flicks, so each noodle flies in as a leaf, thick along the spine and thin at the wings.
  5. Boil 90 seconds, until the leaves float and the spine loses its chalky centre, adding the bok choy for the last 20 seconds, then drain and fold through the sauce with a spoonful of chilli oil.

How to tell it is going right

This dish has no standard of its own yet. It is judged on the techniques it exercises: checked at the pan, while there is still something to be done about it.

Shaping by hand 50 recipes

  • The pieces match: weighed, or judged against each other rather than by dividing the mass by eye, so that no piece is half again the size of its neighbour.
  • Surfaces are smooth and unbroken with no cracks, tears, or dry patches, and the seams are sealed and sitting underneath.
  • The shape is defined and it holds while the tray waits: it has not slumped, spread, or relaxed back out of its form.
  • Nothing is trapped that should not be: no air pocket where filling should be, no filling breaking through the surface, and no excess flour greying the outside.
  • They finish together in the heat: no piece done while another is still raw, which is the only test that the first mark was met.

Where it goes wrong The dough was worked past its point or shaped without a rest, so it fought back: the pieces spring out of shape on the tray and bake tight, uneven, and torn along the surface.

Salted water & the float test 89 recipes

  • The water was salted before anything went in, and tasting it confirms it: seasoned on the tongue, with the salt dissolved rather than sitting on the pot floor.
  • There is enough water, hot enough, that the pot returns to its boil quickly after the food goes in and never stops moving.
  • Where the dish uses the float as its signal, the signal is obeyed: the pieces rise and they come out then, not several minutes later once something else was finished.
  • Nothing is stuck to the bottom of the pot or welded to itself, and the water has not thickened into a cloudy sludge.
  • Drained on a bite rather than on the clock: resistance at the centre, with no chalky raw core.

Where it goes wrong The pot was too small and the water unsalted, so it never recovered its boil: the food sat in slack water, went gummy at the surface, and comes out bland in a way that no sauce afterwards can fix.

From the pass

This dough is deliberately underhydrated at about 45 percent, stiff enough to hold a cut edge under the blade. Wetter and the loaf slumps, the knife drags and you shave lumps rather than leaves. Each leaf is thick at the spine and thin at the wing, so one noodle gives two textures, but that only survives if the water is at a hard rolling boil when it lands. Add the aged vinegar off the heat: boiled, its aroma flattens and only sourness is left.
