Tamil & the South · Side

Vatha Kuzhambu

  • Intermediate
  • 40 min
  • Serves 4
  • $$
  • Vegetarian
  • Vegan

bright · aromatic · gently hot · sweet-edged Leads with acid holding everything up to the light, aromatics blooming from the first bite, trailing gentle sweetness.

Ingredients

  • 80 ml gingelly oil, the unrefined dark sesame oil, not the toasted Chinese kind
  • 1 tsp mustard seeds
  • 1 tsp urad dal
  • 1/2 tsp fenugreek seeds
  • 20 curry leaves
  • 1/4 tsp asafoetida
  • 30 g sundakkai vathal or manathakkali vathal, the sun-dried salted berries
  • 4 dried red chillies, broken
  • 60 g shallots, peeled and left whole
  • 40 g tamarind, soaked in 400 ml hot water and squeezed
  • 2 tsp sambar powder
  • 1/2 tsp turmeric
  • 15 g jaggery
  • 1 tsp rice flour slaked in 2 tbsp water
  • Salt

The skills inside

Straining & passing through a sieve

Allergen screen

Found: gluten · sesame · mustard

Screened for: gluten, dairy, egg, nuts, peanuts, fish, shellfish, molluscs, soy, sesame, celery, mustard, lupin, alcohol. Not screened: sulphites.

Method

  1. Heat the gingelly oil until a mustard seed sent in cracks at once, then splutter the mustard, urad dal and fenugreek in that order, 20 seconds apart, so the fenugreek goes gold and not brown.
  2. Add the curry leaves, asafoetida, chillies and vathal and fry 2 min: the dried berries should darken and puff slightly, which is the only chance they get to take on fat. 2 min
  3. Add the shallots whole and fry 3 min until their edges catch, then pour in the strained tamarind water with the sambar powder, turmeric and salt. 3 min
  4. Simmer uncovered 20 to 25 min. It is done when the raw green edge of the tamarind is gone, the volume has dropped by a third, and a clear layer of oil has risen and stays risen. 25 min
  5. Stir in the jaggery and the slaked rice flour and give it 2 min more to thicken to a pouring gravy. 2 min

How to tell it is going right

This dish has no standard of its own yet. It is judged on the technique it exercises: checked at the pan, while there is still something to be done about it.

  • Nothing solid got through: a finger drawn through what came out finds no fibre, seed, skin, curd, or grit.
  • Where the dish wants body it was worked through rather than left to drip, and the solids left in the mesh are spent and dry rather than still holding liquid worth having.
  • It is judged on the tongue rather than by eye: smooth, with no residual grain left behind after the mouthful has gone.
  • The mesh was fine enough for the job and held its shape, and nothing bypassed it by going over the rim.
  • The yield is close to what went in less the spent solids: not half of it abandoned in the sieve because it stopped running and nobody pushed.

Where it goes wrong It was pushed through a mesh too coarse for it, or the sieve was overloaded and slopped at the rim, so the fibres it was meant to remove are in the finished sauce and it reads grainy on the tongue.

From the pass

The oil is not a cooking medium here, it is the preservative, which is why the quantity looks alarming and why the dish is finished only when it floats clear on top. That layer plus the tamarind's acid and a firm hand with salt is what let this keep a week on a Tamil counter with no refrigeration, and it is the reason vathal exists at all: vegetables salted and dried in the sun so a household has something to cook in the months when nothing grows. Two failures to watch. Fenugreek taken past gold turns the whole pot bitter and cannot be corrected. And tamarind pulled off early tastes raw and green rather than sour, so judge the reduction by the oil separating, not by the clock.
