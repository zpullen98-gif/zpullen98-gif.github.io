Delmarva Oyster Fritters

  • Intermediate
  • 40 min
  • Serves 4
  • $$

briny · gently hot · bright · herb-fresh Leads with a briny, maritime salinity, a warm current of chile, lifted green at the very end.

Ingredients

  • 500ml shucked oysters, drained, liquor reserved
  • 150g flour
  • 1 tsp baking powder
  • 1/2 tsp salt
  • 1/4 tsp cayenne
  • 1 egg
  • 120ml oyster liquor and milk combined
  • 2 tbsp parsley
  • Neutral oil for frying
  • Lemon and hot sauce

Equipment

Deep-fry pot

Method

  1. Chop the oysters coarsely if they are large; leave small ones whole.
  2. Whisk the flour, baking powder, salt, and cayenne.
  3. Beat the egg with the liquor and milk and stir into the dry ingredients to a thick batter. Fold in the oysters and parsley.
  4. Drop heaped spoonfuls into 180C oil and fry 3-4 min, turning, until deep gold. 4 min
  5. Drain, salt, and serve immediately with lemon and hot sauce.

How to tell it is going right

This dish has no standard of its own yet. It is judged on the techniques it exercises: checked at the pan, while there is still something to be done about it.

Deep frying 22 recipes

  • The oil was at temperature before anything went in, measured rather than guessed, and the vessel is no more than two-thirds full.
  • Everything went in dry. Surfaces are patted down and any coating is not dripping, because water hitting hot oil erupts and is the one accident this technique actually has.
  • The oil recovers between batches. It is back at temperature before the next load goes in rather than falling further with each one.
  • A steady vigorous bubbling that slows as it finishes: the bubbles are the food’s own water leaving, so a piece that has gone quiet has stopped drying out and is done.
  • Seasoned or finished in the first seconds out of the oil, while the surface is still hot and will hold what lands on it. Anything applied to a cooled crust sits on top and falls off.

Where it goes wrong Too much went in at once and the oil temperature collapsed. The crust forms slowly while the food sits in warm fat absorbing it rather than shedding steam, and it comes out heavy and greasy instead of crisp, which no amount of draining afterwards will fix.

Keeping the cooking liquid 26 recipes

  • It was actually kept: the liquid went into a container rather than down the drain, and the volume saved is enough to be worth using.
  • It is clean: strained of solids and grit, and not clouded with the debris of whatever broke up in it.
  • It tastes of what it cooked and tastes concentrated rather than washed out, and it is not so salty or so bitter that the only honest use is the bin.
  • It was cooled quickly and stored cold, not left standing warm on the stove to be dealt with later.
  • Used, it does the job the dish gives it (carrying flavour into a sauce, loosening the dish, or setting when chilled) rather than merely adding water back.

Where it goes wrong The pot was left to cool slowly on the stove and only strained the next day, so it soured overnight: what should have been the best ingredient in the dish goes in the bin, and the recipe gets water instead.

From the pass

The oyster liquor in the batter is what makes these taste of the bay rather than of fried dough: every drop from the shucking goes in, and the batter should be barely thick enough to hold. Do not overmix once the oysters go in; they break and weep, and a weeping fritter fries flat. The Delmarva fritter is a sandwich on the shore: served on white bread or saltines with mustard, at church suppers and fire-hall fundraisers where the recipe is guarded by committee.
