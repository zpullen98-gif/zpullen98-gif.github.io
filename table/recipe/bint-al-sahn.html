Yemeni & the Gulf · Bread

Bint al Sahn

  • Advanced
  • 45 min
  • Serves 4
  • $$
  • Vegetarian

sweet-edged · rich Leads with a sweet edge rounding the corners, richness that coats and comforts.

Ingredients

  • 500 g plain flour
  • 4 eggs
  • 7 g instant yeast
  • 1 tbsp sugar
  • 1 tsp salt
  • about 150 ml warm water
  • 250 g samn (clarified butter), melted
  • 150 g honey, sidr honey if you have it
  • 1 tsp nigella seeds

Allergen screen

Found: gluten · dairy · egg

Screened for: gluten, dairy, egg, nuts, peanuts, fish, shellfish, molluscs, soy, sesame, celery, mustard, lupin, alcohol. Not screened: sulphites.

Method

  1. Mix the flour, yeast, sugar and salt, work in the eggs and enough warm water to give a soft, slightly tacky dough, and knead until it stretches to a translucent window without tearing.
  2. Prove covered until doubled, about 1 hour, then divide into 10 equal balls, coat each one in melted samn and rest them 20 minutes so the gluten slackens. 60 min
  3. Stretch each ball on an oiled worktop with flat palms, working from the middle outward, until the sheet is thin enough to read through and wider than the tin.
  4. Lay a sheet in a buttered 26 cm round tin, brush it with samn and repeat, folding the overhang inward, until all ten are stacked; give the top sheet a heavier coat.
  5. Bake at 200C (390F) for 25 to 30 minutes, until the top is deep gold and the edges have crisped and pulled from the tin, then brush with more samn while hot, pour over the honey so it sinks between the layers, and scatter the nigella. 30 min

How to tell it is going right

This dish has no standard of its own yet. It is judged on the techniques it exercises: checked at the pan, while there is still something to be done about it.

Resting dough: the pause that does the work 68 recipes

  • It actually rested, covered, for the time stated, not cut short because the rest of the schedule was tight.
  • It rolls and shapes without fighting: it stays where it is put, and it does not spring back or shrink on the bench between passes.
  • The surface is smooth and supple rather than dry, cracked, or skinned over.
  • It is at the temperature the next step needs: cold where the fat must stay solid, cool and relaxed where it must be rolled thin.
  • Baked, it holds the dimensions it was shaped to rather than shrinking back from the edges of the tin or the rim of the dish.

Where it goes wrong The rest was skipped or shortened, so the gluten was still tight: the dough fought the pin and sprang back thick and uneven, then shrank down the sides of the tin in the oven.

Proofing: the rise before the oven 77 recipes

  • Judged on volume against the container rather than on the clock: visibly grown, and grown by roughly the amount the recipe describes.
  • The poke test agrees: a floured fingertip pressed a centimetre in leaves a dent that springs back slowly and only part of the way. Straight back means it is not ready; no spring at all means it has gone.
  • The surface is domed, smooth, and slightly sheened: not cratered, wrinkled, or collapsed, and not skinned over and dry from proofing uncovered.
  • Handled, it feels alive and airy: light for its size, with bubbles visible under the skin.
  • It springs in the oven: the piece lifts in the first minutes and the scores open. A shape that does not move at all was proofed past its point before it went in.

Where it goes wrong It was proofed to the clock in a kitchen colder or warmer than the recipe assumed: overproofed, it collapses as the blade touches it and bakes flat and dense with a sour edge; underproofed, it tears at the side and bakes tight.

From the pass

There is no butter block and no folding here. Each sheet is stretched paper thin, coated in samn and stacked, and in the oven the water in the dough flashes to steam and lifts one sheet off the next while the fat stops them welding together. Roll the dough with a pin instead of stretching it by hand and you drive that air out: the sheets fuse and it bakes as one bready slab. Honey goes on hot, so it thins and travels.
