Shrimp Creole

  • Easy
  • 45 min
  • Serves 4
  • $$$

umami-deep · aromatic · gently hot · rich Leads with savory depth stacked from the pantry’s bass notes, aromatics blooming from the first bite, on a rich, rounded finish.

Ingredients

  • 600g shrimp
  • The trinity + garlic
  • 800g crushed tomatoes
  • Bay, thyme, cayenne, Worcestershire
  • Hot sauce, lemon, scallions
  • Rice

The skills inside

Sweating aromatics: soft, never browned

Allergen screen

Found: fish · shellfish

Screened for: gluten, dairy, egg, nuts, peanuts, fish, shellfish, molluscs, soy, sesame, celery, mustard, lupin, alcohol. Not screened: sulphites.

Method

  1. Sweat the trinity in butter; garlic and seasonings after.
  2. Tomatoes in; simmer 20 min to a bright, loose sauce. 20 min
  3. Balance: hot sauce, Worcestershire, pinch of sugar if the tomatoes bite.
  4. Shrimp for 4 minutes at the end; over rice. 4 min

How to tell it is going right

This dish has no standard of its own yet. It is judged on the technique it exercises: checked at the pan, while there is still something to be done about it.

  • No colour anywhere: tipped under the light the pieces are translucent to pale gold, with no tan edges, no brown tips, and no browned flecks suspended in the fat.
  • The pan is quiet: a low steady hiss, not a crackle. A crackle means the moisture has gone and browning has already started.
  • The largest piece pressed against the side of the pan collapses without resistance and with no crunch left at its centre.
  • The fat is still clear and loose in the pan and the pan floor is clean: nothing caught, nothing browned on to deglaze, because there was never any fond to build.
  • It tastes sweet and mild with no raw allium bite at the back of the throat, and none of the roasted sweetness that belongs to the next technique up the heat scale.

Where it goes wrong The heat was too high and nothing covered the pan, so the pieces took colour before they softened; the finished dish carries a roasty sweetness where it wanted a clean one, and the pieces are still firm at the centre under browned edges.

From the pass

No roux here: Creole cooking shows its French-Spanish-African braid with tomato instead, and the sauce stays bright and loose where gumbo goes dark and deep; that's the city-versus-country line in Louisiana cooking, drawn in a single pot. Simmer the tomatoes only twenty minutes: long enough to marry, short enough to stay vivid. The shrimp get four minutes at the end, full stop. A pinch of sugar is legal if the tomatoes bite back.
