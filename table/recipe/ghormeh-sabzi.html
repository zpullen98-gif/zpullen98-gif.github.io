Persian · Main

Ghormeh Sabzi

  • Intermediate
  • 45 min
  • Serves 4
  • $$$

herb-fresh · bright · earthy · rich Leads with fresh herbs arguing happily on top, acid holding everything up to the light, on a rich, rounded finish.

Ingredients

  • 700 g lamb shoulder, cut in 3 cm dice
  • 300 g onions, finely diced
  • 90 ml sunflower oil
  • 5 g ground turmeric
  • 300 g flat-leaf parsley leaves
  • 200 g coriander leaves
  • 200 g garlic chives or leek greens
  • 30 g dried fenugreek leaves
  • 180 g dried red kidney beans, soaked overnight
  • 6 dried limes (about 30 g)
  • 1.2 L water
  • 12 g salt
  • 2 g black pepper

Method

  1. Fry the onions in 45 ml of the oil until deep gold, add the turmeric and the lamb and brown the meat on all faces.
  2. Chop the parsley, coriander and chives very fine and fry them in the remaining oil over medium heat, stirring, until the volume collapses by two thirds and the smell turns dark and nutty, 15 to 20 minutes; stir the fenugreek in only for the final 2 minutes. 20 min
  3. Tip the herbs onto the meat, add the drained beans and the water, and pierce each dried lime twice with a skewer before dropping it in.
  4. Simmer with the lid ajar until the lamb yields to a spoon, the beans are creamy and a slick of dark green oil stands on the surface, about 2.5 hours. 300 min
  5. Press the limes against the side of the pot to release their juice, then taste and correct salt and sourness.

How to tell it is going right

This dish has no standard of its own yet. It is judged on the techniques it exercises: checked at the pan, while there is still something to be done about it.

Soaking dried goods back to life 64 recipes

  • Fully submerged with room to swell: the level is well above everything at the start and still above it at the end.
  • Given the full time, and judged by what is in the bowl rather than by the hours on the clock.
  • Tested at the CENTRE of the largest piece, cut or pressed. Anything with a hard dry core will still have one after cooking, because the outside softens first and stops the water getting further in.
  • The soaking water was dealt with on purpose: kept because it holds flavour, or thrown away because it holds what should not be eaten, but never simply left undecided.
  • Drained before it is used, so the dish does not silently gain all the water that came with it.

Where it goes wrong Too little water, or too little time. The pieces swell until they are packed tight against each other and the ones in the middle never fully take up water; they will not soften later in the dish either, so the finished plate has hard pieces scattered through it.

Searing: the hard crust 136 recipes

  • The pan keeps a hard, steady sizzle from the moment the food lands and does not fall quiet; silence within the first half minute means the pan lost its heat and the surface is now steaming rather than browning.
  • It releases itself when tested: slide a thin edge under and it lifts without tearing. Anything that grips the pan has not formed a crust yet and is not ready to be moved.
  • Deep even brown across the whole face, edge to edge, when it is turned under the light: no pale ring left around a browned centre, and no black patches that taste bitter on their own.
  • The pan floor carries a dry browned layer that dissolves and lifts the moment liquid hits it: not a wet grey film, and not black flecks that stay put and stay bitter.
  • Cut into it: the browned band is thin, a few millimetres at most, sitting straight on top of correctly cooked interior; a thick grey collar under the crust means the heat was too low for too long.

Where it goes wrong The pan was not hot enough, or too much went in at once, so the food released its water and sat in it: the surface goes grey and steams, it sticks when moved, and the crust that eventually forms arrives over an interior already cooked through.

From the pass

Frying the herbs down is what makes the stew dark and oily rather than grassy; raw herbs give a soup the colour of pond water. Fenugreek is the exception and about a tenth of the herb weight, added at the very end: the dry leaf has no moisture to protect it, scorches in seconds, and scorched fenugreek turns the whole pot bitter. Pierce the dried limes rather than splitting them, since the harshest bitterness sits in the seeds.
