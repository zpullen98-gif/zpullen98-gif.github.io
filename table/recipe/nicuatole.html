Nicuatole

  • Intermediate
  • 30 min
  • Serves 4
  • $
  • Vegetarian
  • Vegan

fiery Leads with real fire that builds and stays.

Ingredients

  • 250 g fresh nixtamal masa, from white corn
  • 1.2 L cold water
  • 220 g sugar
  • 1 stick canela
  • 2 g fine salt

Allergen screen

None found among the 14 screened.

Screened for: gluten, dairy, egg, nuts, peanuts, fish, shellfish, molluscs, soy, sesame, celery, mustard, lupin, alcohol. Not screened: sulphites.

Method

  1. Simmer the canela in 400 ml of the water for 10 minutes, until the liquid is fragrant and faintly amber, then lift the stick out. 10 min
  2. Slake the masa in the remaining 800 ml of cold water, rubbing it between your fingers until the liquid is uniformly milky, then press it through a fine sieve and discard the bran left in the mesh.
  3. Combine both liquids with the sugar and salt in a heavy pan and bring to the boil over medium heat, dragging a wooden spoon across the bottom the whole time.
  4. Hold it at a lazy boil 8-10 minutes: it thickens abruptly, the spoon leaves a channel that holds for a second, and the surface turns glossy and stops tasting chalky. 10 min
  5. Pour it hot into a wetted shallow mould and leave it uncovered at room temperature for about 2 hours, until it is set firm enough to cut into clean squares. 120 min

From the pass

Nicuatole sets on maize starch alone. The grains have to reach a full boil and stay there several minutes to swell and burst, or the gel is weak and the block weeps; it firms as it cools and the loose starch chains re-link. Slake the masa cold and sieve it, because tipped into hot water it lumps on contact. Do not chill it hard or freeze it: the gel goes grainy and pushes its water out.
