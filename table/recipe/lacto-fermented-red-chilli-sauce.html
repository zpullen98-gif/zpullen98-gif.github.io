Lacto-Fermented Red Chilli Sauce

  • Intermediate
  • 30 min
  • $$ roughly 2 of 4 for cost
  • Vegetarian
  • Vegan

gently hot · aromatic · warm-spiced · tangy-ferment Leads with a warm current of chile, aromatics blooming from the first bite, with a lingering ferment tang.

Ingredients

  • 1 kg red chillies such as fresno or red jalapeno, stems removed
  • 60 g garlic cloves, peeled
  • 32 g fine non-iodised sea salt (3 percent of the mash weight)
  • 120 ml white wine vinegar, to finish

Method

  1. Blitz the chillies and garlic to a coarse mash, weigh it, then work in salt at 3 percent of that weight until the mash slackens and turns wet and glossy.
  2. Pack into a jar, press down until liquid rises over the solids, weight them under, and leave 5 cm of headspace beneath an airlock or a lid you burp daily.
  3. Ferment at 20 to 24C (68 to 75F): it bubbles hard by day 3, the colour dulls from scarlet to brick, and the raw green smell turns sour and fruity by day 14.
  4. Blend smooth with the vinegar and check that a strip reads pH 3.6 or lower before it leaves the jar.
  5. Bottle and refrigerate; pass it through a coarse sieve first if you want it pourable.

How to tell it is going right

This dish has no standard of its own yet. It is judged on the techniques it exercises: checked at the pan, while there is still something to be done about it.

  • Held somewhere with a steady temperature it can actually work at, chosen on purpose rather than wherever there happened to be room.
  • There is visible activity before it is called started: bubbles, rise, or movement in the liquid. A ferment showing nothing after its first hours is not slow, it is not going.
  • It smells clean: sour, yeasty, or alcoholic, with nothing putrid, and with nothing fuzzy or coloured growing where air meets it.
  • Everything is under the liquid or under a cover, as the method requires: nothing solid left sitting dry in the air to spoil while the rest works.
  • Judged by taste and behaviour and then STOPPED: chilled, baked, or refrigerated at the point wanted, rather than left running because the stated hours had not elapsed.

Where it goes wrong It was run on the clock in a kitchen colder or warmer than the recipe assumed. Fermentation is a rate and not a duration, so the same number of hours gives an under-developed ferment in a cold room and a slack, over-soured one in a warm one, and only tasting it would have said which.

From the pass

Salt at 3 percent of mash weight is the whole safety argument: it slows every organism in the jar except lactobacilli, which keep working and take the mash under pH 4.0 inside two weeks, below the line where anything dangerous grows. Whatever sits above the brine will mould, so weight the mash down. A flat white skin is kahm yeast and skims off. Fuzzy blue, green or black growth is mould, and that jar goes out.
