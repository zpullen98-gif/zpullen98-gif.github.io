Cuban · Soup

Frijoles Negros Dormidos

  • Intermediate
  • 30 min
  • Serves 4
  • $$
  • Vegetarian
  • Vegan

bright · earthy · briny · aromatic Leads with acid holding everything up to the light, an earthy, grounding base, opening aromatic and staying there.

Ingredients

  • 500g dried black beans
  • 2.5 litres cold water
  • 1 green cubanelle pepper, halved and seeded
  • 120ml olive oil
  • 200g white onion, finely diced
  • 150g green cubanelle pepper, finely diced
  • 30g garlic (about 9 cloves)
  • 6g ground cumin
  • 5g dried oregano
  • 3 bay leaves
  • 60ml vino seco
  • 30ml white vinegar
  • 12g sugar
  • 15g salt

Method

  1. Soak the beans overnight in the cold water with the halved pepper; do not drain them, that black liquor is the body of the dish.
  2. Bring to a boil, skim the grey foam, then hold at a bare tremble, topping up so the beans stay just covered, until a bean crushes to smooth paste with no chalky point in the middle, about 90 minutes. 90 min
  3. Meanwhile sweat the diced onion, pepper and garlic in the olive oil until slumped, stir in the cumin and oregano until fragrant, then mash in a ladle of cooked beans to thicken.
  4. Scrape the sofrito into the pot with the bay and the salt and simmer uncovered until the liquor slides off a spoon in a glossy sheet rather than running clear.
  5. Take off the heat and stir in the vinegar, the vino seco and the sugar; cool, refrigerate overnight and reheat gently to serve over white rice.

How to tell it is going right

This dish has no standard of its own yet. It is judged on the techniques it exercises: checked at the pan, while there is still something to be done about it.

Keeping the cooking liquid 55 recipes

  • It was actually kept: the liquid went into a container rather than down the drain, and the volume saved is enough to be worth using.
  • It is clean: strained of solids and grit, and not clouded with the debris of whatever broke up in it.
  • It tastes of what it cooked and tastes concentrated rather than washed out, and it is not so salty or so bitter that the only honest use is the bin.
  • It was cooled quickly and stored cold, not left standing warm on the stove to be dealt with later.
  • Used, it does the job the dish gives it (carrying flavour into a sauce, loosening the dish, or setting when chilled) rather than merely adding water back.

Where it goes wrong The pot was left to cool slowly on the stove and only strained the next day, so it soured overnight: what should have been the best ingredient in the dish goes in the bin, and the recipe gets water instead.

Skimming: foam, fat and a clear broth 63 recipes

  • It is held below a rolling boil for the whole time. Agitation drives fat and fine protein into the liquid as an emulsion, and nothing skimmed afterwards will take them back out.
  • The grey foam that rises in the first minutes is lifted off and discarded rather than stirred back in.
  • Fat is taken from the surface where it gathers, the pot moved off the centre of the heat if that is what it takes to collect it in one place.
  • The liquid is visibly clearer at the end than it was at the start: it is possible to see down into it.
  • Nothing lifted off goes back in. What was skimmed is discarded, not tipped back once the ladle is full.

Where it goes wrong The pot was allowed to boil, usually while something else was being attended to. Fat and protein emulsify into the liquid under agitation and cannot be lifted off afterwards, so the finished broth stays cloudy and greasy however patiently it is skimmed from then on.

From the pass

Acid is what keeps a black bean leathery: vinegar, wine or tomato added early stops the pectin in the cell walls from dissolving, and no amount of boiling will fix it. Hold all three until a bean crushes clean between finger and thumb. The overnight sleep is not superstition either; leached starch sets as the pot cools and thickens the liquor without a spoonful of flour.
