Torta Ahogada

  • Intermediate
  • 60 min
  • Serves 4
  • $

bright · fiery · warm-spiced · aromatic Leads with acid holding everything up to the light, real fire that builds and stays, opening aromatic and staying there.

Ingredients

  • 4 birote rolls (Guadalajara's sour, hard-crusted torpedo: the crustiest sour roll you can find; it must survive drowning)
  • 500g carnitas, warm and rough-chopped
  • Refried beans, a thin structural smear
  • The drowning sauce: 8 ripe tomatoes simmered with garlic, oregano and clove, blended and kept hot; plus the OPTIONAL widow-maker: chile de árbol salsa, fiercely hot
  • Pickled red onions with lime; napkins beyond reason

The skills inside

Confit

Allergen screen

None found among the 14 screened.

Screened for: gluten, dairy, egg, nuts, peanuts, fish, shellfish, molluscs, soy, sesame, celery, mustard, lupin, alcohol. Not screened: sulphites.

Method

  1. Split the rolls keeping a hinge; smear beans; pack with warm carnitas.
  2. Ladle the hot tomato sauce over the WHOLE sandwich: ahogada means drowned; half-drowned (media) is permitted for beginners.
  3. Add the árbol salsa by the spoon according to your relationship with consequence.
  4. Crown with pickled onions; serve immediately in a shallow bowl with a fork and your sleeves rolled. Eat fast; the birote holds, but not forever.

From the pass

Guadalajara's hangover institution and point of civic pride, the birote roll, sour and hard-crusted from the city's particular fermentation, is the only bread on earth that can be submerged in hot salsa and still fight back. Legend credits a vendor who dropped a torta in sauce and a customer who said serve it anyway. 'Bien ahogada' (fully drowned, full árbol) is a rite of passage with a countdown.
