Kottayam Meen Curry with Kudampuli

  • Intermediate
  • 35 min
  • Serves 4
  • $$

aromatic · bright · earthy · fiery Leads with aromatics blooming from the first bite, acid holding everything up to the light, on a slow, honest burn.

Ingredients

  • 700 g seer fish (neymeen) steaks, 2 cm thick
  • 6 pieces kudampuli (Malabar tamarind), rinsed
  • 3 tbsp coconut oil
  • 1 tsp black mustard seeds
  • 1/4 tsp fenugreek seeds
  • 3 sprigs curry leaves
  • 60 g shallots, sliced
  • 20 g ginger, crushed
  • 8 garlic cloves, crushed
  • 4 tbsp Kashmiri chilli powder
  • 1 tsp turmeric powder
  • 500 ml water
  • 2 tsp salt

Allergen screen

Found: fish · mustard

Screened for: gluten, dairy, egg, nuts, peanuts, fish, shellfish, molluscs, soy, sesame, celery, mustard, lupin, alcohol. Not screened: sulphites.

Method

  1. Soak the kudampuli in 100 ml warm water until the pieces are soft and the water runs amber.
  2. Warm the coconut oil in a manchatti over low heat, pop the mustard and fenugreek until the seeds crackle and the fenugreek turns one shade darker, then add curry leaves, shallots, ginger and garlic and cook until the shallots are soft and smell sweet, with no browning.
  3. Take the pot off the fire, stir in the chilli powder and turmeric so they bloom in the residual heat until the paste smells toasted rather than acrid.
  4. Return to low heat, add the water plus the kudampuli and its soaking liquid, and simmer until the gravy darkens to brick red and the raw chilli smell has gone.
  5. Slide in the fish, cover and cook without stirring, swirling the pot by its rim, until the flesh flakes at a fork and clear red oil beads at the edge.
  6. Take off the heat and leave the pot uncovered to settle for at least an hour.

From the pass

Kudampuli is not tamarind: its acid firms fish protein and keeps the curry sound for days, but more than about six pieces turns it bitter and metallic. The unglazed manchatti holds a bare simmer through its own mass and does not react with that acid the way thin aluminium does. Once the fish is in, never stir; swirl the pot by the rim or the steaks shred into threads.
