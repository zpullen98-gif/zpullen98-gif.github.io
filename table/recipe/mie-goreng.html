Mie Goreng

  • Easy
  • 25 min
  • Serves 4
  • $$

aromatic · bright · gently hot · sweet-edged Leads with aromatics blooming from the first bite, acid holding everything up to the light, trailing gentle sweetness.

Ingredients

  • 400g fresh yellow noodles (or instant noodles, parboiled, the honest midnight road)
  • 3 tbsp kecap manis + 1 tbsp soy + 1 tsp oyster sauce
  • 3 garlic, 3 shallots, 2 chilies, pounded; 150g chicken or shrimp
  • Handful of choy sum or cabbage; 2 eggs
  • Fried shallots, lime, pickled chilies; krupuk if they are near

Equipment

Wok

Allergen screen

Found: gluten · egg · shellfish · molluscs · soy

Screened for: gluten, dairy, egg, nuts, peanuts, fish, shellfish, molluscs, soy, sesame, celery, mustard, lupin, alcohol. Not screened: sulphites.

Method

  1. Fry the paste; protein in until just done.
  2. Push aside; scramble eggs in the gap.
  3. Noodles and greens in; sauces down the hot wok wall; toss violently over maximum flame 2 min. 2 min
  4. The noodles should catch and char in spots: wok breath, Indonesian accent. Shallots and lime over.

How to tell it is going right

This dish has no standard of its own yet. It is judged on the techniques it exercises: checked at the pan, while there is still something to be done about it.

Wok technique 26 recipes

  • Everything went in dry and went in in order. Wet protein or wet vegetables drop the wok below the heat that browns, and what follows is a stew in a round pan however hard the burner is working.
  • The wok stays loud from the first moment to the last. A wok that falls quiet has been overloaded, and the food in it is now boiling in its own liquid.
  • Every piece is coated and glossy with no pooled sauce in the bottom of the dish: the sauce should cling by reduction and starch, not sit under the food.
  • Vegetables keep their colour and a bite at the centre. Grey-green greens or a limp stem mean too long in the pan, and nothing later in the dish recovers them.
  • It carries the faint scorched note that only comes from food touching metal above about 200C, distinct from bitterness, which means the aromatics were burnt and is a different failure entirely.

Where it goes wrong Too much went in at once, or it went in wet. The wok loses its heat, the food releases water and simmers, the sauce thins instead of clinging, and the dish arrives pale and slack with liquid pooling under it.

Frying the paste until the oil splits 93 recipes

  • It is being fried, not simmered: the paste is in fat over live heat and moving, holding a steady sizzle throughout rather than bubbling as a wet mass.
  • The colour has deepened and darkened from raw to cooked, and the volume has visibly reduced and tightened.
  • The split, the signal the whole technique is named for, is unmistakable when it comes: clear fat separates out and pools at the edges and on the surface, and the paste pulls away from the pan floor in one mass.
  • The smell has turned from sharp and raw to round and fragrant, with no raw bite left from any of the aromatics that went into it.
  • Nothing has caught: no black specks through the paste, no bitterness, and a pan floor that is browned but not burnt.

Where it goes wrong It was taken off as soon as it smelled good, before the fat separated; the raw edge of the aromatics is still in it, and the finished dish tastes thin and sharp no matter how long it simmers afterwards.

From the pass

Mie goreng is nasi goreng's noodle sibling and Indonesia's 2am ambassador. Same law applies: the kecap manis must scorch on the wok steel, and the slight char spots are the flavor: a pale mie goreng is a steamed one.
