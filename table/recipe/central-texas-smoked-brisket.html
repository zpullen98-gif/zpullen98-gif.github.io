Central Texas Smoked Brisket

  • Advanced
  • 12 h
  • Serves 4
  • $$$

smoky · rich · charred Leads with wood-smoke running underneath, richness that coats and comforts, with a bitter-edged char at the rim.

Ingredients

  • 1 whole packer brisket, 5–6kg, trimmed to a ¼-inch fat cap, edges rounded (aerodynamics against drying)
  • The rub, in its entirety: coarse salt and coarse black pepper, 50/50. Central Texas has spoken; everything else is another state
  • Post oak if you can get it; oak or hickory otherwise; a smoker or kettle rigged for indirect at a steady 120°C
  • Butcher paper for the wrap: paper breathes where foil braises
  • A 10–14 hour day, a chair, and something to read

Equipment

Thermometer · Smoker

The skills inside

Low & slow smokingBraisingResting meat & slicing against the grain

Allergen screen

None found among the 14 screened.

Screened for: gluten, dairy, egg, nuts, peanuts, fish, shellfish, molluscs, soy, sesame, celery, mustard, lupin, alcohol. Not screened: sulphites.

Method

  1. Rub the night before. Smoke fat-side toward the heat at 120°C, thin blue smoke only: white billowing smoke is bitterness in progress.
  2. Ride it to the STALL: around 70°C internal the temperature freezes for hours as the meat sweats. This is normal. Do not panic. Do not crank the heat.
  3. When the bark is set and mahogany (usually mid-stall), wrap tightly in butcher paper; continue to 95°C internal, when a probe slides in like warm butter: feel outranks number.
  4. REST 1–2 hours minimum, wrapped, in a cooler. Slice the flat across the grain in pencil-width slices; turn the point 90 degrees and slice again. The slice should drape, not fall apart. 120 min

How it should come out

  • Bark is uniform near-black mahogany and stays put: a thumb drawn across it neither smears nor lifts, and the coarse pepper is still visible as grain in the crust.
  • Resistance to the probe is equal at three points along the flat, thick end included, at 95C internal; one soft spot and two firm ones means it goes back on.
  • The fat cap has rendered translucent and spreadable, and the seam fat inside the point gives under finger pressure with no hard white core left in it.
  • Slices are pencil-width, about 6mm, cut across the grain on the flat and again after the point is turned 90 degrees; a slice held at one end drapes over a finger, parts under a gentle tug, and does not shred under its own weight or spring back.
  • A slice from the thin end of the flat, not the point, beads moisture immediately when squeezed between two fingers, and the board carries beads and smears rather than a spreading pool; the crust tastes of pepper first with the salt behind it, and no slice needs sauce.

Where it goes wrong Panic at the stall and the pit pushed to 150C, so the brisket hits 95C hours early with the collagen only half converted: the flat slices firm and dry, the bark tastes scorched rather than smoked, and no rest recovers it.

From the pass

Brisket is a patience exam with a meat certificate: the stall is evaporative cooling doing its work, the paper wrap (the Franklin method) is the compromise between bark and speed, and the long rest redistributes what twelve hours of smoke fought for. Salt, pepper, oak, time: the Central Texas liturgy has four words, and juiciness is the sermon.
