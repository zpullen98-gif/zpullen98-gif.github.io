Catalonia · Dessert

Panellets de Pinyons

  • Intermediate
  • 40 min
  • $ roughly 1 of 4 for cost
  • Vegetarian

nutty-toasted · bright Leads with toasted, nutty warmth throughout, acid holding everything up to the light.

Ingredients

  • 250 g ground blanched almonds
  • 250 g caster sugar
  • 150 g sweet potato, boiled in its skin, peeled and riced
  • zest of 1 lemon
  • 1 egg white
  • 1 egg, beaten, for glazing
  • 200 g pine nuts

Equipment

Sheet pan · Grater / zester

Method

  1. Rice the sweet potato while hot and spread it out to cool completely, then knead it with the almonds, sugar, lemon zest and enough egg white to make a stiff, barely tacky marzipan.
  2. Wrap the paste and rest it overnight in the fridge so the almond oil and the sugar syrup even out and it stops sticking to your hands.
  3. Roll into 20 g balls. Rinse the pine nuts and drain them damp, then roll each ball through them, pressing so the nuts bed in halfway and stand upright.
  4. Brush with beaten egg and bake on the top shelf at 200C (390F) for 8 to 10 minutes, until the pine nut tips are gold but the base is still pale. 10 min
  5. Cool them on the tray without moving them; they are fragile hot and set firm as they come down to room temperature.

How to tell it is going right

This dish has no standard of its own yet. It is judged on the techniques it exercises: checked at the pan, while there is still something to be done about it.

The overnight rest: letting a dish marry 29 recipes

  • Cooled quickly before it went in (spread out, decanted, or stood in cold water) rather than carried to the fridge hot in the pot it was cooked in.
  • Protected from the rest of the fridge once cold: covered, or given space where nothing strong is sitting beside it. A rest this long is long enough to take on whatever it shares the shelf with, and some dishes want the open air.
  • Given the whole time. This is the one technique whose entire content is waiting, and cutting it short leaves the dish exactly as it was when it went in.
  • Brought back deliberately: reheated gently, or brought up to the temperature it is meant to be eaten at, rather than served straight out of the cold because it is late.
  • Tasted and corrected AFTER the rest. Seasoning and brightness both flatten in the cold, so the dish is finished at the end of the wait rather than at the start of it.

Where it goes wrong It went into the fridge hot and covered. It held its own heat for hours in the range where food spoils, and the lid sweated and dripped back into it all night: what comes out is duller and wetter than what went in, which is the exact opposite of the point.

Blanching & shocking 76 recipes

  • A large volume at a hard rolling boil, and enough of it that adding the food barely interrupts it; a pot that goes quiet has started stewing rather than blanching.
  • Timed rather than watched, and short. This is an interruption in the cooking of something, not the cooking of it.
  • The ice bath was made and standing beside the pot BEFORE anything went into the water, not started once the timer went off.
  • It went straight into the ice water, fully submerged, and stayed until cold at the centre: a piece still warm inside is still cooking, and the arrest has not happened yet.
  • Drained and dried afterwards rather than left sitting in water, so it does not carry that water into whatever it meets next.

Where it goes wrong The ice bath was not ready, so the food sat draining in a colander while its own residual heat carried on cooking it. By the time it cooled it was a stage past where it was pulled, and exact arrest is the entire reason for blanching rather than simply boiling.

From the pass

Panellets fail on the bench or in the oven, and both faults are water. Too much sweet potato and the paste slumps and sheds its pine nuts; too little and it cracks as you roll. Bake hot, high and fast: the aim is to colour the nuts before the heat reaches the centre, because almond paste that dries through goes sandy and hard instead of staying fudgy. Rest the paste overnight.
