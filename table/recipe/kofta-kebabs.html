Kofta Kebabs

  • Easy
  • 30 min
  • $$ roughly 2 of 4 for cost

bright · herb-fresh · charred · warm-spiced Leads with acid holding everything up to the light, fresh herbs arguing happily on top, fading in warm spice.

Ingredients

  • 500g ground lamb or beef (20% fat)
  • 1 onion, grated and squeezed dry
  • Parsley, mint
  • 1 tsp each cumin, coriander, allspice; ½ tsp cinnamon
  • Flat metal skewers ideal
  • Yogurt sauce, flatbread

Equipment

Grill · Grater / zester

Method

  1. Knead meat, onion, herbs, spices until tacky: the protein binds itself.
  2. Chill 30 min; form onto skewers, pressing finger-dents. 30 min
  3. Grill or broil hot, 3–4 min per side. 4 min
  4. Serve on flatbread with yogurt and sumac onions.

How to tell it is going right

This dish has no standard of its own yet. It is judged on the techniques it exercises: checked at the pan, while there is still something to be done about it.

The broiler: heat from above 22 recipes

  • The element is fully lit and the compartment is hot before anything goes under it. A broiler switched on with the food already in place browns nothing for several minutes and then burns it in one.
  • The distance was set before the dish went in, not adjusted afterwards with a hot dish in one hand: how close it sits is the only heat control this method has.
  • Somebody stayed with it for the whole time. The window between pale and bitter is tens of seconds, and nothing under a broiler is ever safe to walk away from.
  • The surface has coloured evenly right across, having been turned at least once; every grill has a hot corner and none of them say where it is.
  • What is underneath is no further cooked than when it went in. Top heat is there to finish a surface, and anything that came out hotter all the way through was reheated rather than glazed.

Where it goes wrong It was treated as an oven setting: food in, timer set, cook walks away. Radiant heat from above has no gentle phase, so the surface goes from pale to bitter inside a minute while the inside is untouched, and there is no stage in between to catch.

Salting to draw the water out 41 recipes

  • Salted evenly over every surface rather than tipped on in one place and tossed once.
  • Laid out so the water has somewhere to go: a colander, a rack, or a sieve over a bowl, never a flat dish where it sits back in what it gave up.
  • Given real time, and there is visible liquid to show for it: a pool underneath, a drip, or a wrung handful.
  • That liquid is removed. It is squeezed, blotted or poured away, and the food is dry to the touch before it goes anywhere near heat.
  • The dish is seasoned knowing salt has already gone on, tasted before any more is added.

Where it goes wrong It went into the dish still wet, and was salted again out of habit. The water it was carrying dilutes and steams whatever it meets, so the result is watery and over-seasoned at once, and those are two faults that cannot be corrected in the same direction.

From the pass

Grate-and-squeeze the onion: flavor without the water that makes kofta slump off the skewer and fall through the grill. Kneading until tacky is the binder: the salt-dissolved proteins glue the meat to itself, no egg or breadcrumb needed. Twenty percent fat is structural; lean kofta is a meatloaf lollipop. The finger-dents aren't decoration either: they add surface for char and keep the cylinder gripping the flat skewer as it turns.
