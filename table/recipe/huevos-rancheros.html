Huevos Rancheros

  • Easy
  • 30 min
  • $ roughly 1 of 4 for cost
  • Vegetarian

herb-fresh · gently hot · rich · earthy Leads with fresh herbs arguing happily on top, a warm current of chile, settling low and earthy.

Ingredients

  • 8 corn tortillas, 2 per plate, lightly fried until pliable-crisp
  • Salsa ranchera: 6 ripe tomatoes, 2 jalapeños, ½ onion, 2 garlic; charred, blended coarse, simmered with a pinch of cumin
  • 8 eggs, fried sunny, whites set and yolks loose
  • Refried black beans, warm; queso fresco, cilantro, avocado
  • A hot comal and the confidence of a ranch cook

Method

  1. Char and blend the salsa coarse: ranchera is rustic; a smooth salsa is a different breakfast; simmer 10 min until it thickens and tastes of the griddle. 10 min
  2. Fry tortillas 20 seconds a side in a little oil: soft with crisped edges, the raft consistency.
  3. Fry the eggs in the same pan, basting the whites.
  4. Build: tortillas, a smear of beans, eggs riding on top, salsa ladled around and over the whites but NOT drowning the yolks; cheese and cilantro.

How to tell it is going right

This dish has no standard of its own yet. It is judged on the techniques it exercises: checked at the pan, while there is still something to be done about it.

Tortillas on the comal 27 recipes

  • It puffs. On the second turn the tortilla lifts into a dome as the trapped water flashes to steam and splits the two faces apart; a tortilla that never puffs was pressed too thick, cooked too cool, or turned too often.
  • Freckled, not blackened: small brown spots across a pale field. An evenly browned tortilla has been on too long and will be a cracker by the time it reaches the table.
  • It folds without cracking while warm, and still folds after five minutes under a cloth. A tortilla that splits along the fold has lost too much water on the comal.
  • The edges are unbroken and roughly round, with no ragged fringe: a fringed edge means the masa was too dry when it was pressed, and no amount of heat will fix it afterwards.
  • It smells of toasted corn rather than of scorch or of raw lime. Raw lime means the nixtamal was under-rinsed; scorch means the comal was above the working heat.

Where it goes wrong The masa was too dry or the comal too cool. A dry masa cracks at the edge on pressing and cannot generate the steam that puffs it; a cool comal dries the tortilla through before the water can flash, so it leaves the heat stiff, pale and already stale.

Griddle & plancha work 45 recipes

  • Full contact with the metal: the food lies flat, with no dome in the middle and no curled edge lifting it away, so browning reaches the entire face rather than a ring of it.
  • Contact is immediate and audible, and the food stays where it is put rather than sliding around in a slick of its own released liquid.
  • The cooked face is an unbroken sheet of even colour: the whole surface browned, not a pattern of marks with pale ground between them, and not a dark centre with pale corners.
  • The metal around the food is dry: no spreading wet patch steaming out from under it, nothing weeping onto the surface and boiling there.
  • Turned once or twice and no more, and lifted while the crust is still brown: a piece flipped repeatedly never holds heat long enough to build one.

Where it goes wrong The surface was crowded and its temperature crashed, so everything gave up its water at once and stewed on the metal: pale, damp, grey at the edges, and stuck fast where the crust never got the chance to form.

From the pass

This is mid-morning ranch food (almuerzo, the second breakfast after early chores), and the architecture is fixed: tortilla raft, bean mortar, egg cargo, salsa weather. The yolks stay visible because they are the sauce's second act. Charring the vegetables before blending is the fifteen seconds of extra work that makes it taste like a ranch and not a jar.
