Buttermilk Biscuits

  • Intermediate
  • 35 min
  • Serves 4
  • $$
  • Vegetarian

rich Leads with richness that coats and comforts.

Ingredients

  • 300g flour (soft Southern flour if you can get it)
  • 1 tbsp baking powder, ½ tsp baking soda, 1 tsp salt, 1 tsp sugar
  • 115g FROZEN butter, grated
  • 240ml cold buttermilk
  • Melted butter for the tops

Equipment

Grater / zester

The skills inside

Rubbing fat into flour

Allergen screen

Found: gluten · dairy

Screened for: gluten, dairy, egg, nuts, peanuts, fish, shellfish, molluscs, soy, sesame, celery, mustard, lupin, alcohol. Not screened: sulphites.

Method

  1. Toss grated frozen butter through the dry mix.
  2. Add buttermilk; stir until it barely holds.
  3. Fold the shaggy mass over itself 5 times: those are your layers. Pat 2.5cm thick.
  4. Cut straight down, no twisting; bake at 230°C, 12–14 min, touching each other. 14 min

How to tell it is going right

This dish has no standard of its own yet. It is judged on the technique it exercises: checked at the pan, while there is still something to be done about it.

Rubbing fat into flour 33 recipes

  • The fat is cold when it starts and still firm when the rubbing stops, and the bowl has not warmed under the hands.
  • Stopped early, while it still looks like coarse crumbs with pieces of fat visible in it, not worked on to a smooth even sand, which means the pieces have gone.
  • Nothing is greasy or glossy and nothing has smeared into a paste against the side of the bowl.
  • Liquid added last and only enough to bring it together: pressed, it just holds, with no wet patch and nothing sticking to the hand.
  • Rested cold before it is rolled or shaped, so the gluten relaxes and the fat firms again after being handled.

Where it goes wrong The fat was too warm, or it was rubbed until the mixture looked uniform. Once the fat has melted into the flour there are no solid pieces left to steam apart in the oven, so the bake is sandy or tough instead of flaky, and nothing done later puts those layers back.

From the pass

Twisting the cutter seals the edges shut, and touching biscuits climb each other and rise taller, the physics of breakfast. Frozen grated butter is the shortcut to flaky: distinct shards that steam into layers, no pastry cutter required. The five folds ARE the lamination. Soft Southern flour (lower protein) is why the same recipe rises higher below the Mason-Dixon; buttermilk's acid fires the baking soda the moment they meet, so the oven should be waiting, not preheating.
