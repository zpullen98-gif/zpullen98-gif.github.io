Buss Up Shut (Paratha Roti)

  • Advanced
  • 45 min
  • $$ roughly 2 of 4 for cost
  • Vegetarian

rich Leads with richness that coats and comforts.

Ingredients

  • 500g all-purpose flour
  • 2 tsp baking powder
  • 1.5 tsp fine salt
  • 1 tsp sugar
  • 320ml warm water
  • 80g soft butter or ghee, for layering
  • 3 tbsp neutral oil, for the tawa
  • 2 tbsp extra flour, for dusting the spiral

Method

  1. Whisk the flour, baking powder, salt and sugar, add the water in one go and knead 3 minutes to a soft, slightly tacky dough; cover and rest 30 minutes, until a pressed dent no longer springs back. 3 min
  2. Divide into 4 loyas. Roll each to a 25cm disc, spread it thinly with soft butter, dust with flour, then cut one straight line from the centre to the edge.
  3. Roll the disc up from that cut into a tight cone, stand the cone on its point and press it down into a squat puck; rest the pucks 30 minutes under a cloth. 30 min
  4. Roll each puck out to about 30cm, working gently from the centre outward; if fat squeezes through the surface you are pressing too hard and the spiral is being crushed shut.
  5. Cook on a medium tawa, oiling both faces, turning every 30 seconds until brown freckles rise and the roti balloons in patches, roughly 2 minutes a side. 2 min
  6. Take it straight off the heat into a clean cloth and clap it hard between both hands, or beat it with two wooden spatulas, until it collapses into rags.

From the pass

The cut and the cone build a spiral of dough sheets held apart by fat, and steam inflates them on the tawa. Clap it while it is still hot and pliable and those layers fracture into shreds. Let it cool first and the fat sets solid, the sheets weld together, and you end up tearing a stiff disc instead of bussing it up.
