Holiday Braised Brisket

  • Intermediate
  • 4 h 30 m
  • Serves 4
  • $$$

aromatic · umami-deep · bright · sweet-edged Leads with aromatics blooming from the first bite, savory depth stacked from the pantry’s bass notes, trailing gentle sweetness.

Ingredients

  • 2kg beef brisket, flat cut with the fat cap ON: trimmed to a ½cm blanket, never removed
  • 4 onions, sliced, a genuine mountain; they are the sauce
  • 400ml stock + 250ml red wine (or the sweeter road: a can of tomatoes + a spoon of brown sugar and vinegar); 3 tbsp tomato paste; garlic, bay, thyme
  • Carrots in fat chunks for the final hour
  • THE SCHEDULE: cooked TODAY, sliced and re-sauced TOMORROW: the two-day method is the recipe

The skills inside

BraisingSearing: the hard crustRendering fat: and cooking in what runs outResting meat & slicing against the grain

Allergen screen

Found: alcohol

Screened for: gluten, dairy, egg, nuts, peanuts, fish, shellfish, molluscs, soy, sesame, celery, mustard, lupin, alcohol. Not screened: sulphites.

Method

  1. Sear the brisket hard on both sides in a wide pot; out. Brown the onion mountain patiently in the drippings, 20 min; paste and garlic in. 20 min
  2. Wine and stock; brisket back, fat cap UP; braise covered at 150°C, 3.5–4 h until a fork twists in with mild resistance: collapsing-soft slices badly. 240 min
  3. OVERNIGHT: chill the whole pot. The fat lifts off in a sheet; the meat firms.
  4. Next day: slice the COLD brisket across the grain in neat ribbons; shingle back into the defatted sauce; reheat covered at 160°C, 40 min. The slices drink the gravy and arrive intact and yielding. 40 min

How to tell it is going right

This dish has no standard of its own yet. It is judged on the techniques it exercises: checked at the pan, while there is still something to be done about it.

Braising 29 recipes

  • The pieces went into the liquid already deep brown on their faces, and the pot floor gave up its fond to that liquid rather than keeping it.
  • The liquid comes partway up the pieces and stays there for the whole cook (not submerged, not run dry), and it never boils: the surface trembles and no more.
  • It went long enough for the collagen to convert: a fork twists in it with almost no resistance, and the piece still lifts out whole rather than falling into shreds.
  • The liquid has become a sauce: it has body, it coats the back of a spoon, and it tastes concentrated rather than thin and washed out.
  • The meat is moist and seasoned through to its centre, not dry fibres sitting inside a good sauce.

Where it goes wrong The oven or the burner ran too hot and the liquid boiled, so the fibres tightened and squeezed their moisture out: it shreds dry into a thin greasy liquid, and more time makes it worse rather than better.

Resting meat & slicing against the grain 37 recipes

  • It came off the heat BEFORE the target temperature, measured with a probe rather than by time, because carryover keeps driving the inside up after it is out.
  • The probe went into the coldest likely part rather than the easiest one to reach: the reading that matters is the lowest one, not the first one.
  • Rested for a time matched to its size, somewhere warm, and not cut into partway through to see how it is doing.
  • The board is nearly dry when it is finally cut. A spreading pool means it was cut too early, and that liquid should have stayed inside.
  • The grain was found and the knife went ACROSS it, so the fibres are cut short, and the blade was sharp enough that the slices hold together rather than tearing.

Where it goes wrong It was cut straight off the heat, usually because everything else was ready. The muscle is still contracted and the juice still driven to the centre, so it runs out onto the board; the meat is drier than it ever needed to be, and the flavour that left it is sitting in a puddle under the knife.

From the pass

Every Jewish holiday table (Rosh Hashanah, Passover, Hanukkah) runs on brisket, and the grandmothers' two-day method is pure technique dressed as tradition: cold brisket slices cleanly where warm brisket shreds, the fat removes itself, and the reheat marries slice to sauce. Against the grain is the entire game on this cut; mark the grain direction BEFORE cooking, because the sear will hide it.
