Risotto alla Milanese

  • Intermediate
  • 35 min
  • Serves 4
  • $$$$
  • Vegetarian

rich · umami-deep · floral · bright Leads with richness that coats and comforts, savory depth stacked from the pantry’s bass notes, with a clean, bright snap.

Ingredients

  • 320g carnaroli or arborio rice
  • 1 small onion, minced
  • 100ml dry white wine
  • 1.2L hot chicken or veg stock
  • Big pinch saffron
  • 60g butter, 60g Parmigiano

Screened for: gluten, dairy, egg, nuts, peanuts, fish, shellfish, molluscs, soy, sesame, celery, mustard, lupin, alcohol. Not screened: sulphites.

Method

  1. Sweat onion in half the butter; toast rice 2 min until edges turn translucent. 2 min
  2. Deglaze with wine. Add hot stock a ladle at a time, stirring, ~18 min. 18 min
  3. Bloom saffron in a ladle of stock and add halfway through.
  4. Off heat, beat in cold butter and Parmigiano (the mantecatura) until it flows like lava.

How to tell it is going right

This dish has no standard of its own yet. It is judged on the techniques it exercises: checked at the pan, while there is still something to be done about it.

Deglazing & pan sauces 46 recipes

  • The pan still holds its fond. Nothing has been rinsed, wiped, or soaked, and the browned layer is intact on the floor.
  • Excess fat was poured off first and the browned layer left behind; the fat is not the flavour and a sauce carrying all of it will not come together.
  • The liquid went into a still-hot pan and the floor came clean: scraped while it bubbles, the base goes from stuck brown to bare metal and the sauce darkens as it takes it up.
  • The fond was brown and not black. A burnt floor dissolves into bitterness that cannot be seasoned out, and is better washed away than deglazed.
  • Reduced first and seasoned afterwards, tasted at the consistency it will be served at rather than at the start.

Where it goes wrong The pan was left to stand and go cold before anybody came back to it. Cold fond will not dissolve, so the scraping lifts nothing, and the sauce arrives thin and pale with the flavour it was supposed to carry still welded to the metal.

Sweating aromatics: soft, never browned 192 recipes

  • No colour anywhere: tipped under the light the pieces are translucent to pale gold, with no tan edges, no brown tips, and no browned flecks suspended in the fat.
  • The pan is quiet: a low steady hiss, not a crackle. A crackle means the moisture has gone and browning has already started.
  • The largest piece pressed against the side of the pan collapses without resistance and with no crunch left at its centre.
  • The fat is still clear and loose in the pan and the pan floor is clean: nothing caught, nothing browned on to deglaze, because there was never any fond to build.
  • It tastes sweet and mild with no raw allium bite at the back of the throat, and none of the roasted sweetness that belongs to the next technique up the heat scale.

Where it goes wrong The heat was too high and nothing covered the pan, so the pieces took colour before they softened; the finished dish carries a roasty sweetness where it wanted a clean one, and the pieces are still firm at the centre under browned edges.

From the pass

Stirring rubs starch off the grain (that, not cream, is where the creaminess comes from), but stop at al dente, because the rice keeps cooking on the plate and the texture Milan wants is all'onda, loose enough to move in waves when you shake the pan. The mantecatura is non-negotiable: cold butter and Parmigiano beaten in off the heat, an emulsion finished with your wrist. Milanese legend credits the saffron to a Duomo glassworker's apprentice in 1574, gilding his wedding rice with the pigment from his day job.
