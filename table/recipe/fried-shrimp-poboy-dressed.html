Fried Shrimp Po'boy (Dressed)

  • Intermediate
  • 45 min
  • Serves 4
  • $$$

rich · gently hot · bright · herb-fresh Leads with richness that coats and comforts, a warm current of chile, lifted green at the very end.

Ingredients

  • 600g Gulf shrimp, peeled: size 21/25, big enough to matter
  • Wet: 250ml buttermilk + hot sauce; Dry: 150g corn flour (fish fry) + 50g flour, seasoned hard with cayenne, garlic, paprika
  • 1 loaf real po'boy French bread: the shattering-crust, cotton-crumb kind; this bread cannot be substituted, only approximated
  • Dressed means: shredded iceberg, tomato slices, dill pickle rounds, mayonnaise; all four, no debate
  • Hot sauce and lemon at the table

The skills inside

Allergen screen

Found: gluten · dairy · egg · fish · shellfish

Screened for: gluten, dairy, egg, nuts, peanuts, fish, shellfish, molluscs, soy, sesame, celery, mustard, lupin, alcohol. Not screened: sulphites.

Method

  1. Soak shrimp in the buttermilk-hot sauce bath 20 min; dredge through the seasoned corn flour with dry hands. 20 min
  2. Fry at 180°C in small batches, 2 min, to gold and crunch; salt in the air. 2 min
  3. Split the loaf keeping a hinge; drag mayo edge to edge; build the dressing.
  4. Pile the shrimp shamelessly; close; press gently; cut on the bias. Lean forward when you eat it: the drip line is part of the design.

How to tell it is going right

This dish has no standard of its own yet. It is judged on the techniques it exercises: checked at the pan, while there is still something to be done about it.

The coat before the fry: dredge, crumb, batter 61 recipes

  • The coat is continuous before it goes anywhere near the fat: held up to the light, no bare patch of food shows through anywhere, seams and thin edges included.
  • It is bonded rather than resting on top: a gentle shake sheds loose dust only, and the coat does not slide off as a sheet when the piece is handled.
  • It bubbles hard and steadily from the moment it enters the fat, and the bubbling visibly slows as it finishes; those bubbles are the surface water leaving, and a quiet entry means the fat is too cool.
  • Out and drained, it is an even gold-brown all over and it is still crisp several minutes later on a rack; a coat that softens within a minute of coming out never lost its water.
  • Broken open, there is no paste of raw flour under the crust and no hollow gap where the coat has lifted away from the food.

Where it goes wrong The fat was below temperature or the pan was crowded, so the coat drank fat instead of sealing against it: it comes out pale and heavy, greasy to the fingers, and slumps off the food as it cools.

Knife cuts: dice, julienne, bias 89 recipes

  • Take any three pieces from anywhere in the pile: they match closely enough in every dimension that you would have to measure them to argue otherwise.
  • The cut faces are flat and clean rather than ragged or crushed; leaves and herbs are cut through, not bruised, and the board carries no dark smear where they were.
  • The stated size is the actual size, held against a rule or a known reference rather than called about right.
  • Everything in the pile is the shape that was asked for: no wedges, no half pieces, and no offcuts swept in to avoid waste.
  • They cook evenly, which is the entire point: at the end of cooking nothing is still raw and nothing has collapsed.

Where it goes wrong The knife was dull, or the vegetable was never squared off first, so the pieces vary: the small ones are mush by the time the large ones are cooked, and the dish has two textures where it wanted one.

From the pass

The po'boy was born in 1929 when the Martin brothers fed striking streetcar workers free sandwiches: 'here comes another poor boy.' The bread is the whole argument: airy Leidenheimer-style loaves that shatter and vanish, holding fried shrimp like a paper bag holds fireworks. 'Dressed' is a complete sentence in this city; answer it correctly.
