Colombian · Starter

Aborrajados de Plátano Maduro

  • Intermediate
  • 35 min
  • $$ roughly 2 of 4 for cost
  • Vegetarian

rich · herb-fresh · sweet-edged Leads with richness that coats and comforts, fresh herbs arguing happily on top, trailing gentle sweetness.

Ingredients

  • 3 very ripe plantains, skins fully black, about 750 g
  • 200 g fresh melting cheese, in fingers
  • 60 g bocadillo (guava paste), in thin slices
  • 100 g plain flour
  • 1 egg
  • 120 ml milk
  • 10 g sugar
  • 5 g baking powder
  • 2 g salt
  • 1 L neutral oil, for frying

Method

  1. Peel the plantains and cut them into 3 cm lengths, then fry at 160 C (320 F) until they are the colour of honey and give easily under the back of a spoon, about 4 minutes. 4 min
  2. Press each piece flat between two boards to roughly 1 cm; it should spread without splitting at the edges.
  3. Sandwich a finger of cheese and a slice of bocadillo between two flattened pieces and pinch the edges closed.
  4. Whisk the flour, egg, milk, sugar, baking powder and salt to the thickness of double cream; it should coat a spoon and fall from it in a slow ribbon.
  5. Raise the oil to 180 C (355 F), dip each parcel and fry until the batter is set and pale gold, about 2 minutes, so the cheese is soft but still inside. 2 min

From the pass

Ripeness is the whole recipe. At black skin the plantain's starch has turned to sugar, which is why it mashes flat instead of cracking and why it browns in minutes. So the first fry is gentle, at 160 C (320 F), only to soften it, and the second is hot and short, to set the batter before those sugars burn. An under ripe plantain will never flatten, and no amount of extra time in the oil will fix it.
