Khorkhog

  • Advanced
  • 45 min
  • $$ roughly 2 of 4 for cost

briny · charred · aromatic Leads with a briny, maritime salinity, char used as punctuation, opening aromatic and staying there.

Ingredients

  • 2 kg mutton on the bone (leg and rib), in large pieces
  • 60 g fat-tail or kidney fat, sliced
  • 10 dense, dry basalt or granite stones, about fist sized
  • 3 onions, quartered
  • 500 g waxy potatoes, halved
  • 3 carrots, in thick lengths
  • 1 head of garlic, halved across
  • 400 ml water
  • 18 g fine salt
  • 3 g black pepper, ground

Equipment

Grill · Sheet pan

Method

  1. Build a hard wood fire and bury the stones in the embers for at least 45 minutes, until they glow dull red; use dense dry stones only, because porous or damp ones burst. 45 min
  2. Season the mutton and pack a heavy lidded metal pot in layers: a stone, then meat, fat and vegetables, then another stone, keeping the stones off the pot wall.
  3. Pour in the water and set the lid on loosely enough that steam can escape freely; a clamped vessel full of steam and glowing stone is a bomb.
  4. Set the pot on the coals for 1 hour 30 minutes to 2 hours 30 minutes depending on the age of the animal, turning it now and then, until the meat slips off the rib and the water has gone dark and fatty; look in at the hour and add a cup of boiling water if the pot has gone quiet. 60 min
  5. Lift the stones out with tongs onto a metal tray and leave them there until they are warm rather than searing. A stone straight from the pot is at boiling point and greasy and will burn skin in seconds, so hand them round only once they have cooled, and in a folded cloth.

How to tell it is going right

This dish has no standard of its own yet. It is judged on the techniques it exercises: checked at the pan, while there is still something to be done about it.

Grilling over live coals 77 recipes

  • The fire was ready before the food was: coals evenly covered in grey ash with no flames licking through and no raw smoke coming off, and a hot zone and a cooler one both available.
  • The grate was hot and clean when the food landed, and the food released cleanly from it when the time came to turn rather than tearing away.
  • Marks are browned, not burnt: deep colour where the metal touched, with no carbon flaking off and no soot transferred onto the food.
  • The interior reaches its target with a thin gradient, not charred outside and cold within, and not driven through by a flare-up.
  • What comes off the fire smells of fire and food only: no raw wood smoke, no fuel, nothing acrid at the back of the nose.

Where it goes wrong It went on over live flames instead of settled coals, so the outside charred bitter in the flare-ups while the middle stayed cold, and everything on the plate tastes of soot.

Steaming: basket, leaf and lid 130 recipes

  • Steam was running before the food went in: the water at a rolling boil and vapour coming steadily through the basket, rather than the whole thing warming up together.
  • Nothing touches the water at any point: the food sits clear of it throughout, and the level neither drops enough to boil dry nor rises enough to wet it.
  • The lid stayed on. Every lift costs the chamber its heat, and the number of lifts should be as close to none as the dish allows.
  • Lifted out, the surface is glossy and plump with its colour heightened rather than dulled: greens brighter than they went in, not olive.
  • Cooked through with the shape intact: it yields at the centre without having slumped or split, and there is no more than a spoonful of clean condensate beneath it.

Where it goes wrong The lid was loose or dripping and the condensate fell back onto the food, so it arrives waterlogged and pale with its seasoning washed down into the bottom of the dish.

From the pass

Khorkhog is Mongolian, cooked out on the steppe in a milk churn or a lidded pot set in the fire. Mutton is a sheep over two years old, its collagen cross linked with age, so it wants both real heat and real time, and its fat carries branched chain fatty acids that read as gamey to anyone raised on lamb. Do not trim it: that fat is the seasoning here, and there is little else. The stones heat the load from the inside as well as the outside. Vent the lid, always.
