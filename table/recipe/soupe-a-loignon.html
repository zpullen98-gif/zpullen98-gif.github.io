French · Soup

Soupe à l'Oignon

  • Intermediate
  • 1 h 30 m
  • Serves 4
  • $$$

rich · charred · bright · sweet-edged Leads with richness that coats and comforts, char used as punctuation, trailing gentle sweetness.

Ingredients

  • 1kg yellow onions, thinly sliced
  • 60g butter
  • 1 tbsp flour
  • 150ml dry white wine
  • 1.5L beef stock
  • Baguette, Gruyère
  • Thyme

The skills inside

Allergen screen

Found: gluten · dairy · alcohol

Screened for: gluten, dairy, egg, nuts, peanuts, fish, shellfish, molluscs, soy, sesame, celery, mustard, lupin, alcohol. Not screened: sulphites.

Method

  1. Cook onions in butter over medium-low with a pinch of salt 45–60 min to deep mahogany. 60 min
  2. Stir in flour; deglaze with wine, add stock and thyme; simmer 20 min. 20 min
  3. Ladle into ovenproof bowls; top with toasted baguette and a blanket of Gruyère.
  4. Broil until molten and bronzed.

How it should come out

  • A spoonful lifted from any depth of the pot shows mahogany strands throughout, with no pale or yellow ones hiding near the top.
  • The kilo of raw onions has collapsed to roughly a fifth of its volume: a jammy tangle with no rings still holding their shape.
  • Broth is dark and clean-bodied, just filming the back of a spoon; never gluey, never pale, and with no raw-flour taste.
  • The Gruyère lid is bronzed and blistered, sealed to the rim of the bowl, and lifts off in one sheet with the crouton beneath it still holding under spoon pressure rather than dissolved to mush.
  • Sweet first, beef-savoury behind it, and no bitterness in the finish: a scorched edge at the back of the throat is the tell that the hour was rushed.

Where it goes wrong The heat was pushed to save the hour, so the onions carry black scorched shreds around pale undercooked centres and the finished bowl tastes bitter where it should taste sweet.

From the pass

If the onions catch, deglaze with a splash of water and keep going: those dark patches dissolving off the pan floor ARE the soup. What you cannot do is hurry: caramelizing a kilo of onions to mahogany takes the better part of an hour, and high heat gives you bitter black edges on raw centers instead. Les Halles market workers ate this at dawn against the cold; the Gruyère lid broiled onto the bowl is the only roof the dish needs.
