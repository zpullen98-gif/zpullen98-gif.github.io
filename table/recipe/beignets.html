Cajun & Creole · Dessert

Beignets

  • Intermediate
  • 2 h
  • Serves 4
  • $$
  • Vegetarian

rich Leads with richness that coats and comforts.

Ingredients

  • 500g flour
  • 250ml warm milk + 60ml water
  • 1 egg, 60g sugar, 40g butter
  • 7g yeast, pinch salt
  • Oil for frying; an avalanche of powdered sugar

Equipment

Deep-fry pot

Allergen screen

Found: gluten · dairy · egg

Screened for: gluten, dairy, egg, nuts, peanuts, fish, shellfish, molluscs, soy, sesame, celery, mustard, lupin, alcohol. Not screened: sulphites.

Method

  1. Make a soft, slightly sticky dough; rise 1–2 h (or overnight cold, better). 120 min
  2. Roll 1cm thick; cut rectangles; no second proof needed.
  3. Fry at 180°C, flipping, until puffed and gold, ~2 min per side. 2 min
  4. Bury in powdered sugar while hot. No restraint permitted.

How to tell it is going right

This dish has no standard of its own yet. It is judged on the techniques it exercises: checked at the pan, while there is still something to be done about it.

Proofing: the rise before the oven 77 recipes

  • Judged on volume against the container rather than on the clock: visibly grown, and grown by roughly the amount the recipe describes.
  • The poke test agrees: a floured fingertip pressed a centimetre in leaves a dent that springs back slowly and only part of the way. Straight back means it is not ready; no spring at all means it has gone.
  • The surface is domed, smooth, and slightly sheened: not cratered, wrinkled, or collapsed, and not skinned over and dry from proofing uncovered.
  • Handled, it feels alive and airy: light for its size, with bubbles visible under the skin.
  • It springs in the oven: the piece lifts in the first minutes and the scores open. A shape that does not move at all was proofed past its point before it went in.

Where it goes wrong It was proofed to the clock in a kitchen colder or warmer than the recipe assumed: overproofed, it collapses as the blade touches it and bakes flat and dense with a sour edge; underproofed, it tears at the side and bakes tight.

Deep frying 94 recipes

  • The oil was at temperature before anything went in, measured rather than guessed, and the vessel is no more than two-thirds full.
  • Everything went in dry. Surfaces are patted down and any coating is not dripping, because water hitting hot oil erupts and is the one accident this technique actually has.
  • The oil recovers between batches. It is back at temperature before the next load goes in rather than falling further with each one.
  • A steady vigorous bubbling that slows as it finishes: the bubbles are the food’s own water leaving, so a piece that has gone quiet has stopped drying out and is done.
  • Seasoned or finished in the first seconds out of the oil, while the surface is still hot and will hold what lands on it. Anything applied to a cooled crust sits on top and falls off.

Where it goes wrong Too much went in at once and the oil temperature collapsed. The crust forms slowly while the food sits in warm fat absorbing it rather than shedding steam, and it comes out heavy and greasy instead of crisp, which no amount of draining afterwards will fix.

From the pass

They puff into pillows because of trapped gas layers in the rolled dough: fry immediately after cutting, no second proof, and the steam does the rest. The overnight cold rise is better than the warm one: slower fermentation, better flavor, and dough that rolls without a fight. Powdered sugar in an avalanche, no restraint permitted: the Café du Monde serving standard is 'geological'. Eat them hot; a cold beignet is just fried bread with regrets.
