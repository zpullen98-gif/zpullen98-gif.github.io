Kimchi Fried Rice

  • Easy
  • 15 min
  • $ roughly 1 of 4 for cost

tangy-ferment · briny · nutty-toasted · gently hot Leads with ferment tang with real depth behind it, a briny, maritime salinity, with warmth in the tail.

Ingredients

  • 3 cups day-old rice
  • 1.5 cups sour, well-fermented kimchi, chopped + 3 tbsp of its juice
  • 100g bacon or spam (optional)
  • 1 tbsp gochujang, 1 tsp soy, sesame oil
  • Fried egg per person, scallions, sesame, nori

Method

  1. Render the pork; fry kimchi in the fat 3 min until its edges catch. 3 min
  2. Rice in; press and toss with gochujang and kimchi juice.
  3. Sesame oil off heat.
  4. Fried egg on top, yolk runny; scissors on the nori.

How to tell it is going right

This dish has no standard of its own yet. It is judged on the techniques it exercises: checked at the pan, while there is still something to be done about it.

Fermentation 53 recipes

  • Held somewhere with a steady temperature it can actually work at, chosen on purpose rather than wherever there happened to be room.
  • There is visible activity before it is called started: bubbles, rise, or movement in the liquid. A ferment showing nothing after its first hours is not slow, it is not going.
  • It smells clean: sour, yeasty, or alcoholic, with nothing putrid, and with nothing fuzzy or coloured growing where air meets it.
  • Everything is under the liquid or under a cover, as the method requires: nothing solid left sitting dry in the air to spoil while the rest works.
  • Judged by taste and behaviour and then STOPPED: chilled, baked, or refrigerated at the point wanted, rather than left running because the stated hours had not elapsed.

Where it goes wrong It was run on the clock in a kitchen colder or warmer than the recipe assumed. Fermentation is a rate and not a duration, so the same number of hours gives an under-developed ferment in a cold room and a slack, over-soured one in a warm one, and only tasting it would have said which.

Rendering fat: and cooking in what runs out 73 recipes

  • It started cold or low and came up slowly: fat that met a hot pan seizes and scorches before the inside can melt, and there is no recovering it.
  • The fat in the pan is clear and clean-smelling, pale to golden, with no dark sediment on the floor and nothing acrid in the steam.
  • What is left behind is crisp and deep gold with no soft pale fat still clinging to it and no black burnt edges.
  • There is a real volume of it: enough to film the pan floor and be poured off and kept, not a dry smear that never ran.
  • Whatever cooks in it browns in it and takes its flavour, rather than sitting in a greasy pool that never gets hot enough to colour anything.

Where it goes wrong The heat was raised to hurry it along, so the outside seized and browned before the interior fat could melt; very little runs out, and the solids come out burnt on the outside and rubbery within.

From the pass

Old, sour kimchi is the good stuff for frying; fresh kimchi is for eating cold; fermentation past its crisp prime is exactly when the funk and acidity concentrate into something worth caramelizing. Frying it in the pork fat until the edges catch is where the flavor turns. The kimchi juice is the seasoning liquid, not a garnish: three tablespoons of it stain the rice through. Runny yolk on top, scissors on the nori, and Korea's definitive leftovers dish is complete.
