Dry-Brined Roast Turkey with Real Gravy

  • Advanced
  • 5 h
  • $$ roughly 2 of 4 for cost

rich · herb-fresh Leads with richness that coats and comforts, fresh herbs arguing happily on top.

Ingredients

  • 1 turkey, 5–6kg: smaller birds cook truer; two small beat one giant
  • Dry brine: 1 tbsp salt per 2kg + 1 tsp baking powder + herbs, rubbed on THREE DAYS ahead, bird left UNCOVERED in the fridge
  • 150g butter, softened with sage and thyme, worked under the breast skin
  • The gravy engine: neck and giblets simmered into stock; the roasting pan's drippings; 40g flour
  • 2 onions, carrots and celery under the bird as the rack; the actual rack retired

Method

  1. Three days out: dry-brine and refrigerate uncovered: the salt penetrates, the skin dries to parchment, and the wait does what basting pretends to.
  2. Roast day: herb butter under the breast skin; bird on the vegetable bed, legs toward the oven's back (the hot corner), at 220°C for 30 min, then 165°C. 30 min
  3. Pull at 65°C in the breast, 74°C in the thigh: carryover finishes it; rest 45 MINUTES uncovered; the rest is not optional, it is half the recipe. 45 min
  4. Gravy: pan on the burner, flour into the drippings, giblet stock whisked in, simmered 10 min and strained. Carve off the bone in the kitchen; platter the slices over a ladle of gravy. 10 min

How it should come out

  • Skin uniformly deep brown, taut and dry enough to click under a fingernail, unbroken over the breast where the herb butter went under.
  • The bird came out on two thermometer readings, not a time: 65°C at the deepest part of the breast and 74°C at the thigh joint, the probe kept off the bone.
  • Carved at a full 45 minutes: a slice weeps at the cut edge and the board stays nearly dry. A flood on the board says the bird was cut early.
  • Breast slices hold together coming off the knife and read moist white through their whole thickness; at the thigh the meat pulls easily from the bone and the juices there run clear rather than pink.
  • A slice of breast eaten alone, no gravy, is seasoned to its centre. The gravy is strained, coats a spoon, and carries no fat slick on top and no floury edge underneath.

Where it goes wrong The bird is timed rather than probed and the breast rides up to thigh temperature: white meat comes off the knife in dry crumbling slabs, and the gravy ends up doing the work the roast was meant to do.

From the pass

Three truths the holiday table deserves: the dry brine beats the wet bucket (seasoned meat, crackling skin, zero cooler logistics); basting is theater that opens the oven and lengthens the wait; and the 45-minute rest is where the juice you fought for decides to stay. Legs to the back exploits the oven's hot zone: dark meat needs the heat the breast fears.
