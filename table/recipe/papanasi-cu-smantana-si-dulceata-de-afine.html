Papanași cu Smântână și Dulceață de Afine

  • Intermediate
  • 35 min
  • $$ roughly 2 of 4 for cost
  • Vegetarian

silky · rich · bright Leads with a silken, spoon-slow texture, richness that coats and comforts, with a clean, bright snap.

Ingredients

  • 500 g branza de vaci, fresh cow's curd cheese
  • 2 eggs
  • 60 g caster sugar
  • 150 g plain flour, plus more for shaping
  • 40 g fine semolina
  • 8 g baking powder
  • zest of 1 lemon
  • pinch of salt
  • 1 litre sunflower oil, for frying
  • 250 g smantana
  • 250 g dulceata de afine, blueberry preserve

Equipment

Grater / zester

Method

  1. Hang the curd cheese in a cloth for an hour if it is at all wet, then press it through a sieve; it should take a thumbprint without weeping.
  2. Beat in the eggs, sugar, zest and salt, then fold in the flour, semolina and baking powder, adding only enough extra flour to give a soft dough that stays faintly sticky.
  3. With floured hands shape eight rings about 8 cm across with a 2 cm hole, plus eight small balls from the trimmings.
  4. Heat the oil to 165C (330F) and fry in batches, turning once, for 6 to 7 minutes, until deep bronze and light in the hand; a skewer through the thickest part comes out clean. 7 min
  5. Drain briefly, sit a ball in each ring, spoon over cold smantana and then the preserve, and eat at once while the crust is still crisp.

How to tell it is going right

This dish has no standard of its own yet. It is judged on the techniques it exercises: checked at the pan, while there is still something to be done about it.

Folding: keeping the air in 34 recipes

  • The air was actually built first and it shows: whatever was creamed or whipped has changed colour and grown in volume, pale and light rather than dense.
  • The two mixtures were brought closer before being combined: a little of the light one worked into the heavy one first, so the rest is not beaten flat trying to move it.
  • Folded and not stirred: a broad implement down through the middle, across the base, up and over, with the bowl turned between strokes.
  • Stopped the moment the streaks disappear. It is still visibly aerated and holds its shape briefly when the spoon lifts away.
  • Into the tin and into the oven straight away: a batter left standing on the bench gives back the air it was built for.

Where it goes wrong It was stirred until it looked perfectly smooth. Every stroke past the point where the streaks vanish knocks out air that cannot be put back, so the bake comes out dense and short of the tin, and often wet through the middle where there was no structure to hold it.

Straining & passing through a sieve 148 recipes

  • Nothing solid got through: a finger drawn through what came out finds no fibre, seed, skin, curd, or grit.
  • Where the dish wants body it was worked through rather than left to drip, and the solids left in the mesh are spent and dry rather than still holding liquid worth having.
  • It is judged on the tongue rather than by eye: smooth, with no residual grain left behind after the mouthful has gone.
  • The mesh was fine enough for the job and held its shape, and nothing bypassed it by going over the rim.
  • The yield is close to what went in less the spent solids: not half of it abandoned in the sieve because it stopped running and nobody pushed.

Where it goes wrong It was pushed through a mesh too coarse for it, or the sieve was overloaded and slopped at the rim, so the fibres it was meant to remove are in the finished sauce and it reads grainy on the tongue.

From the pass

Everything hangs on how wet the cheese is. Loose curd forces in more flour and the papanas turns bready; drained curd carries a batter that puffs. Fry at 165C (330F) and no hotter: baking powder needs time to lift a dense wet dough, and at 180C and above the crust locks before the middle cooks, so they colour beautifully, then sink and weep oil from a raw centre.
