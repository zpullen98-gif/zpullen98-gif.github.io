Pad See Ew

  • Intermediate
  • 25 min
  • Serves 4
  • $

bright · sweet-edged · gently hot · smoky Leads with acid holding everything up to the light, a sweet edge rounding the corners, on a drift of smoke.

Ingredients

  • 400g fresh wide rice noodles (sen yai), separated gently at room temperature: cold fridge noodles crack; warm them 20 seconds if needed
  • 250g chicken thighs or pork, thin slices; 2 eggs
  • 3 big handfuls Chinese broccoli (gai lan), stems bias-cut, leaves separate
  • Sauce: 2 tbsp dark sweet soy + 1.5 tbsp oyster sauce + 1 tbsp light soy + 1 tsp sugar + splash of white vinegar
  • High-smoke-point oil; a wok and the hottest burner in the house; white pepper and chili-vinegar (prik nam som) at the table

Screened for: gluten, dairy, egg, nuts, peanuts, fish, shellfish, molluscs, soy, sesame, celery, mustard, lupin, alcohol. Not screened: sulphites.

Method

  1. Wok to maximum; sear the meat; push aside; scramble the eggs hard against the steel.
  2. Gai lan stems in for 30 seconds; then noodles and sauce, poured around the rim.
  3. Now the discipline: TOSS, then LET THEM SIT 20–30 seconds untouched to char, toss, sit again: the noodles must kiss the steel long enough to catch smoke and dark-soy caramel at the edges.
  4. Leaves in last, one toss, out. Serve immediately with white pepper and the chili vinegar: the sour cuts the sweet soy; Thais add it at the table by reflex.

How to tell it is going right

This dish has no standard of its own yet. It is judged on the techniques it exercises: checked at the pan, while there is still something to be done about it.

Wok technique 26 recipes

  • Everything went in dry and went in in order. Wet protein or wet vegetables drop the wok below the heat that browns, and what follows is a stew in a round pan however hard the burner is working.
  • The wok stays loud from the first moment to the last. A wok that falls quiet has been overloaded, and the food in it is now boiling in its own liquid.
  • Every piece is coated and glossy with no pooled sauce in the bottom of the dish: the sauce should cling by reduction and starch, not sit under the food.
  • Vegetables keep their colour and a bite at the centre. Grey-green greens or a limp stem mean too long in the pan, and nothing later in the dish recovers them.
  • It carries the faint scorched note that only comes from food touching metal above about 200C, distinct from bitterness, which means the aromatics were burnt and is a different failure entirely.

Where it goes wrong Too much went in at once, or it went in wet. The wok loses its heat, the food releases water and simmers, the sauce thins instead of clinging, and the dish arrives pale and slack with liquid pooling under it.

Sugar stages & caramel 46 recipes

  • Nothing is stirred once it is boiling, and any crystals clinging to the side of the pan are brushed down rather than left to fall in.
  • Judged by temperature or by a tested stage rather than by time: consecutive stages are a few degrees apart and look identical in the pan.
  • Colour is read off the heat and in good light, remembering that sugar keeps darkening after the flame is out.
  • Stopped decisively at the point wanted: off the heat and into the next liquid or onto a cold surface, rather than left to coast in a hot pan.
  • The result is clear and smooth rather than grainy, cloudy, or seized into a solid mass.

Where it goes wrong It was stirred after coming to the boil, or a stray crystal from the side of the pan fell back in. One crystal seeds the whole pan and the syrup turns grainy within seconds, and once sugar has crystallised the only cure is more water and starting the whole thing again.

From the pass

Pad see ew is pad thai's quieter sibling and the wok-hei exam: sweet dark soy caramelizes on hot steel only if you stop stirring: the sit-and-char rhythm is everything, and constant tossing steams the noodles pale. One portion at a time in a home wok, same law as char kway teow two shelves over. The chili vinegar on the table is the dish's missing half; try it once and you will never eat it plain again.
