Peruvian · Starter

Papa a la Huancaína

  • Intermediate
  • 30 min
  • $$ roughly 2 of 4 for cost
  • Vegetarian

rich · briny · aromatic · warm-spiced Leads with richness that coats and comforts, a briny, maritime salinity, fading in warm spice.

Ingredients

  • 800 g yellow waxy potatoes (papa amarilla), unpeeled
  • 4 fresh ají amarillo, halved and deveined
  • 200 g queso fresco
  • 120 ml evaporated milk
  • 6 soda crackers
  • 45 ml neutral oil
  • half a red onion, chopped
  • 2 garlic cloves
  • 5 g salt
  • 2 eggs, hard boiled
  • 60 g black botija olives
  • 4 lettuce leaves

Method

  1. Boil the deveined ají for 2 minutes, drain, and repeat twice with fresh water; the flesh should slump and the raw green smell give way to apricot. 2 min
  2. Sweat the onion and garlic in the oil over medium heat until translucent with no colour, add the drained ají and cook 2 minutes more, until the oil stains orange. Cool to room temperature. 2 min
  3. Boil the potatoes whole in well salted water until a skewer meets no resistance, about 20 minutes, peel them warm, then cool and slice 1 cm thick. 20 min
  4. Blend the cooled ají base with the queso fresco, crackers, evaporated milk and salt in short bursts, to a sauce that ribbons off the spatula and holds its line for a second.
  5. Pour over the potatoes on lettuce, top with egg and olives, and serve at room temperature; fridge cold, the sauce sets stiff and reads only of raw onion.

How to tell it is going right

This dish has no standard of its own yet. It is judged on the techniques it exercises: checked at the pan, while there is still something to be done about it.

Sautéing & the shallow fry 80 recipes

  • Everything sits in a single layer with visible space between the pieces: nothing stacked, nothing touching, or the pan is too small for the quantity.
  • The pieces move freely and keep moving, sliding across the pan rather than sticking and tearing when the pan is worked.
  • Light even browning arrived at quickly: colour on most faces, not the deep crust of a sear and not grey.
  • A thin film of fat with the food glossed by it: no pool left in the pan at the end, and nothing greasy on the fingers or the plate.
  • Cooked through but still firm, having spent only a few minutes in the pan: anything that has gone soft has been in there too long.

Where it goes wrong Too much went in at once, the pan cooled, and the food released its water: what was meant to brown in three minutes stews for ten and comes out grey, slack and wet.

The wobble & the skewer: testing a bake 135 recipes

  • Tested at the true centre and the thickest point, not at the convenient edge: the edge is done long before the answer is.
  • The skewer comes out as the recipe describes, clean and dry or carrying the stated moist crumb, and it does the same on a second test in a different spot.
  • The wobble is read in motion: nudged, the centre moves as one set mass with a faint tremble, rather than a liquid ripple travelling across the surface.
  • The other signs agree with the test: risen and just shrinking from the sides, the top set and dry to a light touch with no wet gloss on it.
  • Pressed lightly at the middle it springs back and leaves no lasting dent.

Where it goes wrong It was judged on the clock and on how the edges looked, so a centre still liquid went to the rack and sank as it cooled, a dense wet seam under a perfectly browned top.

From the pass

Ají amarillo keeps its heat in the pale placenta and its bitterness in a waxy skin. Devein it and boil it through three changes of water and you keep the fruit and the apricot smell without the burn. The sauce is an oil in water emulsion held by cracker starch and cheese protein: blend it long and friction heat melts the fat out of the cheese, leaving it grainy and greasy. Short bursts, cool ingredients.
