Chicken & Andouille Gumbo

  • Advanced
  • 2 h 30 m
  • $ roughly 1 of 4 for cost

herb-fresh · charred Leads with fresh herbs arguing happily on top, char used as punctuation.

Ingredients Method Check the result Pairings Your notes Videos

Ingredients

  • 120ml oil + 120g flour (the roux)
  • The trinity: 2 onions, 2 celery ribs, 1 green pepper, diced
  • 500g chicken thighs, 400g andouille, sliced
  • 1.5L stock
  • Cajun seasoning, bay, thyme
  • Scallions, filé powder optional, rice

Method

  1. Make a dark roux: whisk oil and flour over medium 30–45 min to milk-chocolate brown. Do not walk away. 45 min
  2. Trinity into the hot roux: it will roar and stop the cooking.
  3. Whisk in stock gradually; add browned sausage and chicken; simmer 1 hour. 60 min
  4. Skim, season, scallions. Serve over rice.

Stop at the stage the recipe calls for. Darker roux develops a deeper toasted flavour and has less thickening power. The numbered stages are a visual comparison, not a cooking timer.

    Pale and evenly mixed, with no dry pockets. A pale roux is used for sauces such as béchamel.

  2. 2

  3. 3

Making a rouxRoux, slurry and liaison

How to tell it is going right

This dish has no standard of its own yet. It is judged on the techniques it exercises: checked at the pan, while there is still something to be done about it.

Making a roux 32 recipes

  • Taken to the stage the dish named and no further, judged against that colour (pale, blond, or brown to brick), and it got there slowly enough to have coloured evenly.
  • It is smooth and mobile: a paste with no dry pockets of flour and no lumps, loosening as it warms rather than sitting stiff in the pan.
  • The raw-flour smell has gone and been replaced by the toasted, biscuity smell of its stage, with nothing acrid behind it.
  • No black specks anywhere. One scorched fleck condemns the batch; it cannot be stirred back in and it will be tasted in the finished sauce.
  • Let down into liquid it goes smooth without lumps and thickens to the body the dish wants, leaving no floury taste on the tongue.

Where it goes wrong The heat was too high and the corners of the pan went unstirred, so it scorched at the edge; black specks run through the finished sauce and carry a bitterness that no amount of seasoning covers.

Skimming: foam, fat and a clear broth 73 recipes

  • It is held below a rolling boil for the whole time. Agitation drives fat and fine protein into the liquid as an emulsion, and nothing skimmed afterwards will take them back out.
  • The grey foam that rises in the first minutes is lifted off and discarded rather than stirred back in.
  • Fat is taken from the surface where it gathers, the pot moved off the centre of the heat if that is what it takes to collect it in one place.
  • The liquid is visibly clearer at the end than it was at the start: it is possible to see down into it.
  • Nothing lifted off goes back in. What was skimmed is discarded, not tipped back once the ladle is full.

Where it goes wrong The pot was allowed to boil, usually while something else was being attended to. Fat and protein emulsify into the liquid under agitation and cannot be lifted off afterwards, so the finished broth stays cloudy and greasy however patiently it is skimmed from then on.

From the pass

The roux is a color, not a time: chocolate roux makes deep gumbo, blond roux makes étouffée, burnt roux means start over with no appeals; thirty-plus minutes of whisking with no walking away, because the difference between chocolate and burnt is about ninety seconds of inattention. The trinity going into the hot roux roars and stops the cooking at exactly the color you chose. Filé at the table, never in the pot. Gumbo is West African okra cooking, French roux and Choctaw filé in one bowl, Louisiana's whole story, simmered.
