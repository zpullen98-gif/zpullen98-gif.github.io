Conecuh Sausage and White Beans

  • Intermediate
  • 2 h 30 m
  • Serves 4
  • $$

smoky · aromatic · rich · warm-spiced Leads with wood-smoke running underneath, aromatics blooming from the first bite, fading in warm spice.

Ingredients

  • 500g smoked sausage (Conecuh or similar), sliced
  • 500g dried great northern beans, soaked
  • 1 onion, diced
  • 3 celery ribs, diced
  • 4 garlic cloves, minced
  • 1 bay leaf
  • 1 tsp thyme
  • 1.5L chicken stock
  • Salt, black pepper, and hot sauce
  • Cornbread to serve

The skills inside

Sweating aromatics: soft, never brownedRendering fat: and cooking in what runs outMashing & puréeing

Allergen screen

Found: celery

Screened for: gluten, dairy, egg, nuts, peanuts, fish, shellfish, molluscs, soy, sesame, celery, mustard, lupin, alcohol. Not screened: sulphites.

Method

  1. Brown the sausage in a heavy pot until it renders and colors; lift out and reserve.
  2. Sweat the onion, celery, and garlic in the fat until soft.
  3. Add the drained beans, bay, thyme, and stock and simmer 90 min until the beans are creamy. 90 min
  4. Return the sausage and simmer 20 min more, mashing some beans against the pot to thicken. 20 min
  5. Season and serve with cornbread and hot sauce.

How to tell it is going right

This dish has no standard of its own yet. It is judged on the techniques it exercises: checked at the pan, while there is still something to be done about it.

Rendering fat: and cooking in what runs out 63 recipes

  • It started cold or low and came up slowly: fat that met a hot pan seizes and scorches before the inside can melt, and there is no recovering it.
  • The fat in the pan is clear and clean-smelling, pale to golden, with no dark sediment on the floor and nothing acrid in the steam.
  • What is left behind is crisp and deep gold with no soft pale fat still clinging to it and no black burnt edges.
  • There is a real volume of it: enough to film the pan floor and be poured off and kept, not a dry smear that never ran.
  • Whatever cooks in it browns in it and takes its flavour, rather than sitting in a greasy pool that never gets hot enough to colour anything.

Where it goes wrong The heat was raised to hurry it along, so the outside seized and browned before the interior fat could melt; very little runs out, and the solids come out burnt on the outside and rubbery within.

Mashing & puréeing 90 recipes

  • Smooth to the degree the dish asks for, judged on the tongue rather than by eye: no lumps, no fibres, no grit, unless texture is the point.
  • No gluey elasticity: a spoonful drops cleanly off the spoon rather than stretching in a rope behind it.
  • The consistency holds: it mounds on a spoon and stays there, neither slumping flat nor standing so stiff that a spoon has to cut it.
  • Nothing separates on the plate: no water or fat weeping out into a ring within a minute of serving.
  • The colour is even throughout, with no streaks of unincorporated fat, liquid, or seasoning at the edges of the bowl.

Where it goes wrong A starchy ingredient met a high-speed blade, so the cells burst and released their starch: what should be light comes out as an elastic glue that lifts off the spoon in one mass.

From the pass

Conecuh County sausage is Alabama's smoky pride, hickory-smoked in the same small town since 1947, and a pot of white beans is its natural home; the sausage seasons the whole pot the way a hock would, with more snap. Salt only near the end: early salt keeps the bean skins tough for hours, the oldest bean law there is. Sear a few slices to lay on top of the finished bowl. Cornbread alongside, because Alabama and West Virginia agree on at least that much.
