Yucatecan · Starter

Panuchos Yucatecos

  • Advanced
  • 50 min
  • Serves 4
  • $$

fiery · rich · bright Leads with real fire that builds and stays, richness that coats and comforts, with a clean, bright snap.

Ingredients

  • 500 g fresh nixtamal masa, or 300 g masa harina hydrated with 380 ml warm water
  • 5 g fine salt
  • 250 g black beans, soaked overnight
  • 1 white onion, halved
  • 3 sprigs epazote
  • 1 habanero, left whole
  • 40 g pork lard
  • 400 ml neutral oil, for shallow frying
  • 300 g shredded cochinita pibil, or poached turkey breast
  • 150 g white cabbage, shaved fine
  • 200 g pickled red onion
  • 2 avocados, sliced

Method

  1. Simmer the beans with the onion, epazote and whole habanero until they collapse, about 90 minutes, then lift out the aromatics and blend the beans with just enough of their own liquor to pour. 90 min
  2. Push the puree through a sieve, then dry it in a pan with the lard over medium heat, stirring, until it pulls away from the base in one mass and a spoon track holds open for three seconds.
  3. Press 30 g balls of masa into 12 cm rounds and cook on a hot comal, 30 seconds, flip, 45 seconds, flip again and press the centre gently with a cloth until the tortilla balloons.
  4. While it is still hot, slit the puffed upper skin along a third of the rim with a knife tip, spoon in a tablespoon of bean paste, and press the pocket flat and shut.
  5. Shallow fry at 180 C (355 F) for about 40 seconds a side, until the surface blisters and stiffens to biscuit colour, drain, then top with the meat, cabbage, pickled onion and avocado.

How to tell it is going right

This dish has no standard of its own yet. It is judged on the techniques it exercises: checked at the pan, while there is still something to be done about it.

Tortillas on the comal 25 recipes

  • It puffs. On the second turn the tortilla lifts into a dome as the trapped water flashes to steam and splits the two faces apart; a tortilla that never puffs was pressed too thick, cooked too cool, or turned too often.
  • Freckled, not blackened: small brown spots across a pale field. An evenly browned tortilla has been on too long and will be a cracker by the time it reaches the table.
  • It folds without cracking while warm, and still folds after five minutes under a cloth. A tortilla that splits along the fold has lost too much water on the comal.
  • The edges are unbroken and roughly round, with no ragged fringe: a fringed edge means the masa was too dry when it was pressed, and no amount of heat will fix it afterwards.
  • It smells of toasted corn rather than of scorch or of raw lime. Raw lime means the nixtamal was under-rinsed; scorch means the comal was above the working heat.

Where it goes wrong The masa was too dry or the comal too cool. A dry masa cracks at the edge on pressing and cannot generate the steam that puffs it; a cool comal dries the tortilla through before the water can flash, so it leaves the heat stiff, pale and already stale.

Soaking dried goods back to life 29 recipes

  • Fully submerged with room to swell: the level is well above everything at the start and still above it at the end.
  • Given the full time, and judged by what is in the bowl rather than by the hours on the clock.
  • Tested at the CENTRE of the largest piece, cut or pressed. Anything with a hard dry core will still have one after cooking, because the outside softens first and stops the water getting further in.
  • The soaking water was dealt with on purpose: kept because it holds flavour, or thrown away because it holds what should not be eaten, but never simply left undecided.
  • Drained before it is used, so the dish does not silently gain all the water that came with it.

Where it goes wrong Too little water, or too little time. The pieces swell until they are packed tight against each other and the ones in the middle never fully take up water; they will not soften later in the dish either, so the finished plate has hard pieces scattered through it.

From the pass

The pocket is not cut into the tortilla, it is steam. On a hot comal both faces set into skins before the interior water flashes, and the trapped steam lifts them apart. Dry masa never seals, so it never puffs: the dough should feel like fresh putty and show no cracked edges when pressed. Fry hot and brief, because a slow fry heats the bean paste through, it swells, and the seam splits.
