Vispipuuro

  • Easy
  • 50 min
  • $$ roughly 2 of 4 for cost
  • Vegetarian

rich Leads with richness that coats and comforts.

Ingredients

  • 400 g lingonberries or cranberries
  • 1 litre water
  • 120 g fine semolina
  • 120 g caster sugar
  • 1 g fine salt
  • 400 ml cold whole milk, to serve

Equipment

Fine sieve

Method

  1. Simmer the berries in the water for 10 minutes until they have all burst, then strain through a sieve, pressing hard on the solids, and return the juice to the pan. Discard the skins. 10 min
  2. Rain the semolina into the simmering juice in a thin stream while whisking, add the sugar and salt, and cook at a bare simmer for 10 minutes, stirring, until the porridge holds the track of the whisk. 10 min
  3. Pour it into the cold bowl of a mixer, stand the bowl in a sink of iced water and stir now and then for about 20 minutes, until it is below 20C (68F). A fridge does the same job in about an hour. Whipping it warm does not work. 20 min
  4. Whip on high for 6 minutes to 8 minutes: it will roughly double, turn from wine dark to pale pink and begin to hold soft peaks. 6 min
  5. Spoon into bowls and pour the cold milk around it rather than over it.

How to tell it is going right

This dish has no standard of its own yet. It is judged on the techniques it exercises: checked at the pan, while there is still something to be done about it.

Whipping a meringue 40 recipes

  • Bowl and whisk are clean and dry with no trace of fat or yolk. Grease prevents the foam outright, and there is no recovering a bowl that had it partway through.
  • The sugar went in gradually once the whites were already foaming, rather than all at once at the start.
  • No grit: a little rubbed between finger and thumb feels smooth, so the sugar has dissolved into the foam rather than sitting in it.
  • Stiff peaks means the tip stands with a slight bow when the whisk lifts, and the bowl can be tipped without the mass moving in it.
  • Glossy and smooth rather than chalky or dull, with no liquid gathering underneath it in the bowl.

Where it goes wrong It was whipped past stiff peaks. A minute beyond glory the protein network tightens and tears, the foam turns grainy and matte, water weeps out beneath it, and folding that into anything gives a mixture that collapses on the way to the oven.

Straining & passing through a sieve 148 recipes

  • Nothing solid got through: a finger drawn through what came out finds no fibre, seed, skin, curd, or grit.
  • Where the dish wants body it was worked through rather than left to drip, and the solids left in the mesh are spent and dry rather than still holding liquid worth having.
  • It is judged on the tongue rather than by eye: smooth, with no residual grain left behind after the mouthful has gone.
  • The mesh was fine enough for the job and held its shape, and nothing bypassed it by going over the rim.
  • The yield is close to what went in less the spent solids: not half of it abandoned in the sieve because it stopped running and nobody pushed.

Where it goes wrong It was pushed through a mesh too coarse for it, or the sieve was overloaded and slopped at the rim, so the fibres it was meant to remove are in the finished sauce and it reads grainy on the tongue.

From the pass

No egg, no cream, no gelatine: the foam here is held up by cold starch alone. Semolina swells and leaks amylose, which sets to a weak elastic gel as it cools, and whipping tears that gel into bubble walls stiff enough not to drain. Warm, the same gel is fluid and the air simply walks out, which is why it must go below 20C (68F) first. Berry acid slowly cuts starch chains, so cook it briefly and whip it long, not the other way round.
