Jajangmyeon

  • Intermediate
  • 45 min
  • $$ roughly 2 of 4 for cost

umami-deep · briny · rich · bright Leads with savory depth stacked from the pantry’s bass notes, a briny, maritime salinity, with a clean, bright snap.

Ingredients

  • 4 tbsp chunjang (Korean black bean paste): fried FIRST in oil, alone; raw chunjang is bitter and unrepentant
  • 300g pork belly or shoulder, small dice; 1 large onion + 1 zucchini + ½ cabbage wedge, all in chunky dice; potato optional
  • 1 tbsp sugar; 2 tbsp oyster sauce; 350ml stock or water; cornstarch slurry
  • 500g fresh thick wheat noodles (jajang or udon-style)
  • Julienned cucumber to crown; danmuji (yellow pickled radish) on the side, the required companion

Method

  1. Fry the chunjang in 3 tbsp oil over medium, stirring, 3–4 min until glossy and nutty: this step converts bitterness to depth and cannot be skipped; set the fried paste aside. 4 min
  2. Render the pork; add vegetables and stir-fry until edges soften.
  3. Return the paste with sugar, oyster sauce and stock; simmer 5 min; thicken with slurry to a lava-thick black gravy. 5 min
  4. Boil noodles, rinse briefly, bowl them; ladle the black sauce over in a generous crater-fill; cucumber on top. Mix until every strand is night-colored. Eat with the radish alongside.

How to tell it is going right

This dish has no standard of its own yet. It is judged on the techniques it exercises: checked at the pan, while there is still something to be done about it.

Wok technique 28 recipes

  • Everything went in dry and went in in order. Wet protein or wet vegetables drop the wok below the heat that browns, and what follows is a stew in a round pan however hard the burner is working.
  • The wok stays loud from the first moment to the last. A wok that falls quiet has been overloaded, and the food in it is now boiling in its own liquid.
  • Every piece is coated and glossy with no pooled sauce in the bottom of the dish: the sauce should cling by reduction and starch, not sit under the food.
  • Vegetables keep their colour and a bite at the centre. Grey-green greens or a limp stem mean too long in the pan, and nothing later in the dish recovers them.
  • It carries the faint scorched note that only comes from food touching metal above about 200C, distinct from bitterness, which means the aromatics were burnt and is a different failure entirely.

Where it goes wrong Too much went in at once, or it went in wet. The wok loses its heat, the food releases water and simmers, the sauce thins instead of clinging, and the dish arrives pale and slack with liquid pooling under it.

Rendering fat: and cooking in what runs out 73 recipes

  • It started cold or low and came up slowly: fat that met a hot pan seizes and scorches before the inside can melt, and there is no recovering it.
  • The fat in the pan is clear and clean-smelling, pale to golden, with no dark sediment on the floor and nothing acrid in the steam.
  • What is left behind is crisp and deep gold with no soft pale fat still clinging to it and no black burnt edges.
  • There is a real volume of it: enough to film the pan floor and be poured off and kept, not a dry smear that never ran.
  • Whatever cooks in it browns in it and takes its flavour, rather than sitting in a greasy pool that never gets hot enough to colour anything.

Where it goes wrong The heat was raised to hurry it along, so the outside seized and browned before the interior fat could melt; very little runs out, and the solids come out burnt on the outside and rubbery within.

From the pass

Korea's Chinese-restaurant icon, delivered by the million (it is THE moving-day and payday meal, and famously eaten alone on Black Day, April 14th, by the unattached). The fried-paste step is the entire secret: chunjang goes from acrid to chocolate-deep in three minutes of oil. Slurp loudly, wear the splatter, and know that a white shirt near jajangmyeon is a decision, not an accident.
