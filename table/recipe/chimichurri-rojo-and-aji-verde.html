The Saucier · Sauce

Chimichurri Rojo & Ají Verde

  • Easy
  • 20 min
  • Serves 4
  • $$$
  • Vegetarian

rich · bright · gently hot · herb-fresh Leads with richness that coats and comforts, acid holding everything up to the light, lifted green at the very end.

Ingredients

  • Rojo: the green chimichurri's kit (parsley, garlic, oregano, vinegar, oil) PLUS 2 tbsp sweet paprika + 1 tsp smoked + chili flakes
  • Ají verde: 3 jalapeños, 100g cotija or parmesan, 100ml mayo, huacatay if the universe provides, cilantro storm, garlic, lime
  • Salt, patience of at least one hour for the rojo
  • A blender for the ají (this one WANTS the machine), a knife for the rojo
  • Grilled meats, roast chicken, fries, eggs: they all apply

Equipment

Blender

Allergen screen

Found: dairy · egg

Screened for: gluten, dairy, egg, nuts, peanuts, fish, shellfish, molluscs, soy, sesame, celery, mustard, lupin, alcohol. Not screened: sulphites.

Method

  1. Rojo: hand-chop as for green chimichurri; bloom the paprikas in 2 tbsp warmed oil first (30 seconds, off heat) and fold through with the rest of the oil and vinegar; rest 1 h. 60 min
  2. Ají verde: blitz jalapeños, cheese, mayo, garlic, cilantro, lime to a smooth pale-green cream.
  3. Salt both; loosen the ají with water if needed to pouring-cream body.
  4. Serve the rojo on beef and pork; the ají on rotisserie chicken, fries, and everything else: Lima's green sauce takes prisoners.

From the pass

Two South American finishing sauces, two philosophies: the rojo is rustic and knife-cut with bloomed paprika doing the smoky work; Peru's ají verde is a creamy ambush: mild at first taste, insistent by the third. Peruvian chicken shops guard their exact ratios like state secrets, and now you hold a working key.
