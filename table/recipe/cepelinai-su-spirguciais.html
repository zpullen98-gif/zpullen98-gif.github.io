Cepelinai su Spirgučiais

  • Advanced
  • 65 min
  • $$ roughly 2 of 4 for cost

rich · herb-fresh · smoky Leads with richness that coats and comforts, fresh herbs arguing happily on top, on a drift of smoke.

Ingredients

  • 1 kg floury potatoes, peeled, for grating
  • 300 g floury potatoes, peeled, for boiling
  • 400 g coarsely minced pork shoulder
  • 1 onion, grated
  • 2 tsp dried marjoram
  • 15 g salt, plus a heavy hand for the pot
  • 1 tsp ground black pepper
  • 1 tbsp potato starch, if needed
  • 150 g smoked streaky bacon, diced small
  • 1 onion, finely chopped, for the spirgučiai
  • 200 g sour cream
  • 10 g dill, chopped

Equipment

Grater / zester

Method

  1. Boil the 300 g of potatoes until a knife slides out clean, drain them very dry over the heat, rice them and leave to cool.
  2. Grate the raw kilo on the finest drum, wring it hard in a cloth over a bowl, then let the liquid stand 20 minutes; pour off the grey water and scrape the chalky white starch from the bottom back into the pulp. 20 min
  3. Mix pulp, riced potato and salt to a dough that holds a fingerprint, adding the starch only if it sags; work the pork with grated onion, marjoram and pepper and roll it into 8 ovals.
  4. With wet hands flatten a palmful of dough, seat a meat oval in it and close it into a zeppelin, then roll it between your palms to find any seam or thin patch before it reaches the water.
  5. Slide them into heavily salted water held at a bare tremble around 90C (195F), never a rolling boil, and poach 25 to 30 minutes until they float high and feel springy rather than soft. 30 min
  6. Render the bacon slowly until the fat runs and the pieces crisp, soften the onion in it, then spoon it hot over the drained dumplings with cold sour cream and dill.

How to tell it is going right

This dish has no standard of its own yet. It is judged on the techniques it exercises: checked at the pan, while there is still something to be done about it.

Rendering fat: and cooking in what runs out 73 recipes

  • It started cold or low and came up slowly: fat that met a hot pan seizes and scorches before the inside can melt, and there is no recovering it.
  • The fat in the pan is clear and clean-smelling, pale to golden, with no dark sediment on the floor and nothing acrid in the steam.
  • What is left behind is crisp and deep gold with no soft pale fat still clinging to it and no black burnt edges.
  • There is a real volume of it: enough to film the pan floor and be poured off and kept, not a dry smear that never ran.
  • Whatever cooks in it browns in it and takes its flavour, rather than sitting in a greasy pool that never gets hot enough to colour anything.

Where it goes wrong The heat was raised to hurry it along, so the outside seized and browned before the interior fat could melt; very little runs out, and the solids come out burnt on the outside and rubbery within.

Shaping by hand 85 recipes

  • The pieces match: weighed, or judged against each other rather than by dividing the mass by eye, so that no piece is half again the size of its neighbour.
  • Surfaces are smooth and unbroken with no cracks, tears, or dry patches, and the seams are sealed and sitting underneath.
  • The shape is defined and it holds while the tray waits: it has not slumped, spread, or relaxed back out of its form.
  • Nothing is trapped that should not be: no air pocket where filling should be, no filling breaking through the surface, and no excess flour greying the outside.
  • They finish together in the heat: no piece done while another is still raw, which is the only test that the first mark was met.

Where it goes wrong The dough was worked past its point or shaped without a rest, so it fought back: the pieces spring out of shape on the tray and bake tight, uneven, and torn along the surface.

From the pass

The ratio is the dish: roughly three parts pressed raw potato to one part boiled and riced. Raw potato carries the flavour and ungelatinised starch, while the cooked potato carries gelatinised starch, which is the only real glue. Pour away the starch that settles out of the pressed liquid and they dissolve in the pot. Let the water boil rather than tremble and they split along the seam.
