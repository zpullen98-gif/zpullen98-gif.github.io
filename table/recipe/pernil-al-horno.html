Pernil al Horno

  • Intermediate
  • 30 min
  • $ roughly 1 of 4 for cost

tangy-ferment · aromatic · warm-spiced · briny Leads with ferment tang with real depth behind it, aromatics blooming from the first bite, ending saline and clean.

Ingredients

  • 3 kg bone-in pork shoulder with the skin on
  • 45 g garlic, about 12 cloves
  • 25 g coarse salt
  • 2 tbsp dried oregano
  • 2 tsp coarsely ground black pepper
  • 45 ml olive oil
  • 45 ml white vinegar or sour orange juice
  • 1 tsp ground annatto

Method

  1. Slide a knife between the skin and the fat along one long edge and work it back into a flap, leaving it hinged at the far side. Do not cut it off.
  2. Mash the garlic with the salt, oregano and pepper in the pilón to a paste, then loosen it with the oil, vinegar and annatto.
  3. Stab the meat all over to the depth of the blade and push the paste into every cut, coat the exposed flesh, then lay the skin flap back loose and leave the skin itself bare. Refrigerate uncovered overnight.
  4. Roast with the flap folded back at 150C (300F) until the meat twists off the bone with a fork, 5 to 6 hours; the fat under the flap should have rendered to a shallow pool. 360 min
  5. Lay the skin flat over the meat, blot it dry, and blast at 230C (450F) until it blisters, lifts and goes hard, 20 to 30 minutes. 30 min
  6. Rest 30 minutes, crack the cuerito off in sheets, and pull the meat through the pan juices. 30 min

How to tell it is going right

This dish has no standard of its own yet. It is judged on the techniques it exercises: checked at the pan, while there is still something to be done about it.

Roasting in a hot oven 56 recipes

  • The oven was fully up to heat before anything went in, given time beyond the indicator light rather than judged by it.
  • Every piece has air around it. Nothing touches its neighbour and nothing is packed to the edge of the tray, because crowded food steams itself in its own moisture and never browns.
  • Colour is even across the whole tray, the tray having been turned at least once: no pale row at the back and no dark row at the front.
  • It came out BEFORE the target temperature, not at it. Carryover keeps driving the inside up by several degrees after it leaves the oven, and that rise is part of the cooking rather than an error in it.
  • The temperature was read at the coldest part rather than the easiest one to reach, and read again after the rest.

Where it goes wrong The tray was crowded, or the oven was not truly up to heat. The food releases steam faster than a cool box can carry it away, so it sits pale and slack, and the extra minutes spent chasing colour take the inside well past where it should have stopped.

Where it goes wrong A starchy ingredient met a high-speed blade, so the cells burst and released their starch: what should be light comes out as an elastic glue that lifts off the spoon in one mass.

From the pass

The cuerito is a drying problem, not a roasting one. Loosen the skin before it goes in so the fat beneath renders out and drains away; foil over the top, or pan juices touching the skin, steams it into leather that no final blast will rescue. Salt the flesh under the skin rather than the skin itself, and leave it uncovered in the fridge overnight. The 230C (450F) blast only puffs skin that is already dry.
