Baked Alaska

  • Advanced
  • 2 h
  • $$ roughly 2 of 4 for cost
  • Vegetarian

rich · nutty-toasted Leads with richness that coats and comforts, toasted, nutty warmth throughout.

Ingredients

  • 1 disc of sponge or pound cake, 20cm, brushed with a little liqueur or syrup
  • 1L ice cream: one flavor or layered in a bowl-mold (the dome is traditional); FROZEN ROCK-HARD, 24 h
  • Italian meringue: 4 egg whites, 220g sugar cooked to 118°C with 60ml water, whipped in as syrup until stiff, glossy and cool
  • A piping bag or palette knife for the meringue armor: full coverage, no gaps; the meringue is the insulation
  • A 240°C oven for 3–4 minutes, or a torch, or (for the tableside legend) warmed rum and a match

Equipment

Piping bag · Sheet pan · Torch

Method

  1. Mold the ice cream in a bowl lined with film; freeze solid; unmold onto the cake disc; back into the freezer.
  2. Make the Italian meringue: the hot syrup cooks the whites, which is what lets the dessert survive the oven.
  3. Work fast: armor the entire dome and cake edge in thick meringue swoops, sealing to the tray; freeze again 1 h (or up to 2 days, the make-ahead secret). 60 min
  4. Blast at 240°C 3–4 min until the peaks bronze, or torch it, and serve immediately, cutting with a hot knife: hot toasted shell, frozen heart, cake foundation, all in one slice. 4 min

How it should come out

  • The go/no-go at the oven door: the meringue held a straight peak off the whisk and the bowl was cool to the palm before any of it went on, and the assembled dome is hard to a knuckle-rap everywhere, base included. A dome that gives anywhere goes back to the freezer, not into the oven.
  • Sealed all round: at least 2cm of meringue over the dome, covering the cake edge and meeting the tray, with no window of ice cream showing and no crack at the base.
  • Peaks bronzed with the valleys still cream-white after 3 to 4 minutes; an evenly tanned dome means it stayed in and the heat reached the ice cream.
  • Cut with a hot knife, the face shows three bands with hard borders: hot toasted shell, ice cream holding its own edge, cake beneath. A melted ring under the meringue and the oven won.
  • No beads of syrup on the meringue and no liquid on the plate a minute after service, and the cake base is still dry enough to lift the slice on a spatula.

Where it goes wrong The meringue goes onto ice cream that has softened at the surface, or goes on still warm from the syrup: the two meet as slush, the dome slides and the seal splits at the base, and the slice arrives as cream soup under browned foam.

From the pass

The dessert is a physics demonstration with a dessert license: meringue is mostly trapped air, and air is such a poor conductor that the oven never learns there is ice cream inside. Delmonico's named it for the Alaska Purchase in 1867, and the flaming-rum service is the showman's cadenza. The whole thing waits fully assembled in the freezer for days: the most theatrical dessert in this guide is secretly the best make-ahead in it.
