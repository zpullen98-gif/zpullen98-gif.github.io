Chocolate Chip Cookies

  • Easy
  • 45 min
  • $$ roughly 2 of 4 for cost
  • Vegetarian

rich · floral · sweet-edged Leads with richness that coats and comforts, a floral thread lifting the aroma, trailing gentle sweetness.

Ingredients

  • 225g butter, browned and cooled slightly
  • 200g brown sugar + 100g white
  • 2 eggs, 2 tsp vanilla
  • 300g flour, 1 tsp baking soda, 1 tsp salt
  • 250g chocolate, half chips, half chopped bar
  • Flaky salt

Method

  1. Brown the butter: nutty, amber, sediment and all.
  2. Whisk with sugars, then eggs and vanilla until glossy.
  3. Fold in dry, then chocolate. REST the dough 1–24 h in the fridge. 600 min
  4. Bake 190°C, 11–12 min, until edges set and centers look slightly undone. Flaky salt while warm. 12 min

How to tell it is going right

This dish has no standard of its own yet. It is judged on the techniques it exercises: checked at the pan, while there is still something to be done about it.

Folding: keeping the air in 34 recipes

  • The air was actually built first and it shows: whatever was creamed or whipped has changed colour and grown in volume, pale and light rather than dense.
  • The two mixtures were brought closer before being combined: a little of the light one worked into the heavy one first, so the rest is not beaten flat trying to move it.
  • Folded and not stirred: a broad implement down through the middle, across the base, up and over, with the bowl turned between strokes.
  • Stopped the moment the streaks disappear. It is still visibly aerated and holds its shape briefly when the spoon lifts away.
  • Into the tin and into the oven straight away: a batter left standing on the bench gives back the air it was built for.

Where it goes wrong It was stirred until it looked perfectly smooth. Every stroke past the point where the streaks vanish knocks out air that cannot be put back, so the bake comes out dense and short of the tin, and often wet through the middle where there was no structure to hold it.

Resting dough: the pause that does the work 71 recipes

  • It actually rested, covered, for the time stated, not cut short because the rest of the schedule was tight.
  • It rolls and shapes without fighting: it stays where it is put, and it does not spring back or shrink on the bench between passes.
  • The surface is smooth and supple rather than dry, cracked, or skinned over.
  • It is at the temperature the next step needs: cold where the fat must stay solid, cool and relaxed where it must be rolled thin.
  • Baked, it holds the dimensions it was shaped to rather than shrinking back from the edges of the tin or the rim of the dish.

Where it goes wrong The rest was skipped or shortened, so the gluten was still tight: the dough fought the pin and sprang back thick and uneven, then shrank down the sides of the tin in the oven.

From the pass

Resting the dough hydrates the flour and deepens the toffee notes: a 24-hour cookie genuinely tastes like a different recipe, which is the pastry-counter secret hiding in plain sight. Browning the butter first stacks nutty Maillard flavor under the sugar. Half chips, half chopped bar: the bar shards spread into strata while the chips hold their shape. Pull them looking slightly underdone at the center; carryover finishes what the oven started, and flaky salt lands while they're warm.
