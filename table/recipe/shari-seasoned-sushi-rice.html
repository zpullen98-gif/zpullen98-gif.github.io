Shari, Seasoned Sushi Rice

  • Intermediate
  • 25 min
  • Serves 4
  • $
  • Vegetarian
  • Vegan

umami-deep · bright Leads with savory depth stacked from the pantry’s bass notes, acid holding everything up to the light.

Ingredients

  • 600 g Japanese short grain rice
  • 600 ml cold water
  • 5 cm piece of kombu
  • 90 ml rice vinegar
  • 36 g sugar
  • 12 g salt

Equipment

Stand mixer

Allergen screen

None found among the 14 screened.

Screened for: gluten, dairy, egg, nuts, peanuts, fish, shellfish, molluscs, soy, sesame, celery, mustard, lupin, alcohol. Not screened: sulphites.

Method

  1. Wash the rice in cold water, turning it with an open hand and changing the water four or five times, until it runs faintly cloudy rather than clear. Over-washing chips the grains. Drain in a sieve for 30 minutes. 30 min
  2. Cook it with the measured water and the kombu laid on top; lift the kombu out as the water comes to the boil, cover, cook on the lowest heat 12 minutes, then leave the lid on off the heat for 10 minutes more. 12 min
  3. Warm the vinegar, sugar and salt together just until the crystals dissolve, no further, or the acid boils off.
  4. Tip the hot rice into a wide wooden or plastic tub, pour the seasoning over the back of a paddle so it lands spread out, and cut through the rice at a slant, folding rather than stirring, while fanning it.
  5. Stop when the rice is at body temperature, about 37C (99F), the grains gloss individually and a squeezed handful holds together. Cover with a damp cloth and use it within the hour.

How to tell it is going right

This dish has no standard of its own yet. It is judged on the techniques it exercises: checked at the pan, while there is still something to be done about it.

Soaking dried goods back to life 59 recipes

  • Fully submerged with room to swell: the level is well above everything at the start and still above it at the end.
  • Given the full time, and judged by what is in the bowl rather than by the hours on the clock.
  • Tested at the CENTRE of the largest piece, cut or pressed. Anything with a hard dry core will still have one after cooking, because the outside softens first and stops the water getting further in.
  • The soaking water was dealt with on purpose: kept because it holds flavour, or thrown away because it holds what should not be eaten, but never simply left undecided.
  • Drained before it is used, so the dish does not silently gain all the water that came with it.

Where it goes wrong Too little water, or too little time. The pieces swell until they are packed tight against each other and the ones in the middle never fully take up water; they will not soften later in the dish either, so the finished plate has hard pieces scattered through it.

Straining & passing through a sieve 110 recipes

  • Nothing solid got through: a finger drawn through what came out finds no fibre, seed, skin, curd, or grit.
  • Where the dish wants body it was worked through rather than left to drip, and the solids left in the mesh are spent and dry rather than still holding liquid worth having.
  • It is judged on the tongue rather than by eye: smooth, with no residual grain left behind after the mouthful has gone.
  • The mesh was fine enough for the job and held its shape, and nothing bypassed it by going over the rim.
  • The yield is close to what went in less the spent solids: not half of it abandoned in the sieve because it stopped running and nobody pushed.

Where it goes wrong It was pushed through a mesh too coarse for it, or the sieve was overloaded and slopped at the rim, so the fibres it was meant to remove are in the finished sauce and it reads grainy on the tongue.

From the pass

Season the rice hot: the starch granules are still swollen and open, and they drink the vinegar in. Let it cool first and the seasoning sits on the outside and reads harsh. Cut with the edge of the paddle instead of stirring, since crushed grains give paste, and fan to drive off surface moisture so the rice glosses rather than sweats. Never refrigerate it. Near 4C (39F) the starch retrogrades and goes chalky for good.
