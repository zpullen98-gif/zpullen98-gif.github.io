French · Dessert

Crème Brûlée

  • Intermediate
  • 60 min
  • $$ roughly 2 of 4 for cost
  • Vegetarian

rich · floral · charred · silky Leads with richness that coats and comforts, a floral thread lifting the aroma, melting away silken.

Ingredients

  • 500ml cream
  • 1 vanilla bean or 2 tsp paste
  • 6 egg yolks
  • 80g sugar + more for the crust

Equipment

Ramekins · Torch · Bain-marie

Method

  1. Warm cream with vanilla. Whisk yolks and sugar; temper in the cream.
  2. Strain into ramekins; bake in a water bath at 150°C, 30–35 min, until just wobbly. 35 min
  3. Chill 4+ hours.
  4. Sprinkle a thin, even layer of sugar; torch to amber glass.

How it should come out

  • The sugar is one unbroken amber sheet edge to edge, right up to the ramekin wall: no black scorched spots, no white grains left unmelted.
  • The back of a spoon breaks it in a single crack and the shards come away large; the sugar layer is under 2mm.
  • Nudged at the end of the bake, the custard moves as one slow mass under a set surface: no loose ripple or liquid slack at the centre, and no ramekin that has stopped moving altogether. Chilled, it is set edge to edge with nothing sloshing.
  • The first spoonful leaves a clean-walled trench that holds its shape, with no watery pool collecting in it and no grain or bubbles in the custard.
  • The custard is still fridge-cold to the spoon while the caramel over it is warm; if both are the same temperature, it was torched too early.

Where it goes wrong It is baked to firm instead of to the wobble: held in until nothing moves at all, so carryover pushes it past the set: the custard puffs, tightens grainy at the edges and weeps a ring of watery whey that pools in the first spoonful.

From the pass

Two thin torched layers beat one thick one: better crack, no burnt patches, because a deep bed of sugar scorches on top before the bottom melts. The custard itself is an exercise in restraint: yolks only, a water bath to hold the oven's violence at arm's length, and out at the barely-wobbly stage since carryover finishes the set. The point of the dish is contrast, cold silk under hot glass, which is why the torching happens at the last minute, never ahead.
