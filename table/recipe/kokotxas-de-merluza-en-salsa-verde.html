Kokotxas de Merluza en Salsa Verde

  • Advanced
  • 25 min
  • $$ roughly 2 of 4 for cost

herb-fresh · aromatic · briny · fiery Leads with fresh herbs arguing happily on top, aromatics blooming from the first bite, on a slow, honest burn.

Ingredients

  • 500 g hake kokotxas, the throats, grey membrane trimmed
  • 120 ml olive oil
  • 4 garlic cloves, sliced paper thin
  • 1 dried guindilla chilli
  • 8 small clams, purged in salted water
  • 100 ml txakoli or another sharp dry white
  • 30 g flat parsley leaves, very finely chopped
  • fine salt

Method

  1. Salt the kokotxas lightly, leave 10 minutes, then blot both faces on a cloth until the surface is matt rather than slippery. 10 min
  2. Warm the oil with garlic and chilli in a wide cazuela over the lowest heat until the garlic is pale gold, then fish it out and set it aside.
  3. Take the pan off the heat until the oil is hand warm, lay the kokotxas in skin side down in a single layer, and set it back on the barest heat for 3 minutes, until white beads of gel gather round each edge. 3 min
  4. Off the heat again, swirl the pan flat and slow for 3 to 4 minutes; the oil and the seeping gel will bind and turn pale and cloudy. Add the wine and the clams, swirl over low heat until the shells gape and the sauce falls from the spoon in a ribbon. 4 min
  5. Kill the heat, shower in the parsley, swirl once more so it stays bright green, and serve in the cazuela.

How to tell it is going right

This dish has no standard of its own yet. It is judged on the technique it exercises: checked at the pan, while there is still something to be done about it.

Salted water & the float test 188 recipes

  • The water was salted before anything went in, and tasting it confirms it: seasoned on the tongue, with the salt dissolved rather than sitting on the pot floor.
  • There is enough water, hot enough, that the pot returns to its boil quickly after the food goes in and never stops moving.
  • Where the dish uses the float as its signal, the signal is obeyed: the pieces rise and they come out then, not several minutes later once something else was finished.
  • Nothing is stuck to the bottom of the pot or welded to itself, and the water has not thickened into a cloudy sludge.
  • Drained on a bite rather than on the clock: resistance at the centre, with no chalky raw core.

Where it goes wrong The pot was too small and the water unsalted, so it never recovered its boil: the food sat in slack water, went gummy at the surface, and comes out bland in a way that no sauce afterwards can fix.

From the pass

Kokotxas are almost pure collagen, so salsa verde is pil pil with parsley: the gel that seeps from the throats emulsifies the garlic oil. Above a bare tremble they clench, squeeze out water instead of gel, and the pan becomes a puddle of oil floating on fish juice. Keep the cazuela hand warm, swirl it flat on the worktop, and add the wine only once the sauce has already gone pale and thick.
