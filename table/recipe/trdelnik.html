Czech & Slovak · Dessert

Trdelník

  • Advanced
  • 50 min
  • $$ roughly 2 of 4 for cost
  • Vegetarian

rich · sweet-edged · earthy · nutty-toasted Leads with richness that coats and comforts, a sweet edge rounding the corners, closing toasted and warm.

Ingredients

  • 500 g strong white flour
  • 250 ml whole milk at 35C (95F)
  • 10 g fresh yeast, or 4 g dried
  • 60 g caster sugar
  • 2 egg yolks
  • 60 g butter, soft
  • 6 g fine salt
  • zest of 1 lemon
  • 100 g butter, melted, for brushing
  • 150 g caster sugar, for the coating
  • 100 g walnuts, finely chopped
  • 5 g ground cinnamon

Equipment

Rolling pin · Grater / zester

Method

  1. Whisk the yeast and a spoon of the sugar into the warm milk and wait for a foam cap.
  2. Work in the flour, yolks, remaining sugar, salt and zest, then the soft butter, kneading until the dough is smooth and pulls a thin translucent window.
  3. Prove until doubled, roll out to 5 mm and cut ribbons 2 cm wide; wind them round greased foil wrapped wooden rollers with the edges just touching, and press the two ends down onto the roller.
  4. Brush with melted butter, roll in the mixed sugar, walnut and cinnamon, and prove again on the roller until visibly puffed and the seams have started to close.
  5. Bake at 200C (400F), turning the rollers every few minutes, until the sugar has run into a shining caramel and the seams have knitted, about eighteen minutes.
  6. Twist each one free while it is still hot, then stand them upright to cool so the caramel sets rather than pools.

How to tell it is going right

This dish has no standard of its own yet. It is judged on the techniques it exercises: checked at the pan, while there is still something to be done about it.

Egg wash & the baker’s shine 34 recipes

  • Applied thin and even over every surface that will show, with no bare patches and no ridges or brush lines standing in it.
  • Nothing pooled: no wash run down into the seams, collected in the corners, or welded to the tray around the base.
  • It has not glued shut what needs to move: layers, scores, and cut edges are still free to open in the heat.
  • Baked, the finish is even and glossy across the whole piece and deep gold rather than patchy, with no darker bands where the wash sat heavy.
  • The shine is on the outside only: nothing inside the piece tastes of egg.

Where it goes wrong The wash went on heavy and ran into the scores and down to the tray, so the piece baked sealed along its seams, browned unevenly, and carries dark rubbery ridges where the egg collected.

Sugar stages & caramel 53 recipes

  • Nothing is stirred once it is boiling, and any crystals clinging to the side of the pan are brushed down rather than left to fall in.
  • Judged by temperature or by a tested stage rather than by time: consecutive stages are a few degrees apart and look identical in the pan.
  • Colour is read off the heat and in good light, remembering that sugar keeps darkening after the flame is out.
  • Stopped decisively at the point wanted: off the heat and into the next liquid or onto a cold surface, rather than left to coast in a hot pan.
  • The result is clear and smooth rather than grainy, cloudy, or seized into a solid mass.

Where it goes wrong It was stirred after coming to the boil, or a stray crystal from the side of the pan fell back in. One crystal seeds the whole pan and the syrup turns grainy within seconds, and once sugar has crystallised the only cure is more water and starting the whole thing again.

From the pass

Labelled honestly: this is sold in Prague as an ancient Czech tradition and it is not one. The spit cake is Transylvanian kurtoskalacs, which reached the tourist streets in the 1990s; the older Slovak claim is the Skalica trdelnik, a denser walnut cake baked another way. Mechanically the sugar has to caramelise before the thin ribbon dries out, so keep the heat high and the roller turning, and twist it off while hot or it welds to the form.
