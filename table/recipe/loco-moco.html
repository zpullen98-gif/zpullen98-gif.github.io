Pacific & Hawaiian · Breakfast

Loco Moco

  • Easy
  • 35 min
  • Serves 4
  • $$

umami-deep · herb-fresh · charred Leads with savory depth stacked from the pantry’s bass notes, fresh herbs arguing happily on top, with a bitter-edged char at the rim.

Ingredients

  • 2 cups hot steamed white rice, the foundation, packed in a bowl
  • 2 beef patties (200g each), seasoned simply, smashed thin-edged
  • 2 eggs, fried sunny with lacy edges and loose yolks
  • Brown gravy: pan drippings + 1 tbsp flour + 300ml beef stock + splash of soy and Worcestershire
  • Green onions; hot sauce within reach

Equipment

Skillet

The skills inside

Searing: the hard crust

Allergen screen

Found: gluten · egg · fish · soy

Screened for: gluten, dairy, egg, nuts, peanuts, fish, shellfish, molluscs, soy, sesame, celery, mustard, lupin, alcohol. Not screened: sulphites.

Method

  1. Sear the patties hard in a skillet for a real crust; set aside.
  2. Gravy in the same pan: flour into the drippings, stock whisked in, soy and Worcestershire, simmered to spoon-coating.
  3. Fry the eggs in a corner of the pan, basting with the gravy pan's fat.
  4. Stack in order: rice, patty, gravy poured generously, egg crowning; scallions. Break the yolk into the gravy: that is the moment the dish is named for.

How to tell it is going right

This dish has no standard of its own yet. It is judged on the technique it exercises: checked at the pan, while there is still something to be done about it.

Searing: the hard crust 113 recipes

  • The pan keeps a hard, steady sizzle from the moment the food lands and does not fall quiet; silence within the first half minute means the pan lost its heat and the surface is now steaming rather than browning.
  • It releases itself when tested: slide a thin edge under and it lifts without tearing. Anything that grips the pan has not formed a crust yet and is not ready to be moved.
  • Deep even brown across the whole face, edge to edge, when it is turned under the light: no pale ring left around a browned centre, and no black patches that taste bitter on their own.
  • The pan floor carries a dry browned layer that dissolves and lifts the moment liquid hits it: not a wet grey film, and not black flecks that stay put and stay bitter.
  • Cut into it: the browned band is thin, a few millimetres at most, sitting straight on top of correctly cooked interior; a thick grey collar under the crust means the heat was too low for too long.

Where it goes wrong The pan was not hot enough, or too much went in at once, so the food released its water and sat in it: the surface goes grey and steams, it sticks when moved, and the crust that eventually forms arrives over an interior already cooked through.

From the pass

Born in 1949 at a Hilo diner when teenagers wanted something cheaper than a sandwich and bigger than breakfast: the Lincoln Grill obliged, and Hawaii adopted it statewide. The loco moco is gravy engineering: rice below to catch, yolk above to enrich, and the whole thing eaten with a spoon. It is breakfast, lunch, and post-surf medicine in one bowl.
