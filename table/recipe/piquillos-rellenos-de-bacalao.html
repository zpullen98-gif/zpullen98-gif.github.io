Piquillos Rellenos de Bacalao

  • Intermediate
  • 50 min
  • Serves 4
  • $$

warm-spiced · rich · aromatic · smoky Leads with warm spice humming through the middle, richness that coats and comforts, on a drift of smoke.

Ingredients

  • 16 whole piquillo peppers from Lodosa, drained but never rinsed
  • 300 g salt cod, desalted, in pieces
  • 500 ml whole milk
  • 1 onion, 150 g, in a fine dice
  • 60 ml olive oil
  • 60 g plain flour
  • 1 garlic clove
  • nutmeg and salt
  • 4 broken or torn piquillos, for the sauce
  • 150 ml single cream
  • 100 ml light fish stock or the poaching milk

Equipment

Piping bag · Sheet pan

Method

  1. Poach the cod in the milk with the garlic clove at 70C (160F), just under a shiver, for 6 minutes, until the flakes part under a fork. Lift it out, keep the milk hot, and flake the fish, discarding skin and bone. 6 min
  2. Sweat the onion in the oil until soft and blond, about 12 minutes. Scatter in the flour and cook 3 minutes until it smells of biscuit, then beat in the hot milk a ladle at a time until the bechamel pulls away from the base in one mass. 12 min
  3. Fold in the flaked cod, season with nutmeg and salt, spread on a cold tray, press cling film onto the surface and chill until it is firm enough to hold a piped ridge.
  4. Pipe the filling into each pepper through its open end, stopping just short of the rim so there is room to expand, and set them seam down in a wide pan.
  5. Blend the broken peppers with the cream and stock, pour around the parcels, and warm at a bare tremble for 6 to 8 minutes, until the filling is hot through and the sauce has reduced to the colour of terracotta. 8 min

How to tell it is going right

This dish has no standard of its own yet. It is judged on the techniques it exercises: checked at the pan, while there is still something to be done about it.

Knife cuts: dice, julienne, bias 115 recipes

  • Take any three pieces from anywhere in the pile: they match closely enough in every dimension that you would have to measure them to argue otherwise.
  • The cut faces are flat and clean rather than ragged or crushed; leaves and herbs are cut through, not bruised, and the board carries no dark smear where they were.
  • The stated size is the actual size, held against a rule or a known reference rather than called about right.
  • Everything in the pile is the shape that was asked for: no wedges, no half pieces, and no offcuts swept in to avoid waste.
  • They cook evenly, which is the entire point: at the end of cooking nothing is still raw and nothing has collapsed.

Where it goes wrong The knife was dull, or the vegetable was never squared off first, so the pieces vary: the small ones are mush by the time the large ones are cooked, and the dish has two textures where it wanted one.

The bare simmer: holding liquid below the boil 125 recipes

  • The surface is read rather than assumed: bubbles break slowly in one or two places, or the surface merely shivers, and it does not reach a rolling boil at any point.
  • It was held there: the pot was watched and the heat adjusted rather than set once and left, and there is no tide mark up the pot wall from a boil-over.
  • The liquid stayed as clear as it should be: no clouding from agitation, and no fat churned into an emulsion through it.
  • Whatever is in the liquid is still intact: nothing shredded, broken up, or worried apart by movement.
  • The level is roughly where the elapsed time should have left it; a large drop is the evidence that it ran hotter than this while nobody was looking.

Where it goes wrong It was brought to a boil and then left on the same heat, so it churned for the whole cook: the liquid clouded, the fat emulsified into it, and the solids broke apart into the broth.

From the pass

Piquillos from Lodosa are roasted over wood and peeled dry, never washed, so their smoke sits on the surface; rinsing them throws away the thing you paid for. The filling has to be a stiff bechamel, cooked until it leaves the pan in one mass and then chilled hard, because starch pastes loosen as they warm. Pipe a soft one and the pepper splits along its seam and empties into the sauce.
