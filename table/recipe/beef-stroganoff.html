Beef Stroganoff

  • Intermediate
  • 40 min
  • $$$ roughly 3 of 4 for cost

rich · umami-deep Leads with richness that coats and comforts, savory depth stacked from the pantry’s bass notes.

Ingredients

  • 500g beef fillet or sirloin, finger strips
  • 300g mushrooms, thick slices
  • 1 onion; 1 tbsp flour; 1 tsp mustard
  • 300ml beef stock, 150ml sour cream (smetana)
  • Butter; fried shoestring potatoes or buttered noodles

Method

  1. Sear beef in butter fast and hard, in batches, pink inside; remove.
  2. Brown mushrooms properly; add onion; dust with flour.
  3. Stock and mustard; reduce to a light gravy.
  4. Sour cream in, beef back, one minute, done. Never boil, never overcook the fillet.

How to tell it is going right

This dish has no standard of its own yet. It is judged on the techniques it exercises: checked at the pan, while there is still something to be done about it.

Searing: the hard crust 141 recipes

  • The pan keeps a hard, steady sizzle from the moment the food lands and does not fall quiet; silence within the first half minute means the pan lost its heat and the surface is now steaming rather than browning.
  • It releases itself when tested: slide a thin edge under and it lifts without tearing. Anything that grips the pan has not formed a crust yet and is not ready to be moved.
  • Deep even brown across the whole face, edge to edge, when it is turned under the light: no pale ring left around a browned centre, and no black patches that taste bitter on their own.
  • The pan floor carries a dry browned layer that dissolves and lifts the moment liquid hits it: not a wet grey film, and not black flecks that stay put and stay bitter.
  • Cut into it: the browned band is thin, a few millimetres at most, sitting straight on top of correctly cooked interior; a thick grey collar under the crust means the heat was too low for too long.

Where it goes wrong The pan was not hot enough, or too much went in at once, so the food released its water and sat in it: the surface goes grey and steams, it sticks when moved, and the crust that eventually forms arrives over an interior already cooked through.

The bare simmer: holding liquid below the boil 183 recipes

  • The surface is read rather than assumed: bubbles break slowly in one or two places, or the surface merely shivers, and it does not reach a rolling boil at any point.
  • It was held there: the pot was watched and the heat adjusted rather than set once and left, and there is no tide mark up the pot wall from a boil-over.
  • The liquid stayed as clear as it should be: no clouding from agitation, and no fat churned into an emulsion through it.
  • Whatever is in the liquid is still intact: nothing shredded, broken up, or worried apart by movement.
  • The level is roughly where the elapsed time should have left it; a large drop is the evidence that it ran hotter than this while nobody was looking.

Where it goes wrong It was brought to a boil and then left on the same heat, so it churned for the whole cook: the liquid clouded, the fat emulsified into it, and the solids broke apart into the broth.

From the pass

A 19th-century Russian count's dish: the beef is barely cooked twice, which means barely cooked once each time: seared in ripping heat, removed, and returned only to warm through in the sauce; gray stroganoff beef is the most common tragedy in home kitchens. The mustard in the sauce is the old Petersburg signature, there in the earliest printed recipe. Sour cream off the boil, always. Egg noodles or shoestring potatoes underneath, depending which side of the emigration you learned it from.
