Idaho Basque Croquetas

  • Advanced
  • 3 h
  • $$ roughly 2 of 4 for cost

rich · warm-spiced · earthy Leads with richness that coats and comforts, warm spice humming through the middle, settling low and earthy.

Ingredients

  • 85g butter
  • 100g flour
  • 600ml whole milk, warmed
  • 150g jamon or cured ham, minced fine
  • 1/4 tsp nutmeg
  • Salt and white pepper
  • For coating: 100g flour, 2 eggs beaten, 200g fine breadcrumbs
  • Oil for frying

Equipment

Sheet pan · Deep-fry pot

Method

  1. Melt the butter, stir in the flour, and cook 3 min without coloring. 3 min
  2. Add the warm milk slowly, whisking hard, and cook 10-15 min until the bechamel is very thick and pulls from the pan. 15 min
  3. Stir in the minced ham, nutmeg, salt, and pepper. Spread in a tray, cover, and chill overnight until firm.
  4. Shape into small cylinders, then coat in flour, egg, and breadcrumbs.
  5. Fry at 190C for 2 min until deep gold. They should be crisp outside and molten within. 2 min

How to tell it is going right

This dish has no standard of its own yet. It is judged on the techniques it exercises: checked at the pan, while there is still something to be done about it.

The coat before the fry: dredge, crumb, batter 80 recipes

  • The coat is continuous before it goes anywhere near the fat: held up to the light, no bare patch of food shows through anywhere, seams and thin edges included.
  • It is bonded rather than resting on top: a gentle shake sheds loose dust only, and the coat does not slide off as a sheet when the piece is handled.
  • It bubbles hard and steadily from the moment it enters the fat, and the bubbling visibly slows as it finishes; those bubbles are the surface water leaving, and a quiet entry means the fat is too cool.
  • Out and drained, it is an even gold-brown all over and it is still crisp several minutes later on a rack; a coat that softens within a minute of coming out never lost its water.
  • Broken open, there is no paste of raw flour under the crust and no hollow gap where the coat has lifted away from the food.

Where it goes wrong The fat was below temperature or the pan was crowded, so the coat drank fat instead of sealing against it: it comes out pale and heavy, greasy to the fingers, and slumps off the food as it cools.

Shaping by hand 85 recipes

  • The pieces match: weighed, or judged against each other rather than by dividing the mass by eye, so that no piece is half again the size of its neighbour.
  • Surfaces are smooth and unbroken with no cracks, tears, or dry patches, and the seams are sealed and sitting underneath.
  • The shape is defined and it holds while the tray waits: it has not slumped, spread, or relaxed back out of its form.
  • Nothing is trapped that should not be: no air pocket where filling should be, no filling breaking through the surface, and no excess flour greying the outside.
  • They finish together in the heat: no piece done while another is still raw, which is the only test that the first mark was met.

Where it goes wrong The dough was worked past its point or shaped without a rest, so it fought back: the pieces spring out of shape on the tray and bake tight, uneven, and torn along the surface.

From the pass

Boise holds one of the largest Basque communities outside Spain, and its restaurants keep croquetas on every menu. The overnight chill is not optional: a warm bechamel will burst in the fryer and leave you with breadcrumbs in oil.
