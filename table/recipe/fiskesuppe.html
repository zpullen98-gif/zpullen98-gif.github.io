Scandinavian · Soup

Fiskesuppe

  • Intermediate
  • 50 min
  • $$$ roughly 3 of 4 for cost

rich · bright · herb-fresh · earthy Leads with richness that coats and comforts, acid holding everything up to the light, settling low and earthy.

Ingredients

  • 400g mixed fish: salmon + cod, chunked; 150g shrimp
  • 1 leek, 1 carrot, ½ celeriac, cut in fine batons
  • 1L good fish stock; 200ml cream + 100ml milk
  • 2 tbsp butter + 2 tbsp flour; white wine splash
  • The Bergen signature: 1–2 tsp white vinegar + 1 tsp sugar; dill and chives

Method

  1. Sweat vegetables in butter, keeping their color; dust with flour.
  2. Stock gradually; simmer 10 min; wine, cream, milk. 10 min
  3. Season with the sweet-sour: vinegar and sugar until the soup brightens: it should taste faintly, elegantly tangy.
  4. Fish in at a bare shiver, 3–4 min; shrimp last minute; herbs; serve with crusty bread. 4 min

How to tell it is going right

This dish has no standard of its own yet. It is judged on the techniques it exercises: checked at the pan, while there is still something to be done about it.

Knife cuts: dice, julienne, bias 150 recipes

  • Take any three pieces from anywhere in the pile: they match closely enough in every dimension that you would have to measure them to argue otherwise.
  • The cut faces are flat and clean rather than ragged or crushed; leaves and herbs are cut through, not bruised, and the board carries no dark smear where they were.
  • The stated size is the actual size, held against a rule or a known reference rather than called about right.
  • Everything in the pile is the shape that was asked for: no wedges, no half pieces, and no offcuts swept in to avoid waste.
  • They cook evenly, which is the entire point: at the end of cooking nothing is still raw and nothing has collapsed.

Where it goes wrong The knife was dull, or the vegetable was never squared off first, so the pieces vary: the small ones are mush by the time the large ones are cooked, and the dish has two textures where it wanted one.

The bare simmer: holding liquid below the boil 183 recipes

  • The surface is read rather than assumed: bubbles break slowly in one or two places, or the surface merely shivers, and it does not reach a rolling boil at any point.
  • It was held there: the pot was watched and the heat adjusted rather than set once and left, and there is no tide mark up the pot wall from a boil-over.
  • The liquid stayed as clear as it should be: no clouding from agitation, and no fat churned into an emulsion through it.
  • Whatever is in the liquid is still intact: nothing shredded, broken up, or worried apart by movement.
  • The level is roughly where the elapsed time should have left it; a large drop is the evidence that it ran hotter than this while nobody was looking.

Where it goes wrong It was brought to a boil and then left on the same heat, so it churned for the whole cook: the liquid clouded, the fat emulsified into it, and the solids broke apart into the broth.

From the pass

Bergen fish soup's identity is the sweet-and-sour finish, a Norwegian trick that lifts cream and fish out of heaviness. The fish poaches in the residual heat; if the soup boils after the fish goes in, Bergen disowns you.
