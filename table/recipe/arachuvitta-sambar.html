Arachuvitta Sambar

  • Intermediate
  • 45 min
  • $$ roughly 2 of 4 for cost
  • Vegetarian
  • Vegan

bright · gently hot · sweet-edged · earthy Leads with acid holding everything up to the light, a warm current of chile, settling low and earthy.

Ingredients

  • 200 g toor dal (thuvaram paruppu)
  • 1 tsp turmeric
  • 30 g tamarind, soaked in 300 ml hot water
  • 150 g small onions (chinna vengayam), peeled whole
  • 2 drumsticks (murungakkai), cut in 6 cm lengths
  • 150 g brinjal and ash gourd, in large chunks
  • 20 g coriander seeds
  • 15 g chana dal
  • 6 dried red chillies
  • 5 black peppercorns
  • 1/4 tsp fenugreek seeds
  • 40 g fresh grated coconut
  • 1/2 tsp asafoetida
  • 5 g jaggery
  • 35 ml gingelly oil
  • 1 tsp mustard seeds
  • 2 sprigs curry leaves
  • Salt

Equipment

Grater / zester

Method

  1. Cook the toor dal with the turmeric until it collapses under the back of a spoon into a smooth puree, then whisk it loose with a little hot water.
  2. In 2 tsp of the gingelly oil, roast the coriander seeds and chana dal until deep gold, then the chillies, pepper and fenugreek for 30 seconds; kill the heat and stir the coconut through the residual warmth until it is fawn and smells toasted. Grind to a fine paste.
  3. Simmer the small onions, drumstick and vegetables in 300 ml plain salted water until the drumstick gives to a fingernail, about 10 minutes. 10 min
  4. Pour in the strained tamarind extract with the jaggery and boil hard for 5 minutes, until the raw metallic smell has gone and the liquid smells rounded. 5 min
  5. Stir in the dal and the ground paste and hold at a bare tremble for 5 minutes only, until the surface turns glossy and the sambar coats a spoon. 5 min
  6. Heat the remaining oil until it shimmers, crackle the mustard until it pops and greys, throw in the curry leaves and asafoetida for three seconds until the leaves go translucent, and pour it over.

How to tell it is going right

This dish has no standard of its own yet. It is judged on the techniques it exercises: checked at the pan, while there is still something to be done about it.

Tadka: blooming spices in fat 22 recipes

  • The fat is at tempering heat before anything goes in, around 180C (350F): one test seed dropped in surfaces and cracks within two or three seconds. A seed that sinks and sits quiet means the fat is cold and everything will steep rather than bloom; fat already hazing and thinning has gone past 200C (390F), and the first thing in will be black before the last thing is in.
  • Ask what went in and in what order, then read it back off the pan: the hardest seeds lead, with ten to twenty seconds before the next addition, and the softest seeds, any leaf and any powdered aromatic go last with only seconds left. Everything tipped in on one drop is a fail even when the pan smells right, because the most delicate thing in it cannot survive the time the hardest one needs.
  • The pan gave a hard scatter of popping that rose, peaked and thinned before it left the heat. A pan that stayed silent never reached popping heat and the seeds are still hard and raw against the teeth; seeds still cracking loudly as it is poured finished blooming in the dish instead of in the fat, where nothing was watching them.
  • Cumin is exactly one shade darker than it went in and no more, a warm even brown that smells nutty and sweet; anything near coffee colour, or smelling of scorch, has already carried bitterness into the fat. Fenugreek is pale gold, checked under the light with nothing copper or brown among it, and this is the mark to judge first: past gold it turns the whole pot bitter, and no salt, sugar or souring will pull it back.
  • The leaf is blistered and gone translucent, still green at the edge and crisp rather than black or limp, and it cracked sharply when it met the fat. It reached the dish still at full heat and answered with a violent hiss, and it reads on the surface afterwards as bright separate fat with whole spices suspended through it, not a dull dark sediment settled on the bottom.

Where it goes wrong The fat was pushed past hazing before anything went in, or every spice was tipped in together, so the hardest and the most delicate cooked on one clock: in the seconds the hardest seeds need to pop, fenugreek goes from gold to brown and the leaf goes black. Nothing can be corrected after that. The tempering pours in dark and smells scorched over the top of the dish, the specks read black rather than brown when the surface is turned to the light, and a flat acrid bitterness arrives at the back of the palate after the swallow and stays there through the next mouthful.

Roasting in a hot oven 56 recipes

  • The oven was fully up to heat before anything went in, given time beyond the indicator light rather than judged by it.
  • Every piece has air around it. Nothing touches its neighbour and nothing is packed to the edge of the tray, because crowded food steams itself in its own moisture and never browns.
  • Colour is even across the whole tray, the tray having been turned at least once: no pale row at the back and no dark row at the front.
  • It came out BEFORE the target temperature, not at it. Carryover keeps driving the inside up by several degrees after it leaves the oven, and that rise is part of the cooking rather than an error in it.
  • The temperature was read at the coldest part rather than the easiest one to reach, and read again after the rest.

Where it goes wrong The tray was crowded, or the oven was not truly up to heat. The food releases steam faster than a cool box can carry it away, so it sits pale and slack, and the extra minutes spent chasing colour take the inside well past where it should have stopped.

From the pass

Two rules the dal cannot save you from. Tamarind's acid keeps the vegetables' pectin cross-linked, so anything simmered in the sour liquid from the start stays hard: soften the drumstick and the small onions in plain water first, then pour the tamarind over. And the ground coconut masala is aroma, not structure. Give it five minutes at a tremble; boiled hard, the coconut fat breaks out grainy and the roasted coriander goes flat.
