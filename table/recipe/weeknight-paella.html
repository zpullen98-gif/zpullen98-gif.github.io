Weeknight Paella

  • Intermediate
  • 50 min
  • Serves 4
  • $$$$

floral · smoky · sweet-edged · aromatic Leads with a floral thread lifting the aroma, wood-smoke running underneath, opening aromatic and staying there.

Ingredients

  • 320g bomba or calasparra rice (arborio in a pinch)
  • 1L hot chicken stock + saffron
  • Chicken thighs, chorizo optional (heresy but delicious), shrimp
  • Sofrito: onion, garlic, grated tomato, 1 tsp pimentón
  • Widest shallowest pan you own

Equipment

Grater / zester

The skills inside

Allergen screen

Found: shellfish

Screened for: gluten, dairy, egg, nuts, peanuts, fish, shellfish, molluscs, soy, sesame, celery, mustard, lupin, alcohol. Not screened: sulphites.

Method

  1. Brown chicken; make the sofrito in the same pan until jammy.
  2. Add rice; toast 1 min. Add all the hot saffron stock. NOW STOP STIRRING. 1 min
  3. Simmer 18 min, rotating the pan; add shrimp for the last 5. 18 min
  4. Crank the heat 90 seconds for socarrat, the crackling bottom crust. Rest 5 min under a towel. 5 min

How to tell it is going right

This dish has no standard of its own yet. It is judged on the technique it exercises: checked at the pan, while there is still something to be done about it.

Frying the paste until the oil splits 38 recipes

  • It is being fried, not simmered: the paste is in fat over live heat and moving, holding a steady sizzle throughout rather than bubbling as a wet mass.
  • The colour has deepened and darkened from raw to cooked, and the volume has visibly reduced and tightened.
  • The split, the signal the whole technique is named for, is unmistakable when it comes: clear fat separates out and pools at the edges and on the surface, and the paste pulls away from the pan floor in one mass.
  • The smell has turned from sharp and raw to round and fragrant, with no raw bite left from any of the aromatics that went into it.
  • Nothing has caught: no black specks through the paste, no bitterness, and a pan floor that is browned but not burnt.

Where it goes wrong It was taken off as soon as it smelled good, before the fat separated; the raw edge of the aromatics is still in it, and the finished dish tastes thin and sharp no matter how long it simmers afterwards.

From the pass

Stirring makes risotto; not stirring makes paella: the socarrat, that crackling caramelized bottom crust, only forms on rice left alone against hot metal, and it is the reward for restraint. Bomba rice matters because it drinks three times its volume without bursting. The chorizo is heresy by Valencian law and delicious by every other jurisdiction; commit either way. Rotate the pan for even heat, listen for the faint crackle in the last ninety seconds, and rest it under a towel before serving.
