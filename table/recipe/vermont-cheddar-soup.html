Vermont Cheddar Soup

  • Easy
  • 45 min
  • $$ roughly 2 of 4 for cost
  • Vegetarian

rich · herb-fresh Leads with richness that coats and comforts, fresh herbs arguing happily on top.

Ingredients

  • 55g butter
  • 1 onion, diced fine
  • 2 carrots, diced fine
  • 2 celery ribs, diced fine
  • 55g flour
  • 750ml vegetable stock
  • 500ml whole milk
  • 400g sharp Vermont cheddar, grated
  • 1 tsp dry mustard
  • Dash of hot sauce
  • Salt and white pepper
  • Chives to finish

Equipment

Grater / zester

Method

  1. Sweat the vegetables in butter until soft without color, 10 min. 10 min
  2. Stir in the flour and cook 2 min to a blond roux. 2 min
  3. Add the stock slowly, whisking, then the milk. Simmer 15 min until smooth and lightly thickened. 15 min
  4. Off the heat, add the cheese in handfuls, stirring until each melts before adding more. Never let it boil after the cheese goes in.
  5. Season with mustard, hot sauce, salt, and white pepper. Serve with chives.

How to tell it is going right

This dish has no standard of its own yet. It is judged on the techniques it exercises: checked at the pan, while there is still something to be done about it.

Making a roux 32 recipes

  • Taken to the stage the dish named and no further, judged against that colour (pale, blond, or brown to brick), and it got there slowly enough to have coloured evenly.
  • It is smooth and mobile: a paste with no dry pockets of flour and no lumps, loosening as it warms rather than sitting stiff in the pan.
  • The raw-flour smell has gone and been replaced by the toasted, biscuity smell of its stage, with nothing acrid behind it.
  • No black specks anywhere. One scorched fleck condemns the batch; it cannot be stirred back in and it will be tasted in the finished sauce.
  • Let down into liquid it goes smooth without lumps and thickens to the body the dish wants, leaving no floury taste on the tongue.

Where it goes wrong The heat was too high and the corners of the pan went unstirred, so it scorched at the edge; black specks run through the finished sauce and carry a bitterness that no amount of seasoning covers.

  • The cut faces are flat and clean rather than ragged or crushed; leaves and herbs are cut through, not bruised, and the board carries no dark smear where they were.
  • The stated size is the actual size, held against a rule or a known reference rather than called about right.
  • Everything in the pile is the shape that was asked for: no wedges, no half pieces, and no offcuts swept in to avoid waste.
  • They cook evenly, which is the entire point: at the end of cooking nothing is still raw and nothing has collapsed.

Where it goes wrong The knife was dull, or the vegetable was never squared off first, so the pieces vary: the small ones are mush by the time the large ones are cooked, and the dish has two textures where it wanted one.

From the pass

Cheese added to a boiling pot goes stringy and greasy: the pan comes off the heat first, always. Grate the cheddar yourself; the anti-caking dust on pre-grated cheese makes the soup gritty.
