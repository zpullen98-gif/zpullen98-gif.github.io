Moong Dal Khichdi

  • Easy
  • 15 min
  • Serves 4
  • $$
  • Vegetarian

rich · earthy · aromatic · fiery Leads with richness that coats and comforts, an earthy, grounding base, on a slow, honest burn.

Ingredients

  • 150 g split skinned moong dal
  • 150 g rice
  • 1.2 L water
  • 40 g ghee
  • 5 g cumin seeds
  • 1 g asafoetida
  • 15 g ginger, grated
  • 1 green chilli, slit
  • 3 g ground turmeric
  • 8 g salt

Equipment

Grater / zester

The skills inside

Roasting in a hot oven

Allergen screen

Found: dairy

Screened for: gluten, dairy, egg, nuts, peanuts, fish, shellfish, molluscs, soy, sesame, celery, mustard, lupin, alcohol. Not screened: sulphites.

Method

  1. Dry roast the moong dal in a pan over medium heat, shaking it, for 3 to 4 minutes until it smells nutty and a few grains freckle brown, then rinse it with the rice until the water runs clear. 4 min
  2. Heat half the ghee in a heavy pot and crackle the cumin until the seeds darken a shade and stop spitting, then add the asafoetida, ginger and chilli and stir for 30 seconds only, since asafoetida scorches fast.
  3. Add the rice, dal, turmeric, salt and water, bring to a boil and simmer covered for 25 minutes, or pressure cook to 3 whistles, until the grains have lost their edges and the mass drops off a spoon in a soft ribbon. 25 min
  4. Beat in the rest of the ghee off the heat and loosen with boiling water until it pours slowly, then serve straight away.

How to tell it is going right

This dish has no standard of its own yet. It is judged on the technique it exercises: checked at the pan, while there is still something to be done about it.

Roasting in a hot oven 42 recipes

  • The oven was fully up to heat before anything went in, given time beyond the indicator light rather than judged by it.
  • Every piece has air around it. Nothing touches its neighbour and nothing is packed to the edge of the tray, because crowded food steams itself in its own moisture and never browns.
  • Colour is even across the whole tray, the tray having been turned at least once: no pale row at the back and no dark row at the front.
  • It came out BEFORE the target temperature, not at it. Carryover keeps driving the inside up by several degrees after it leaves the oven, and that rise is part of the cooking rather than an error in it.
  • The temperature was read at the coldest part rather than the easiest one to reach, and read again after the rest.

Where it goes wrong The tray was crowded, or the oven was not truly up to heat. The food releases steam faster than a cool box can carry it away, so it sits pale and slack, and the extra minutes spent chasing colour take the inside well past where it should have stopped.

From the pass

Khichdi is the opposite of pilaf: at 1:4 grain to water the rice goes past absorption into full gelatinisation, and collapse is the point, so wash it but do not chase separate grains. Roasting the dal dry first sets its surface protein and stops it dissolving into slime. Add the second half of the ghee off the heat, where it stays aromatic instead of cooking flat, and serve it looser than looks right; it tightens within minutes of leaving the pot.
