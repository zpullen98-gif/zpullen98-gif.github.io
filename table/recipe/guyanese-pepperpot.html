Guyanese Pepperpot

  • Intermediate
  • 35 min
  • $$$ roughly 3 of 4 for cost

warm-spiced · fiery · sweet-edged Leads with warm spice humming through the middle, real fire that builds and stays, trailing gentle sweetness.

Ingredients

  • 1.2 kg beef shin, in 5 cm pieces
  • 600 g oxtail, cut through the joints
  • 400 g pork trotter, split
  • 1.5 L water
  • 250 ml cassareep
  • 60 g soft brown sugar
  • 1 cinnamon stick
  • 6 cloves
  • 1 strip of orange peel
  • 10 g thyme
  • 15 g fine salt
  • 2 wiri wiri peppers, or 1 scotch bonnet, left whole and unpierced

Method

  1. Cover the meats with the water, bring them to the boil and skim off the grey foam until the surface runs clear.
  2. Stir in the cassareep, sugar, cinnamon, cloves, orange peel, thyme and salt, and lay the peppers on top unbroken.
  3. Hold it at a bare simmer for 3 hours, until the trotter is sticky, the oxtail slides off its bone and the liquid has gone black and glossy. 180 min
  4. Lift the peppers out before they burst, then taste: cassareep should read bitter, sweet and smoky at once, and it takes more salt than you expect.
  5. Cool it quickly and keep it in the fridge no longer than 3 days, bringing it back to a rolling boil every time you reheat it. It is better on the second day and better again on the third.

How to tell it is going right

This dish has no standard of its own yet. It is judged on the techniques it exercises: checked at the pan, while there is still something to be done about it.

The bare simmer: holding liquid below the boil 183 recipes

  • The surface is read rather than assumed: bubbles break slowly in one or two places, or the surface merely shivers, and it does not reach a rolling boil at any point.
  • It was held there: the pot was watched and the heat adjusted rather than set once and left, and there is no tide mark up the pot wall from a boil-over.
  • The liquid stayed as clear as it should be: no clouding from agitation, and no fat churned into an emulsion through it.
  • Whatever is in the liquid is still intact: nothing shredded, broken up, or worried apart by movement.
  • The level is roughly where the elapsed time should have left it; a large drop is the evidence that it ran hotter than this while nobody was looking.

Where it goes wrong It was brought to a boil and then left on the same heat, so it churned for the whole cook: the liquid clouded, the fat emulsified into it, and the solids broke apart into the broth.

Salted water & the float test 188 recipes

  • The water was salted before anything went in, and tasting it confirms it: seasoned on the tongue, with the salt dissolved rather than sitting on the pot floor.
  • There is enough water, hot enough, that the pot returns to its boil quickly after the food goes in and never stops moving.
  • Where the dish uses the float as its signal, the signal is obeyed: the pieces rise and they come out then, not several minutes later once something else was finished.
  • Nothing is stuck to the bottom of the pot or welded to itself, and the water has not thickened into a cloudy sludge.
  • Drained on a bite rather than on the clock: resistance at the centre, with no chalky raw core.

Where it goes wrong The pot was too small and the water unsalted, so it never recovered its boil: the food sat in slack water, went gummy at the surface, and comes out bland in a way that no sauce afterwards can fix.

From the pass

Cassareep is the juice of bitter cassava boiled down to a black syrup. The boiling drives off the cyanide that makes the raw juice poisonous and concentrates sugars and phenols that hold bacteria back, which is why a pepperpot traditionally lives on the stove for weeks and is brought back to the boil each morning. Buy it made rather than pressing your own, and at home use the fridge and that daily boil. The trotter and oxtail are in the pot for gelatine: it makes the sauce cling.
