Chicken Teriyaki

  • Easy
  • 25 min
  • Serves 4
  • $

sweet-edged · umami-deep Leads with a sweet edge rounding the corners, savory depth stacked from the pantry’s bass notes.

Ingredients

  • 4 chicken thighs, boneless, skin-on
  • 3 tbsp soy sauce
  • 3 tbsp mirin
  • 2 tbsp sake
  • 1 tbsp sugar

The skills inside

Resting meat & slicing against the grain

Allergen screen

Found: gluten · soy · alcohol

Screened for: gluten, dairy, egg, nuts, peanuts, fish, shellfish, molluscs, soy, sesame, celery, mustard, lupin, alcohol. Not screened: sulphites.

Method

  1. Crisp the thighs skin-down over medium, pressing, 8 min; flip 3 min. Drain fat. 8 min
  2. Add the mixed sauce; simmer and turn to glaze, 2–3 min, until syrupy. 3 min
  3. Rest, slice, spoon over the glaze; serve with rice.

How it should come out

  • The skin is one continuous lacquered sheet, mahogany and flat against the meat, with no shrunken lifted edges and no black patches where the sugar caught.
  • The glaze is thin enough to see the skin through and thick enough to hang: a drip off the spoon stretches before it falls, and no thin brown puddle spreads from under the thigh. On the tongue it is savoury and salty first with the sweetness behind, not candy.
  • The skin holds its own under the knife. It parts with the meat rather than sliding off as a soft sheet, and the glaze sits on it as a film instead of soaking into a spongy layer.
  • Sliced across after resting, the meat is uniformly opaque and the board takes a trace of clear juice, not a flood.
  • No fat slick anywhere: what sits under the chicken and around the slices is glaze, not oil, and it does not separate into a rim as the plate stands.

Where it goes wrong Soft skin under a sticky coat, because the sauce went into the pan before the fat had finished rendering and before the pan was drained. The glaze steams the crust it was meant to sit on, and the thigh arrives sweet, slippery and flabby with the skin loose from the meat.

From the pass

Real teriyaki is a pan glaze, not a marinade: teri means luster, and the luster comes from soy, mirin and sake reducing onto already-crisp skin in the final minutes. Marinated first, the sugars burn before the skin ever crisps, which is the take-out version's chewy fate. Render the thighs patiently, drain the fat so the glaze grips instead of sliding, and pull it while the syrup still flows; it tightens as it cools.
