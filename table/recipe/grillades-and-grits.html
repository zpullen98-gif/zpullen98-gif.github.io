Grillades and Grits

  • Intermediate
  • 2 h
  • $$$ roughly 3 of 4 for cost

charred · rich · aromatic · umami-deep Leads with char used as punctuation, richness that coats and comforts, finishing long and savory.

Ingredients

  • 800g beef or veal rounds, pounded thin, cut in palm-size squares
  • Seasoned flour for dredging; oil for browning + the roux after
  • The trinity: 1 onion, 1 bell pepper, 2 celery ribs, diced; 4 garlic cloves
  • 400g crushed tomatoes + 500ml beef stock; bay, thyme, Worcestershire, hot sauce
  • Grits: 200g stone-ground, cooked slow in milk-water with butter and a little cheese

Method

  1. Dredge and brown the meat hard in batches; set aside.
  2. Make a peanut-butter-colored roux in the drippings; trinity in until it sighs; garlic, tomatoes, stock.
  3. Meat back; simmer low and lidded 1–1.5 h until fork-tender and the gravy is deep russet. 300 min
  4. Start the grits at the hour mark: slow, stirred, enriched. Grillades over grits, gravy over everything, green onions over that.

How to tell it is going right

This dish has no standard of its own yet. It is judged on the techniques it exercises: checked at the pan, while there is still something to be done about it.

Making a roux 32 recipes

  • Taken to the stage the dish named and no further, judged against that colour (pale, blond, or brown to brick), and it got there slowly enough to have coloured evenly.
  • It is smooth and mobile: a paste with no dry pockets of flour and no lumps, loosening as it warms rather than sitting stiff in the pan.
  • The raw-flour smell has gone and been replaced by the toasted, biscuity smell of its stage, with nothing acrid behind it.
  • No black specks anywhere. One scorched fleck condemns the batch; it cannot be stirred back in and it will be tasted in the finished sauce.
  • Let down into liquid it goes smooth without lumps and thickens to the body the dish wants, leaving no floury taste on the tongue.

Where it goes wrong The heat was too high and the corners of the pan went unstirred, so it scorched at the edge; black specks run through the finished sauce and carry a bitterness that no amount of seasoning covers.

Rendering fat: and cooking in what runs out 73 recipes

  • It started cold or low and came up slowly: fat that met a hot pan seizes and scorches before the inside can melt, and there is no recovering it.
  • The fat in the pan is clear and clean-smelling, pale to golden, with no dark sediment on the floor and nothing acrid in the steam.
  • What is left behind is crisp and deep gold with no soft pale fat still clinging to it and no black burnt edges.
  • There is a real volume of it: enough to film the pan floor and be poured off and kept, not a dry smear that never ran.
  • Whatever cooks in it browns in it and takes its flavour, rather than sitting in a greasy pool that never gets hot enough to colour anything.

Where it goes wrong The heat was raised to hurry it along, so the outside seized and browned before the interior fat could melt; very little runs out, and the solids come out burnt on the outside and rubbery within.

From the pass

The grand dame of the New Orleans brunch table: debutante breakfasts, Christmas morning, and every white-tablecloth Jazz brunch runs on grillades. It is pot roast logic at breakfast tempo: thin meat, dark roux, trinity, time. The grits must be stone-ground and patient; instant grits under grillades is how you get quietly disinvited.
