Weeknight Chicken Biryani

  • Advanced
  • 1 h 30 m
  • $$$ roughly 3 of 4 for cost

herb-fresh · warm-spiced · floral · rich Leads with fresh herbs arguing happily on top, warm spice humming through the middle, on a rich, rounded finish.

Ingredients

  • 600g chicken thighs in yogurt + biryani masala + ginger-garlic
  • 400g basmati, soaked 30 min
  • 2 onions, fried to birista (crisp brown)
  • Saffron in warm milk
  • Mint, cilantro, ghee
  • Whole spices: bay, cardamom, cinnamon, clove

Method

  1. Parboil rice with whole spices to 70%; a grain should snap, not squash.
  2. Layer: marinated chicken on the bottom, rice, birista, herbs, saffron milk, ghee.
  3. Seal the lid (foil or dough); 10 min medium, then 25 min lowest heat (dum). 10 min
  4. Rest 10 min. Dig from the bottom up. 10 min

How to tell it is going right

This dish has no standard of its own yet. It is judged on the technique it exercises: checked at the pan, while there is still something to be done about it.

Marinating: acid, salt and time 70 recipes

  • Contact is total: every surface is wetted, and the pieces are not welded into a clump with dry faces where they pressed together.
  • The stated time was kept and kept cold, read against a clock, because this is the one technique where longer is a different result rather than a safer one.
  • It worked at the surface and no further: cut a piece and the marinade’s colour stops a few millimetres in. A protein gone opaque and chalky to the centre before any heat reached it has been cooked by the acid.
  • The texture is still springy and whole, not mushy, mealy, or fraying at the surface when it is lifted.
  • Where the next step browns it, the surface was patted dry first: a wet surface cannot brown, and the marinade’s sugars will scorch before the food colours.

Where it goes wrong It was left well beyond the stated time on the assumption that more is better, so acid and salt worked through the thin edges: those cook to cotton and read grainy while the thick centre tastes of nothing at all.

From the pass

Seventy-percent rice is the whole exam: a parboiled grain should snap between the fingers, not squash, because it finishes cooking in the dum steam above the chicken; fully cooked rice becomes porridge upstairs. The sealed lid is the oven: trap the steam and the pot becomes a pressure chamber where saffron, birista and marinade juices distill through the layers. Dig from the bottom up at the table, so every serving carries the full stratigraphy.
