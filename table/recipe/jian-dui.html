Dessert Atlas · Dessert

Jian Dui

  • Advanced
  • 1 h 30 m
  • Serves 4
  • $
  • Vegetarian
  • Vegan

nutty-toasted · fiery Leads with toasted, nutty warmth throughout, real fire that builds and stays.

Ingredients

  • 300g glutinous rice flour; 60g sugar dissolved in ~220ml just-boiled water (the hot-water dough is the stretch)
  • 250g sweet red bean paste (or lotus paste), chilled and rolled into 12 balls
  • 150g raw white sesame seeds in a bowl; a second bowl of water for wet-rolling
  • Oil for deep frying: starting LOW at 130°C and finishing at 160°C; the two-stage fry is the inflation mechanism
  • A slotted spoon and gentle pressure: you will be massaging them as they fry

Equipment

Deep-fry pot

The skills inside

Draining fried food: a rack, never paper

Allergen screen

Found: gluten · sesame

Screened for: gluten, dairy, egg, nuts, peanuts, fish, shellfish, molluscs, soy, sesame, celery, mustard, lupin, alcohol. Not screened: sulphites.

Method

  1. Make the hot-water dough; knead smooth; rest 10 min. Wrap each chilled paste ball in dough, sealing seamlessly: cracks become blowouts. 10 min
  2. Wet each ball's surface; roll through sesame and press the seeds home between your palms.
  3. Fry at 130°C, rolling constantly; as they firm, press each gently against the pot with the spoon; they rebound and BEGIN TO INFLATE; keep pressing and rolling as the shell expands hollow.
  4. Raise to 160°C for the final minutes until deep gold and audibly crisp. Drain upright; eat warm: crackling sesame shell, chewy wall, soft sweet heart.

How to tell it is going right

This dish has no standard of its own yet. It is judged on the technique it exercises: checked at the pan, while there is still something to be done about it.

Draining fried food: a rack, never paper 28 recipes

  • The rack was in place before the first thing went into the oil, not fetched afterwards while food waited in a spider over the pot.
  • It lands on a rack with air moving underneath it. Anything set down on paper or a flat plate traps its own steam against its base.
  • Salted within seconds of landing, while it is still hot enough to take seasoning.
  • A minute later the underside is still as crisp as the top: no soft flat patch where it has been sitting.
  • Nothing is stacked or piled while hot, however tempting it is to keep it together. A pile steams itself soft from the inside out.

Where it goes wrong It was drained on paper, or piled up to keep warm. Steam leaving the food has nowhere to go but back into the crust, so the underside softens within a minute: the crispness the frying just bought is gone before the plate reaches the table.

From the pass

The sesame ball's magic trick is the press-and-puff: trapped steam plus glutinous stretch means every gentle squeeze against the pot inflates the ball a little more, until a modest dumpling becomes a golden balloon; dim sum masters can blow them to softball size with a fist-sized heart of air. Tang-dynasty palace records mention them; Chinatown bakeries sell them at dawn; and the two-temperature fry is the entire difference between a dense sphere and a hollow marvel.
