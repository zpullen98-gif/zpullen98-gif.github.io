Porrusalda

  • Easy
  • 20 min
  • $$ roughly 2 of 4 for cost
  • Vegetarian
  • Vegan

rich · briny · aromatic · warm-spiced Leads with richness that coats and comforts, a briny, maritime salinity, fading in warm spice.

Ingredients

  • 800 g leeks, white and pale green only, in 2 cm rings
  • 600 g floury potatoes, peeled
  • 2 carrots, 150 g, in rounds
  • 60 ml olive oil
  • 2 garlic cloves, sliced
  • 1.2 litres water or a light vegetable broth
  • salt
  • extra virgin olive oil, to finish

Method

  1. Drop the leek rings into a bowl of cold water and swish, then lift them out with your hands so the grit stays behind. Drain them hard and shake dry; wet leeks steam instead of sweating.
  2. Sweat the leeks and garlic in the oil with a good pinch of salt over low heat for 15 minutes, lid ajar, until they slump and turn silky without taking any colour at all. 15 min
  3. Tear the potatoes with a knife tip so every face is ragged, add them with the carrot, cover with the water and simmer at a lazy blip for 25 minutes, until a potato collapses under the back of a spoon. 25 min
  4. Crush a third of the potato against the side of the pot and stir it through; the broth should thicken to the weight of single cream. Rest 10 minutes off the heat, season, and finish each bowl with raw oil. 10 min

How to tell it is going right

This dish has no standard of its own yet. It is judged on the techniques it exercises: checked at the pan, while there is still something to be done about it.

The bare simmer: holding liquid below the boil 183 recipes

  • The surface is read rather than assumed: bubbles break slowly in one or two places, or the surface merely shivers, and it does not reach a rolling boil at any point.
  • It was held there: the pot was watched and the heat adjusted rather than set once and left, and there is no tide mark up the pot wall from a boil-over.
  • The liquid stayed as clear as it should be: no clouding from agitation, and no fat churned into an emulsion through it.
  • Whatever is in the liquid is still intact: nothing shredded, broken up, or worried apart by movement.
  • The level is roughly where the elapsed time should have left it; a large drop is the evidence that it ran hotter than this while nobody was looking.

Where it goes wrong It was brought to a boil and then left on the same heat, so it churned for the whole cook: the liquid clouded, the fat emulsified into it, and the solids broke apart into the broth.

Sweating aromatics: soft, never browned 198 recipes

  • No colour anywhere: tipped under the light the pieces are translucent to pale gold, with no tan edges, no brown tips, and no browned flecks suspended in the fat.
  • The pan is quiet: a low steady hiss, not a crackle. A crackle means the moisture has gone and browning has already started.
  • The largest piece pressed against the side of the pan collapses without resistance and with no crunch left at its centre.
  • The fat is still clear and loose in the pan and the pan floor is clean: nothing caught, nothing browned on to deglaze, because there was never any fond to build.
  • It tastes sweet and mild with no raw allium bite at the back of the throat, and none of the roasted sweetness that belongs to the next technique up the heat scale.

Where it goes wrong The heat was too high and nothing covered the pan, so the pieces took colour before they softened; the finished dish carries a roasty sweetness where it wanted a clean one, and the pieces are still firm at the centre under browned edges.

From the pass

A soup of two ingredients has to be built out of texture. Tearing the potatoes gives ragged faces that shed starch into the water, and crushing a third of them at the end thickens the broth to the weight of single cream with no flour anywhere. The one real failure is colour: brown the leeks even slightly and the soup turns grey and faintly bitter. Sweat them salted and covered, and never let them sizzle.
