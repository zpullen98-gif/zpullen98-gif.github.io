Uzbek & the Silk Road · Sauce

Suzma

  • Intermediate
  • 20 min
  • Serves 4
  • $
  • Vegetarian

herb-fresh · aromatic · silky · rich Leads with fresh herbs arguing happily on top, aromatics blooming from the first bite, on a rich, rounded finish.

Ingredients

  • 1 litre whole milk
  • 100 g live natural yoghurt at room temperature
  • 6 g fine salt
  • 1 small garlic clove, crushed to a paste
  • 2 tbsp dill, finely chopped

Allergen screen

Found: dairy

Screened for: gluten, dairy, egg, nuts, peanuts, fish, shellfish, molluscs, soy, sesame, celery, mustard, lupin, alcohol. Not screened: sulphites.

Method

  1. Heat the milk to 85C (185F) and hold it there 10 minutes, stirring so it does not catch, then cool to 43C (110F), warm to a fingertip but not hot. 10 min
  2. Whisk in the yoghurt, cover and keep at blood heat 6 to 8 hours, until the surface is set firm enough to hold the mark of a spoon and smells cleanly sour. 480 min
  3. Stir in the salt, pour into a doubled muslin, tie it and hang in the fridge 8 to 12 hours, until the curd holds its shape like soft cheese and the whey has stopped dripping. 600 min
  4. Beat smooth with the garlic and dill and serve cold, spooned over manti, stirred into shurpa or eaten with non.

From the pass

Holding the milk at 85C denatures the whey proteins so they knit into the casein net; skip that step and the curd is weak and drains down to a grainy, chalky paste. Salt before hanging rather than after, because salt pulls whey out faster and firms what is left behind. Hang it somewhere cold: draining warm lets the culture keep working and the suzma turns bitter.
