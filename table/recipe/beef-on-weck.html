Beef on Weck

  • Intermediate
  • 3 h
  • $$ roughly 2 of 4 for cost

clean · simple A quiet dish that lets its main ingredient speak: season well and stand aside.

Ingredients

  • 2kg top round roast
  • 2 tbsp kosher salt
  • 1 tbsp black pepper
  • 500ml beef stock
  • For the kummelweck rolls: 6 kaiser rolls, 1 egg white, 2 tbsp pretzel salt, 2 tbsp caraway seeds
  • Fresh horseradish, grated

Equipment

Thermometer · Grater / zester

Method

  1. Season the roast hard, rest overnight, then roast at 230C for 15 min, drop to 135C, and cook to 52C internal for rare. Rest 30 min. 15 min
  2. Brush the rolls with egg white and press on the salt and caraway; bake 5 min at 200C to set the crust. 5 min
  3. Slice the beef as thin as you can, against the grain.
  4. Warm the stock, dip the top half of each roll in the jus briefly, and pile in the beef.
  5. Serve with grated horseradish and extra jus for dipping.

How to tell it is going right

This dish has no standard of its own yet. It is judged on the techniques it exercises: checked at the pan, while there is still something to be done about it.

The overnight rest: letting a dish marry 29 recipes

  • Cooled quickly before it went in (spread out, decanted, or stood in cold water) rather than carried to the fridge hot in the pot it was cooked in.
  • Protected from the rest of the fridge once cold: covered, or given space where nothing strong is sitting beside it. A rest this long is long enough to take on whatever it shares the shelf with, and some dishes want the open air.
  • Given the whole time. This is the one technique whose entire content is waiting, and cutting it short leaves the dish exactly as it was when it went in.
  • Brought back deliberately: reheated gently, or brought up to the temperature it is meant to be eaten at, rather than served straight out of the cold because it is late.
  • Tasted and corrected AFTER the rest. Seasoning and brightness both flatten in the cold, so the dish is finished at the end of the wait rather than at the start of it.

Where it goes wrong It went into the fridge hot and covered. It held its own heat for hours in the range where food spoils, and the lid sweated and dripped back into it all night: what comes out is duller and wetter than what went in, which is the exact opposite of the point.

Roasting in a hot oven 56 recipes

  • The oven was fully up to heat before anything went in, given time beyond the indicator light rather than judged by it.
  • Every piece has air around it. Nothing touches its neighbour and nothing is packed to the edge of the tray, because crowded food steams itself in its own moisture and never browns.
  • Colour is even across the whole tray, the tray having been turned at least once: no pale row at the back and no dark row at the front.
  • It came out BEFORE the target temperature, not at it. Carryover keeps driving the inside up by several degrees after it leaves the oven, and that rise is part of the cooking rather than an error in it.
  • The temperature was read at the coldest part rather than the easiest one to reach, and read again after the rest.

Where it goes wrong The tray was crowded, or the oven was not truly up to heat. The food releases steam faster than a cool box can carry it away, so it sits pale and slack, and the extra minutes spent chasing colour take the inside well past where it should have stopped.

From the pass

Western New York's own sandwich, and Buffalo's quieter masterpiece beside the wing. The kummelweck salt is the seasoning for the meat, coarse salt and caraway crusted on the roll, so do not salt the beef again at the table, and keep the horseradish fierce; the sandwich is calibrated around sinus-clearing doses. The top bun gets dipped in jus, the beef is sliced rare and thin, and the whole thing exists in a fifty-mile radius that considers exporting it unnecessary.
