Ragù alla Bolognese

  • Intermediate
  • 3 h
  • $$$ roughly 3 of 4 for cost

umami-deep · bright Leads with savory depth stacked from the pantry’s bass notes, acid holding everything up to the light.

Ingredients

  • 300g ground beef + 200g ground pork
  • 100g pancetta, minced
  • 1 onion, 1 carrot, 1 celery rib, fine dice
  • 2 tbsp tomato paste
  • 150ml white wine
  • 300ml whole milk
  • 400ml stock
  • Tagliatelle, Parmigiano

Method

  1. Render pancetta; sweat the soffritto 10 min until soft, not browned. 10 min
  2. Brown the meats hard, breaking them up; stir in tomato paste and cook 2 min. 2 min
  3. Deglaze with wine, add milk and stock; simmer partly covered 2–3 hours, topping up with stock. 180 min
  4. Toss with tagliatelle and a splash of pasta water; finish with Parmigiano.

How it should come out

  • The finished colour is a dull brick-brown, not red. Still red at three hours means the paste was never cooked out or the pot never went long enough.
  • Fat has risen to a clear orange film across the top and stirs back in without separating again. Tilt the pan: solids and liquid move together, with no water running ahead.
  • A grain of meat crushes to nothing between finger and thumb. Springy pellets mean the simmer was a boil.
  • No white flecks or curdled grain anywhere. The milk has gone invisible into the sauce, which is the hard mark to hit when the wine went in ahead of it.
  • Tossed with tagliatelle and a splash of pasta water, the sauce clings the length of each ribbon and the bowl bottom stays clean.

Where it goes wrong A bitter, ashy line under everything, because the pot was left to catch on the bottom between top-ups and the catch got stirred back in. The ragù still looks right, only darker than it should be, so the mistake is usually found on the tongue rather than in the pan.

From the pass

Milk isn't optional: its proteins buffer the wine's acidity and keep three-hours-cooked meat tender instead of grainy, which is why the recipe the Accademia Italiana della Cucina deposited at Bologna's Chamber of Commerce in 1982 includes it without apology. The failure mode is impatience: a hard boil squeezes the meat dry, while the barest tremble of a simmer dissolves it into the sauce. And in Bologna it goes on tagliatelle: the ragù clings to a rough egg noodle in a way it never will to slick spaghetti.
