Baltic · Dessert

Sklandrausis

  • Intermediate
  • 45 min
  • $$ roughly 2 of 4 for cost
  • Vegetarian

rich · earthy · sweet-edged · silky Leads with richness that coats and comforts, an earthy, grounding base, melting away silken.

Ingredients

  • 250 g wholegrain rye flour
  • 60 g butter, melted
  • 120 ml very hot water
  • 4 g salt
  • 300 g floury potatoes, boiled and riced
  • 60 ml sour cream
  • 1 egg yolk
  • 1 tsp caraway seeds
  • 300 g carrots, boiled soft and pureed
  • 40 g honey, plus a little to glaze
  • 1 egg
  • 60 ml double cream, plus 2 tbsp to glaze

Method

  1. Stir the melted butter into the hot water, pour it onto the rye flour with the salt and work fast to a warm, plastic dough; cover and rest it 30 minutes while it is still warm. 30 min
  2. Pinch off eight pieces, press each into a disc on the bench and draw a 2 cm wall up from the rim with your fingers to make a shallow open case, then prick the bases.
  3. Beat the riced potato with sour cream, yolk, caraway and salt to a stiff paste and spread it 1 cm deep in each case.
  4. Beat the carrot puree with honey, whole egg and cream until smooth and just pourable, then spoon it over the potato to sit just below the rim.
  5. Bake at 200C (390F) for 25 to 30 minutes, until the carrot has set with no wobble left and the rye wall is dry and dark at the edge. 30 min
  6. Brush the hot tops with the cream loosened with honey and eat them warm or cold, the same day.

How to tell it is going right

This dish has no standard of its own yet. It is judged on the techniques it exercises: checked at the pan, while there is still something to be done about it.

Resting dough: the pause that does the work 71 recipes

  • It actually rested, covered, for the time stated, not cut short because the rest of the schedule was tight.
  • It rolls and shapes without fighting: it stays where it is put, and it does not spring back or shrink on the bench between passes.
  • The surface is smooth and supple rather than dry, cracked, or skinned over.
  • It is at the temperature the next step needs: cold where the fat must stay solid, cool and relaxed where it must be rolled thin.
  • Baked, it holds the dimensions it was shaped to rather than shrinking back from the edges of the tin or the rim of the dish.

Where it goes wrong The rest was skipped or shortened, so the gluten was still tight: the dough fought the pin and sprang back thick and uneven, then shrank down the sides of the tin in the oven.

Where it goes wrong A starchy ingredient met a high-speed blade, so the cells burst and released their starch: what should be light comes out as an elastic glue that lifts off the spoon in one mass.

From the pass

Rye builds no gluten net, so the case is shaped by starch rather than stretch: near boiling water gelatinises enough of it to make the dough plastic under your fingers, and melted butter keeps it short. Mix rye with cold water and the wall cracks as you pinch it up, then leaks carrot custard across the tray. Work the dough warm and shape it the moment it has rested.
