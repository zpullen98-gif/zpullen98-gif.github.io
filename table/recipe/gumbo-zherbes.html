New Orleans · Soup

Gumbo Z'herbes

  • Advanced
  • 3 h
  • Serves 4
  • $$

smoky · aromatic · gently hot · rich Leads with wood-smoke running underneath, aromatics blooming from the first bite, on a rich, rounded finish.

Ingredients

  • An ODD number of greens, 7 or 9 for luck: collards, mustard, turnip, spinach, watercress, cabbage, beet tops, carrot tops, parsley — the more, the more friends
  • 1 smoked ham hock + 300g smoked sausage + 200g brisket or ham (Lenten version: omit and go maroon-style meatless)
  • Dark roux: 100ml oil + 100g flour; the trinity; garlic generously
  • Filé powder, thyme, bay, cayenne; the strained pot likker from the greens, every drop
  • Rice; and Holy Thursday on the calendar, traditionally

Allergen screen

Found: gluten · mustard

Screened for: gluten, dairy, egg, nuts, peanuts, fish, shellfish, molluscs, soy, sesame, celery, mustard, lupin, alcohol. Not screened: sulphites.

Method

  1. Boil all the greens together in ample water 30 min; RESERVE the likker; chop or grind the greens fine. 30 min
  2. Make a milk-chocolate roux; trinity; garlic; then the meats, browned.
  3. Greens back in with their likker; simmer low 1.5–2 h: the gumbo should turn deep green-brown and thick as a sermon. 120 min
  4. Filé off the heat; serve over rice. Count your greens; each one is a friend you'll make this year, says the tradition.

How to tell it is going right

This dish has no standard of its own yet. It is judged on the techniques it exercises: checked at the pan, while there is still something to be done about it.

Keeping the cooking liquid 26 recipes

  • It was actually kept: the liquid went into a container rather than down the drain, and the volume saved is enough to be worth using.
  • It is clean: strained of solids and grit, and not clouded with the debris of whatever broke up in it.
  • It tastes of what it cooked and tastes concentrated rather than washed out, and it is not so salty or so bitter that the only honest use is the bin.
  • It was cooled quickly and stored cold, not left standing warm on the stove to be dealt with later.
  • Used, it does the job the dish gives it (carrying flavour into a sauce, loosening the dish, or setting when chilled) rather than merely adding water back.

Where it goes wrong The pot was left to cool slowly on the stove and only strained the next day, so it soured overnight: what should have been the best ingredient in the dish goes in the bin, and the recipe gets water instead.

Making a roux 27 recipes

  • Taken to the stage the dish named and no further, judged against that colour (pale, blond, or brown to brick), and it got there slowly enough to have coloured evenly.
  • It is smooth and mobile: a paste with no dry pockets of flour and no lumps, loosening as it warms rather than sitting stiff in the pan.
  • The raw-flour smell has gone and been replaced by the toasted, biscuity smell of its stage, with nothing acrid behind it.
  • No black specks anywhere. One scorched fleck condemns the batch; it cannot be stirred back in and it will be tasted in the finished sauce.
  • Let down into liquid it goes smooth without lumps and thickens to the body the dish wants, leaving no floury taste on the tongue.

Where it goes wrong The heat was too high and the corners of the pan went unstirred, so it scorched at the edge; black specks run through the finished sauce and carry a bitterness that no amount of seasoning covers.

From the pass

The green gumbo of Holy Thursday: Leah Chase served it at Dooky Chase's once a year and the city planned around it. The odd number of greens is the superstition (each green, a new friend), the pot likker is the soul, and the dish carries the whole Creole genealogy in one pot: African, French, German, Choctaw. One day a year was the tradition; your kitchen may be more generous.
