The French Omelette

  • Intermediate
  • 8 min
  • Serves 4
  • $$
  • Vegetarian

rich · silky · herb-fresh Leads with richness that coats and comforts, a silken, spoon-slow texture, lifted green at the very end.

Ingredients

  • 3 eggs
  • 15g butter
  • Salt
  • Chives (classic), Gruyère (optional)

Allergen screen

Found: dairy · egg

Screened for: gluten, dairy, egg, nuts, peanuts, fish, shellfish, molluscs, soy, sesame, celery, mustard, lupin, alcohol. Not screened: sulphites.

Method

  1. Beat eggs completely smooth with salt; no streaks of white.
  2. Melt butter over medium in a nonstick pan until foaming, not brown.
  3. Add eggs; stir rapidly with a fork or spatula while shaking the pan until softly set curds form.
  4. Off heat, let the base set 10 seconds, then roll onto the plate seam-side down.

How it should come out

  • Uniform pale blond over the whole surface, seam and both ends included; turn it under the light and no tan or freckled patch appears anywhere.
  • Cigar-shaped, closed at both ends, seam underneath; it holds its roll on the plate and does not slacken open.
  • Skin unbroken and dry to the eye: no curd bursting through a tear, and no butter or water weeping into a ring around it within thirty seconds of plating.
  • Cut across it: cream-fine moist curds top to bottom with no ropey white streaks of unbeaten albumen, the set base no thicker than a coin, and when the plate is tilted the centre slumps once and stops rather than running.
  • Seasoned evenly through: first bite and last taste the same, with no grains of salt sitting on the outside and no flat unsalted mouthful in the middle.

Where it goes wrong The pan ran hot and the eggs were left to sit between stirs, so the omelette comes up freckled tan on the seam side with the curds fused into one rubbery sheet instead of custard.

From the pass

Pale gold outside, custard inside: browning is a flavor choice, and in Paris it's a mistake, because the Maillard notes bully the butter and eggs out of the room. The whole dish is heat management: rapid stirring makes the curds small and cream-fine, and the ten still seconds at the end knit them into a skin that can roll. This is the classic audition piece for a French kitchen precisely because there are three ingredients and nothing in the pan to hide behind.
