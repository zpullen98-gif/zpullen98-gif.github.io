Garde Manger · Salad

Salade Composée, Built in Layers

  • Intermediate
  • 35 min
  • $$ roughly 2 of 4 for cost
  • Vegetarian

briny · aromatic · fiery · rich Leads with a briny, maritime salinity, aromatics blooming from the first bite, on a rich, rounded finish.

Ingredients

  • 400 g new potatoes, matched for size
  • 200 g fine green beans, topped
  • 4 eggs, straight from the fridge at 4C (39F)
  • 80 g tender leaves, butterhead or little gem
  • 60 g vinaigrette
  • 30 g shallot, sliced into fine rings
  • 20 g capers, rinsed and dried
  • 10 g tarragon and chervil leaves
  • 10 g salt per litre for the potato water, 1 percent
  • 20 g salt per litre for the bean water, 2 percent
  • flaky salt and black pepper

Method

  1. Start the potatoes in cold 1 percent salted water, bring to a bare tremble and cook 18 to 22 minutes, until a knife slides to the centre and withdraws with no drag. 22 min
  2. Drain, halve, and dress with a third of the vinaigrette while they read about 55C (131F) to the finger; warm swollen starch drinks the dressing, and a cold potato sheds it.
  3. Boil the beans in 2 percent water for 3 minutes, until they bend without snapping, then plunge into iced water and lift them out the instant they are cold, before the green slides toward olive. 3 min
  4. Lower the fridge-cold eggs into rolling water for 6 minutes 30 seconds, chill 3 minutes in ice, peel and halve; the yolk should be set at the rim and jammy at the centre. 6 min
  5. Season and dress each element on its own, then build from the base up: potatoes, beans, shallot and capers, the leaves turned last in the remaining vinaigrette, eggs on top with flaky salt on the cut yolk.

How to tell it is going right

This dish has no standard of its own yet. It is judged on the techniques it exercises: checked at the pan, while there is still something to be done about it.

Blanching & shocking 76 recipes

  • A large volume at a hard rolling boil, and enough of it that adding the food barely interrupts it; a pot that goes quiet has started stewing rather than blanching.
  • Timed rather than watched, and short. This is an interruption in the cooking of something, not the cooking of it.
  • The ice bath was made and standing beside the pot BEFORE anything went into the water, not started once the timer went off.
  • It went straight into the ice water, fully submerged, and stayed until cold at the centre: a piece still warm inside is still cooking, and the arrest has not happened yet.
  • Drained and dried afterwards rather than left sitting in water, so it does not carry that water into whatever it meets next.

Where it goes wrong The ice bath was not ready, so the food sat draining in a colander while its own residual heat carried on cooking it. By the time it cooled it was a stage past where it was pulled, and exact arrest is the entire reason for blanching rather than simply boiling.

The bare simmer: holding liquid below the boil 183 recipes

  • The surface is read rather than assumed: bubbles break slowly in one or two places, or the surface merely shivers, and it does not reach a rolling boil at any point.
  • It was held there: the pot was watched and the heat adjusted rather than set once and left, and there is no tide mark up the pot wall from a boil-over.
  • The liquid stayed as clear as it should be: no clouding from agitation, and no fat churned into an emulsion through it.
  • Whatever is in the liquid is still intact: nothing shredded, broken up, or worried apart by movement.
  • The level is roughly where the elapsed time should have left it; a large drop is the evidence that it ran hotter than this while nobody was looking.

Where it goes wrong It was brought to a boil and then left on the same heat, so it churned for the whole cook: the liquid clouded, the fat emulsified into it, and the solids broke apart into the broth.

From the pass

Composed means every element is cooked, seasoned and dressed alone, then assembled, so nothing is diluted or wilted by its neighbour. The one time-critical step is the potato: dress it at about 55C (131F), because the swollen starch matrix is still open and absorbs vinaigrette, while a cooled potato has retrograded and simply sheds it onto the plate.
