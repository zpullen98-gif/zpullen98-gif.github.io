Ca Kho To (Caramel Fish)

  • Intermediate
  • 45 min
  • $$ roughly 2 of 4 for cost

sweet-edged · umami-deep · tangy-ferment · aromatic Leads with a sweet edge rounding the corners, savory depth stacked from the pantry’s bass notes, opening aromatic and staying there.

Ingredients

  • 600g catfish or salmon steaks
  • 3 tbsp sugar (for the caramel, nuoc mau)
  • 3 tbsp fish sauce
  • Shallots, garlic, ginger
  • Coconut water or plain water
  • Black pepper, chilies, scallion; clay pot ideal

Method

  1. Melt sugar dry to a dark amber caramel, darker than feels safe.
  2. Add fish sauce (stand back), aromatics, and a splash of coconut water.
  3. Nestle in the fish; simmer uncovered 20–25 min, spooning the sauce over. 25 min
  4. Finish aggressive with black pepper. Rice is not optional.

How to tell it is going right

This dish has no standard of its own yet. It is judged on the technique it exercises: checked at the pan, while there is still something to be done about it.

Sugar stages & caramel 53 recipes

  • Nothing is stirred once it is boiling, and any crystals clinging to the side of the pan are brushed down rather than left to fall in.
  • Judged by temperature or by a tested stage rather than by time: consecutive stages are a few degrees apart and look identical in the pan.
  • Colour is read off the heat and in good light, remembering that sugar keeps darkening after the flame is out.
  • Stopped decisively at the point wanted: off the heat and into the next liquid or onto a cold surface, rather than left to coast in a hot pan.
  • The result is clear and smooth rather than grainy, cloudy, or seized into a solid mass.

Where it goes wrong It was stirred after coming to the boil, or a stray crystal from the side of the pan fell back in. One crystal seeds the whole pan and the syrup turns grainy within seconds, and once sugar has crystallised the only cure is more water and starting the whole thing again.

From the pass

Bitter-edged caramel plus fish sauce is the backbone of Vietnamese kho (sweet, salty, smoky, profound), and the caramel (nuoc mau) must go darker than feels safe, to the edge of burnt, or the dish tastes like candy instead of dinner. Stand back when the fish sauce hits; the pan will shout. Clay pot preferred but not required. Spoon the glaze over as it reduces, finish aggressive with black pepper, and accept that rice is not optional; the sauce demands it.
