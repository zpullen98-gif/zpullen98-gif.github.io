Indiana Sugar Cream Bars

  • Intermediate
  • 60 min
  • Serves 4
  • $$
  • Vegetarian

rich · warm-spiced · sweet-edged · floral Leads with richness that coats and comforts, warm spice humming through the middle, leaving a faint perfume.

Ingredients

  • FOR THE CRUST
  • 250g flour
  • 100g sugar
  • 170g butter
  • FOR THE FILLING
  • 500ml cream
  • 150g brown sugar
  • 50g cornstarch
  • 55g butter
  • 1 tsp vanilla
  • Nutmeg to dust

The skills inside

Rubbing fat into flour

Allergen screen

Found: gluten · dairy

Screened for: gluten, dairy, egg, nuts, peanuts, fish, shellfish, molluscs, soy, sesame, celery, mustard, lupin, alcohol. Not screened: sulphites.

Method

  1. Rub the flour, sugar, and butter to crumbs, press into a lined pan, and bake at 175C for 18 min until pale gold. 18 min
  2. Whisk the brown sugar and cornstarch in a pan, slowly add the cream, and cook stirring until thick.
  3. Off the heat beat in the butter and vanilla and pour over the crust.
  4. Dust with nutmeg and chill until set.
  5. Cut into bars.

How to tell it is going right

This dish has no standard of its own yet. It is judged on the technique it exercises: checked at the pan, while there is still something to be done about it.

Rubbing fat into flour 33 recipes

  • The fat is cold when it starts and still firm when the rubbing stops, and the bowl has not warmed under the hands.
  • Stopped early, while it still looks like coarse crumbs with pieces of fat visible in it, not worked on to a smooth even sand, which means the pieces have gone.
  • Nothing is greasy or glossy and nothing has smeared into a paste against the side of the bowl.
  • Liquid added last and only enough to bring it together: pressed, it just holds, with no wet patch and nothing sticking to the hand.
  • Rested cold before it is rolled or shaped, so the gluten relaxes and the fat firms again after being handled.

Where it goes wrong The fat was too warm, or it was rubbed until the mixture looked uniform. Once the fat has melted into the flour there are no solid pieces left to steam apart in the oven, so the bake is sandy or tough instead of flaky, and nothing done later puts those layers back.

From the pass

Sugar cream pie in a hand-held form, Indiana's unofficial state pie (Hoosier pie, the desperation pie made when the larder held only sugar, cream and flour) translated for the potluck table. As with the pie, whisk the filling without stopping: the cornstarch catches and scorches the instant you turn your back, and a scorched sugar cream cannot be rescued by anything. Nutmeg on top is the traditional finish. Chill fully before cutting or the bars surrender their shape.
