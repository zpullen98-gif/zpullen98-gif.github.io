Axoa de Veau d'Espelette

  • Intermediate
  • 30 min
  • Serves 4
  • $$$

aromatic · gently hot · warm-spiced Leads with aromatics blooming from the first bite, a warm current of chile, fading in warm spice.

Ingredients

  • 1.2 kg veal shoulder, trimmed and cut by knife into 1 cm dice
  • 40 g duck fat
  • 400 g onions, in 5 mm dice
  • 600 g piments doux verts, in 1 cm dice
  • 4 garlic cloves, chopped
  • 1 dried Espelette pod, crumbled, or 1 small green chilli
  • 2 tsp piment d'Espelette
  • 300 ml light veal or chicken stock
  • salt

Method

  1. Cut the shoulder into dice with a knife, never a machine, and keep every piece about a centimetre so they cook at one rate.
  2. Colour the veal in the duck fat in a wide pan in two or three batches, gold on two faces only; if the pan hisses quietly instead of cracking, it is too crowded.
  3. Sweat the onions, diced peppers and garlic in the same fat until they are melting and sweet and the brown fond has dissolved off the base.
  4. Return the meat with both Espelettes and stock barely to cover, then hold it covered at a lazy blip for about an hour and a quarter, until a dice crushes between finger and thumb with no spring left.
  5. Uncover and reduce until the sauce pools around the meat rather than running off it, and salt only now.

How to tell it is going right

This dish has no standard of its own yet. It is judged on the techniques it exercises: checked at the pan, while there is still something to be done about it.

Reducing a sauce 86 recipes

  • A steady simmer with the surface moving, never a rolling boil that throws it up the sides and scorches what is left on the base.
  • Judged by behaviour rather than the clock: it coats the back of a spoon and holds a clean line when a finger is drawn through it.
  • The drop in volume is visible against the side of the pan: there is a line to see, not a guess about whether anything has happened.
  • Seasoned at the end and not the beginning, because salt concentrates at exactly the rate everything else does.
  • Nothing is catching underneath: a spoon drawn across the base comes up clean, with no browned film building where the heat is fiercest.

Where it goes wrong It was seasoned at the start and then reduced by half. The salt concentrated along with everything else, so the sauce reaches perfect body and is too salty to serve. And the only remedy is to dilute it and reduce it all over again.

Knife cuts: dice, julienne, bias 115 recipes

  • Take any three pieces from anywhere in the pile: they match closely enough in every dimension that you would have to measure them to argue otherwise.
  • The cut faces are flat and clean rather than ragged or crushed; leaves and herbs are cut through, not bruised, and the board carries no dark smear where they were.
  • The stated size is the actual size, held against a rule or a known reference rather than called about right.
  • Everything in the pile is the shape that was asked for: no wedges, no half pieces, and no offcuts swept in to avoid waste.
  • They cook evenly, which is the entire point: at the end of cooking nothing is still raw and nothing has collapsed.

Where it goes wrong The knife was dull, or the vegetable was never squared off first, so the pieces vary: the small ones are mush by the time the large ones are cooked, and the dish has two textures where it wanted one.

From the pass

Shoulder is collagen rich, and collagen only unwinds into gelatine above about 70C (158F) held for an hour or more. That gelatine is what makes axoa glossy with no flour in it. Mince the meat instead of dicing it and you rupture the fibres, so the juices run out in the first minutes and the finished pan is grainy and dry. Keep the dice at a centimetre and the pot at a blip, never a rolling boil, which wrings the fibres tight.
