Stock & Fond Atlas · Sauce

Fond de Légumes

  • Easy
  • 30 min
  • Serves 4
  • $$
  • Vegetarian

herb-fresh · gently hot · rich · umami-deep Leads with fresh herbs arguing happily on top, a warm current of chile, finishing long and savory.

Ingredients

  • 50 g butter
  • 300 g onion, sliced thin
  • 150 g leek, white and pale green
  • 120 g fennel
  • 100 g celery
  • 120 g carrot
  • 200 g button mushrooms, sliced
  • 200 g ripe tomato, chopped
  • 8 g salt
  • 1.5 litres cold water
  • 4 parsley stalks
  • 1 bay leaf
  • 6 white peppercorns

Equipment

Fine sieve

The skills inside

Straining & passing through a sieve

Allergen screen

Found: dairy · celery

Screened for: gluten, dairy, egg, nuts, peanuts, fish, shellfish, molluscs, soy, sesame, celery, mustard, lupin, alcohol. Not screened: sulphites.

Method

  1. Cut every vegetable to 5 mm dice or thin slices. Small pieces give up their flavour inside the short window this stock allows.
  2. Melt the butter in a wide pan, add the vegetables and the salt, set the lid ajar and sweat over medium-low heat for 18 minutes, until the onion is glassy and the pan smells sweet, with no browning at all. 18 min
  3. Add the tomato and the cold water, bring to 85C (185F), and hold 30 minutes without ever letting it boil. 30 min
  4. Pull the pot off the heat, drop in the parsley, bay and peppercorns and steep covered for 15 minutes, as you would tea. 15 min
  5. Strain through a fine sieve without pressing, and cool fast. Season it only when you know what it is for.

How to tell it is going right

This dish has no standard of its own yet. It is judged on the technique it exercises: checked at the pan, while there is still something to be done about it.

Straining & passing through a sieve 69 recipes

  • Nothing solid got through: a finger drawn through what came out finds no fibre, seed, skin, curd, or grit.
  • Where the dish wants body it was worked through rather than left to drip, and the solids left in the mesh are spent and dry rather than still holding liquid worth having.
  • It is judged on the tongue rather than by eye: smooth, with no residual grain left behind after the mouthful has gone.
  • The mesh was fine enough for the job and held its shape, and nothing bypassed it by going over the rim.
  • The yield is close to what went in less the spent solids: not half of it abandoned in the sieve because it stopped running and nobody pushed.

Where it goes wrong It was pushed through a mesh too coarse for it, or the sieve was overloaded and slopped at the rim, so the fibres it was meant to remove are in the finished sauce and it reads grainy on the tongue.

From the pass

Vegetables carry no collagen, so time cannot give this body: it can only take flavour away. Past about forty minutes the sugars are spent, the volatile aromatics have gone up with the steam and the onion and fennel start pushing a dull sulphur drone. That is exactly how vegetable stock becomes brown water. Build the flavour instead in a slow salted sweat, hold the pot under 90C, and steep the herbs off the heat so they stay green.
