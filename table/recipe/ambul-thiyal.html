Sri Lankan · Main

Ambul Thiyal

  • Intermediate
  • 25 min
  • Serves 4
  • $$$

aromatic · warm-spiced · earthy · floral Leads with aromatics blooming from the first bite, warm spice humming through the middle, leaving a faint perfume.

Ingredients

  • 700 g tuna or kingfish, cut into 4 cm cubes
  • 25 g goraka, about 8 dried pieces
  • 150 ml water
  • 4 garlic cloves, crushed
  • 3 cm ginger, crushed
  • 2 tsp coarsely ground black pepper
  • 1 tsp chilli powder
  • 0.5 tsp ground turmeric
  • 5 cm Ceylon cinnamon quill
  • 1 sprig curry leaves
  • 10 cm pandan leaf
  • 1 tbsp coconut oil
  • 2 tsp salt

The skills inside

The bare simmer: holding liquid below the boil

Allergen screen

Found: fish

Screened for: gluten, dairy, egg, nuts, peanuts, fish, shellfish, molluscs, soy, sesame, celery, mustard, lupin, alcohol. Not screened: sulphites.

Method

  1. Cover the goraka with 100 ml boiling water and leave until soft, then pound it with the garlic, ginger and 1 tsp of the salt to a black paste.
  2. Turn the fish cubes in the paste with the pepper, chilli, turmeric and remaining salt until every face is coated dark.
  3. Lay the fish in one layer in a clay pot or heavy pan with the cinnamon, curry leaves, pandan, coconut oil and the 150 ml water, and bring to a bare simmer.
  4. Cover and cook, shaking the pan rather than stirring, until the cubes are firm to a fingertip and the liquid has gone to a thick black glaze that clings to them.
  5. Leave it off the heat for at least an hour before eating; the sourness settles into the fish as it cools.

How to tell it is going right

This dish has no standard of its own yet. It is judged on the technique it exercises: checked at the pan, while there is still something to be done about it.

  • The surface is read rather than assumed: bubbles break slowly in one or two places, or the surface merely shivers, and it does not reach a rolling boil at any point.
  • It was held there: the pot was watched and the heat adjusted rather than set once and left, and there is no tide mark up the pot wall from a boil-over.
  • The liquid stayed as clear as it should be: no clouding from agitation, and no fat churned into an emulsion through it.
  • Whatever is in the liquid is still intact: nothing shredded, broken up, or worried apart by movement.
  • The level is roughly where the elapsed time should have left it; a large drop is the evidence that it ran hotter than this while nobody was looking.

Where it goes wrong It was brought to a boil and then left on the same heat, so it churned for the whole cook: the liquid clouded, the fat emulsified into it, and the solids broke apart into the broth.

From the pass

Goraka sours with hydroxycitric acid and a resinous, smoke cured edge; tamarind's tartaric acid is fruitier and arrives faster. That difference is the dish. Goraka's acid with the salt firms the fish protein so the cubes hold their corners, and the near dry finish is preservation, not reduction for looks. Never stir it: shake the pan, or you serve flakes.
