Korean · Soup

Kimchi Jjigae

  • Easy
  • 35 min
  • Serves 4
  • $$

tangy-ferment · rich · herb-fresh · fiery Leads with ferment tang with real depth behind it, richness that coats and comforts, on a slow, honest burn.

Ingredients

  • 2 cups very ripe kimchi + juice
  • 200g pork belly, sliced
  • 1 tbsp gochujang, 1 tbsp gochugaru
  • 300g tofu, thick slabs
  • Scallions, onion, 500ml water or anchovy stock

The skills inside

Salted water & the float test

Allergen screen

Found: gluten · fish · soy

Screened for: gluten, dairy, egg, nuts, peanuts, fish, shellfish, molluscs, soy, sesame, celery, mustard, lupin, alcohol. Not screened: sulphites.

Method

  1. Fry pork belly until its fat runs; add kimchi and fry 5 min more. 5 min
  2. Add pastes, water, onion; boil hard 15 min: jjigae likes violence. 15 min
  3. Tofu slabs in gently for the last 5.
  4. Scallions; serve volcanic with rice.

How to tell it is going right

This dish has no standard of its own yet. It is judged on the technique it exercises: checked at the pan, while there is still something to be done about it.

Salted water & the float test 180 recipes

  • The water was salted before anything went in, and tasting it confirms it: seasoned on the tongue, with the salt dissolved rather than sitting on the pot floor.
  • There is enough water, hot enough, that the pot returns to its boil quickly after the food goes in and never stops moving.
  • Where the dish uses the float as its signal, the signal is obeyed: the pieces rise and they come out then, not several minutes later once something else was finished.
  • Nothing is stuck to the bottom of the pot or welded to itself, and the water has not thickened into a cloudy sludge.
  • Drained on a bite rather than on the clock: resistance at the centre, with no chalky raw core.

Where it goes wrong The pot was too small and the water unsalted, so it never recovered its boil: the food sat in slack water, went gummy at the surface, and comes out bland in a way that no sauce afterwards can fix.

From the pass

The frying step turns sour kimchi's sharpness into depth: boiled-only jjigae tastes thin, because the pork fat and the hard fry mellow the acid into something rounder before the water ever arrives. Then jjigae likes violence: a hard boil, not a polite simmer, is what emulsifies the fat and pastes into the broth's body. Over-the-hill kimchi is the correct kimchi. Tofu slides in last and gently, to arrive at the table as slabs rather than rubble.
