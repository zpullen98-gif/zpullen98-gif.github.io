Moro de Guandules con Coco

  • Intermediate
  • 25 min
  • $$ roughly 2 of 4 for cost
  • Vegetarian
  • Vegan

rich · umami-deep · herb-fresh · briny Leads with richness that coats and comforts, savory depth stacked from the pantry’s bass notes, ending saline and clean.

Ingredients

  • 400 g long grain rice, rinsed until the water runs clear
  • 250 g guandules (pigeon peas), fresh or drained
  • 400 ml coconut milk
  • 300 ml water
  • 45 ml neutral oil, plus a spoonful for the concón
  • 50 g sazón dominicano
  • 20 g tomato paste
  • 5 g dried oregano, rubbed
  • 2 leaves cilantro ancho
  • 40 g pitted green olives
  • 10 g salt

Method

  1. Fry the sazón and tomato paste in the oil in a heavy caldero until the oil runs orange, then add the guandules and turn them until they are coated.
  2. Pour in the coconut milk and water with the oregano, cilantro ancho, olives and salt, bring to the boil and taste the liquid: it should read a shade saltier than you want the finished rice.
  3. Stir in the rice, level it once, and boil uncovered without touching it until the surface goes from soup to a pocked, glossy porridge and craters open, about 8 minutes. 8 min
  4. Fold the rice over on itself a single time, drop the heat to its lowest, cover tightly and leave it 20 minutes with the lid down. 20 min
  5. Uncover, trickle the extra oil around the rim, raise the heat and listen; pull the pot 3 minutes after a fine steady crackle starts. 3 min

From the pass

Coconut milk is the southern tell and it rewrites the timing: its sugars and fat brown well below the temperature plain starch needs, so the concón forms in about three minutes rather than ten, and a minute past the first fine crackle it is burnt rather than toasted. Listen instead of looking. Once the lid is down do not stir; broken grains leak amylose and the moro goes gummy.
