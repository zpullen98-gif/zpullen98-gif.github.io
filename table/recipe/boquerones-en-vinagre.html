Boquerones en Vinagre

  • Intermediate
  • 45 min
  • Serves 4
  • $$

bright · herb-fresh · aromatic · umami-deep Leads with acid holding everything up to the light, fresh herbs arguing happily on top, finishing long and savory.

Ingredients

  • 500 g fresh anchovies, held at -20C (-4F) for 48 hours first
  • 250 ml white wine vinegar
  • 100 ml cold water
  • 10 g fine sea salt
  • 3 garlic cloves, sliced paper thin
  • 15 g flat parsley, chopped
  • 200 ml mild olive oil

Allergen screen

Found: fish · alcohol

Screened for: gluten, dairy, egg, nuts, peanuts, fish, shellfish, molluscs, soy, sesame, celery, mustard, lupin, alcohol. Not screened: sulphites.

Method

  1. Pinch off each head and pull it downward to draw the guts with it, open the fish along the belly, and lift the backbone out from the head end, leaving the fillets joined at the tail.
  2. Rinse the fillets in cold water, changing it until the water stays clear, because leftover blood turns the finished fish grey.
  3. Lay them skin down in a shallow dish, pour over the vinegar, water and salt, and refrigerate 4 hours for small fish, 6 for fat ones. 240 min
  4. Cut one fillet across to check: the moment no translucent line is left at the centre, drain the lot.
  5. Layer in a container with the garlic and parsley, cover completely with the oil, and leave a day before eating. Use within five days.

From the pass

This is an acid cure, not a salt one. Acetic acid unfolds the muscle proteins much as heat does, which is why the flesh goes white and firm with no pan involved, and the change front travels in from both faces, so thickness sets the time and the clock only follows it. Leave them in past white and they go chalky and taste of nothing but vinegar. Oil is the stop: drained and covered, the acid has nothing left to work on.
