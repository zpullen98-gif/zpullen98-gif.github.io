California Roll

  • Advanced
  • 1 h 30 m
  • $$$$ roughly 4 of 4 for cost

briny · bright · nutty-toasted · umami-deep Leads with a briny, maritime salinity, acid holding everything up to the light, finishing long and savory.

Ingredients

  • 300g sushi rice
  • 400ml water
  • 60ml rice vinegar, 2 tbsp sugar, 1 tsp salt
  • 4 sheets nori
  • 200g cooked crab or surimi
  • 1 avocado, sliced
  • 1 cucumber, in batons
  • 2 tbsp toasted sesame seeds or tobiko
  • Soy sauce, wasabi, pickled ginger

Method

  1. Rinse the rice until the water runs clear, cook it, then fold through the warmed vinegar, sugar, and salt. Cool to body temperature.
  2. Lay a nori sheet on a rolling mat covered in cling film and spread rice over it, then flip so the nori faces up: this is the inside-out roll.
  3. Lay the crab, avocado, and cucumber along the center.
  4. Roll tightly with the mat, pressing to compact.
  5. Roll the outside in sesame seeds and cut with a wet knife into eight pieces.

How to tell it is going right

This dish has no standard of its own yet. It is judged on the technique it exercises: checked at the pan, while there is still something to be done about it.

  • Take any three pieces from anywhere in the pile: they match closely enough in every dimension that you would have to measure them to argue otherwise.
  • The cut faces are flat and clean rather than ragged or crushed; leaves and herbs are cut through, not bruised, and the board carries no dark smear where they were.
  • The stated size is the actual size, held against a rule or a known reference rather than called about right.
  • Everything in the pile is the shape that was asked for: no wedges, no half pieces, and no offcuts swept in to avoid waste.
  • They cook evenly, which is the entire point: at the end of cooking nothing is still raw and nothing has collapsed.

Where it goes wrong The knife was dull, or the vegetable was never squared off first, so the pieces vary: the small ones are mush by the time the large ones are cooked, and the dish has two textures where it wanted one.

From the pass

Created in Los Angeles in the 1960s to introduce American diners to sushi by hiding the nori inside and swapping raw fish for crab and avocado. Wet the knife between every cut, or the rice drags and the roll loses its shape.
