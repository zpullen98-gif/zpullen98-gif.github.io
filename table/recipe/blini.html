Blini

  • Intermediate
  • 1 h 30 m
  • $$$$ roughly 4 of 4 for cost
  • Vegetarian

rich · smoky · sweet-edged Leads with richness that coats and comforts, wood-smoke running underneath, trailing gentle sweetness.

Ingredients

  • 300g flour; 7g yeast; 1 tbsp sugar; ½ tsp salt: yeast is what makes them blini, not pancakes
  • 500ml warm milk; 2 eggs, separated; 50g melted butter
  • More butter for the pan and for brushing every single finished blin
  • Toppings in rank: smetana, jam, honey, smoked salmon, or caviar if the occasion earns it
  • A stack plate under a towel

Method

  1. Yeasted batter from milk, yolks, flour; rise 1 h until it breathes and bubbles. 60 min
  2. Fold in whipped whites, lightness insurance.
  3. Fry thin in a buttered pan, swirling; lace-edged, golden, one minute a side.
  4. Brush each with butter and stack under the towel. Serve the stack whole; let the table demolish it.

How to tell it is going right

This dish has no standard of its own yet. It is judged on the techniques it exercises: checked at the pan, while there is still something to be done about it.

Folding: keeping the air in 34 recipes

  • The air was actually built first and it shows: whatever was creamed or whipped has changed colour and grown in volume, pale and light rather than dense.
  • The two mixtures were brought closer before being combined: a little of the light one worked into the heavy one first, so the rest is not beaten flat trying to move it.
  • Folded and not stirred: a broad implement down through the middle, across the base, up and over, with the bowl turned between strokes.
  • Stopped the moment the streaks disappear. It is still visibly aerated and holds its shape briefly when the spoon lifts away.
  • Into the tin and into the oven straight away: a batter left standing on the bench gives back the air it was built for.

Where it goes wrong It was stirred until it looked perfectly smooth. Every stroke past the point where the streaks vanish knocks out air that cannot be put back, so the bake comes out dense and short of the tin, and often wet through the middle where there was no structure to hold it.

Proofing: the rise before the oven 78 recipes

  • Judged on volume against the container rather than on the clock: visibly grown, and grown by roughly the amount the recipe describes.
  • The poke test agrees: a floured fingertip pressed a centimetre in leaves a dent that springs back slowly and only part of the way. Straight back means it is not ready; no spring at all means it has gone.
  • The surface is domed, smooth, and slightly sheened: not cratered, wrinkled, or collapsed, and not skinned over and dry from proofing uncovered.
  • Handled, it feels alive and airy: light for its size, with bubbles visible under the skin.
  • It springs in the oven: the piece lifts in the first minutes and the scores open. A shape that does not move at all was proofed past its point before it went in.

Where it goes wrong It was proofed to the clock in a kitchen colder or warmer than the recipe assumed: overproofed, it collapses as the blade touches it and bakes flat and dense with a sour edge; underproofed, it tears at the side and bakes tight.

From the pass

Blini are older than Russia's written history: pagan sun symbols eaten by the stack at Maslenitsa, the butter festival before Lent. The yeast gives them a sour-tender crumb no baking-powder pancake reaches; the mandatory butter-brushing between layers is why the stack glistens like the sun it represents.
