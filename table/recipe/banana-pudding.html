Banana Pudding

  • Intermediate
  • 60 min
  • $$ roughly 2 of 4 for cost
  • Vegetarian

silky · rich · floral · fiery Leads with a silken, spoon-slow texture, richness that coats and comforts, on a slow, honest burn.

Ingredients

  • The custard, from scratch and non-negotiable: 500ml whole milk, 100g sugar, 3 tbsp cornstarch, 3 egg yolks, 2 tbsp butter, 1 tbsp vanilla, pinch salt
  • 1 box vanilla wafers: the industrial cookie is canonical here; this is the wafer's finest hour
  • 4–5 ripe bananas, sliced at assembly (not before)
  • Topping schism: meringue baked gold (the church-cookbook original) or sweetened whipped cream (the potluck majority)
  • A glass dish or trifle bowl (the layers are meant to be seen) and 4+ hours of fridge time

Equipment

Piping bag

Method

  1. Cook the custard: whisk milk, sugar, starch, yolks over medium, stirring always, until it thickens and one big bubble breaks the surface; butter and vanilla off heat; press film to the surface and cool slightly.
  2. Layer while the custard is still warm: wafers, bananas, custard: three stories high, custard flooding every floor; the warm custard is what softens the wafers into their famous cake-like second life.
  3. Crown with meringue and bake 10 min at 180°C, or chill first and pipe cream later, per allegiance. 10 min
  4. Refrigerate 4 h minimum, overnight for the devout. Serve in great scoops that show the strata. 240 min

How to tell it is going right

This dish has no standard of its own yet. It is judged on the techniques it exercises: checked at the pan, while there is still something to be done about it.

Whipping a meringue 40 recipes

  • Bowl and whisk are clean and dry with no trace of fat or yolk. Grease prevents the foam outright, and there is no recovering a bowl that had it partway through.
  • The sugar went in gradually once the whites were already foaming, rather than all at once at the start.
  • No grit: a little rubbed between finger and thumb feels smooth, so the sugar has dissolved into the foam rather than sitting in it.
  • Stiff peaks means the tip stands with a slight bow when the whisk lifts, and the bowl can be tipped without the mass moving in it.
  • Glossy and smooth rather than chalky or dull, with no liquid gathering underneath it in the bowl.

Where it goes wrong It was whipped past stiff peaks. A minute beyond glory the protein network tightens and tears, the foam turns grainy and matte, water weeps out beneath it, and folding that into anything gives a mixture that collapses on the way to the oven.

Tempering a custard 59 recipes

  • The hot liquid went into the eggs a little at a time with constant whisking, and only then did everything go back to the pot.
  • The heat under it stayed gentle throughout and it was never allowed to boil.
  • Stirred continuously and right into the corners of the pan, where it thickens first and sets unnoticed.
  • Judged by behaviour: it thickens enough to coat the back of a spoon and hold a clean line when a finger is drawn through it.
  • Taken off before it looks finished, and strained. Residual heat carries it the rest of the way, and a custard held on the heat until it looks right arrives past right.

Where it goes wrong The hot liquid went in too fast, or the pot went back over full heat to save time. The yolks set into threads before they could thicken smoothly, and a scrambled custard does not come back; straining hides a little of it and nothing hides the rest.

From the pass

The dish is secretly about time: the wafers are engineered crisp specifically so the custard can transform them, and hour-four banana pudding and hour-twelve banana pudding are two different desserts, both correct. Southern churches and barbecue joints treat it as the mandatory closer (a plate of brisket without banana pudding is legally incomplete in several states of mind). Boxed pudding mix is the shortcut everyone takes and the difference everyone tastes.
