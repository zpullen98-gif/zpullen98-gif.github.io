Cooked Meringue, Italian and Swiss

  • Advanced
  • 25 min
  • $$ roughly 2 of 4 for cost
  • Vegetarian

rich · bright Leads with richness that coats and comforts, acid holding everything up to the light.

Ingredients

  • For Italian: 100 g egg white at 20C (68F)
  • For Italian: 200 g caster sugar
  • For Italian: 60 g water, 30 percent of the sugar
  • For Italian: 1 g cream of tartar
  • For Swiss: 100 g egg white
  • For Swiss: 180 g caster sugar
  • For Swiss: 1 g fine salt
  • White wine vinegar, for wiping out the bowl

Method

  1. Wipe bowl and whisk with vinegar and dry them; a trace of fat or yolk stops the foam climbing at all.
  2. For Italian, start the whites and cream of tartar at medium speed while the sugar and water cook to 118C (244F), soft ball, brushing crystals off the pan sides with a wet brush.
  3. With the whites at soft peak, pour the syrup in a thin steady thread down the inside of the bowl, never on to the moving whisk, then whip until the bowl is barely warm at 30C (86F) and the peak holds a straight beak.
  4. For Swiss, whisk whites and sugar together in a bowl over barely simmering water, the base clear of the water, to 65C (150F), until no grit is left between finger and thumb.
  5. Move it to the mixer and whip 8 to 10 minutes on high until the bowl is cool and the mass is dense, glossy and holds a sharp ridge. 10 min
  6. Use Italian where it must stay light and stable, in mousse, buttercream and torched tops; use Swiss where you want density, for buttercream and for shapes piped to dry at 90C (194F).

How to tell it is going right

This dish has no standard of its own yet. It is judged on the techniques it exercises: checked at the pan, while there is still something to be done about it.

Icing & frosting 17 recipes

  • The temperatures were chosen, not defaulted: the bake fully cool where the finish is meant to sit on top of it, or both deliberately warm where the method wants the two to fuse, and the proof either way is a finish that stayed where it was put.
  • Smooth on the tongue: no grit of undissolved sugar and no lumps of anything that was never fully worked in.
  • The consistency matches the job: it spreads or pours the way the method intends, then holds the edge, swirl, or drip it was given.
  • The surface underneath is intact: covered rather than scrubbed, with no crumbs dragged through the finish and no torn patches showing.
  • By serving it has settled into the finish the method wants (matte, glossy, or soft) and a cut comes away clean rather than pulling the top off with it.

Where it goes wrong A finish meant to sit on top went on while the bake was still warm. The layer that touched first melted, slid, and soaked in; what is left sets greasy and streaked, and no amount of chilling afterwards puts that finish back.

Whipping a meringue 40 recipes

  • Bowl and whisk are clean and dry with no trace of fat or yolk. Grease prevents the foam outright, and there is no recovering a bowl that had it partway through.
  • The sugar went in gradually once the whites were already foaming, rather than all at once at the start.
  • No grit: a little rubbed between finger and thumb feels smooth, so the sugar has dissolved into the foam rather than sitting in it.
  • Stiff peaks means the tip stands with a slight bow when the whisk lifts, and the bowl can be tipped without the mass moving in it.
  • Glossy and smooth rather than chalky or dull, with no liquid gathering underneath it in the bowl.

Where it goes wrong It was whipped past stiff peaks. A minute beyond glory the protein network tightens and tears, the foam turns grainy and matte, water weeps out beneath it, and folding that into anything gives a mixture that collapses on the way to the oven.

From the pass

Both cook the white so the foam holds for days. Italian sets it with 118C (244F) syrup, which brings the whipping mass to about 72C (162F); Swiss sets it in the bowl at 65C (150F). Whip the whites past soft peak before the syrup lands and the bubble walls have no stretch left, so the meringue turns grainy and weeps its syrup out.
