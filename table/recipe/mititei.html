Romanian & Bulgarian · Starter

Mititei

  • Intermediate
  • 30 min
  • $$$ roughly 3 of 4 for cost

charred · aromatic · warm-spiced · rich Leads with char used as punctuation, aromatics blooming from the first bite, on a rich, rounded finish.

Ingredients

  • 700 g beef chuck, coarsely minced and very cold
  • 300 g pork belly, coarsely minced
  • 16 g fine salt
  • 6 g bicarbonate of soda
  • 8 garlic cloves, pounded to a paste
  • 200 ml cold beef bone stock
  • 2 tsp ground black pepper
  • 1 tsp ground allspice
  • 1 tsp ground coriander seed
  • 1 tsp dried thyme
  • half tsp ground caraway
  • sunflower oil, for the grill bars

Equipment

Grill · Piping bag · Mortar & pestle

Method

  1. Knead the cold minced meats with the salt and bicarbonate of soda alone for 4 to 5 minutes, until the mass turns shiny and tacky and pulls away from the bowl in strings. 5 min
  2. Work in the garlic and ground spices, then beat in the cold stock a third at a time, waiting until each addition has vanished into the paste before adding the next.
  3. Turn your hand over to test it: the paste should cling upside down. Cover and rest it in the fridge overnight, since there is no casing to hold these together and the bicarbonate needs the time.
  4. Pipe the paste through a wide nozzle into cigars about 8 cm long and 3 cm thick, wetting your hands to square off the ends.
  5. Grill over hot bare coals with no flame licking up, turning every minute and brushing with water beaten with a little garlic, until the surfaces are dark and lacquered and one springs back when pressed, about 8 minutes. 8 min

How to tell it is going right

This dish has no standard of its own yet. It is judged on the techniques it exercises: checked at the pan, while there is still something to be done about it.

Pounding a curry paste 23 recipes

  • Smeared thin across a pale surface it reads as one substance, edge to edge: no pale gritty specks standing proud of the smear, no shard that drags a line through it as the smear is pulled. Flecks mean something hard went in after something wet and skated instead of shattering.
  • Rubbed between finger and thumb it feels like wet clay, dense and smooth, and takes the print of the thumb. Anything that rolls into a hard grain or grits like sand has not been broken down, and the order of work was wrong.
  • The fibrous aromatics have surrendered: pinch a little and pull, and nothing draws out as a thread or a hair. Listen to the last minutes of work as well, which should be a dull wet slap in the mortar; a dry knock or rattle still present means hard pieces are being chased around rather than crushed.
  • One even colour throughout when it is turned under the light, deep and saturated, with no marbling and no streak in which a single component can still be read. A dull grey cast over the surface says it was worked long past its point.
  • Heaped up and left alone it stands in a soft peak and holds the mark of the pestle for a full minute: no clear or coloured liquid creeps out at the base, no oily film separates on top. It should also come to the pass cool, no warmer than about 25 C (77 F); a mortar warm to the back of the hand means friction has driven off the top aroma and the paste will smell flat.

Where it goes wrong Everything went into the mortar together, or the wet ingredients arrived before the hard, dry ones had been reduced to dust, so the moisture cushioned every blow and the hard pieces slid rather than broke. What comes to the pass is a wet mince rather than a paste: hard flecks and fibrous threads through a loose slurry, liquid weeping out of the heap within a minute, colour marbled instead of single, and on the plate it reads as separate raw items sitting next to each other rather than one flavour.

Grilling over live coals 77 recipes

  • The fire was ready before the food was: coals evenly covered in grey ash with no flames licking through and no raw smoke coming off, and a hot zone and a cooler one both available.
  • The grate was hot and clean when the food landed, and the food released cleanly from it when the time came to turn rather than tearing away.
  • Marks are browned, not burnt: deep colour where the metal touched, with no carbon flaking off and no soot transferred onto the food.
  • The interior reaches its target with a thin gradient, not charred outside and cold within, and not driven through by a flare-up.
  • What comes off the fire smells of fire and food only: no raw wood smoke, no fuel, nothing acrid at the back of the nose.

Where it goes wrong It went on over live flames instead of settled coals, so the outside charred bitter in the flare-ups while the middle stayed cold, and everything on the plate tastes of soot.

From the pass

Bicarbonate of soda pushes the meat's pH away from the point where its proteins hold least water, so they swell and grip the stock instead of squeezing it onto the coals. The bounce and the juice are borrowed water. Past roughly 8 g per kilo it tastes of soap, and skipping the overnight rest leaves a metallic edge and slack grey sausages that sag through the bars.
