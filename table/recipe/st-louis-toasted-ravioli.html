Missouri · Starter

St. Louis Toasted Ravioli

  • Intermediate
  • 45 min
  • Serves 4
  • $

umami-deep · nutty-toasted · rich Leads with savory depth stacked from the pantry’s bass notes, toasted, nutty warmth throughout, on a rich, rounded finish.

Ingredients

  • 24 meat or cheese ravioli, fresh or thawed
  • 100g flour
  • 2 eggs beaten with 2 tbsp milk
  • 200g Italian breadcrumbs
  • 50g Parmesan
  • 1 tsp dried oregano
  • Oil for frying
  • Marinara and grated Parmesan to serve

Equipment

Deep-fry pot · Grater / zester

The skills inside

The coat before the fry: dredge, crumb, batter

Allergen screen

Found: gluten · dairy · egg

Screened for: gluten, dairy, egg, nuts, peanuts, fish, shellfish, molluscs, soy, sesame, celery, mustard, lupin, alcohol. Not screened: sulphites.

Method

  1. Set up flour, egg wash, and the breadcrumbs mixed with Parmesan and oregano.
  2. Dredge each ravioli in flour, then egg, then the breadcrumb mixture, pressing it on.
  3. Heat the oil to 180C.
  4. Fry the ravioli in batches 2-3 min until deep gold and crisp. 3 min
  5. Drain, dust with more Parmesan, and serve hot with warm marinara for dipping.

How to tell it is going right

This dish has no standard of its own yet. It is judged on the technique it exercises: checked at the pan, while there is still something to be done about it.

The coat before the fry: dredge, crumb, batter 61 recipes

  • The coat is continuous before it goes anywhere near the fat: held up to the light, no bare patch of food shows through anywhere, seams and thin edges included.
  • It is bonded rather than resting on top: a gentle shake sheds loose dust only, and the coat does not slide off as a sheet when the piece is handled.
  • It bubbles hard and steadily from the moment it enters the fat, and the bubbling visibly slows as it finishes; those bubbles are the surface water leaving, and a quiet entry means the fat is too cool.
  • Out and drained, it is an even gold-brown all over and it is still crisp several minutes later on a rack; a coat that softens within a minute of coming out never lost its water.
  • Broken open, there is no paste of raw flour under the crust and no hollow gap where the coat has lifted away from the food.

Where it goes wrong The fat was below temperature or the pan was crowded, so the coat drank fat instead of sealing against it: it comes out pale and heavy, greasy to the fingers, and slumps off the food as it cools.

From the pass

A St. Louis Hill neighborhood accident, said to be born when a cook dropped ravioli in oil instead of water. Toasted here means fried: pat the ravioli dry first so the coating grips, and serve them the instant they drain.
