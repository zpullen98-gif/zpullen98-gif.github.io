Provence & the Midi · Sauce

Tapenade Noire au Mortier

  • Easy
  • 15 min
  • Serves 4
  • $$

briny · umami-deep · bright · aromatic Leads with a briny, maritime salinity, savory depth stacked from the pantry’s bass notes, opening aromatic and staying there.

Ingredients

  • 250 g black Tanche olives from Nyons, stoned
  • 60 g capers packed in salt
  • 6 salted anchovy fillets
  • 1 small garlic clove
  • 1 tsp thyme leaves
  • 100 ml olive oil
  • Juice of half a lemon
  • 1 tbsp marc or brandy (optional)
  • Black pepper

Allergen screen

Found: fish · alcohol

Screened for: gluten, dairy, egg, nuts, peanuts, fish, shellfish, molluscs, soy, sesame, celery, mustard, lupin, alcohol. Not screened: sulphites.

Method

  1. Rinse the capers and anchovies, soak them ten minutes and taste one of each; if either is still fierce, soak and taste again before anything else happens.
  2. Pound the garlic and thyme first, then the anchovies, then the capers, then the olives, adding each only once the one before has broken down.
  3. Work in the oil a spoonful at a time so it is absorbed rather than pooling, then the lemon and the marc, and finish with pepper alone.
  4. Leave it an hour before serving; it should stay rough enough that you can still see the olive skins in it.

How to tell it is going right

This dish has no standard of its own yet. It is judged on the techniques it exercises: checked at the pan, while there is still something to be done about it.

Building an emulsion 39 recipes

  • It visibly thickens as the fat goes in, heavier and more opaque than either part was alone, which is the emulsion announcing itself.
  • At no point is there a pool of free fat sitting ahead of the work: fat went in no faster than it disappeared into the body.
  • Glossy and homogeneous, with no slick floating on top and no visible droplets standing apart in it.
  • It clings: coating what it is put on and holding a line, rather than running off and splitting into a puddle at the plate’s edge.
  • It survives standing: minutes later there is no ring of weeping liquid underneath and no film of fat breaking out on the surface.

Where it goes wrong The fat went in faster than the shear could break it up, or the temperature drifted out of the range the method needs: it breaks into grease over a thin liquid, and whisking the broken thing harder does nothing. The rescue is a fresh base, with the broken sauce worked into it slowly, as if it were the fat.

Mashing & puréeing 106 recipes

  • Smooth to the degree the dish asks for, judged on the tongue rather than by eye: no lumps, no fibres, no grit, unless texture is the point.
  • No gluey elasticity: a spoonful drops cleanly off the spoon rather than stretching in a rope behind it.
  • The consistency holds: it mounds on a spoon and stays there, neither slumping flat nor standing so stiff that a spoon has to cut it.
  • Nothing separates on the plate: no water or fat weeping out into a ring within a minute of serving.
  • The colour is even throughout, with no streaks of unincorporated fat, liquid, or seasoning at the edges of the bowl.

Where it goes wrong A starchy ingredient met a high-speed blade, so the cells burst and released their starch: what should be light comes out as an elastic glue that lifts off the spoon in one mass.

From the pass

The name comes from tapeno, the caper, so capers are half the flavour and not a garnish. They and the anchovies both arrive packed in salt, and the only way to know how much salt is already in the bowl is to rinse and taste before pounding; add none of your own. Pound in order of hardness so each thing breaks down, and skip the blender, which whips the lot into a flat, greasy black emulsion.
