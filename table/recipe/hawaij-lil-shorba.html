Yemeni & the Gulf · Sauce

Hawaij lil Shorba

  • Easy
  • 10 min
  • Serves 4
  • $
  • Vegetarian
  • Vegan

earthy · warm-spiced · gently hot · aromatic Leads with an earthy, grounding base, warm spice humming through the middle, opening aromatic and staying there.

Ingredients

  • 40 g cumin seed
  • 25 g black peppercorns
  • 15 g coriander seed
  • 10 g green cardamom pods, husks discarded
  • 4 cloves
  • 8 g ground turmeric

The skills inside

Dry-pan toasting: spices, seeds and nuts

Allergen screen

None found among the 14 screened.

Screened for: gluten, dairy, egg, nuts, peanuts, fish, shellfish, molluscs, soy, sesame, celery, mustard, lupin, alcohol. Not screened: sulphites.

Method

  1. Dry toast the cumin, coriander, peppercorns and cloves in an unoiled pan over medium heat, shaking constantly, until the cumin darkens one shade and the smell turns nutty rather than green, 2 to 3 minutes. 3 min
  2. Tip them straight onto a cold plate; left sitting in the hot pan they carry on cooking and go acrid.
  3. Crack the cardamom pods, discard the husks, and grind the seeds with the cooled spices to a fine powder, sieving and regrinding the tailings so no pepper grit survives.
  4. Stir the turmeric through the cold ground spice and store airtight, in the dark.

How to tell it is going right

This dish has no standard of its own yet. It is judged on the technique it exercises: checked at the pan, while there is still something to be done about it.

Dry-pan toasting: spices, seeds and nuts 63 recipes

  • A single layer on dry metal with nothing added: no fat, and nothing else sharing the pan or the tray and competing for the heat.
  • It is kept moving: shaken, stirred, or turned. Anything left still scorches on the face touching the metal while the rest of it is barely warm.
  • It is judged by smell before sight: once it is fragrant there are seconds left, not minutes, and the nose reaches that point well before the colour does.
  • It leaves the pan the instant it is done, onto a cold surface. A hot pan keeps toasting whatever stays in it, and carryover here is the difference between done and ruined.
  • The colour is even and light rather than patchy, with nothing black at the edges: one scorched piece taints the whole quantity it is ground into.

Where it goes wrong It was left alone for a moment while something else was attended to. Dry heat has no margin: the pieces against the metal go from fragrant to acrid inside a minute, and the bitterness cannot be picked back out of a ground mixture.

From the pass

Two blends share this name and they are not interchangeable. This one, for soup and rice, is built on cumin, black pepper and coriander with turmeric for colour; hawaij for coffee is ginger, cardamom, cinnamon and clove, with no cumin and no turmeric at all. Turmeric is a dried rhizome, not a seed: in the toasting pan it scorches to dust and bitterness long before the cumin is ready, so it goes in cold, after grinding.
