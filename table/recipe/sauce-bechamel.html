Sauce Béchamel

  • Easy
  • 20 min
  • $$ roughly 2 of 4 for cost
  • Vegetarian

warm-spiced · rich Leads with warm spice humming through the middle, richness that coats and comforts.

Ingredients

  • 50g butter + 50g flour: equal weights, the white roux
  • 600ml whole milk, warm
  • 1 bay leaf, ½ onion studded with 2 cloves (the onion piqué, old school, still correct)
  • Nutmeg, a whisper; salt, white pepper
  • This is Mother #1: lasagne, moussaka, croque monsieur, gratins all call her

Method

  1. Melt butter; flour in; cook the roux 2 min, foaming, NOT coloring: white sauce, white roux. 2 min
  2. Warm milk in thirds, whisking each addition smooth before the next: lumps are just dry pockets ambushed by liquid.
  3. Onion piqué and bay in; simmer gently 8–10 min, stirring the corners: flour needs time to lose its rawness. 10 min
  4. Season with nutmeg, salt, white pepper; strain. Add gruyère and it becomes Mornay; press film to the surface if holding.

How to tell it is going right

This dish has no standard of its own yet. It is judged on the technique it exercises: checked at the pan, while there is still something to be done about it.

Making a roux 32 recipes

  • Taken to the stage the dish named and no further, judged against that colour (pale, blond, or brown to brick), and it got there slowly enough to have coloured evenly.
  • It is smooth and mobile: a paste with no dry pockets of flour and no lumps, loosening as it warms rather than sitting stiff in the pan.
  • The raw-flour smell has gone and been replaced by the toasted, biscuity smell of its stage, with nothing acrid behind it.
  • No black specks anywhere. One scorched fleck condemns the batch; it cannot be stirred back in and it will be tasted in the finished sauce.
  • Let down into liquid it goes smooth without lumps and thickens to the body the dish wants, leaving no floury taste on the tongue.

Where it goes wrong The heat was too high and the corners of the pan went unstirred, so it scorched at the edge; black specks run through the finished sauce and carry a bitterness that no amount of seasoning covers.

From the pass

The whole roux family obeys one law: cold-into-hot or hot-into-cold, never hot-into-hot: warm milk into warm roux, whisked in stages, cannot lump. And the 10-minute simmer is not optional: béchamel that tastes of flour was rushed, not ruined.
