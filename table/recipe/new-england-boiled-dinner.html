New England Boiled Dinner

  • Easy
  • 3 h
  • $$$ roughly 3 of 4 for cost

earthy · gently hot · rich · floral Leads with an earthy, grounding base, a warm current of chile, leaving a faint perfume.

Ingredients

  • 1.5kg corned beef brisket
  • 1 cabbage, cut in wedges
  • 6 carrots, in large pieces
  • 6 potatoes, halved
  • 4 turnips, quartered
  • 6 beets, cooked separately
  • 2 bay leaves
  • 1 tbsp peppercorns
  • Butter, salt, and pepper

Method

  1. Cover the corned beef with cold water, add bay and peppercorns, and simmer very gently 2.5 hours until a fork slides in easily. Never let it boil hard. 300 min
  2. Cook the beets separately so they do not stain the pot.
  3. Add potatoes, carrots, and turnips to the beef broth and cook 20 min. 20 min
  4. Add the cabbage wedges for the last 10 min. 10 min
  5. Slice the beef across the grain and serve on a platter with the vegetables around it, butter and pepper on the table.

How to tell it is going right

This dish has no standard of its own yet. It is judged on the techniques it exercises: checked at the pan, while there is still something to be done about it.

Resting meat & slicing against the grain 56 recipes

  • It came off the heat BEFORE the target temperature, measured with a probe rather than by time, because carryover keeps driving the inside up after it is out.
  • The probe went into the coldest likely part rather than the easiest one to reach: the reading that matters is the lowest one, not the first one.
  • Rested for a time matched to its size, somewhere warm, and not cut into partway through to see how it is doing.
  • The board is nearly dry when it is finally cut. A spreading pool means it was cut too early, and that liquid should have stayed inside.
  • The grain was found and the knife went ACROSS it, so the fibres are cut short, and the blade was sharp enough that the slices hold together rather than tearing.

Where it goes wrong It was cut straight off the heat, usually because everything else was ready. The muscle is still contracted and the juice still driven to the centre, so it runs out onto the board; the meat is drier than it ever needed to be, and the flavour that left it is sitting in a puddle under the knife.

Salted water & the float test 188 recipes

  • The water was salted before anything went in, and tasting it confirms it: seasoned on the tongue, with the salt dissolved rather than sitting on the pot floor.
  • There is enough water, hot enough, that the pot returns to its boil quickly after the food goes in and never stops moving.
  • Where the dish uses the float as its signal, the signal is obeyed: the pieces rise and they come out then, not several minutes later once something else was finished.
  • Nothing is stuck to the bottom of the pot or welded to itself, and the water has not thickened into a cloudy sludge.
  • Drained on a bite rather than on the clock: resistance at the centre, with no chalky raw core.

Where it goes wrong The pot was too small and the water unsalted, so it never recovered its boil: the food sat in slack water, went gummy at the surface, and comes out bland in a way that no sauce afterwards can fix.

From the pass

Cook the beets apart or the whole dinner turns pink: they bleed into everything they simmer with, and a rose-tinted corned beef alarms even the tolerant. Next-day leftovers get chopped and fried into red flannel hash, which is the point of making too much; the hash is arguably the better meal and the beets finally get to dye everything on purpose. Add the vegetables in stages by density. This is Yankee thrift cooking that fed the region for two centuries and apologizes to no one.
