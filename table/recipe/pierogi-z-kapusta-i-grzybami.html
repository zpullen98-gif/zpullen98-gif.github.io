Pierogi z Kapustą i Grzybami

  • Intermediate
  • 65 min
  • Serves 4
  • $$
  • Vegetarian

rich · earthy · tangy-ferment · umami-deep Leads with richness that coats and comforts, an earthy, grounding base, finishing long and savory.

Ingredients

  • 300g plain flour
  • 1 egg (50g)
  • 140g warm water
  • 30g soured cream, for the dough
  • 6g salt
  • 40g dried porcini
  • 400g sauerkraut, drained
  • 200g onion, finely diced
  • 80g butter, for the filling
  • 2g coarse black pepper
  • 1 bay leaf
  • 50g butter, to fry
  • 100g soured cream, to serve

Allergen screen

Found: gluten · dairy · egg

Screened for: gluten, dairy, egg, nuts, peanuts, fish, shellfish, molluscs, soy, sesame, celery, mustard, lupin, alcohol. Not screened: sulphites.

Method

  1. Cover the porcini with 200g boiling water and leave 20 minutes. Lift them out, chop them, and keep the strained liquor. 20 min
  2. Squeeze the sauerkraut dry and chop it. Sweat the onion in the 80g butter until golden, add kraut, mushrooms, bay, pepper and the liquor, and cook down 25 minutes until a spoon drawn across the pan leaves a dry track. Cool completely. 25 min
  3. Rub flour, salt, egg, soured cream and warm water into a soft dough, knead 5 minutes, and rest 30 minutes under a bowl. Unrested it shrinks straight back off the pin. 5 min
  4. Roll 2mm thin and cut 8cm discs. Wipe any floury rim with a wet finger, load 15g of cold filling, and crimp firmly: flour is a release agent, not a glue.
  5. Simmer in well salted water at a lazy bubble, never a hard boil, for 90 seconds after they rise. Drain, then fry in the 50g butter until blistered brown. Serve with soured cream.

How to tell it is going right

This dish has no standard of its own yet. It is judged on the techniques it exercises: checked at the pan, while there is still something to be done about it.

Pie crust & blind baking 22 recipes

  • The shell goes into the oven cold and the oven is fully up to heat, so the structure sets before the fat has time to melt out of it.
  • Lined and weighted right into the corners rather than heaped in the middle, so the sides are held against the tin as the fat softens.
  • Docked where it is not meant to puff, and the base stays flat rather than lifting into a dome under its own steam.
  • The weights come out and the base gets dry heat of its own before anything wet arrives. Pale and damp is not baked, and a filling will not finish it.
  • The edges are set and coloured and the shell releases from the tin rather than gripping it.

Where it goes wrong It went in warm, or the weights only filled the middle. The sides slump down the tin before the pastry sets, so the case is shallower than it should be, the filling overflows it, and the base is still raw underneath: the soggy bottom, arrived at three ways at once.

Keeping the cooking liquid 36 recipes

  • It was actually kept: the liquid went into a container rather than down the drain, and the volume saved is enough to be worth using.
  • It is clean: strained of solids and grit, and not clouded with the debris of whatever broke up in it.
  • It tastes of what it cooked and tastes concentrated rather than washed out, and it is not so salty or so bitter that the only honest use is the bin.
  • It was cooled quickly and stored cold, not left standing warm on the stove to be dealt with later.
  • Used, it does the job the dish gives it (carrying flavour into a sauce, loosening the dish, or setting when chilled) rather than merely adding water back.

Where it goes wrong The pot was left to cool slowly on the stove and only strained the next day, so it soured overnight: what should have been the best ingredient in the dish goes in the bin, and the recipe gets water instead.

From the pass

Two mechanisms decide this one. The dough is warm water and enriched, which relaxes gluten quickly and keeps the sheet extensible; skip the rest and it springs back, so you roll thinner and thinner chasing the shape. Then the filling must be dry and cold, because warm wet kraut steams inside the pierog and forces the seam apart in the pot. And floating is not doneness, it is buoyancy from trapped steam. Give them 90 seconds beyond it.
