Cholar Dal

  • Intermediate
  • 35 min
  • $$ roughly 2 of 4 for cost
  • Vegetarian

warm-spiced · earthy · sweet-edged · rich Leads with warm spice humming through the middle, an earthy, grounding base, on a rich, rounded finish.

Ingredients

  • 250g chana dal (Bengal gram)
  • 1 tsp turmeric
  • 2 bay leaves
  • 1.5 tsp salt
  • 900ml water
  • 40g fresh coconut, cut into 5mm chips
  • 3 tbsp ghee
  • 1 tsp cumin seeds
  • 2 dried red chillies
  • 5cm cassia bark, 4 green cardamom, 4 cloves
  • 1 tsp ginger paste
  • 30g nolen gur or jaggery, chopped
  • 20g raisins
  • 0.5 tsp garam masala

Method

  1. Soak the dal 2 hours, drain, then boil with the turmeric, bay, salt and water, skimming the grey foam, 25 minutes, until a grain crushes between finger and thumb yet is still visibly a separate grain. 120 min
  2. Fry the coconut chips in the ghee until the edges go the colour of pale toast, then lift them out before they darken and turn bitter.
  3. In the same ghee temper the cumin, dried chillies, cassia, cardamom and cloves until the cumin darkens a shade and the cardamom smells sweet rather than green.
  4. Pour the tempering and the ginger into the dal, stir in the jaggery, and simmer 8 minutes until the dal holds a spoon trail for two full seconds. 8 min
  5. Fold in the coconut, raisins and garam masala and rest off the heat 5 minutes. 5 min

How to tell it is going right

This dish has no standard of its own yet. It is judged on the techniques it exercises: checked at the pan, while there is still something to be done about it.

Tempering a custard 59 recipes

  • The hot liquid went into the eggs a little at a time with constant whisking, and only then did everything go back to the pot.
  • The heat under it stayed gentle throughout and it was never allowed to boil.
  • Stirred continuously and right into the corners of the pan, where it thickens first and sets unnoticed.
  • Judged by behaviour: it thickens enough to coat the back of a spoon and hold a clean line when a finger is drawn through it.
  • Taken off before it looks finished, and strained. Residual heat carries it the rest of the way, and a custard held on the heat until it looks right arrives past right.

Where it goes wrong The hot liquid went in too fast, or the pot went back over full heat to save time. The yolks set into threads before they could thicken smoothly, and a scrambled custard does not come back; straining hides a little of it and nothing hides the rest.

Skimming: foam, fat and a clear broth 73 recipes

  • It is held below a rolling boil for the whole time. Agitation drives fat and fine protein into the liquid as an emulsion, and nothing skimmed afterwards will take them back out.
  • The grey foam that rises in the first minutes is lifted off and discarded rather than stirred back in.
  • Fat is taken from the surface where it gathers, the pot moved off the centre of the heat if that is what it takes to collect it in one place.
  • The liquid is visibly clearer at the end than it was at the start: it is possible to see down into it.
  • Nothing lifted off goes back in. What was skimmed is discarded, not tipped back once the ladle is full.

Where it goes wrong The pot was allowed to boil, usually while something else was being attended to. Fat and protein emulsify into the liquid under agitation and cannot be lifted off afterwards, so the finished broth stays cloudy and greasy however patiently it is skimmed from then on.

From the pass

This is the dal you eat with luchi, so it has to stay grain-distinct: boiled to the point where a grain crushes on the palate but has not surrendered its skin. The trap is the gur. Sugar competes with the starch for water and firms the seed coat, so dal cooked sweet from the start never softens further, however long it sits. Soften and salt first, sweeten at the end.
