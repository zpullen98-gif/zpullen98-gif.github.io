Cold-Smoked Salmon

  • Advanced
  • 45 min
  • Serves 4
  • $$

smoky · gently hot · rich Leads with wood-smoke running underneath, a warm current of chile, on a rich, rounded finish.

Ingredients

  • 1.4 kg salmon side, skin on, pin bones pulled, held at -20C (-4F) for 7 days first
  • 42 g coarse sea salt (3% of the fish weight)
  • 28 g demerara sugar (2%)
  • 5 g black peppercorns, cracked
  • 2 large handfuls oak or alder smoking dust

Equipment

Sheet pan

Allergen screen

Found: fish

Screened for: gluten, dairy, egg, nuts, peanuts, fish, shellfish, molluscs, soy, sesame, celery, mustard, lupin, alcohol. Not screened: sulphites.

Method

  1. Mix the salt, sugar and pepper, rub it over the flesh side, and cure skin down in a tray at 4C (39F): 24 hours for a thin side, 36 for a thick one. 600 min
  2. Rinse briefly, pat dry, then leave uncovered on a rack in the fridge for 12 hours until the surface turns tacky and faintly glossy and you can tap it without wetting a finger. 600 min
  3. Cold smoke below 30C (86F) for about 6 hours, in two sessions if the box warms up, until the flesh is the colour of old brass. 360 min
  4. Wrap and rest in the fridge for 48 hours so the smoke moves inward and stops smelling like a bonfire. 600 min
  5. Slice long and thin off the skin with the knife held almost flat.

From the pass

Smoke sticks to a pellicle, the dry tacky protein film that forms in cold moving air. Smoke a wet fillet and the phenols dissolve in surface water and run off, leaving patchy colour and a sooty edge. Hold the box under 30C (86F): warmer and the proteins set and you have hot smoked fish instead. Lox is the other branch of the family, a salt brined belly cut, never smoked at all.
