Iowa Snickers Salad

  • Easy
  • 20 min
  • Serves 4
  • $$
  • Vegetarian

rich · sweet-edged · floral Leads with richness that coats and comforts, a sweet edge rounding the corners, leaving a faint perfume.

Ingredients

  • 4 tart apples, diced
  • 4 Snickers bars, chopped
  • 250ml whipped cream or whipped topping
  • 100g vanilla pudding, prepared
  • 1 tbsp caramel sauce

Allergen screen

Found: dairy

Screened for: gluten, dairy, egg, nuts, peanuts, fish, shellfish, molluscs, soy, sesame, celery, mustard, lupin, alcohol. Not screened: sulphites.

Method

  1. Dice the apples and chop the Snickers bars into bite-sized pieces.
  2. Fold the prepared vanilla pudding into the whipped cream.
  3. Gently fold in the apples and chopped candy bars.
  4. Drizzle with caramel and fold once.
  5. Chill 30 min and serve: assemble close to serving so the apples stay crisp and the candy does not dissolve. 30 min

How to tell it is going right

This dish has no standard of its own yet. It is judged on the techniques it exercises: checked at the pan, while there is still something to be done about it.

Chilling to firm: the fridge as binder 45 recipes

  • It went cold before it was handled any further, and it went in spread flat and covered rather than as a warm mass that insulates its own centre.
  • It is cold through rather than cold at the edge: tested at the middle it is as firm as it is at the rim.
  • It holds its shape unsupported; it can be cut, portioned, rolled or lifted without slumping, tearing, or sticking to the hands.
  • The fat in it has gone solid: it feels firm and waxy to the fingers rather than greasy and yielding.
  • It survives the step it was chilled for, going into heat or a coating or a mould still holding its form rather than collapsing the moment it is touched.

Where it goes wrong It was given twenty minutes instead of the hours it needed, so it is firm at the rim and soft at the core: it slumps out of shape as it is portioned, and everything made from it spreads or breaks apart in the pan.

Sugar stages & caramel 46 recipes

  • Nothing is stirred once it is boiling, and any crystals clinging to the side of the pan are brushed down rather than left to fall in.
  • Judged by temperature or by a tested stage rather than by time: consecutive stages are a few degrees apart and look identical in the pan.
  • Colour is read off the heat and in good light, remembering that sugar keeps darkening after the flame is out.
  • Stopped decisively at the point wanted: off the heat and into the next liquid or onto a cold surface, rather than left to coast in a hot pan.
  • The result is clear and smooth rather than grainy, cloudy, or seized into a solid mass.

Where it goes wrong It was stirred after coming to the boil, or a stray crystal from the side of the pan fell back in. One crystal seeds the whole pan and the syrup turns grainy within seconds, and once sugar has crystallised the only cure is more water and starting the whole thing again.

From the pass

A tongue-in-cheek Midwestern 'salad' that appears at every Iowa gathering. Assemble it near serving time; make it too early and the apples weep and the candy bars go soft, turning a crisp dish into pudding.
