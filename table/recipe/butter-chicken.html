Butter Chicken

  • Intermediate
  • 60 min
  • Serves 4
  • $$

rich · aromatic · gently hot · warm-spiced Leads with richness that coats and comforts, aromatics blooming from the first bite, fading in warm spice.

Ingredients

  • 600g chicken thighs, in yogurt + ginger-garlic + chili powder 1h
  • 400g tomato purée
  • 80g butter, 100ml cream
  • 1 tbsp garam masala, 1 tsp each kasuri methi, sugar
  • Ginger, garlic, green chili

The skills inside

Charring over open flameThe broiler: heat from aboveMarinating: acid, salt and timeMashing & puréeing

Allergen screen

Screened for: gluten, dairy, egg, nuts, peanuts, fish, shellfish, molluscs, soy, sesame, celery, mustard, lupin, alcohol. Not screened: sulphites.

Method

  1. Char the marinated chicken hard under a broiler or in a dry pan; set aside.
  2. Simmer tomatoes with aromatics 20 min; blend silky; strain if you're fancy. 20 min
  3. Mount with butter and cream; season with garam masala and crushed kasuri methi.
  4. Fold in chicken and its juices; simmer 5 min. 5 min

How to tell it is going right

This dish has no standard of its own yet. It is judged on the techniques it exercises: checked at the pan, while there is still something to be done about it.

The broiler: heat from above 22 recipes

  • The element is fully lit and the compartment is hot before anything goes under it. A broiler switched on with the food already in place browns nothing for several minutes and then burns it in one.
  • The distance was set before the dish went in, not adjusted afterwards with a hot dish in one hand: how close it sits is the only heat control this method has.
  • Somebody stayed with it for the whole time. The window between pale and bitter is tens of seconds, and nothing under a broiler is ever safe to walk away from.
  • The surface has coloured evenly right across, having been turned at least once; every grill has a hot corner and none of them say where it is.
  • What is underneath is no further cooked than when it went in. Top heat is there to finish a surface, and anything that came out hotter all the way through was reheated rather than glazed.

Where it goes wrong It was treated as an oven setting: food in, timer set, cook walks away. Radiant heat from above has no gentle phase, so the surface goes from pale to bitter inside a minute while the inside is untouched, and there is no stage in between to catch.

Charring over open flame 26 recipes

  • The surface met the fiercest heat available (flame, a broiler, or bare hot metal) with nothing between them, and it shows as true blistering and blackening rather than gentle, even browning.
  • The char matches the surface’s fate: where it will be eaten, black sits in patches over deep colour with every face having had its turn, and where the skin is to be peeled away, it may go to an even black, because the char there works on the flesh below and then leaves with the skin.
  • It smells of smoke and toasted surface with no acrid edge that stings the nose: acrid means it went past, and acrid does not cook out.
  • Just under the char it is cooked (softened, steaming, collapsed, or puffed, whichever the dish wants) because the flame did the surface and the trapped heat did the rest.
  • A taste carries the smoke without mouthfuls of ash: the black that flakes away is a seasoning, not a layer.

Where it goes wrong It was left on one face too long or held too close, so one side carbonised to ash while the rest stayed raw, and the smell turned acrid, which follows the dish through every later step and cannot be seasoned away.

From the pass

Kasuri methi (dried fenugreek leaf, crushed between your palms into the finished sauce) is the flavor people can't name but always miss; without it this is just chicken in tomato cream. The char matters equally: murgh makhani was invented at Delhi's Moti Mahal in the 1950s as a way to revive day-old tandoori chicken in butter and tomato, so the smoky edges aren't garnish, they're the dish's origin story. Balance the tomatoes' acidity with sugar until the sauce stops arguing.
