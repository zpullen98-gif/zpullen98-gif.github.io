South Dakota Walleye and Chips

  • Intermediate
  • 50 min
  • $$ roughly 2 of 4 for cost

bright Leads with acid holding everything up to the light.

Ingredients

  • 800g walleye fillets
  • 250g flour
  • 1 tsp baking powder
  • 1 tsp salt
  • 330ml cold beer
  • Extra flour for dusting
  • 1kg potatoes, cut in chips
  • Oil for frying
  • Tartar sauce and lemon

Deep-fry pot

Method

  1. Fry the chipped potatoes at 150C until soft, rest, then fry again at 190C until crisp; salt at once.
  2. Pat the walleye dry and dust with flour.
  3. Whisk the flour, baking powder, salt, and cold beer into a batter.
  4. Dip the fish and fry at 190C for 3-4 min until deep gold and the fish flakes. 4 min
  5. Serve with the chips, tartar, and lemon.

How to tell it is going right

This dish has no standard of its own yet. It is judged on the techniques it exercises: checked at the pan, while there is still something to be done about it.

The coat before the fry: dredge, crumb, batter 80 recipes

  • The coat is continuous before it goes anywhere near the fat: held up to the light, no bare patch of food shows through anywhere, seams and thin edges included.
  • It is bonded rather than resting on top: a gentle shake sheds loose dust only, and the coat does not slide off as a sheet when the piece is handled.
  • It bubbles hard and steadily from the moment it enters the fat, and the bubbling visibly slows as it finishes; those bubbles are the surface water leaving, and a quiet entry means the fat is too cool.
  • Out and drained, it is an even gold-brown all over and it is still crisp several minutes later on a rack; a coat that softens within a minute of coming out never lost its water.
  • Broken open, there is no paste of raw flour under the crust and no hollow gap where the coat has lifted away from the food.

Where it goes wrong The fat was below temperature or the pan was crowded, so the coat drank fat instead of sealing against it: it comes out pale and heavy, greasy to the fingers, and slumps off the food as it cools.

Deep frying 96 recipes

  • The oil was at temperature before anything went in, measured rather than guessed, and the vessel is no more than two-thirds full.
  • Everything went in dry. Surfaces are patted down and any coating is not dripping, because water hitting hot oil erupts and is the one accident this technique actually has.
  • The oil recovers between batches. It is back at temperature before the next load goes in rather than falling further with each one.
  • A steady vigorous bubbling that slows as it finishes: the bubbles are the food’s own water leaving, so a piece that has gone quiet has stopped drying out and is done.
  • Seasoned or finished in the first seconds out of the oil, while the surface is still hot and will hold what lands on it. Anything applied to a cooled crust sits on top and falls off.

Where it goes wrong Too much went in at once and the oil temperature collapsed. The crust forms slowly while the food sits in warm fat absorbing it rather than shedding steam, and it comes out heavy and greasy instead of crisp, which no amount of draining afterwards will fix.

From the pass

Walleye from the Missouri River reservoirs is South Dakota's prize fish. Mix the beer batter at the last moment so the carbonation lifts it crisp, and do not overcook the lean walleye: it flakes dry in seconds past done.
