Feijoada

  • Advanced
  • 4 h
  • $ roughly 1 of 4 for cost

smoky · rich · warm-spiced · aromatic Leads with wood-smoke running underneath, richness that coats and comforts, opening aromatic and staying there.

Ingredients

  • 500g black beans, soaked overnight
  • 600g mixed pork: shoulder, ribs, smoked sausage, bacon (traditionally ears and trotters too)
  • 2 onions, 6 garlic cloves, 3 bay leaves
  • Orange slices, white rice, collard ribbons, farofa
  • Hot sauce

Method

  1. Simmer beans with bay; separately brown the meats in stages.
  2. Unite everything; simmer 2–3 h, lazy bubbles, until the broth turns black velvet. 180 min
  3. Mash a ladle of beans against the pot wall to thicken.
  4. Serve with rice, quick-fried collards, farofa and orange slices: the orange is digestive strategy, not decoration.

How to tell it is going right

This dish has no standard of its own yet. It is judged on the techniques it exercises: checked at the pan, while there is still something to be done about it.

Soaking dried goods back to life 69 recipes

  • Fully submerged with room to swell: the level is well above everything at the start and still above it at the end.
  • Given the full time, and judged by what is in the bowl rather than by the hours on the clock.
  • Tested at the CENTRE of the largest piece, cut or pressed. Anything with a hard dry core will still have one after cooking, because the outside softens first and stops the water getting further in.
  • The soaking water was dealt with on purpose: kept because it holds flavour, or thrown away because it holds what should not be eaten, but never simply left undecided.
  • Drained before it is used, so the dish does not silently gain all the water that came with it.

Where it goes wrong Too little water, or too little time. The pieces swell until they are packed tight against each other and the ones in the middle never fully take up water; they will not soften later in the dish either, so the finished plate has hard pieces scattered through it.

Mashing & puréeing 114 recipes

  • Smooth to the degree the dish asks for, judged on the tongue rather than by eye: no lumps, no fibres, no grit, unless texture is the point.
  • No gluey elasticity: a spoonful drops cleanly off the spoon rather than stretching in a rope behind it.
  • The consistency holds: it mounds on a spoon and stays there, neither slumping flat nor standing so stiff that a spoon has to cut it.
  • Nothing separates on the plate: no water or fat weeping out into a ring within a minute of serving.
  • The colour is even throughout, with no streaks of unincorporated fat, liquid, or seasoning at the edges of the bowl.

Where it goes wrong A starchy ingredient met a high-speed blade, so the cells burst and released their starch: what should be light comes out as an elastic glue that lifts off the spoon in one mass.

From the pass

Brazil's Saturday ritual: feijoada is eaten at noon because nobody is expected to function afterward, and the full apparatus (rice, collards, farofa, orange) is the dish; the pot alone is only half the ceremony. The orange cuts the richness and the old wisdom says it helps digest the pork. Trust two centuries of precedent. Build it on the cheap smoked and salted cuts; the black beans want their fat and smoke. The crackling farofa on top is texture insurance no serious table skips.
