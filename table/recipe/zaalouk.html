Moroccan · Starter

Zaalouk

  • Easy
  • 40 min
  • $ roughly 1 of 4 for cost
  • Vegetarian
  • Vegan

bright · herb-fresh · briny · aromatic Leads with acid holding everything up to the light, fresh herbs arguing happily on top, opening aromatic and staying there.

Ingredients

  • 2 eggplants, charred whole or cubed and steamed
  • 4 tomatoes, grated
  • 4 garlic cloves
  • 1 tsp each cumin and paprika
  • Olive oil, lemon, cilantro

Equipment

Grater / zester

Method

  1. Cook garlic in olive oil; add tomatoes and spices; reduce to a jam.
  2. Mash in the eggplant flesh; keep cooking until thick enough to stand a spoon.
  3. Finish with lemon and cilantro.
  4. Warm or room temp, with bread, as part of a salad spread.

How to tell it is going right

This dish has no standard of its own yet. It is judged on the techniques it exercises: checked at the pan, while there is still something to be done about it.

Charring over open flame 31 recipes

  • The surface met the fiercest heat available (flame, a broiler, or bare hot metal) with nothing between them, and it shows as true blistering and blackening rather than gentle, even browning.
  • The char matches the surface’s fate: where it will be eaten, black sits in patches over deep colour with every face having had its turn, and where the skin is to be peeled away, it may go to an even black, because the char there works on the flesh below and then leaves with the skin.
  • It smells of smoke and toasted surface with no acrid edge that stings the nose: acrid means it went past, and acrid does not cook out.
  • Just under the char it is cooked (softened, steaming, collapsed, or puffed, whichever the dish wants) because the flame did the surface and the trapped heat did the rest.
  • A taste carries the smoke without mouthfuls of ash: the black that flakes away is a seasoning, not a layer.

Where it goes wrong It was left on one face too long or held too close, so one side carbonised to ash while the rest stayed raw, and the smell turned acrid, which follows the dish through every later step and cannot be seasoned away.

Mashing & puréeing 114 recipes

  • Smooth to the degree the dish asks for, judged on the tongue rather than by eye: no lumps, no fibres, no grit, unless texture is the point.
  • No gluey elasticity: a spoonful drops cleanly off the spoon rather than stretching in a rope behind it.
  • The consistency holds: it mounds on a spoon and stays there, neither slumping flat nor standing so stiff that a spoon has to cut it.
  • Nothing separates on the plate: no water or fat weeping out into a ring within a minute of serving.
  • The colour is even throughout, with no streaks of unincorporated fat, liquid, or seasoning at the edges of the bowl.

Where it goes wrong A starchy ingredient met a high-speed blade, so the cells burst and released their starch: what should be light comes out as an elastic glue that lifts off the spoon in one mass.

From the pass

Moroccan 'salads' are mostly cooked, served warm or room-temperature in a mosaic of small plates, and zaalouk anchors the table, eggplant and tomato reduced to a smoky jam thick enough to stand a spoon. The reduction is the recipe: keep cooking past 'sauce' to 'spread', because watery zaalouk is just ratatouille with doubts. Char the eggplants whole if you can; the smoke earns its place. Bread is the only utensil the dish recognizes.
