Ceviche Clásico

  • Intermediate
  • 30 min
  • $ roughly 1 of 4 for cost

bright · fiery · herb-fresh Leads with acid holding everything up to the light, real fire that builds and stays, lifted green at the very end.

Ingredients

  • 500g impeccably fresh white fish (sole, sea bass), 2cm cubes
  • 10–12 limes, juiced at the last minute
  • 1 red onion, shaved thin, rinsed
  • 1 ají limo or habanero, minced; cilantro
  • Sweet potato and choclo corn to serve; salt

Method

  1. Salt the fish 2 min: it firms the flesh. 2 min
  2. Douse in lime; steep 5–10 minutes only, stirring once. Milky-opaque edges, raw heart. 10 min
  3. Fold in onion, chili, cilantro.
  4. Serve immediately, with the leche de tigre (the milky lime broth) pooling, sweet potato alongside.

How to tell it is going right

This dish has no standard of its own yet. It is judged on the technique it exercises: checked at the pan, while there is still something to be done about it.

  • Take any three pieces from anywhere in the pile: they match closely enough in every dimension that you would have to measure them to argue otherwise.
  • The cut faces are flat and clean rather than ragged or crushed; leaves and herbs are cut through, not bruised, and the board carries no dark smear where they were.
  • The stated size is the actual size, held against a rule or a known reference rather than called about right.
  • Everything in the pile is the shape that was asked for: no wedges, no half pieces, and no offcuts swept in to avoid waste.
  • They cook evenly, which is the entire point: at the end of cooking nothing is still raw and nothing has collapsed.

Where it goes wrong The knife was dull, or the vegetable was never squared off first, so the pieces vary: the small ones are mush by the time the large ones are cooked, and the dish has two textures where it wanted one.

From the pass

Modern Lima cured the old hours-long soak: ceviche is a ten-minute dish: the fish 'cooks' in citric acid only at its surface, staying silk at the center, and the old marathon marinades were making ceviche jerky. The leche de tigre, the milky cure left in the bowl, is drunk as a hangover remedy with a straight face and sold in glasses on its own. Bitter orange was the original acid before limes arrived; the ají and red onion are not garnish but load-bearing. Cold plates, cold fish, no waiting.
