Aloo Posto

  • Easy
  • 25 min
  • Serves 4
  • $$
  • Vegetarian
  • Vegan

earthy · fiery Leads with an earthy, grounding base, real fire that builds and stays.

Ingredients

  • 500g waxy potato, cut in 1.5cm dice
  • 70g white poppy seeds (posto)
  • 5 kancha lonka (green chillies)
  • 60ml mustard oil
  • 0.5 tsp kalojeere (nigella seeds)
  • 0.5 tsp turmeric
  • 1.25 tsp salt
  • 0.5 tsp sugar
  • 150ml water

Method

  1. Soak the posto in hot water for 30 minutes, drain, then grind with 2 green chillies, a pinch of salt and 3 tbsp cold water until the paste ribbons off the spoon and feels smooth, not sandy, between finger and thumb. 30 min
  2. Heat 45ml mustard oil until the raw bite flattens, drop the flame to medium and crackle the kalojeere for 10 seconds.
  3. Add the potato, turmeric and salt and fry over medium heat 8 minutes until the cut faces are gold and the edges have gone translucent. 8 min
  4. Pour in the water, cover, and cook 8 minutes until a cube yields to a spoon but still holds its corners. 8 min
  5. Take the pan off a hard boil, stir the posto paste through with the sugar, and cook gently 4 minutes until it tightens around the potato and oil beads on the surface. It must stay ivory. Finish off the heat with the last 15ml raw mustard oil and the remaining chillies, slit. 4 min

How to tell it is going right

This dish has no standard of its own yet. It is judged on the techniques it exercises: checked at the pan, while there is still something to be done about it.

Salted water & the float test 89 recipes

  • The water was salted before anything went in, and tasting it confirms it: seasoned on the tongue, with the salt dissolved rather than sitting on the pot floor.
  • There is enough water, hot enough, that the pot returns to its boil quickly after the food goes in and never stops moving.
  • Where the dish uses the float as its signal, the signal is obeyed: the pieces rise and they come out then, not several minutes later once something else was finished.
  • Nothing is stuck to the bottom of the pot or welded to itself, and the water has not thickened into a cloudy sludge.
  • Drained on a bite rather than on the clock: resistance at the centre, with no chalky raw core.

Where it goes wrong The pot was too small and the water unsalted, so it never recovered its boil: the food sat in slack water, went gummy at the surface, and comes out bland in a way that no sauce afterwards can fix.

Knife cuts: dice, julienne, bias 89 recipes

  • Take any three pieces from anywhere in the pile: they match closely enough in every dimension that you would have to measure them to argue otherwise.
  • The cut faces are flat and clean rather than ragged or crushed; leaves and herbs are cut through, not bruised, and the board carries no dark smear where they were.
  • The stated size is the actual size, held against a rule or a known reference rather than called about right.
  • Everything in the pile is the shape that was asked for: no wedges, no half pieces, and no offcuts swept in to avoid waste.
  • They cook evenly, which is the entire point: at the end of cooking nothing is still raw and nothing has collapsed.

Where it goes wrong The knife was dull, or the vegetable was never squared off first, so the pieces vary: the small ones are mush by the time the large ones are cooked, and the dish has two textures where it wanted one.

From the pass

Poppy seed is around 45 per cent oil inside a hard shell, so it becomes a paste only if the shell is softened by soaking and then ground wet and cool. Grind it dry or hot and the oil cracks out: what you get is a gritty, greasy sludge that will not bind to anything. In the pan there is a second trap. Past pale ivory the paste scorches, and scorched posto tastes bitter and sandy. Low flame, short cook.
