Filipino · Dessert

Bibingka

  • Intermediate
  • 30 min
  • $$$ roughly 3 of 4 for cost
  • Vegetarian

rich Leads with richness that coats and comforts.

Ingredients

  • 300 g rice flour
  • 8 g baking powder
  • 150 g caster sugar
  • 3 g fine salt
  • 3 eggs (150 g)
  • 300 ml coconut milk
  • 60 g unsalted butter, melted
  • 1 cooked salted duck egg (itlog na maalat), peeled and sliced into rounds
  • 60 g kesong puti or another mild fresh white cheese set with vegetarian rennet, sliced
  • 2 banana leaves
  • 30 g unsalted butter, for brushing
  • 40 g fresh coconut, grated
  • 30 g caster sugar, to serve

Equipment

Grater / zester

The skills inside

Method

  1. Pass the banana leaves over a gas flame until they turn glossy and deep green and go limp, then wipe them and line two 15 cm tins, letting the leaf stand above the rim.
  2. Whisk the rice flour, baking powder, sugar and salt together. Beat the eggs with the coconut milk and melted butter, pour that into the dry mix, whisk smooth, and rest it 10 minutes. 10 min
  3. Fill the lined tins two thirds full and bake at 200C (400F) for 15 minutes, until risen and just set on top. 15 min
  4. Lay on the salted egg and cheese and return them for 10 minutes to 15 minutes, until the top is patched brown and a skewer comes out clean. 10 min
  5. Brush with butter and serve hot, with the grated coconut and sugar alongside.

How to tell it is going right

This dish has no standard of its own yet. It is judged on the technique it exercises: checked at the pan, while there is still something to be done about it.

The wobble & the skewer: testing a bake 135 recipes

  • Tested at the true centre and the thickest point, not at the convenient edge: the edge is done long before the answer is.
  • The skewer comes out as the recipe describes, clean and dry or carrying the stated moist crumb, and it does the same on a second test in a different spot.
  • The wobble is read in motion: nudged, the centre moves as one set mass with a faint tremble, rather than a liquid ripple travelling across the surface.
  • The other signs agree with the test: risen and just shrinking from the sides, the top set and dry to a light touch with no wet gloss on it.
  • Pressed lightly at the middle it springs back and leaves no lasting dent.

Where it goes wrong It was judged on the clock and on how the edges looked, so a centre still liquid went to the rack and sank as it cooled, a dense wet seam under a perfectly browned top.

From the pass

Heat is what makes a banana leaf usable: the flame collapses its rigid cells so it folds into a tin without splitting, and it releases most of the grassy perfume that ends up in the cake. The crumb is rice flour, held by starch and egg alone, which makes it tender and faintly sandy and also means it stales within the day as the starch recrystallises. Hold the egg and cheese back until the batter has set at 15 minutes, or they sink to the bottom.
