Alaska · Dessert

Akutaq

  • Intermediate
  • 60 min
  • Serves 4
  • $$
  • Vegetarian
  • Vegan

clean · simple A quiet dish that lets its main ingredient speak: season well and stand aside.

Ingredients

  • 250g solid vegetable shortening or rendered fat, at room temperature
  • 100g sugar
  • 120ml water, added slowly
  • 400g wild berries (blueberries, salmonberries, crowberries)
  • Pinch of salt

Allergen screen

None found among the 14 screened.

Screened for: gluten, dairy, egg, nuts, peanuts, fish, shellfish, molluscs, soy, sesame, celery, mustard, lupin, alcohol. Not screened: sulphites.

Method

  1. Whip the room-temperature fat with a wooden spoon or mixer until it is white, light, and fluffy; this takes real effort and is the whole technique.
  2. Beat in the sugar gradually.
  3. Add the water a spoonful at a time, whipping constantly, until the mixture is airy and holds soft peaks.
  4. Fold in the berries gently by hand.
  5. Chill until firm and serve cold in small portions.

How to tell it is going right

This dish has no standard of its own yet. It is judged on the techniques it exercises: checked at the pan, while there is still something to be done about it.

Whipping a meringue 17 recipes

  • Bowl and whisk are clean and dry with no trace of fat or yolk. Grease prevents the foam outright, and there is no recovering a bowl that had it partway through.
  • The sugar went in gradually once the whites were already foaming, rather than all at once at the start.
  • No grit: a little rubbed between finger and thumb feels smooth, so the sugar has dissolved into the foam rather than sitting in it.
  • Stiff peaks means the tip stands with a slight bow when the whisk lifts, and the bowl can be tipped without the mass moving in it.
  • Glossy and smooth rather than chalky or dull, with no liquid gathering underneath it in the bowl.

Where it goes wrong It was whipped past stiff peaks. A minute beyond glory the protein network tightens and tears, the foam turns grainy and matte, water weeps out beneath it, and folding that into anything gives a mixture that collapses on the way to the oven.

Chilling to firm: the fridge as binder 25 recipes

  • It went cold before it was handled any further, and it went in spread flat and covered rather than as a warm mass that insulates its own centre.
  • It is cold through rather than cold at the edge: tested at the middle it is as firm as it is at the rim.
  • It holds its shape unsupported; it can be cut, portioned, rolled or lifted without slumping, tearing, or sticking to the hands.
  • The fat in it has gone solid: it feels firm and waxy to the fingers rather than greasy and yielding.
  • It survives the step it was chilled for, going into heat or a coating or a mould still holding its form rather than collapsing the moment it is touched.

Where it goes wrong It was given twenty minutes instead of the hours it needed, so it is firm at the rim and soft at the core: it slumps out of shape as it is portioned, and everything made from it spreads or breaks apart in the pan.

From the pass

Akutaq is a traditional food of the Yup'ik, Inupiat, and other Alaska Native peoples, historically made with rendered seal or caribou fat and berries and carried as high-energy food. Whipping the fat until genuinely light is the skill; rushed, it stays greasy and heavy.
