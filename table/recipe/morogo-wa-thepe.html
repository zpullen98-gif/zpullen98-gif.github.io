Morogo wa Thepe

  • Easy
  • 20 min
  • $ roughly 1 of 4 for cost
  • Vegetarian
  • Vegan

nutty-toasted · fiery Leads with toasted, nutty warmth throughout, real fire that builds and stays.

Ingredients

  • 600 g amaranth leaves, thick stalks stripped out
  • 45 ml sunflower oil
  • 150 g onion, sliced
  • 350 g ripe tomatoes, coarsely grated and skins discarded
  • 1 green chilli, split lengthways
  • 100 ml water
  • 40 g raw peanuts, roasted and coarsely pounded
  • 8 g salt

Grater / zester

Method

  1. Strip the leaves off any woody stalk and wash them through three changes of water; grit sits in the leaf crowns and cooking will not shift it.
  2. Fry the onion in the oil over medium heat until slack and just colouring, then add the chilli and grated tomato and cook until the tomato has gone from bright and watery to dark, thick and beading oil at the edges, about 10 minutes. 10 min
  3. Pack the wet leaves in on top, put the lid on and let them collapse in their own steam, turning once when the top layer has wilted.
  4. Uncover, add the water and simmer until the leaf stalks are tender and the pan liquid has reduced to a dark sauce that clings rather than pools.
  5. Salt now, fold in the pounded peanuts off the heat, and serve against stiff maize pap.

How to tell it is going right

This dish has no standard of its own yet. It is judged on the techniques it exercises: checked at the pan, while there is still something to be done about it.

Sautéing & the shallow fry 80 recipes

  • Everything sits in a single layer with visible space between the pieces: nothing stacked, nothing touching, or the pan is too small for the quantity.
  • The pieces move freely and keep moving, sliding across the pan rather than sticking and tearing when the pan is worked.
  • Light even browning arrived at quickly: colour on most faces, not the deep crust of a sear and not grey.
  • A thin film of fat with the food glossed by it: no pool left in the pan at the end, and nothing greasy on the fingers or the plate.
  • Cooked through but still firm, having spent only a few minutes in the pan: anything that has gone soft has been in there too long.

Where it goes wrong Too much went in at once, the pan cooled, and the food released its water: what was meant to brown in three minutes stews for ten and comes out grey, slack and wet.

Steaming: basket, leaf and lid 130 recipes

  • Steam was running before the food went in: the water at a rolling boil and vapour coming steadily through the basket, rather than the whole thing warming up together.
  • Nothing touches the water at any point: the food sits clear of it throughout, and the level neither drops enough to boil dry nor rises enough to wet it.
  • The lid stayed on. Every lift costs the chamber its heat, and the number of lifts should be as close to none as the dish allows.
  • Lifted out, the surface is glossy and plump with its colour heightened rather than dulled: greens brighter than they went in, not olive.
  • Cooked through with the shape intact: it yields at the centre without having slumped or split, and there is no more than a spoonful of clean condensate beneath it.

Where it goes wrong The lid was loose or dripping and the condensate fell back onto the food, so it arrives waterlogged and pale with its seasoning washed down into the bottom of the dish.

From the pass

Amaranth leaves are almost entirely water, so the tomato base has to be cooked down to a dark paste before they go in; a wet base plus wet leaves gives grey soup you cannot reduce back without overcooking the greens. Salt at the end for the same reason. The peanuts are not garnish: their fat carries the leaf bitterness and their protein thickens the sauce enough to cling to pap. Stop while the leaves are still dark green, khaki means the flavour has gone with the chlorophyll.
