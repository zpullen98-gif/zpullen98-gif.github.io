Italian · Soup

Pasta e Fagioli

  • Easy
  • 45 min
  • Serves 4
  • $$
  • Vegetarian

umami-deep · rich · floral · aromatic Leads with savory depth stacked from the pantry’s bass notes, richness that coats and comforts, opening aromatic and staying there.

Ingredients

  • 2 cans cannellini or borlotti beans
  • 1 onion, 1 carrot, 1 celery, diced
  • 3 garlic cloves
  • 2 tbsp tomato paste
  • 1L stock
  • 150g ditalini
  • Rosemary, Parmigiano rind if you have it

Equipment

Grater / zester

The skills inside

Sweating aromatics: soft, never browned

Allergen screen

Found: gluten · dairy · celery

Screened for: gluten, dairy, egg, nuts, peanuts, fish, shellfish, molluscs, soy, sesame, celery, mustard, lupin, alcohol. Not screened: sulphites.

Method

  1. Sweat vegetables in olive oil; add garlic, rosemary, tomato paste.
  2. Add beans, stock, and the cheese rind; simmer 20 min. 20 min
  3. Blend a third of the soup for body; return and cook pasta directly in it.
  4. Finish with good oil and grated cheese.

How it should come out

  • Ladled, it falls in a slow ribbon and mounds slightly in the bowl rather than spreading flat to the rim.
  • Broth is opaque and velvety; no clear liquid separates out around the beans after a minute standing.
  • Most beans are still whole with their skins intact rather than the pot gone to paste, and the ditalini is still firm at the tooth when the bowl reaches the table: pasta is distinguishable from bean by bite.
  • Surface carries a slick of raw finishing oil, beaded and green-smelling rather than cooked in, with grated cheese that has not yet melted flat.
  • Tasted at the bowl it needs no salt at the table and is not sharp with it: the rind and the finishing cheese are already in the total. Rosemary reads as background perfume, with no loose needles in the spoon.

Where it goes wrong It sat on the stove or was reheated, and the ditalini has drunk the broth: what arrives is a stiff bean porridge with soft grey pasta and no liquid left to call soup.

From the pass

Cooking the pasta directly in the soup is the whole dish: the starch it sheds turns bean broth into velvet, which is why pasta boiled separately leaves you with beans in dishwater. The Parmigiano rind is the poor kitchen's stock cube, collagen and glutamate simmered back into service. This is cucina povera at its most instructive, and it punishes the make-ahead cook honestly, because the ditalini keep drinking broth as they stand. Eat it the day it's made, or hold the pasta back.
