Ravioli del Plin

  • Advanced
  • 1 h 30 m
  • Serves 4
  • $$$

rich · umami-deep · warm-spiced · nutty-toasted Leads with richness that coats and comforts, savory depth stacked from the pantry’s bass notes, closing toasted and warm.

Ingredients

  • 250g 00 flour
  • 8 egg yolks (150g)
  • 1 whole egg (50g)
  • 4g salt
  • 200g roast veal or pork shoulder, cooled
  • 120g roast rabbit or chicken thigh, cooled
  • 150g cooked spinach, squeezed dry
  • 60g Parmigiano Reggiano, grated
  • 1 egg, to bind the filling
  • 1g grated nutmeg
  • 80g unsalted butter
  • 6 sage leaves
  • 40g roasting juices
  • Salt and black pepper

Equipment

Piping bag · Grater / zester

Method

  1. Work the yolks, whole egg and salt into the flour, knead 10 minutes until the surface turns silky, then wrap and rest 1 hour. A yolk dough is dry, and without that rest it cracks along the edges on the pin. 10 min
  2. Mince the roast meats fine with the spinach, then bind with the egg, Parmigiano, nutmeg, salt and pepper. It should hold its shape on a spoon rather than slump.
  3. Roll one sheet to about 0.8mm, thin enough to read print through. Pipe hazelnut-sized mounds in a line 1cm from the long edge, then fold the near edge over them.
  4. Pinch hard between each mound with thumb and forefinger, pushing the trapped air out sideways as you go. That pinch, the plin, is the entire seal. Cut through each pinch with a fluted wheel.
  5. Cook 90 seconds in salted water, then finish in butter foamed with sage and the roasting juices until the sauce clings and the pasta darkens a shade.

How to tell it is going right

This dish has no standard of its own yet. It is judged on the techniques it exercises: checked at the pan, while there is still something to be done about it.

Salting to draw the water out 41 recipes

  • Salted evenly over every surface rather than tipped on in one place and tossed once.
  • Laid out so the water has somewhere to go: a colander, a rack, or a sieve over a bowl, never a flat dish where it sits back in what it gave up.
  • Given real time, and there is visible liquid to show for it: a pool underneath, a drip, or a wrung handful.
  • That liquid is removed. It is squeezed, blotted or poured away, and the food is dry to the touch before it goes anywhere near heat.
  • The dish is seasoned knowing salt has already gone on, tasted before any more is added.

Where it goes wrong It went into the dish still wet, and was salted again out of habit. The water it was carrying dilutes and steams whatever it meets, so the result is watery and over-seasoned at once, and those are two faults that cannot be corrected in the same direction.

Kneading dough 47 recipes

  • The work changed it: what began shaggy and rough is one smooth, cohesive mass, with nothing raggy hanging off it.
  • The surface has gone satin and barely tacky, releasing cleanly from hands and board instead of leaving a trail.
  • Worked to the point this dough asks for and no further: springy and elastic where structure is wanted, just brought together where tenderness is.
  • Torn or cut open, the inside is uniform: no dry streaks, no unmixed pockets, no core of flour that never met the water.
  • After its rest it rolls and shapes without snapping back. A dough that fights the pin needed more rest, not more force.

Where it goes wrong Sticking was fought with flour instead of technique or patience, stroke after stroke, until the dough had taken in far more than the formula asked; it turns tight and dry under the hands, and bakes dense however well everything else goes.

From the pass

Two things seal a plin, and neither is water. The dough sits near 45 percent hydration, so it is tacky and welds to itself; flour the bench under the fold and nothing will hold. The pinch does the rest, driving out the trapped air that would otherwise expand in the pot and split the seam from inside. At 0.8mm they overcook in seconds, so count ninety and lift. Any that burst were dusted, not undercooked.
