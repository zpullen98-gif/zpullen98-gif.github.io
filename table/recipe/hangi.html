Aotearoa & Maori · Main

Hāngī

  • Advanced
  • 3 h
  • $$$ roughly 3 of 4 for cost

clean · simple A quiet dish that lets its main ingredient speak: season well and stand aside.

Ingredients

  • 3 kg pork shoulder, on the bone
  • 2 kg lamb or mutton shoulder
  • 1 chicken, about 1.8 kg
  • 2 kg kūmara, whole
  • 2 kg potatoes, whole
  • 1.5 kg crown pumpkin, in wedges
  • 2 green cabbages, quartered
  • 60 dense volcanic stones, fist sized
  • dry mānuka or other hardwood, enough for a four hour fire
  • 4 hessian sacks, soaked
  • 2 cotton sheets, soaked
  • 20 L water
  • salt

Method

  1. Dig a pit about a metre square and 60 cm deep, stack the stones through a criss cross of hardwood and burn it down for three to four hours, until the stones glow dull red and knock together with a dead thud.
  2. Rake out the embers and level the stones, then lay wet cabbage leaves and a soaked sack over them until the steam coming off is white rather than smoky.
  3. Set the salted meat baskets straight onto the sacking and the vegetable baskets above them, so fat and juice run down through nothing you want dry.
  4. Throw a bucket of water onto the stones at the edges, cover the lot with the soaked sheets and sacks and shovel the earth back fast, until not one wisp of steam escapes.
  5. Patch any leak the moment you see it and leave the mound shut for three and a half hours.
  6. Open from the downwind side, peel the sacking back away from yourself, and lift the baskets straight up so no soil falls in.

How to tell it is going right

This dish has no standard of its own yet. It is judged on the techniques it exercises: checked at the pan, while there is still something to be done about it.

Grilling over live coals 77 recipes

  • The fire was ready before the food was: coals evenly covered in grey ash with no flames licking through and no raw smoke coming off, and a hot zone and a cooler one both available.
  • The grate was hot and clean when the food landed, and the food released cleanly from it when the time came to turn rather than tearing away.
  • Marks are browned, not burnt: deep colour where the metal touched, with no carbon flaking off and no soot transferred onto the food.
  • The interior reaches its target with a thin gradient, not charred outside and cold within, and not driven through by a flare-up.
  • What comes off the fire smells of fire and food only: no raw wood smoke, no fuel, nothing acrid at the back of the nose.

Where it goes wrong It went on over live flames instead of settled coals, so the outside charred bitter in the flare-ups while the middle stayed cold, and everything on the plate tastes of soot.

Steaming: basket, leaf and lid 130 recipes

  • Steam was running before the food went in: the water at a rolling boil and vapour coming steadily through the basket, rather than the whole thing warming up together.
  • Nothing touches the water at any point: the food sits clear of it throughout, and the level neither drops enough to boil dry nor rises enough to wet it.
  • The lid stayed on. Every lift costs the chamber its heat, and the number of lifts should be as close to none as the dish allows.
  • Lifted out, the surface is glossy and plump with its colour heightened rather than dulled: greens brighter than they went in, not olive.
  • Cooked through with the shape intact: it yields at the centre without having slumped or split, and there is no more than a spoonful of clean condensate beneath it.

Where it goes wrong The lid was loose or dripping and the condensate fell back onto the food, so it arrives waterlogged and pale with its seasoning washed down into the bottom of the dish.

From the pass

The earth is the lid. Hot stones and wet sacking make the steam, and packed soil holds it against the food at a little over 100C (212F) for hours; nothing roasts, everything steams. Two things ruin it. A porous stone still holds water and will burst, so use dense volcanic rock and reject any that rings rather than thuds. And every wisp of steam leaving the mound is heat leaving: shovel more soil on it at once, or the centre comes out raw.
