Canadian · Sauce

Ketchup aux Fruits

  • Intermediate
  • 40 min
  • Serves 4
  • $$
  • Vegetarian
  • Vegan

warm-spiced · bright Leads with warm spice humming through the middle, acid holding everything up to the light.

Ingredients

  • 1.5 kg ripe tomatoes, peeled and chopped
  • 1 tbsp pickling salt
  • 500 g peaches, peeled and diced
  • 500 g pears, peeled and diced
  • 2 apples, peeled and diced
  • 300 g onion, diced
  • 2 celery ribs, diced
  • 400 g sugar
  • 1 tbsp mixed pickling spice tied in muslin
  • 300 ml cider vinegar
  • 1 tsp ground cinnamon
  • 1/2 tsp ground clove

The skills inside

Reducing a sauceThe bare simmer: holding liquid below the boilSalting to draw the water out

Allergen screen

Found: celery

Screened for: gluten, dairy, egg, nuts, peanuts, fish, shellfish, molluscs, soy, sesame, celery, mustard, lupin, alcohol. Not screened: sulphites.

Method

  1. Toss the tomatoes with the salt and leave them in a colander 30 minutes; what drains out is water you would otherwise boil off for an hour. 30 min
  2. Put the drained tomatoes in a wide preserving pan with the peaches, pears, apples, onion, celery, sugar and the spice bag, and bring to a simmer, stirring until you cannot feel sugar on the base.
  3. Cook uncovered at a lazy blip for about 90 minutes, stirring in from the corners as it thickens, until no free liquid pools around the fruit and a spoon drawn across the base leaves a track that closes slowly. 90 min
  4. Stir in the vinegar, cinnamon and clove and simmer 10 minutes more, then lift out the spice bag and taste for salt and sharpness. 10 min
  5. Ladle hot into sterilised jars and seal, or cool and keep it in the fridge for a month.

How to tell it is going right

This dish has no standard of its own yet. It is judged on the techniques it exercises: checked at the pan, while there is still something to be done about it.

Salting to draw the water out 38 recipes

  • Salted evenly over every surface rather than tipped on in one place and tossed once.
  • Laid out so the water has somewhere to go: a colander, a rack, or a sieve over a bowl, never a flat dish where it sits back in what it gave up.
  • Given real time, and there is visible liquid to show for it: a pool underneath, a drip, or a wrung handful.
  • That liquid is removed. It is squeezed, blotted or poured away, and the food is dry to the touch before it goes anywhere near heat.
  • The dish is seasoned knowing salt has already gone on, tasted before any more is added.

Where it goes wrong It went into the dish still wet, and was salted again out of habit. The water it was carrying dilutes and steams whatever it meets, so the result is watery and over-seasoned at once, and those are two faults that cannot be corrected in the same direction.

Reducing a sauce 86 recipes

  • A steady simmer with the surface moving, never a rolling boil that throws it up the sides and scorches what is left on the base.
  • Judged by behaviour rather than the clock: it coats the back of a spoon and holds a clean line when a finger is drawn through it.
  • The drop in volume is visible against the side of the pan: there is a line to see, not a guess about whether anything has happened.
  • Seasoned at the end and not the beginning, because salt concentrates at exactly the rate everything else does.
  • Nothing is catching underneath: a spoon drawn across the base comes up clean, with no browned film building where the heat is fiercest.

Where it goes wrong It was seasoned at the start and then reduced by half. The salt concentrated along with everything else, so the sauce reaches perfect body and is too salty to serve. And the only remedy is to dilute it and reduce it all over again.

From the pass

Use a wide pan: evaporation is the entire job and a deep pot doubles the time. Vinegar goes in at the end because acetic acid boils off, and an hour of simmering leaves the ketchup tasting flat and only sweet. Once the sugar concentrates it will catch and scorch in seconds, so keep the spoon moving in the corners. It thickens again as it cools, so pull it one shade looser than you want it.
