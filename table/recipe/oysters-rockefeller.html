New Orleans · Starter

Oysters Rockefeller

  • Advanced
  • 60 min
  • $$ roughly 2 of 4 for cost

herb-fresh · briny · rich · bright Leads with fresh herbs arguing happily on top, a briny, maritime salinity, with a clean, bright snap.

Ingredients

  • 24 oysters on the half shell, liquor saved, shells nested in rock salt
  • 150g butter; 1 bunch green onions + celery heart + parsley, minced to a paste
  • 80g watercress or spinach (the Antoine's original is famously green but famously NOT spinach, the recipe is still a secret)
  • 3 tbsp breadcrumbs; 1 tbsp Herbsaint or Pernod, the anise whisper
  • Cayenne; lemon wedges; a broiler at full temper

Method

  1. Sweat the green paste in butter until it collapses into velvet, 8–10 min; crumbs in; Herbsaint in: it should hold a soft shape. 10 min
  2. Nestle the shells into rock-salt-lined pans: the salt levels and insulates.
  3. A generous spoon of the green blanket over each oyster, sealing to the shell edges.
  4. Broil 4–5 min until bubbling and browned at the peaks. Serve in the pan, on the salt, with lemon. 5 min

How to tell it is going right

This dish has no standard of its own yet. It is judged on the techniques it exercises: checked at the pan, while there is still something to be done about it.

The broiler: heat from above 22 recipes

  • The element is fully lit and the compartment is hot before anything goes under it. A broiler switched on with the food already in place browns nothing for several minutes and then burns it in one.
  • The distance was set before the dish went in, not adjusted afterwards with a hot dish in one hand: how close it sits is the only heat control this method has.
  • Somebody stayed with it for the whole time. The window between pale and bitter is tens of seconds, and nothing under a broiler is ever safe to walk away from.
  • The surface has coloured evenly right across, having been turned at least once; every grill has a hot corner and none of them say where it is.
  • What is underneath is no further cooked than when it went in. Top heat is there to finish a surface, and anything that came out hotter all the way through was reheated rather than glazed.

Where it goes wrong It was treated as an oven setting: food in, timer set, cook walks away. Radiant heat from above has no gentle phase, so the surface goes from pale to bitter inside a minute while the inside is untouched, and there is no stage in between to catch.

Sweating aromatics: soft, never browned 198 recipes

  • No colour anywhere: tipped under the light the pieces are translucent to pale gold, with no tan edges, no brown tips, and no browned flecks suspended in the fat.
  • The pan is quiet: a low steady hiss, not a crackle. A crackle means the moisture has gone and browning has already started.
  • The largest piece pressed against the side of the pan collapses without resistance and with no crunch left at its centre.
  • The fat is still clear and loose in the pan and the pan floor is clean: nothing caught, nothing browned on to deglaze, because there was never any fond to build.
  • It tastes sweet and mild with no raw allium bite at the back of the throat, and none of the roasted sweetness that belongs to the next technique up the heat scale.

Where it goes wrong The heat was too high and nothing covered the pan, so the pieces took colour before they softened; the finished dish carries a roasty sweetness where it wanted a clean one, and the pieces are still firm at the centre under browned edges.

From the pass

Invented at Antoine's in 1899 when snails ran short and named Rockefeller because the sauce was so rich: the true recipe has never left the family, and every green-sauced oyster in the world is an educated guess. The Herbsaint is the local tell; the rock salt is not garnish, it is the oven rack. Jules Alciatore took the formula to his grave, which in this town counts as good manners.
