Sri Lankan · Dessert

Wattalappam

  • Intermediate
  • 20 min
  • Serves 4
  • $$
  • Vegetarian

sweet-edged · rich · warm-spiced · nutty-toasted Leads with a sweet edge rounding the corners, richness that coats and comforts, closing toasted and warm.

Ingredients

  • 250 g kithul jaggery, chopped
  • 400 ml thick coconut milk
  • 60 ml water
  • 30 g cashew nuts, halved
  • 5 eggs
  • 0.5 tsp ground cardamom
  • 0.25 tsp freshly grated nutmeg
  • an eighth tsp ground cloves
  • 0.25 tsp salt

Equipment

Grater / zester

Found: egg · nuts

Method

  1. Melt the jaggery with the water over low heat until it is a smooth dark syrup with no grains left on the spoon, then take it off and let it cool to blood heat.
  2. Whisk the eggs with the salt and spices just until no strings of white remain; do not aerate them.
  3. Stir the coconut milk into the cooled jaggery, pour that onto the eggs in a thin stream while whisking, then strain into a heatproof dish.
  4. Cover tightly with foil and steam over water that is barely shivering until the edge is set and the centre wobbles as one piece when nudged.
  5. Scatter over the cashews, cool, then chill until firm enough to slice.

How to tell it is going right

This dish has no standard of its own yet. It is judged on the techniques it exercises: checked at the pan, while there is still something to be done about it.

Chilling to firm: the fridge as binder 28 recipes

  • It went cold before it was handled any further, and it went in spread flat and covered rather than as a warm mass that insulates its own centre.
  • It is cold through rather than cold at the edge: tested at the middle it is as firm as it is at the rim.
  • It holds its shape unsupported; it can be cut, portioned, rolled or lifted without slumping, tearing, or sticking to the hands.
  • The fat in it has gone solid: it feels firm and waxy to the fingers rather than greasy and yielding.
  • It survives the step it was chilled for, going into heat or a coating or a mould still holding its form rather than collapsing the moment it is touched.

Where it goes wrong It was given twenty minutes instead of the hours it needed, so it is firm at the rim and soft at the core: it slumps out of shape as it is portioned, and everything made from it spreads or breaks apart in the pan.

The wobble & the skewer: testing a bake 62 recipes

  • Tested at the true centre and the thickest point, not at the convenient edge: the edge is done long before the answer is.
  • The skewer comes out as the recipe describes, clean and dry or carrying the stated moist crumb, and it does the same on a second test in a different spot.
  • The wobble is read in motion: nudged, the centre moves as one set mass with a faint tremble, rather than a liquid ripple travelling across the surface.
  • The other signs agree with the test: risen and just shrinking from the sides, the top set and dry to a light touch with no wet gloss on it.
  • Pressed lightly at the middle it springs back and leaves no lasting dent.

Where it goes wrong It was judged on the clock and on how the edges looked, so a centre still liquid went to the rack and sank as it cooled, a dense wet seam under a perfectly browned top.

From the pass

This is a coconut milk custard: the eggs set near 80C (175F) and curdle just above it, so steam over water that is barely shivering, 40 to 45 minutes, and pull it while the middle still wobbles as one piece. Jaggery must be melted and cooled to blood heat before it meets the eggs, or it scrambles them into threads. Cover the dish or condensation pits the surface.
