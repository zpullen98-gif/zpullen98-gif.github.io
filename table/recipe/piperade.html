The French Basque & Gascony · Starter

Piperade

  • Easy
  • 25 min
  • $ roughly 1 of 4 for cost
  • Vegetarian

briny · aromatic · warm-spiced Leads with a briny, maritime salinity, aromatics blooming from the first bite, fading in warm spice.

Ingredients

  • 700 g piments doux verts (long sweet green peppers), deseeded and cut in 1 cm strips
  • 250 g onions, sliced thin
  • 600 g ripe tomatoes, peeled, deseeded and roughly chopped
  • 4 garlic cloves, sliced
  • 45 ml olive oil
  • 1.5 tsp piment d'Espelette, plus a pinch to finish
  • 8 eggs
  • 6 g fine salt

Method

  1. Sweat the onions and garlic in the olive oil over low heat with a pinch of salt until they are slack and glassy with no colour at all.
  2. Add the green peppers and cook on until they slump and lose their squeak; a strip should bend double without snapping.
  3. Add the tomatoes and the Espelette and cook uncovered until a spoon dragged across the base leaves a clean channel and no watery juice runs back into it.
  4. Pull the pan to the lowest possible heat, pour in the beaten salted eggs and stir slowly and constantly, scraping the base, until the mass thickens and turns glossy.
  5. Take it off while it still looks slightly too loose, since it carries on setting in the hot pan, and dust with Espelette.

How to tell it is going right

This dish has no standard of its own yet. It is judged on the techniques it exercises: checked at the pan, while there is still something to be done about it.

Reducing a sauce 95 recipes

  • A steady simmer with the surface moving, never a rolling boil that throws it up the sides and scorches what is left on the base.
  • Judged by behaviour rather than the clock: it coats the back of a spoon and holds a clean line when a finger is drawn through it.
  • The drop in volume is visible against the side of the pan: there is a line to see, not a guess about whether anything has happened.
  • Seasoned at the end and not the beginning, because salt concentrates at exactly the rate everything else does.
  • Nothing is catching underneath: a spoon drawn across the base comes up clean, with no browned film building where the heat is fiercest.

Where it goes wrong It was seasoned at the start and then reduced by half. The salt concentrated along with everything else, so the sauce reaches perfect body and is too salty to serve. And the only remedy is to dilute it and reduce it all over again.

Sweating aromatics: soft, never browned 198 recipes

  • No colour anywhere: tipped under the light the pieces are translucent to pale gold, with no tan edges, no brown tips, and no browned flecks suspended in the fat.
  • The pan is quiet: a low steady hiss, not a crackle. A crackle means the moisture has gone and browning has already started.
  • The largest piece pressed against the side of the pan collapses without resistance and with no crunch left at its centre.
  • The fat is still clear and loose in the pan and the pan floor is clean: nothing caught, nothing browned on to deglaze, because there was never any fond to build.
  • It tastes sweet and mild with no raw allium bite at the back of the throat, and none of the roasted sweetness that belongs to the next technique up the heat scale.

Where it goes wrong The heat was too high and nothing covered the pan, so the pieces took colour before they softened; the finished dish carries a roasty sweetness where it wanted a clean one, and the pieces are still firm at the centre under browned edges.

From the pass

Espelette is not paprika under a French name: the pods are dried whole and milled coarse, so it carries fruit and a slow warmth near 4,000 SHU rather than sweet red dust. The eggs are the fragile part. They set from 62C (144F) and weep water past 75C (167F), so the vegetables must be reduced dry and the heat right down before the eggs go in, or you get scrambled egg sitting in a puddle.
