Battera (Pressed Saba Oshizushi)

  • Advanced
  • 45 min
  • $$ roughly 2 of 4 for cost

bright · umami-deep · aromatic Leads with acid holding everything up to the light, savory depth stacked from the pantry’s bass notes, opening aromatic and staying there.

Ingredients

  • 2 mackerel fillets, about 300 g
  • 60 g fine salt
  • 300 ml rice vinegar
  • 20 g sugar
  • 1 strip kombu
  • 400 g seasoned sushi rice (shari), at body temperature
  • 1 sheet shiro-ita kombu (white pressed kombu)
  • wasabi
  • gari (pickled ginger)

Method

  1. Use mackerel that has been frozen at -20C (-4F) for at least 24 hours. Bury the fillets in salt, skin and flesh, and leave 45 minutes, until the surface firms and beads liquid. 600 min
  2. Rinse off the salt, pat dry, then steep in the rice vinegar with the sugar and kombu for 20 minutes, until the outer 2 mm has turned opaque white. 20 min
  3. Peel the fine translucent membrane off the skin, working from tail to head; it should lift in one sheet. Slice the fillet to an even 8 mm, keeping the trimmings.
  4. Wet a wooden oshibako, lay the fish skin side down with trimmings filling the gaps, smear a whisper of wasabi, and pack the shari on top to just over the rim.
  5. Fit the lid and press with steady weight until it stops sinking, rest 20 minutes, then unmould and drape with the softened white kombu sheet. 20 min
  6. Cut with a wet blade, one clean pull per slice, wiping the steel between cuts.

From the pass

Salt before vinegar, always. Salt pulls water out by osmosis and firms the muscle, so the vinegar then soaks in evenly and whitens only the outer few millimetres. Go straight to vinegar on a wet fillet and you get a chalky rim over a raw, slack middle that weeps in the box. Press once with steady weight until the lid stops giving; pumping it mashes the shari to paste.
