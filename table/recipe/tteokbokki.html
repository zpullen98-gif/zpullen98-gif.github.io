Korean · Starter

Tteokbokki

  • Easy
  • 25 min
  • Serves 4
  • $

umami-deep · herb-fresh · fiery · nutty-toasted Leads with savory depth stacked from the pantry’s bass notes, fresh herbs arguing happily on top, closing toasted and warm.

Ingredients

  • 500g cylindrical rice cakes (soaked 20 min if refrigerated)
  • 2 tbsp gochujang, 1 tbsp gochugaru, 1 tbsp sugar, 1 tbsp soy
  • 500ml anchovy stock or water
  • Fish cakes, cabbage, boiled eggs
  • Scallions, sesame

The skills inside

Reducing a sauce

Allergen screen

Found: gluten · egg · fish · soy · sesame

Screened for: gluten, dairy, egg, nuts, peanuts, fish, shellfish, molluscs, soy, sesame, celery, mustard, lupin, alcohol. Not screened: sulphites.

Method

  1. Dissolve the pastes into the stock; bring to a boil.
  2. Rice cakes and fish cakes in; simmer 10–12 min, stirring so nothing welds to the pot. 12 min
  3. The sauce should reduce to a glossy, spicy lava that coats.
  4. Eggs and scallions in the final minutes.

How to tell it is going right

This dish has no standard of its own yet. It is judged on the technique it exercises: checked at the pan, while there is still something to be done about it.

Reducing a sauce 97 recipes

  • A steady simmer with the surface moving, never a rolling boil that throws it up the sides and scorches what is left on the base.
  • Judged by behaviour rather than the clock: it coats the back of a spoon and holds a clean line when a finger is drawn through it.
  • The drop in volume is visible against the side of the pan: there is a line to see, not a guess about whether anything has happened.
  • Seasoned at the end and not the beginning, because salt concentrates at exactly the rate everything else does.
  • Nothing is catching underneath: a spoon drawn across the base comes up clean, with no browned film building where the heat is fiercest.

Where it goes wrong It was seasoned at the start and then reduced by half. The salt concentrated along with everything else, so the sauce reaches perfect body and is too salty to serve. And the only remedy is to dilute it and reduce it all over again.

From the pass

The dish is done when the sauce clings; watery tteokbokki just means three more minutes of patience, because the rice cakes are shedding starch the whole time and the reduction finishes the job on its own schedule. Keep stirring the bottom; tteok weld themselves to a neglected pot. Anchovy stock under the gochujang is the street-cart depth most home versions miss. Royal-court tteokbokki was once a soy-braised dish; the fire-red version conquered Korea from Seoul's snack stalls in the 1950s.
