Salt-Baked Whole Fish

  • Intermediate
  • 60 min
  • Serves 4
  • $

bright · briny Leads with acid holding everything up to the light, a briny, maritime salinity.

Ingredients

  • 1 whole fish (1–1.2kg, branzino, dorade, snapper), gutted and scaled, skin ON and intact: the skin is the wrapper's inner wall
  • 2kg coarse sea salt + 3 egg whites + 2 tbsp water, mixed to wet-sand consistency
  • Lemon slices, fennel fronds or thyme, tucked in the cavity only
  • A serving-worthy baking tray and a spoon and knife for the tableside excavation
  • Ladolemono or just olive oil and lemon: the fish emerges needing almost nothing

Equipment

Sheet pan

The skills inside

Reducing a sauce

Allergen screen

Found: egg · fish

Screened for: gluten, dairy, egg, nuts, peanuts, fish, shellfish, molluscs, soy, sesame, celery, mustard, lupin, alcohol. Not screened: sulphites.

Method

  1. Bed a 1cm layer of the salt mixture on the tray; lay the herbed fish on it; pack the rest over and around into a complete white tomb, sealing to the tray.
  2. Bake 200°C: 25–30 min for 1kg (an inserted skewer, held 5 seconds, should come out warm to the lip). 30 min
  3. Rest 5 min; then the theater: CRACK the hardened dome with a spoon handle and lift the crust away in plates: the skin peels off with it, leaving steamed-in-its-own-moisture flesh. 5 min
  4. Lift the fillets from the bone; oil and lemon over. The fish will not be salty; it will be the most evenly cooked fish of your life.

How it should come out

  • Before the salt goes on: skin whole and unbroken, no knife nicks along the flank or at the vent, and the cavity holding lemon and herbs only. Not one grain of salt goes inside the fish.
  • The dome comes out hard, matte and pale, still sealed to the tray, with no split that steamed open and no browned damp patch where the pack ran thin, and it breaks into slabs under a spoon handle, the skin lifting away stuck to the crust and leaving the flesh clean, unmarked and carrying no salt grains.
  • A skewer into the thickest part, held five seconds, comes out warm on the lip: cold means it goes back in before the crust is touched, hot means it has gone past.
  • The tail end is as moist as the shoulder: evenness is the whole claim of the crust, and a dry tail says the pack was thin at that end.
  • Taste at the belly seam first: that is where salt gets in if the skin was nicked, and one blown bite there condemns the fish.

Where it goes wrong Salt packed into the cavity as well as over the fish: it cures straight through the thin belly wall while the crust is doing its work, and the fillet arrives brined rather than steamed.

From the pass

The salt crust is not a seasoning but an oven within the oven, a hermetic clay that steams the fish in its own juices at perfect, gentle pressure; the technique is ancient Mediterranean and essentially foolproof, which is why restaurants charge theater prices for it. The egg white sets the dome so it cracks in satisfying slabs. Guests assume sorcery; the cook knows it is the easiest whole fish in this entire guide.
