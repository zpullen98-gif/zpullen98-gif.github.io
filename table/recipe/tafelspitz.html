Austrian · Main

Tafelspitz

  • Intermediate
  • 3 h 30 m
  • Serves 4
  • $$

rich · charred · gently hot · herb-fresh Leads with richness that coats and comforts, char used as punctuation, lifted green at the very end.

Ingredients

  • 1.2kg beef tafelspitz (top of the rump cap), in one piece
  • 2 marrow bones; 1 onion, halved and CHARRED cut-side down
  • 2 carrots, ½ celeriac, 1 leek, parsley root if found
  • Peppercorns, bay, lovage
  • Apple-horseradish (Apfelkren) + chive sauce; roasted potatoes

The skills inside

Allergen screen

Found: celery

Screened for: gluten, dairy, egg, nuts, peanuts, fish, shellfish, molluscs, soy, sesame, celery, mustard, lupin, alcohol. Not screened: sulphites.

Method

  1. Char the onion halves black in a dry pan: they bronze the broth.
  2. Start bones and beef in COLD water; bring to a whisper; skim like a monk, never boil.
  3. Vegetables in for the final hour; simmer 3 h total until a fork slides free. 180 min
  4. Serve in courses, Vienna-style: first the broth with the marrow, then sliced beef with both sauces.

How to tell it is going right

This dish has no standard of its own yet. It is judged on the techniques it exercises: checked at the pan, while there is still something to be done about it.

Charring over open flame 26 recipes

  • The surface met the fiercest heat available (flame, a broiler, or bare hot metal) with nothing between them, and it shows as true blistering and blackening rather than gentle, even browning.
  • The char matches the surface’s fate: where it will be eaten, black sits in patches over deep colour with every face having had its turn, and where the skin is to be peeled away, it may go to an even black, because the char there works on the flesh below and then leaves with the skin.
  • It smells of smoke and toasted surface with no acrid edge that stings the nose: acrid means it went past, and acrid does not cook out.
  • Just under the char it is cooked (softened, steaming, collapsed, or puffed, whichever the dish wants) because the flame did the surface and the trapped heat did the rest.
  • A taste carries the smoke without mouthfuls of ash: the black that flakes away is a seasoning, not a layer.

Where it goes wrong It was left on one face too long or held too close, so one side carbonised to ash while the rest stayed raw, and the smell turned acrid, which follows the dish through every later step and cannot be seasoned away.

Resting meat & slicing against the grain 37 recipes

  • It came off the heat BEFORE the target temperature, measured with a probe rather than by time, because carryover keeps driving the inside up after it is out.
  • The probe went into the coldest likely part rather than the easiest one to reach: the reading that matters is the lowest one, not the first one.
  • Rested for a time matched to its size, somewhere warm, and not cut into partway through to see how it is doing.
  • The board is nearly dry when it is finally cut. A spreading pool means it was cut too early, and that liquid should have stayed inside.
  • The grain was found and the knife went ACROSS it, so the fibres are cut short, and the blade was sharp enough that the slices hold together rather than tearing.

Where it goes wrong It was cut straight off the heat, usually because everything else was ready. The muscle is still contracted and the juice still driven to the centre, so it runs out onto the board; the meat is drier than it ever needed to be, and the flavour that left it is sitting in a puddle under the knife.

From the pass

Emperor Franz Joseph ate this daily, so Vienna built a whole restaurant liturgy around boiled beef. The charred onion and the never-boil rule are why the broth is amber and clear instead of gray and cloudy.
