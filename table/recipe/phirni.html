Ingredients

  • 60 g basmati rice, soaked 30 minutes then drained
  • 1 litre full fat milk
  • 100 g sugar
  • 6 green cardamom pods, seeds ground
  • 0.2 g saffron, bloomed in 1 tbsp warm milk
  • 5 ml kewra water
  • 20 g pistachios, slivered
  • 15 g blanched almonds, slivered

Method

  1. Drain the rice and pound or pulse it to the feel of fine sand: pinch some between the fingers and you should still make out separate grains, not flour.
  2. Reduce the milk over medium heat for 20 minutes, scraping the skin off the sides back in, until it has lost a quarter of its volume and faintly coats the spoon. 20 min
  3. Slacken the ground rice with 100 ml of the hot milk, rain it in while whisking, and cook on low for 18 minutes, dragging a spoon across the base without pause, until the grains are soft and a spoon drawn through leaves a clean line. 18 min
  4. Only now stir in the sugar, then take the pan off the heat and add the cardamom, saffron milk and kewra water.
  5. Pour it hot into shallow earthenware bowls, scatter the nuts over, and chill at least 4 hours until the top sets firm enough to hold a spoon mark. 240 min

  1. 1
    Prepare an even batch

    Trim the beans and compare their size. Similar pieces cook more evenly; a thick bean and a fine haricot vert need different checks.

  2. 2
    Blanch and check

    Briefly cook in boiling water for this method. Test a sample for the intended bite; brightness alone cannot tell you its texture.

  3. 3
    Cool for later service

    Transfer the beans to an ice-water bath to stop further cooking promptly. Remove them once cold, rather than leaving them to soak.

  4. 4
    Drain before the finish

    Drain well so carried water does not dilute the finish. Refrigerate promptly if holding for later; reheat and season according to the recipe.

How to tell it is going right

This dish has no standard of its own yet. It is judged on the technique it exercises: checked at the pan, while there is still something to be done about it.

Blanching & shocking 76 recipes

  • The water begins at the heat specified by the method and recovers promptly after the food enters. A brief drop in bubbling is expected; a batch that prevents recovery needs adjustment.
  • Time and the required texture or purpose are checked together. Piece size and the next step matter; where a preservation method specifies a blanching time, a visual check does not replace that timed process.
  • The ice bath was made and standing beside the pot BEFORE anything went into the water, not started once the timer went off.
  • It went straight into the ice water, fully submerged, and stayed until cold at the centre: a piece still warm inside is still cooking, and the arrest has not happened yet.
  • Drained and dried afterwards rather than left sitting in water, so it does not carry that water into whatever it meets next.

Where it goes wrong The ice bath was not ready, so the food sat draining in a colander while its own residual heat carried on cooking it. By the time it cooled it was a stage past where it was pulled, and exact arrest is the entire reason for blanching rather than simply boiling.

From the pass

Grind the rice coarse, to the feel of sand: milled to flour it sets as paste with nothing to chew. Sugar goes in last because it competes with the starch for water and lifts the temperature at which the grains swell, so added early it leaves the rice gritty however long you cook it. Unglazed earthenware wicks moisture out as it chills and tightens the surface.
