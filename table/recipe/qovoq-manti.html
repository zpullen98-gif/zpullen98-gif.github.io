Qovoq Manti

  • Advanced
  • 55 min
  • $$ roughly 2 of 4 for cost
  • Vegetarian

rich · earthy Leads with richness that coats and comforts, an earthy, grounding base.

Ingredients

  • 400 g plain flour
  • 200 ml warm water
  • 6 g salt for the dough
  • 700 g pumpkin or winter squash, peeled and cut into 5 mm dice by hand
  • 300 g onions, finely diced
  • 80 g cold butter, in small cubes
  • 2 tsp cumin seed, crushed
  • 1 tsp coarse black pepper
  • 10 g salt for the filling
  • oil for the steamer trays and 30 g melted butter to finish

Equipment

Steamer · Sheet pan · Rolling pin

Method

  1. Knead flour, salt and water to a firm dough, wrap it and rest 40 minutes, until it rolls out without pulling back on itself. 40 min
  2. Dice the pumpkin small by hand and mix with the onion, cumin and pepper, but hold the salt back until you are ready to fill.
  3. Roll the dough to 1.5 mm and cut 12 cm squares; salt the filling, then put a heaped spoonful and a cube of cold butter on each square, bring the four corners to the centre and pinch the seams shut, then join the two opposite ears underneath.
  4. Oil the steamer trays well and set the parcels a finger apart, then steam over hard boiling water 40 minutes, until the wrappers are translucent and drum tight and no floury bloom is left on top. 40 min
  5. Lift them off with an oiled spatula, brush with melted butter and serve at once with cold suzma.

How to tell it is going right

This dish has no standard of its own yet. It is judged on the techniques it exercises: checked at the pan, while there is still something to be done about it.

Egg wash & the baker’s shine 34 recipes

  • Applied thin and even over every surface that will show, with no bare patches and no ridges or brush lines standing in it.
  • Nothing pooled: no wash run down into the seams, collected in the corners, or welded to the tray around the base.
  • It has not glued shut what needs to move: layers, scores, and cut edges are still free to open in the heat.
  • Baked, the finish is even and glossy across the whole piece and deep gold rather than patchy, with no darker bands where the wash sat heavy.
  • The shine is on the outside only: nothing inside the piece tastes of egg.

Where it goes wrong The wash went on heavy and ran into the scores and down to the tray, so the piece baked sealed along its seams, browned unevenly, and carries dark rubbery ridges where the egg collected.

Kneading dough 57 recipes

  • The work changed it: what began shaggy and rough is one smooth, cohesive mass, with nothing raggy hanging off it.
  • The surface has gone satin and barely tacky, releasing cleanly from hands and board instead of leaving a trail.
  • Worked to the point this dough asks for and no further: springy and elastic where structure is wanted, just brought together where tenderness is.
  • Torn or cut open, the inside is uniform: no dry streaks, no unmixed pockets, no core of flour that never met the water.
  • After its rest it rolls and shapes without snapping back. A dough that fights the pin needed more rest, not more force.

Where it goes wrong Sticking was fought with flour instead of technique or patience, stroke after stroke, until the dough had taken in far more than the formula asked; it turns tight and dry under the hands, and bakes dense however well everything else goes.

From the pass

Dice the pumpkin, never grate it. Cubes release their juice slowly and it stays in the parcel, while grated flesh floods the wrapper and blows the base out on the tray, which is also why the salt goes in only as you fill. Oil the trays and leave a finger between parcels: steam condensing in the gap between two touching manti glues them, and they tear at the seam.
