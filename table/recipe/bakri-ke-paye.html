Bakri ke Paye

  • Advanced
  • 50 min
  • $$ roughly 2 of 4 for cost

aromatic · rich · bright · warm-spiced Leads with aromatics blooming from the first bite, richness that coats and comforts, fading in warm spice.

Ingredients

  • 8 goat trotters, hair singed off, split at the joint
  • 80 g ghee
  • 400 g onions, finely sliced
  • 40 g ginger paste
  • 30 g garlic paste
  • 10 g Kashmiri chilli powder
  • 5 g ground turmeric
  • 10 g ground coriander
  • 3 g black pepper, ground
  • 5 g garam masala
  • 150 g full-fat yoghurt, whisked smooth
  • 15 g fine salt
  • 3 litres water, plus more boiling water for topping up
  • 30 g plain flour, slaked in 100 ml cold water
  • 20 g ginger, in fine matchsticks
  • 15 g coriander leaves, chopped
  • 2 limes, in wedges

Method

  1. Scrub the trotters in a filled basin rather than under a running tap, with a stiff brush, scraping off every trace of singed hair and hoof grime, then blanch them 10 minutes in boiling water and rinse; skip this and the broth tastes of the yard. 10 min
  2. Fry the onions in the ghee until deep brown, stir in the ginger, garlic and ground spices, then take the pan off the heat and beat in the yoghurt a spoonful at a time.
  3. Add the trotters, salt and water, bring to a bare tremble and cook with the lid ajar for 6 hours to 7 hours, skimming through the first hour. Keep the trotters covered the whole way, topping up with boiling water, never cold, as the level falls; expect to add a litre or more before the skin turns translucent and slides off the bone. 360 min
  4. Whisk in the flour slurry and simmer 15 minutes; the broth stays thin while hot, which is correct, and sets when cold. 15 min
  5. Stir through the garam masala and serve with the ginger matchsticks, coriander and lime.

How to tell it is going right

This dish has no standard of its own yet. It is judged on the techniques it exercises: checked at the pan, while there is still something to be done about it.

Skimming: foam, fat and a clear broth 73 recipes

  • It is held below a rolling boil for the whole time. Agitation drives fat and fine protein into the liquid as an emulsion, and nothing skimmed afterwards will take them back out.
  • The grey foam that rises in the first minutes is lifted off and discarded rather than stirred back in.
  • Fat is taken from the surface where it gathers, the pot moved off the centre of the heat if that is what it takes to collect it in one place.
  • The liquid is visibly clearer at the end than it was at the start: it is possible to see down into it.
  • Nothing lifted off goes back in. What was skimmed is discarded, not tipped back once the ladle is full.

Where it goes wrong The pot was allowed to boil, usually while something else was being attended to. Fat and protein emulsify into the liquid under agitation and cannot be lifted off afterwards, so the finished broth stays cloudy and greasy however patiently it is skimmed from then on.

Blanching & shocking 76 recipes

  • A large volume at a hard rolling boil, and enough of it that adding the food barely interrupts it; a pot that goes quiet has started stewing rather than blanching.
  • Timed rather than watched, and short. This is an interruption in the cooking of something, not the cooking of it.
  • The ice bath was made and standing beside the pot BEFORE anything went into the water, not started once the timer went off.
  • It went straight into the ice water, fully submerged, and stayed until cold at the centre: a piece still warm inside is still cooking, and the arrest has not happened yet.
  • Drained and dried afterwards rather than left sitting in water, so it does not carry that water into whatever it meets next.

Where it goes wrong The ice bath was not ready, so the food sat draining in a colander while its own residual heat carried on cooking it. By the time it cooled it was a stage past where it was pulled, and exact arrest is the entire reason for blanching rather than simply boiling.

From the pass

A trotter is skin, tendon and bone with almost no muscle, so there is nothing to overcook and everything to convert. Collagen unwinds into gelatin above roughly 70C (158F), but the reaction is slow and tendon this dense wants six hours or more; that clock sits behind every long braise here. Boil it hard instead and the fat emulsifies into a cloudy grey broth that never clears. Judge it cold: a spoonful that sets to a wobble in the fridge is done. The flour is insurance, not the thickener.
