Lagman

  • Advanced
  • 2 h 30 m
  • $$ roughly 2 of 4 for cost

gently hot · bright · umami-deep · earthy Leads with a warm current of chile, acid holding everything up to the light, settling low and earthy.

Ingredients

  • Hand-pulled noodles: 500g flour, 250ml water, 1 tsp salt, plus oil for the pulling ritual
  • 400g lamb or beef, small cubes
  • 2 peppers, 2 tomatoes, 1 onion, celery, green beans, garlic, the vegetable crowd (kovurma)
  • 2 tbsp tomato paste; 1 tsp ground cumin + coriander; a star anise
  • Black rice vinegar and chili oil (laza) at the table, the Uyghur signature

Method

  1. Rest the dough 1 h; roll ropes, oil them, coil and rest again; then pull (swing, fold, slap) into long noodles (or roll and cut, and keep your dignity). 60 min
  2. Sear meat hard in a kazan; onions, then vegetables in waves, keeping crunch.
  3. Tomato paste, spices, water to a loose sauce; simmer 15 min. 15 min
  4. Boil noodles; bowl them; ladle the kovurma over; vinegar and chili oil to taste.

How to tell it is going right

This dish has no standard of its own yet. It is judged on the techniques it exercises: checked at the pan, while there is still something to be done about it.

Resting dough: the pause that does the work 71 recipes

  • It actually rested, covered, for the time stated, not cut short because the rest of the schedule was tight.
  • It rolls and shapes without fighting: it stays where it is put, and it does not spring back or shrink on the bench between passes.
  • The surface is smooth and supple rather than dry, cracked, or skinned over.
  • It is at the temperature the next step needs: cold where the fat must stay solid, cool and relaxed where it must be rolled thin.
  • Baked, it holds the dimensions it was shaped to rather than shrinking back from the edges of the tin or the rim of the dish.

Where it goes wrong The rest was skipped or shortened, so the gluten was still tight: the dough fought the pin and sprang back thick and uneven, then shrank down the sides of the tin in the oven.

Searing: the hard crust 141 recipes

  • The pan keeps a hard, steady sizzle from the moment the food lands and does not fall quiet; silence within the first half minute means the pan lost its heat and the surface is now steaming rather than browning.
  • It releases itself when tested: slide a thin edge under and it lifts without tearing. Anything that grips the pan has not formed a crust yet and is not ready to be moved.
  • Deep even brown across the whole face, edge to edge, when it is turned under the light: no pale ring left around a browned centre, and no black patches that taste bitter on their own.
  • The pan floor carries a dry browned layer that dissolves and lifts the moment liquid hits it: not a wet grey film, and not black flecks that stay put and stay bitter.
  • Cut into it: the browned band is thin, a few millimetres at most, sitting straight on top of correctly cooked interior; a thick grey collar under the crust means the heat was too low for too long.

Where it goes wrong The pan was not hot enough, or too much went in at once, so the food released its water and sat in it: the surface goes grey and steams, it sticks when moved, and the crust that eventually forms arrives over an interior already cooked through.

From the pass

Lagman traveled the Silk Road with the Uyghur people, the same noodle that became ramen east and spaghetti west, still hand-pulled in Tashkent doorways. One unbroken noodle per portion is the masters' flex; the vinegar-and-chili-oil finish is what makes it lagman and not stew on pasta.
