Idaho Scalloped Potatoes

  • Intermediate
  • 1 h 30 m
  • $$$ roughly 3 of 4 for cost
  • Vegetarian

rich · warm-spiced · aromatic Leads with richness that coats and comforts, warm spice humming through the middle, opening aromatic and staying there.

Ingredients

  • 1.2kg russet potatoes, sliced 3mm thin
  • 55g butter
  • 1 onion, sliced thin
  • 55g flour
  • 750ml whole milk
  • 250ml cream
  • 3 garlic cloves, grated
  • 200g sharp cheddar, grated
  • 1/2 tsp nutmeg
  • Salt and white pepper

Equipment

Grater / zester

The skills inside

Method

  1. Sweat the onion and garlic in the butter, stir in the flour, and cook 2 min. 2 min
  2. Whisk in the milk and cream and simmer to a smooth sauce. Season with nutmeg, salt, and pepper.
  3. Layer the thin-sliced potatoes in a buttered dish, pouring sauce over every layer and scattering cheese between.
  4. Cover with foil and bake at 180C for 45 min, then uncover and bake 25 min more until browned and bubbling. 45 min
  5. Rest 15 min before serving so the layers set. 15 min

How to tell it is going right

This dish has no standard of its own yet. It is judged on the technique it exercises: checked at the pan, while there is still something to be done about it.

Sweating aromatics: soft, never browned 198 recipes

  • No colour anywhere: tipped under the light the pieces are translucent to pale gold, with no tan edges, no brown tips, and no browned flecks suspended in the fat.
  • The pan is quiet: a low steady hiss, not a crackle. A crackle means the moisture has gone and browning has already started.
  • The largest piece pressed against the side of the pan collapses without resistance and with no crunch left at its centre.
  • The fat is still clear and loose in the pan and the pan floor is clean: nothing caught, nothing browned on to deglaze, because there was never any fond to build.
  • It tastes sweet and mild with no raw allium bite at the back of the throat, and none of the roasted sweetness that belongs to the next technique up the heat scale.

Where it goes wrong The heat was too high and nothing covered the pan, so the pieces took colour before they softened; the finished dish carries a roasty sweetness where it wanted a clean one, and the pieces are still firm at the centre under browned edges.

From the pass

Slice the potatoes on a mandoline if you have one: uneven slices cook unevenly and leave you with crunchy pockets. Resting the dish before serving lets the starch set the layers so it cuts into squares instead of sliding apart.
