The Bakery · Bread

English Muffins

  • Intermediate
  • 3 h
  • Serves 4
  • $$
  • Vegetarian

rich Leads with richness that coats and comforts.

Ingredients

  • 450g bread flour; 7g yeast; 1 tbsp sugar; 8g salt
  • 280ml warm milk; 1 egg; 30g soft butter
  • Semolina or cornmeal for dusting, the signature gritty jacket
  • A griddle or heavy skillet; the oven is optional and slightly shameful
  • Butter and jam standing by for the fork-split moment

Allergen screen

Found: gluten · dairy · egg

Screened for: gluten, dairy, egg, nuts, peanuts, fish, shellfish, molluscs, soy, sesame, celery, mustard, lupin, alcohol. Not screened: sulphites.

Method

  1. Soft, slightly sticky dough; knead well; rise 1 h. 60 min
  2. Roll 2cm thick; cut rounds; dust both faces generously with semolina; proof 30–40 min until puffy. 40 min
  3. Griddle on LOW, 6–7 min a side: they bake through on the stovetop, patience over heat; they should sound hollow. 7 min
  4. Cool; then split with a FORK around the equator, never a knife. Toast; watch the craters hold the butter.

How to tell it is going right

This dish has no standard of its own yet. It is judged on the techniques it exercises: checked at the pan, while there is still something to be done about it.

Griddle & plancha work 40 recipes

  • Full contact with the metal: the food lies flat, with no dome in the middle and no curled edge lifting it away, so browning reaches the entire face rather than a ring of it.
  • Contact is immediate and audible, and the food stays where it is put rather than sliding around in a slick of its own released liquid.
  • The cooked face is an unbroken sheet of even colour: the whole surface browned, not a pattern of marks with pale ground between them, and not a dark centre with pale corners.
  • The metal around the food is dry: no spreading wet patch steaming out from under it, nothing weeping onto the surface and boiling there.
  • Turned once or twice and no more, and lifted while the crust is still brown: a piece flipped repeatedly never holds heat long enough to build one.

Where it goes wrong The surface was crowded and its temperature crashed, so everything gave up its water at once and stewed on the metal: pale, damp, grey at the edges, and stuck fast where the crust never got the chance to form.

The wobble & the skewer: testing a bake 62 recipes

  • Tested at the true centre and the thickest point, not at the convenient edge: the edge is done long before the answer is.
  • The skewer comes out as the recipe describes, clean and dry or carrying the stated moist crumb, and it does the same on a second test in a different spot.
  • The wobble is read in motion: nudged, the centre moves as one set mass with a faint tremble, rather than a liquid ripple travelling across the surface.
  • The other signs agree with the test: risen and just shrinking from the sides, the top set and dry to a light touch with no wet gloss on it.
  • Pressed lightly at the middle it springs back and leaves no lasting dent.

Where it goes wrong It was judged on the clock and on how the edges looked, so a centre still liquid went to the rack and sank as it cooled, a dense wet seam under a perfectly browned top.

From the pass

The fork split is the whole religion: a knife slices the crumb flat, a fork tears it open into the nooks and crannies that hold butter like tidepools. Griddled bread is older than ovens: the English muffin is a survivor from that era wearing a semolina coat.
