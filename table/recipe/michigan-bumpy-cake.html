Michigan · Dessert

Michigan Bumpy Cake

  • Advanced
  • 2 h
  • $$ roughly 2 of 4 for cost
  • Vegetarian

rich · silky · floral Leads with richness that coats and comforts, a silken, spoon-slow texture, leaving a faint perfume.

Ingredients

  • For the cake: 250g flour, 60g cocoa, 1 tsp soda, 1/2 tsp salt, 115g butter, 300g sugar, 2 eggs, 240ml buttermilk, 1 tsp vanilla
  • For the bumps: 115g butter, 300g icing sugar, 60ml cream, 1 tsp vanilla
  • For the ganache: 200g dark chocolate, 200ml cream

Equipment

Piping bag

Method

  1. Make a chocolate cake: cream butter and sugar, add eggs, alternate the dry ingredients with buttermilk. Bake in a rectangular pan at 175C for 30 min. Cool. 30 min
  2. Beat the buttercream ingredients stiff and pipe it in thick parallel ridges across the cake: these are the bumps.
  3. Chill until the buttercream is firm.
  4. Warm the cream, pour over the chocolate, and stir to a glossy ganache. Cool slightly.
  5. Pour the ganache over the whole cake so it flows around the buttercream ridges, showing the bumps beneath.

How to tell it is going right

This dish has no standard of its own yet. It is judged on the techniques it exercises: checked at the pan, while there is still something to be done about it.

Icing & frosting 17 recipes

  • The temperatures were chosen, not defaulted: the bake fully cool where the finish is meant to sit on top of it, or both deliberately warm where the method wants the two to fuse, and the proof either way is a finish that stayed where it was put.
  • Smooth on the tongue: no grit of undissolved sugar and no lumps of anything that was never fully worked in.
  • The consistency matches the job: it spreads or pours the way the method intends, then holds the edge, swirl, or drip it was given.
  • The surface underneath is intact: covered rather than scrubbed, with no crumbs dragged through the finish and no torn patches showing.
  • By serving it has settled into the finish the method wants (matte, glossy, or soft) and a cut comes away clean rather than pulling the top off with it.

Where it goes wrong A finish meant to sit on top went on while the bake was still warm. The layer that touched first melted, slid, and soaked in; what is left sets greasy and streaked, and no amount of chilling afterwards puts that finish back.

Creaming butter & sugar 28 recipes

  • The butter started at the right temperature: it took a fingerprint and held its shape, neither greasy and slumping nor hard from the fridge.
  • It has visibly paled, from yellow towards near-white, and grown in volume, and getting there took minutes rather than seconds.
  • The texture is light and aerated: a spoonful is fluffy and holds a soft peak rather than sitting dense on the spoon.
  • Rubbed between finger and thumb it is still faintly gritty with undissolved sugar, and not slick or oily.
  • It is one homogeneous cream: not split, slackened, or separating into a greasy mass with liquid running out of it.

Where it goes wrong The butter was too warm, or it was beaten well past its point, so the fat softened and gave up the air it had taken: the mixture goes slack and oily and the cake bakes dense and greasy with a sunken middle.

From the pass

A Detroit bakery classic from Sanders, defined by its ridges of buttercream under a chocolate glaze. Chill the buttercream bumps hard before glazing or they collapse under the warm ganache.
