Persian · Salad

Salad-e Shirazi

  • Easy
  • 15 min
  • $ roughly 1 of 4 for cost
  • Vegetarian
  • Vegan

herb-fresh · bright · briny Leads with fresh herbs arguing happily on top, acid holding everything up to the light, ending saline and clean.

Ingredients

  • 400 g small ridged cucumbers
  • 400 g firm ripe tomatoes
  • 100 g red onion
  • 75 ml lime juice or verjuice
  • 5 g dried mint
  • 30 ml olive oil
  • 6 g salt
  • 2 g black pepper

Method

  1. Quarter the tomatoes and scrape out the seed jelly, keeping only the firm walls.
  2. Cut the tomato walls, cucumber and onion into an even 5 mm dice; the uniformity is the dish.
  3. Rinse the diced onion under cold water and squeeze it dry in a cloth to pull the sulphurous heat out of it.
  4. Crush the dried mint between your palms into the lime juice and leave it 5 minutes to swell and go fragrant. 5 min
  5. Dress with the mint juice, oil, salt and pepper only as the bowl goes to the table, and toss just twice.

How to tell it is going right

This dish has no standard of its own yet. It is judged on the technique it exercises: checked at the pan, while there is still something to be done about it.

  • Take any three pieces from anywhere in the pile: they match closely enough in every dimension that you would have to measure them to argue otherwise.
  • The cut faces are flat and clean rather than ragged or crushed; leaves and herbs are cut through, not bruised, and the board carries no dark smear where they were.
  • The stated size is the actual size, held against a rule or a known reference rather than called about right.
  • Everything in the pile is the shape that was asked for: no wedges, no half pieces, and no offcuts swept in to avoid waste.
  • They cook evenly, which is the entire point: at the end of cooking nothing is still raw and nothing has collapsed.

Where it goes wrong The knife was dull, or the vegetable was never squared off first, so the pieces vary: the small ones are mush by the time the large ones are cooked, and the dish has two textures where it wanted one.

From the pass

Salt draws water out of cut cucumber and tomato within minutes, so a salad dressed in the kitchen arrives as pink brine with limp dice adrift in it. Salt and acid go on at the table, and the tomato seed jelly is scraped out for the same reason. Use dried mint rather than fresh, and give it five minutes in the lime juice first, or it stays as dust on the tongue instead of perfuming the dressing.
