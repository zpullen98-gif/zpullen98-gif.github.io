Filipino · Breakfast

Tapsilog

  • Intermediate
  • 45 min
  • Serves 4
  • $$

aromatic · bright · sweet-edged Leads with aromatics blooming from the first bite, acid holding everything up to the light, trailing gentle sweetness.

Ingredients

  • 400g beef sirloin, sliced thin; marinade: soy, calamansi or lemon, garlic, sugar, pepper (that is the tapa)
  • 3 cups day-old rice
  • 1 head garlic, chopped, for the sinangag (garlic rice)
  • 2–4 eggs
  • Spiced vinegar on the side

The skills inside

Searing: the hard crustMarinating: acid, salt and time

Allergen screen

Found: gluten · egg · soy

Screened for: gluten, dairy, egg, nuts, peanuts, fish, shellfish, molluscs, soy, sesame, celery, mustard, lupin, alcohol. Not screened: sulphites.

Method

  1. Marinate beef overnight.
  2. Sinangag: fry the garlic gold in oil; scoop half out; toss the rice through the garlic oil, salt, then return the crisp bits.
  3. Sear the tapa hard and fast in a hot pan until the sugar edges caramelize.
  4. Fry eggs sunny with lacy edges. Assemble: tapa + sinangag + itlog = tapsilog.

How to tell it is going right

This dish has no standard of its own yet. It is judged on the techniques it exercises: checked at the pan, while there is still something to be done about it.

Marinating: acid, salt and time 46 recipes

  • Contact is total: every surface is wetted, and the pieces are not welded into a clump with dry faces where they pressed together.
  • The stated time was kept and kept cold, read against a clock, because this is the one technique where longer is a different result rather than a safer one.
  • It worked at the surface and no further: cut a piece and the marinade’s colour stops a few millimetres in. A protein gone opaque and chalky to the centre before any heat reached it has been cooked by the acid.
  • The texture is still springy and whole, not mushy, mealy, or fraying at the surface when it is lifted.
  • Where the next step browns it, the surface was patted dry first: a wet surface cannot brown, and the marinade’s sugars will scorch before the food colours.

Where it goes wrong It was left well beyond the stated time on the assumption that more is better, so acid and salt worked through the thin edges: those cook to cotton and read grainy while the thick centre tastes of nothing at all.

Searing: the hard crust 100 recipes

  • The pan keeps a hard, steady sizzle from the moment the food lands and does not fall quiet; silence within the first half minute means the pan lost its heat and the surface is now steaming rather than browning.
  • It releases itself when tested: slide a thin edge under and it lifts without tearing. Anything that grips the pan has not formed a crust yet and is not ready to be moved.
  • Deep even brown across the whole face, edge to edge, when it is turned under the light: no pale ring left around a browned centre, and no black patches that taste bitter on their own.
  • The pan floor carries a dry browned layer that dissolves and lifts the moment liquid hits it: not a wet grey film, and not black flecks that stay put and stay bitter.
  • Cut into it: the browned band is thin, a few millimetres at most, sitting straight on top of correctly cooked interior; a thick grey collar under the crust means the heat was too low for too long.

Where it goes wrong The pan was not hot enough, or too much went in at once, so the food released its water and sat in it: the surface goes grey and steams, it sticks when moved, and the crust that eventually forms arrives over an interior already cooked through.

From the pass

The -silog suffix is a national breakfast operating system: longsilog, tocilog, bangsilog — any protein + garlic rice + fried egg. Day-old rice is mandatory; fresh rice turns garlic fried rice into porridge.
