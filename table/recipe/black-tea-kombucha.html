Black Tea Kombucha

  • Intermediate
  • 30 min
  • Serves 4
  • $
  • Vegetarian
  • Vegan

umami-deep · floral Leads with savory depth stacked from the pantry’s bass notes, a floral thread lifting the aroma.

Ingredients

  • 3.5 litres filtered water
  • 20 g loose black tea
  • 250 g white sugar (about 70 g per litre)
  • 350 ml mature unflavoured kombucha, as starter (10 percent by volume)
  • 1 kombucha pellicle
  • 350 ml fruit juice, for the second ferment (optional)

The skills inside

Fermentation

Allergen screen

None found among the 14 screened.

Screened for: gluten, dairy, egg, nuts, peanuts, fish, shellfish, molluscs, soy, sesame, celery, mustard, lupin, alcohol. Not screened: sulphites.

Method

  1. Steep the tea in 1 litre of just-boiled water for 10 minutes, strain, and stir in the sugar until no grain is left on the bottom. 10 min
  2. Top up with cold water to 3.5 litres and let it fall below 30C (86F) before it goes near the culture; above 40C (104F) the heat kills it.
  3. Add the starter liquid and the pellicle, cover with tight-woven cloth under a band, and hold at 22 to 26C (72 to 79F).
  4. Taste through a straw from day 7. It is ready when it is sharply tart with only a trace of sweetness behind it, around pH 3.0, usually day 8 to 14.
  5. For fizz, decant into pressure-rated swing-tops with 10 percent fruit juice, leave 2 to 3 days at room temperature, burping one bottle daily as a gauge, then refrigerate to stop it.

How to tell it is going right

This dish has no standard of its own yet. It is judged on the technique it exercises: checked at the pan, while there is still something to be done about it.

  • Held somewhere with a steady temperature it can actually work at, chosen on purpose rather than wherever there happened to be room.
  • There is visible activity before it is called started: bubbles, rise, or movement in the liquid. A ferment showing nothing after its first hours is not slow, it is not going.
  • It smells clean: sour, yeasty, or alcoholic, with nothing putrid, and with nothing fuzzy or coloured growing where air meets it.
  • Everything is under the liquid or under a cover, as the method requires: nothing solid left sitting dry in the air to spoil while the rest works.
  • Judged by taste and behaviour and then STOPPED: chilled, baked, or refrigerated at the point wanted, rather than left running because the stated hours had not elapsed.

Where it goes wrong It was run on the clock in a kitchen colder or warmer than the recipe assumed. Fermentation is a rate and not a duration, so the same number of hours gives an under-developed ferment in a cold room and a slack, over-soured one in a warm one, and only tasting it would have said which.

From the pass

Two organisms working in sequence: yeasts split the sucrose and ferment it to ethanol, then acetic acid bacteria oxidise that ethanol to acid. The starter liquid matters more than the pellicle, because 10 percent by volume puts the jar at pH 4.5 on day one, under the line where mould can take hold. Brown strands and a new pale film are normal. Dry fuzzy blue or green spots standing proud of the surface are mould: bin the lot, liquid included.
