Whole Grilled Fish with Ladolemono

  • Intermediate
  • 40 min
  • $ roughly 1 of 4 for cost

bright · charred · briny · sweet-edged Leads with acid holding everything up to the light, char used as punctuation, trailing gentle sweetness.

Ingredients

  • 2 whole fish (dorade, branzino, ~500g each), gutted, scaled, patted DESERT-DRY inside and out; dry skin is the entire anti-sticking strategy
  • Coarse salt; olive oil for a light rub; lemon halves and oregano sprigs in the cavities
  • THE LADOLEMONO: 100ml best olive oil + juice of 1½ lemons + 1 tsp dried Greek oregano + pinch of salt, whisked to a loose emulsion at the last minute
  • A grill grate scrubbed clean and oiled at the final moment; real coals if the day allows
  • Patience at the flip: the fish releases itself when ready and not one second before

Method

  1. Dry, salt and lightly oil the fish; stuff the cavities; let them come toward room temperature while the coals settle to medium-high.
  2. Lay diagonal on the hot clean grate; DO NOT TOUCH for 6–8 min; attempting an early flip is how skin dies; when ready, the fish lifts freely. 8 min
  3. One flip; 6–8 min more, until the dorsal fin pulls out with no resistance, the Aegean doneness test. 8 min
  4. Whisk the ladolemono; fillet the fish tableside or serve whole; spoon the emulsion over generously. The sauce is four ingredients and the fish is one; that ratio is the philosophy.

How it should come out

  • Skin whole from gill to tail, striped with dark bars, and the grate clean when the fish comes off: nothing of the fish left welded to the bars.
  • The dorsal fin pulls out with no resistance before the fish leaves the coals, and the reading holds up when it is opened: at the shoulder the flesh is opaque and slips off the pin bones, and the line along the spine has set from red to pale grey.
  • The top fillet lifts away in one piece and the backbone comes out whole, leaving the underside fillet intact.
  • The ladolemono reaches the plate as one liquid: cloudy, loose enough to pour, thick enough to coat, and spooned over within a minute of the whisk. Clear oil floating on a lemon layer means it was made ahead and sat.
  • A piece of fish eaten before any sauce touches it is already seasoned to the middle, skin and cavity both: the ladolemono is bringing acid and oil, not the salt.

Where it goes wrong Turned before it let go: half the skin stays on the grate, the flank tears open, and the exposed flesh dries over the coals through the second half of the cook.

From the pass

The Greek island taverna's entire seafood program is this recipe executed with conviction: pristine fish, live fire, and ladolemono, the oil-lemon emulsion that is less a sauce than a magnifying glass. The two techniques that matter travel to every grilled fish you will ever make: bone-dry skin on a clean hot oiled grate, and the no-touch rule until the fish volunteers. The fin-pull test predates thermometers and has never been wrong.
