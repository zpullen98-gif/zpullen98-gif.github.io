Planked Shad

  • Intermediate
  • 1 h 30 m
  • Serves 4
  • $$

rich · bright · smoky · herb-fresh Leads with richness that coats and comforts, acid holding everything up to the light, lifted green at the very end.

Ingredients

  • 1 whole shad, filleted, about 1.2kg
  • An untreated oak or hickory plank, soaked in water 2 hours
  • 85g butter, melted
  • 2 tbsp lemon juice
  • 1 tbsp Old Bay
  • 2 tsp salt
  • 1 tsp black pepper
  • Bacon rashers
  • Lemon and parsley

Equipment

Sheet pan

Allergen screen

Found: dairy · fish

Screened for: gluten, dairy, egg, nuts, peanuts, fish, shellfish, molluscs, soy, sesame, celery, mustard, lupin, alcohol. Not screened: sulphites.

Method

  1. Soak the plank thoroughly so it smolders rather than burns.
  2. Season the fillets with salt, pepper, and Old Bay and set them skin-side down on the plank.
  3. Lay bacon over the flesh and brush with the melted butter and lemon.
  4. Cook beside a hardwood fire, or in a 200C oven with a smoking chip tray, 35-50 min, basting with butter every 15 min. 50 min
  5. The fish is done when it flakes and the plank is charred at the edges. Serve straight from the board with lemon and parsley.

From the pass

A Potomac spring ritual older than the Republic: planked shad feasts were political institutions in Virginia, where campaigns were launched over split fish nailed to oak boards around a fire. The plank is the cooking vessel and the seasoning both — soak it well, and never use treated lumber or softwood; oak or hickory only, or the fish tastes of the hardware store. Shad is famously bony; the slow plank heat softens what filleting can't remove. Butter, salt, and the run's brief calendar.
