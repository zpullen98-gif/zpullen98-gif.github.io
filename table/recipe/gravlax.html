Gravlax

  • Intermediate
  • 30 min
  • $$ roughly 2 of 4 for cost

herb-fresh · earthy · gently hot · bright Leads with fresh herbs arguing happily on top, an earthy, grounding base, with a clean, bright snap.

Ingredients

  • 600g impeccably fresh salmon, skin on, pin-boned, in two matching pieces
  • 3 tbsp salt + 3 tbsp sugar + 1 tbsp crushed white peppercorns
  • 2 huge bunches of dill: a forest, not a sprig
  • Optional: 2 tbsp aquavit or gin
  • Hovmästarsås: mustard, sugar, vinegar, oil, chopped dill; dark rye alongside

Method

  1. Rub the cure into the flesh; splash the spirit; bury both fillets in dill and sandwich flesh-to-flesh.
  2. Wrap tight; weight with a plate and cans; refrigerate 48 h, flipping every 12 and basting with its own brine. 600 min
  3. Unwrap, scrape off the cure and dill, pat dry.
  4. Slice paper-thin on the bias, knife almost flat; serve with mustard sauce and rye.

How to tell it is going right

This dish has no standard of its own yet. It is judged on the techniques it exercises: checked at the pan, while there is still something to be done about it.

Brining & curing 88 recipes

  • Fully committed either way: in a wet brine nothing floats clear of the liquid, and in a dry cure every face carries salt, seams and cavities included.
  • The time was measured against the weight and the thickness rather than the recipe’s convenience, and it stayed cold for all of it.
  • The change is visible and can be felt: the flesh is firmer than raw and its colour has deepened or gone more translucent, rather than still being slack and pale.
  • Rinsed and dried as the recipe asks, and anything meant to crisp afterwards has a surface dry and tacky to the touch before it goes near heat.
  • Cooked and tasted, it is seasoned right through to the centre rather than merely salty at the surface, and not so salty that the dish cannot be eaten.

Where it goes wrong It sat in the brine well past its time because a few extra hours seemed harmless; the surface is over-salted and the texture has gone from firm to spongy and wet, and no cooking takes salt back out.

Where it goes wrong The knife was dull, or the vegetable was never squared off first, so the pieces vary: the small ones are mush by the time the large ones are cooked, and the dish has two textures where it wanted one.

From the pass

Gravlax means 'buried salmon': medieval fishermen cured it in beach sand. The flip-every-12-hours ritual cures it evenly; the 48-hour wait is why it slices like silk instead of sashimi.
