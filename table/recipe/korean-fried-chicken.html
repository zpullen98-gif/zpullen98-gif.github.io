Korean Fried Chicken

  • Advanced
  • 60 min
  • $$ roughly 2 of 4 for cost

aromatic · gently hot · rich · bright Leads with aromatics blooming from the first bite, a warm current of chile, with a clean, bright snap.

Ingredients

  • 1kg chicken wings or boneless thigh chunks
  • Dry brine: salt, pepper, ginger
  • Batter: ½ cup potato starch + ¼ cup flour + ½ tsp baking powder + cold water
  • Sauce: 3 tbsp gochujang, 2 tbsp honey, 1 tbsp each soy, rice vinegar; garlic, butter knob
  • Oil for frying, sesame

Equipment

Thermometer · Deep-fry pot

Brining & curing

Method

  1. Toss chicken in a thin, drippy batter.
  2. Fry once at 165°C, 8 min; rest 5. 8 min
  3. Fry again at 190°C until shattering, 4 min. 4 min
  4. Toss in the warmed sauce or serve it alongside to preserve maximum crunch.

How to tell it is going right

This dish has no standard of its own yet. It is judged on the technique it exercises: checked at the pan, while there is still something to be done about it.

Brining & curing 88 recipes

  • Fully committed either way: in a wet brine nothing floats clear of the liquid, and in a dry cure every face carries salt, seams and cavities included.
  • The time was measured against the weight and the thickness rather than the recipe’s convenience, and it stayed cold for all of it.
  • The change is visible and can be felt: the flesh is firmer than raw and its colour has deepened or gone more translucent, rather than still being slack and pale.
  • Rinsed and dried as the recipe asks, and anything meant to crisp afterwards has a surface dry and tacky to the touch before it goes near heat.
  • Cooked and tasted, it is seasoned right through to the centre rather than merely salty at the surface, and not so salty that the dish cannot be eaten.

Where it goes wrong It sat in the brine well past its time because a few extra hours seemed harmless; the surface is over-salted and the texture has gone from firm to spongy and wet, and no cooking takes salt back out.

From the pass

Potato starch plus the double fry is why Korean fried chicken stays crisp under sauce for an hour; starch crusts fry glassier than flour ones, and the two-stage fry (low to cook, rest, high to crust) drives out the moisture that would soften everything from inside. The thin, drippy batter is intentional: KFC's shell is a shatter, not a bread-crumb coat. Toss in sauce at the last moment, or serve it alongside and let each eater choose their own structural risk.
