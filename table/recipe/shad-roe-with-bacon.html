Shad Roe with Bacon

  • Intermediate
  • 30 min
  • Serves 4
  • $$

rich · bright · herb-fresh Leads with richness that coats and comforts, acid holding everything up to the light, lifted green at the very end.

Ingredients

  • 2 pairs shad roe
  • 6 rashers bacon
  • 3 tbsp butter
  • 2 tbsp flour, seasoned
  • 1 lemon
  • 2 tbsp parsley, chopped
  • Salt and black pepper

Equipment

Skillet

Method

  1. Rinse the roe gently and pat dry; do not separate the pairs or pierce the membrane.
  2. Render the bacon until crisp in a heavy skillet; lift out and keep warm, leaving the fat.
  3. Dredge the roe lightly in seasoned flour.
  4. Cook gently in the bacon fat with the butter, 4-5 min a side, spooning fat over. It should be firm at the edge and just set inside: high heat makes it burst. 5 min
  5. Finish with lemon and parsley, and serve on the bacon.

How to tell it is going right

This dish has no standard of its own yet. It is judged on the techniques it exercises: checked at the pan, while there is still something to be done about it.

Rendering fat: and cooking in what runs out 63 recipes

  • It started cold or low and came up slowly: fat that met a hot pan seizes and scorches before the inside can melt, and there is no recovering it.
  • The fat in the pan is clear and clean-smelling, pale to golden, with no dark sediment on the floor and nothing acrid in the steam.
  • What is left behind is crisp and deep gold with no soft pale fat still clinging to it and no black burnt edges.
  • There is a real volume of it: enough to film the pan floor and be poured off and kept, not a dry smear that never ran.
  • Whatever cooks in it browns in it and takes its flavour, rather than sitting in a greasy pool that never gets hot enough to colour anything.

Where it goes wrong The heat was raised to hurry it along, so the outside seized and browned before the interior fat could melt; very little runs out, and the solids come out burnt on the outside and rubbery within.

The coat before the fry: dredge, crumb, batter 74 recipes

  • The coat is continuous before it goes anywhere near the fat: held up to the light, no bare patch of food shows through anywhere, seams and thin edges included.
  • It is bonded rather than resting on top: a gentle shake sheds loose dust only, and the coat does not slide off as a sheet when the piece is handled.
  • It bubbles hard and steadily from the moment it enters the fat, and the bubbling visibly slows as it finishes; those bubbles are the surface water leaving, and a quiet entry means the fat is too cool.
  • Out and drained, it is an even gold-brown all over and it is still crisp several minutes later on a rack; a coat that softens within a minute of coming out never lost its water.
  • Broken open, there is no paste of raw flour under the crust and no hollow gap where the coat has lifted away from the food.

Where it goes wrong The fat was below temperature or the pan was crowded, so the coat drank fat instead of sealing against it: it comes out pale and heavy, greasy to the fingers, and slumps off the food as it cools.

From the pass

A brief spring rite on the Connecticut River: the shad run gave the state a season and a festival, and the roe sacs are its prize. Cook it low: the membrane traps steam and a hot pan will pop it, spattering the kitchen with the evidence; some cooks prick it once with a pin as insurance. Bacon fat is the traditional medium and the bacon the traditional escort. Medium is done: pink at the center, never gray through. Weeks, not months, and then it's gone.
