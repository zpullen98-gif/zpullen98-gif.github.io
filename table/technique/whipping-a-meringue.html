Whipping a meringue

26 dishes across 18 chapters · 0 marked cooked

On the Path of Study: Semester 6: Pastry Fundamentals, Semester 10: The Restaurateur's Capstone

From the Lexicon, Meringues: French, Swiss, Italian

One foam, three temperaments. FRENCH: raw whites whipped with sugar; lightest, least stable, for baking into pavlova shells and folding into batters. SWISS: whites and sugar warmed over a bain-marie to ~70°C then whipped, denser, glossier, safe from grit, the buttercream base. ITALIAN: 118°C sugar syrup streamed into whipping whites: fully cooked, the most stable of all, for torching (baked Alaska, tarte au citron meringuée) and marshmallow work. Universal laws: no trace of yolk fat, a clean bowl, sugar added gradually, and 'stiff peaks' means the tip stands with a slight bow; chalky over-whip is one minute past glory.
