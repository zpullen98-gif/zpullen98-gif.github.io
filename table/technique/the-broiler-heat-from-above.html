The broiler: heat from above

22 dishes across 16 chapters · 0 marked cooked

On the Path of Study: Semester 2: Stocks, Soups & the Simmer, Semester 9: Seafood Mastery

From the Lexicon, Salamander, Broiler & Top-Down Fire

Heat from above: the finisher's station. The SALAMANDER (the pro's open-fronted overhead broiler, named for the fire-myth lizard): gratins bronzed to order, French onion soup's lid (the cheese-pull economy), glazes flashed, plates crisped and reheated seconds before the pass: it runs all service because top-down radiant heat finishes without re-cooking from below. The home BROILER is the same physics behind a worse door: preheat it seriously, rack close, food watched like a rival (the distance-and-attention tool: everything happens in 90-second windows), and use it for the technique wing's brûlée-adjacent jobs: melting, browning meringue, blistering peppers, finishing casseroles, faux-tandoor naan on a screaming steel. The mindset shift that unlocks it: the broiler is not an oven setting; it is an upside-down grill, and once you cook TO it (thin cuts, close racks, flipped once) it becomes the fastest cooking appliance in the house.

Read it in the Lexicon →

How to tell it is going right

  • The element is fully lit and the compartment is hot before anything goes under it. A broiler switched on with the food already in place browns nothing for several minutes and then burns it in one.
  • The distance was set before the dish went in, not adjusted afterwards with a hot dish in one hand: how close it sits is the only heat control this method has.
  • Somebody stayed with it for the whole time. The window between pale and bitter is tens of seconds, and nothing under a broiler is ever safe to walk away from.
  • The surface has coloured evenly right across, having been turned at least once; every grill has a hot corner and none of them say where it is.
  • What is underneath is no further cooked than when it went in. Top heat is there to finish a surface, and anything that came out hotter all the way through was reheated rather than glazed.

Where it goes wrong It was treated as an oven setting: food in, timer set, cook walks away. Radiant heat from above has no gentle phase, so the surface goes from pale to bitter inside a minute while the inside is untouched, and there is no stage in between to catch.
