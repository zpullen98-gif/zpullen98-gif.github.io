From the Lexicon, Lamination

Folding a butter block into dough and rolling repeatedly to create alternating sheets, dozens to hundreds of layers, so oven heat turns the butter's water to steam and jacks the layers apart: croissants, puff pastry, danish, kouign-amann, and the medialuna's method. The disciplines: dough and butter at matched plasticity (cold but bendable: shattered butter means leaks; warm butter means brioche), rest between folds (gluten relaxes or it fights back), and counted turns (3 letter-folds ≈ 27 layers; 6 ≈ 729 for classic puff). 'Rough puff' cheats with butter chunks and honest speed. Lamination is pastry's showpiece of the principle that structure IS technique.

Observe
Use the cut edge, not only the glossy top, to describe the structure.
Explain
Lamination separates dough with fat; water turning to steam helps lift the layers. Fold schedules and desired rise vary.
Apply
Lamb Shank en Croûte combines tender lamb with pastry. Describe that contrast without assuming the house’s pastry supplier, fold count or assembly method.
