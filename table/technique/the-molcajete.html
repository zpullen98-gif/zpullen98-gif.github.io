The molcajete

3 dishes across 2 chapters · 0 marked cooked

From the Lexicon, Mortar & Pestle: Molcajete, Suribachi, Granite

The pre-blender that blenders never fully replaced: pounding RUPTURES cells and bruises oils out where blades slice and whip air in: pesto's name is the pestle (hand-pounded pesto is visibly glossier and louder-flavored), Thai curry pastes demand the pound (the recipe wing says so repeatedly), guacamole in a MOLCAJETE (Mexican basalt; season by grinding rice until it runs clean) tastes creamier from crushed-not-chopped alliums, and the SURIBACHI's ridged ceramic grinds sesame for goma-ae like nothing else. The heavy granite Thai mortar is the do-everything choice: spice cracking, aioli, sambal (the ulekan's cousin). Ten minutes of pounding is the honest cost; the return is texture and aroma a machine's heat and aeration subtly flatten. Every serious kitchen keeps one within reach: not for nostalgia, for chemistry.
