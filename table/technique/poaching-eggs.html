Poaching eggs

3 dishes across 2 chapters · 0 marked cooked

From the Lexicon, Poach, Simmer, Boil

Three temperatures pretending to be one method. POACH: 71–82°C, water barely shivering: eggs, fish, chicken breast, anything delicate that violent bubbles would shred. SIMMER: 85–95°C, lazy bubbles: stocks, braises, beans, sauces. BOIL: 100°C rolling: pasta, blanching, potatoes, and almost nothing made of protein. The most common home error is treating these as synonyms; the most common professional discipline is watching the bubbles, not the dial. Salt poaching liquids and courts-bouillons like they are the sauce, because for those minutes they are.

Compare a quiet surface with a gentle simmer and a rolling boil. Choose the movement that suits the food and the recipe.

These matched pans illustrate different levels of water movement. A still picture cannot measure temperature or show the rhythm of the bubbles; use the linked lesson and observe your own pan.

One numbered view at a time. No grade or change to your learning record.
  1. 1
    Quiet poaching water

    The surface is mostly calm, without vigorous bubbles disturbing it. Gentle treatment is useful for delicate foods; follow their specific cooking method.

  2. 2
    Gentle simmer

    Small bubbles break the surface with less disturbance than a rolling boil. Watch their frequency and the movement of the liquid, not only the heat setting.

  3. 3
    Rolling boil

    Vigorous bubbles and strong surface movement distinguish this pan. A boil is useful when called for, but it is not interchangeable with a gentle simmer.
