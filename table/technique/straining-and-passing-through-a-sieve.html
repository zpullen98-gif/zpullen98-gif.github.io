Straining & passing through a sieve

34 dishes across 25 chapters · 0 marked cooked

On the Path of Study: Semester 6: Pastry Fundamentals, Semester 9: Seafood Mastery, Semester 10: The Restaurateur's Capstone

From the Lexicon, Sieves: Chinois, Tamis & Food Mill

Texture's final gatekeepers: the difference between good and restaurant-smooth is usually a pass through mesh. CHINOIS (the conical fine-mesh 'China cap'): sauces, custards, and consommé-adjacent liquids pressed through with a ladle's back, the crème anglaise and silky-soup insurance the pastry wing's custard entries demand. TAMIS (drum sieve): purées pushed across with a scraper: pommes purée's cloud (Robuchon's potatoes are a tamis story), flawless raviolo fillings, sifted dry goods. FOOD MILL: the rustic-smooth middle path. Tomato sauce milled (skins and seeds stay behind, BODY stays in; the sauce wing's Escoffier entry argues this against the blender's foam), mashed potatoes without glue (it cuts, never whips, so starch stays calm), applesauce at volume. The hierarchy by finish: mill = rustic-smooth with body; tamis = refined purée; chinois = liquid silk. Straining is not fussiness; it is the visible difference guests can't name and always notice.
