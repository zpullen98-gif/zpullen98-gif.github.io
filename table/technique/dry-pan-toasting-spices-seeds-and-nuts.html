Dry-pan toasting: spices, seeds and nuts

63 dishes across 51 chapters · 0 marked cooked

On the Path of Study: Semester 1: Knife & Fire Foundations

From the Lexicon, Toasting: Nuts, Seeds, Grains & Coconut

The smallest technique with the largest per-second flavor return: dry heat waking pyrazines and oils in anything with fat and starch to brown. THE METHODS ranked: OVEN (160–175°C on a sheet pan) for volume and evenness: nuts 8–12 min, stirred once; STOVETOP DRY PAN for small batches and whole spices: constant motion, trust your nose over your eyes (fragrant means nearly done; golden means done; 'one more minute' means starting over); the aggressive exception: coconut and sesame toast in a blink and burn in the next. THE LAWS: toast WHOLE then chop (surface-area math: chopped pieces scorch at the corners), carryover is real (pull a shade early; hot nuts keep cooking on the pan: decant immediately), toasted flavors FADE in liquid (re-toast garnishes; add toasted elements late), and salt while warm when salting at all. THE APPLICATIONS threading this guide: the nutty register's entire pantry, dukkah and gomasio, toasted-rice powder for larb, the pie crust's blind-baked depth, spices before grinding, and the honest truth that most 'flat' granola, pesto, and praline died of raw nuts. When in doubt, toast, but stand there while you do.
