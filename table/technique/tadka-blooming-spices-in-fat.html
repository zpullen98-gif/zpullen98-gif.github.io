Tadka: blooming spices in fat

21 dishes across 13 chapters · 0 marked cooked

From the Lexicon, Infusion: Steeping Flavor into Fat, Cream & Syrup

Extraction without transformation: coaxing an ingredient's aroma into a carrier while leaving the ingredient itself behind; the technique that turns pantries into perfumeries. THE CARRIERS AND THEIR AFFINITIES: FAT (the flavor-carrier entry's solvent: herb oils, chili crisp, garlic confit oil, vanilla in browned butter): gentle heat (60–90°C), then strain: high heat fries instead of infusing; CREAM & MILK (the pastry kitchen's canvas: vanilla bean, cardamom, bay, tea, toasted nuts steeped for custards and ganache): scald, steep off-heat 15–30 min covered, strain and proceed; SYRUP & SUGAR (the bar wing's program: oleo saccharum's cold extraction, cinnamon-bark simmers, zest-buried sugars): sweetness holds volatiles beautifully; ALCOHOL & VINEGAR (tinctures, house bitters, herb vinegars): the patient cold infusions measured in days. THE VARIABLES: temperature trades speed for delicacy (cold-steeped vanilla is prettier than boiled), surface area accelerates (bruise, crack, tear), and TIME has a ceiling: over-steeped infusions turn bitter and vegetal (tea's lesson generalized: taste at intervals, strain at peak). The restaurant math: infusions are signature flavor at raw-ingredient cost: the house chili oil on the table is branding you can eat.
