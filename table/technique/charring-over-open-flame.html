Charring over open flame

30 dishes across 20 chapters · 0 marked cooked

From the Lexicon, The Maillard Reaction

The browning reaction between amino acids and reducing sugars, roaring above ~140°C, the source of seared steak crust, bread crust, roasted coffee, fried onions, and toasted anything: hundreds of new flavor compounds from one bit of chemistry. Its enemies are water (surfaces must be DRY: a wet steak steams at 100°C forever) and crowding (which traps steam). Its friends: high heat, dry surfaces, a little alkalinity (the pinch of baking soda in stir-fry velveting and pretzel dips). Distinct from CARAMELIZATION, which is sugar alone breaking down at ~160°C+. Half of 'flavor' in savory cooking is engineering these two reactions on purpose.
