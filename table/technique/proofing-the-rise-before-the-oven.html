On the Path of Study: Semester 5: Bread & Fermentation

From the Lexicon, Proofing, Oven Spring & the Crumb

PROOFING is the final rise before baking; the poke test judges it (indent springs back slowly = ready; instantly = underproofed; never = over). OVEN SPRING is the first minutes' dramatic expansion as gasses balloon before the crust sets: steam in the oven delays crust-set and maximizes it, which is why lidded Dutch ovens and steam-injected decks exist, and why SCORING (lame slashes) directs the explosion artfully instead of randomly. THE CRUMB, the interior's hole structure, is the loaf's autopsy: tight crumb from low hydration or underproofing; open irregular crumb from wet dough, gentle handling, and full fermentation. Bakers read crumb the way sommeliers read legs.

Read it in the Lexicon →

How to tell it is going right

  • Judged on volume against the container rather than on the clock: visibly grown, and grown by roughly the amount the recipe describes.
  • The poke test agrees: a floured fingertip pressed a centimetre in leaves a dent that springs back slowly and only part of the way. Straight back means it is not ready; no spring at all means it has gone.
  • The surface is domed, smooth, and slightly sheened: not cratered, wrinkled, or collapsed, and not skinned over and dry from proofing uncovered.
  • Handled, it feels alive and airy: light for its size, with bubbles visible under the skin.
  • It springs in the oven: the piece lifts in the first minutes and the scores open. A shape that does not move at all was proofed past its point before it went in.

Where it goes wrong It was proofed to the clock in a kitchen colder or warmer than the recipe assumed: overproofed, it collapses as the blade touches it and bakes flat and dense with a sour edge; underproofed, it tears at the side and bakes tight.

A chosen film How to Do the "Poke Test" (and 3 ways it can trick you) Grant Bakes Watch for: The indent itself in close-up and how fast it fills: springs back at once is underproofed, comes back slowly is ready, stays put is gone. He also shows where the test lies to you, which is the part most poke-test videos skip.
