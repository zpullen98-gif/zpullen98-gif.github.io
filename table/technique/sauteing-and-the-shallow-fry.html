From the Lexicon, Sweat vs. Sauté vs. Sear

Three pan verbs distinguished by heat and intent. SWEAT: low heat, a little fat, often a lid or parchment; vegetables soften and release moisture WITHOUT color (the start of risotto, velouté, most braises where onion flavor should be sweet, not roasty). SAUTÉ: medium-high, food 'jumping' (sauter) in a wide pan: light browning, fast cooking, constant motion. SEAR: maximum heat, zero motion: deep crust, Maillard at full roar. Recipes say 'cook the onions' and mean one of three different flavors; knowing which one the dish needs is the difference between following recipes and cooking.
