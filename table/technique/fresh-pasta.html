From the Lexicon, Gluten & Hydration

Gluten is the elastic network formed when wheat's glutenin and gliadin meet water and agitation: the structure of bread, the enemy of tenderness in cakes and pie crusts. The baker steers it three ways: FLOUR CHOICE (bread flour ~12–14% protein builds; cake flour ~8% barely does), FAT (shortens gluten strands, hence 'shortening', 'shortbread', 'shortcrust'), and TECHNIQUE (kneading develops it; minimal mixing and resting relax it). HYDRATION is water as a percentage of flour weight: 60% is a tight bagel dough, 75% a country loaf, 85%+ the wet ciabatta school. Baker's percentages, everything relative to flour at 100%, are the professional language; learn them and every formula scales.

Agnolotti del plin: fold, pinch and sealIllustrated study · 6 numbered views

Follow the filled sheet into compact pinched pockets, then examine the wrapper and filling in section.

An illustrative general shaping sequence. Dough thickness, quantities, filling, size and cooking method are recipe-specific. The brown-green filling is a visual example, not an ingredient specification or Brennan’s approved shape.

One numbered view at a time. No grade or change to your learning record.

Whole illustration. Choose a view for a closer look.

  1. 1
    Place the filling

    Spaced small mounds leave clean dough available for the fold and seals. Follow the recipe’s amount and spacing.

  2. 2
    Fold and close

    Fold the sheet over the filling and close the long edge. Avoid trapping air or filling in the seam.

  3. 3
    Pinch between pockets

    Pinching between the mounds creates the defining constrictions. Preserve sealed dough between adjacent portions.

  4. 4
    Separate the plin

    Cut through the sealed areas to separate the pockets. The pinched ends should remain closed.

  5. 5
    Inspect a seam

    Look for a closed end and a compact folded form. An intact appearance still needs a careful hands-on seal check.

  6. 6
    Read the section

    The cut example separates the thin wrapper from the filling. Learn the structure without inferring exact ingredients from its colour.
