The field guide

Look closely. Learn the difference.

These U.S. cut names describe selected portions. The corrected guide takes precedence over the original poster archive.

  1. 1

    Chuck Roast

    A roast from the shoulder region, a familiar choice for slow-cooked beef.

    Part
    Chuck, the shoulder region.
    Kitchen
    Pot roasting and braising suit many chuck roasts.

    Remember Chuck is a region with several muscles, not one uniform steak.

  2. 2

    Ribeye

    A rib-section steak recognized for generous fat marbling within the meat.

    Part
    Rib primal.
    Character
    Rich flavor and a tender texture.

    Remember Marbling is fat within the muscle; it differs from an outer fat cap.

    Practise in the deck: Ribeye
  3. 3

    Strip Steak

    A steak cut from the strip loin beside the backbone, near the tenderloin.

    Part
    Short loin.
    Form
    The strip loin can be cut into steaks or kept as a roast.

    Remember Strip loin and tenderloin are neighboring but distinct muscles.

  4. 4

    Tenderloin

    The exceptionally tender loin muscle commonly portioned into filet mignon steaks.

    Part
    Loin.
    Character
    Fine texture with comparatively little fat.

    Remember Tenderloin is not the same muscle as strip loin or ribeye.

  5. 5

    Brisket

    The breast cut below the chuck and in front of the short plate.

    Part
    Breast.
    Kitchen
    Long, slow cooking is characteristic.

    Remember Brisket is separate from the short plate and its diaphragm cuts.

    Practise in the deck: Brisket
  6. 6

    Hanger Steak

    A cut from the diaphragm, associated with the short plate rather than the flank.

    Part
    Diaphragm; short plate.
    Structure
    A central connective membrane separates its sections.

    Remember Hanger and flank are different cuts. The original poster placed hanger incorrectly.

    Practise in the deck: Hanger Steak

Recorded correction notes

  • On the animal prints “Fore Shank drawn in the body wall between Brisket and Short Plate, under the Rib”. The fore shank is the lower front leg, below the chuck and in front of the brisket, and the space behind the brisket under the ribs is the front of the short plate.The shank is the leg that carries the animal, as the app's Shank card teaches, and no leg sits in the body wall where the map puts it.
  • Short Rib prints “Short Ribs (Braised) and Short Rib (Grilled, Braised) in one panel”. Short ribs are one cut printed twice, so list them once, braised as the English cut in thick blocks and grilled as the flanken cut sawn thin across the bones.The same cut appears under a singular and a plural name in the Short Plate panel, and the Lexicon already teaches the real split as English cut for braising and flanken for fast grilling.
  • Hanger Steak prints “Hanger Steak (Grilled) listed under Flank”. Hanger steak belongs with skirt in the short plate, not the flank, since it is the thick central pillar of the diaphragm hanging from the last rib, one per animal.The app's Hanger Steak card places it on the diaphragm between the last rib and the loin and its Skirt Steak card places the diaphragm in the plate, so the plate has moved a plate cut into the flank panel.
