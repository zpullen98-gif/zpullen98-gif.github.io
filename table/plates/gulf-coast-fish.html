The field guide

Look closely. Learn the difference.

Selected species, shown as recognition illustrations rather than identification certificates. These waters are not exclusive ranges; provenance and availability require supplier records.

  1. 1

    Red Snapper

    A reddish fish with a sloping triangular face and enlarged canine teeth.

    Look for
    Red tones vary; deeper-water fish can be redder.
    Species
    Lutjanus campechanus

    Remember Red snapper names a species, not every red-colored reef fish.

  2. 2

    Red Grouper

    A robust, large-mouthed fish with a reddish-brown body and occasional pale spots.

    Look for
    A broad head and a projecting lower jaw.
    Species
    Epinephelus morio

    Remember Its heavy build contrasts with the more pointed snapper profile.

  3. 3

    Mahi Mahi

    A brightly colored surface-water fish with a green-blue back and gold or silver lower body.

    Look for
    Adult males have a square forehead; females are rounder.
    Species
    Coryphaena hippurus

    Remember The dolphinfish name refers to a fish, not a marine mammal.

  4. 4

    Yellowfin Tuna

    A torpedo-shaped tuna with bright yellow dorsal and anal fins and yellow finlets.

    Look for
    Dark blue back and yellow-to-silver underside.
    Species
    Thunnus albacares

    Remember Those yellow fins distinguish the pictured form from albacore.

  5. 5

    White Shrimp

    A light gray shrimp with very long antennae and a greenish tail.

    Look for
    The carapace lacks grooves.
    Species
    Litopenaeus setiferus

    Remember White is a species name here, not a claim that the raw shell is pure white.

  6. 6

    Blue Crab

    A swimming crab with blue claws and flattened rear legs shaped like paddles.

    Look for
    Blue to olive-green shell.
    Species
    Callinectes sapidus

    Remember Its paddle-shaped rear legs are the useful visual cue.

Recorded correction notes

  • Grouper prints “Flavor: Rich, firm, buttery”. Grouper is a lean, firm white fish with a mild, faintly sweet flavor and large chunky flakes, not a rich or buttery one.The Floor Deck's Grouper card says it is lean but holds moisture well with a flavor that is mild and faintly sweet, and the Lexicon's Fatty vs. Lean Fish entry puts the reef white fish among the lean fish that store fat in the liver, not the flesh.
  • Snapper (Mutton) prints “The card pictures a uniformly red snapper like the Red Snapper card.”. Mutton snapper is olive on the back shading to rosy red on the sides, with a small black spot below the rear of the dorsal fin and blue lines on the face, so the black spot is the mark to look for at the case.The drawing repeats the Red Snapper card and would teach a cook to identify mutton snapper by the wrong fish.
  • Trout (Sea Trout) prints “Trout (Sea Trout), Gulf Coast”. The Gulf Coast sea trout is the spotted seatrout, also called speckled trout, a silver drum with round black spots along the back and tail that is related to redfish and is not a true trout.The Floor Deck's Trout card describes farmed rainbow trout, a freshwater salmonid, and the plate's name and drawing invite a cook to confuse the two.
  • Tarpon prints “Cuts: Head, Collar, Fillet, Tail; Flavor: Rich, firm, clean; Best prep: Grill, Bake, Pan sear”. Tarpon is a catch and release game fish that is not sold, cut or cooked as food, so treat this card as a mistake and not as a table fish.Tarpon flesh is bony and poor eating, there is no commercial tarpon fishery, and Florida allows possession only with a special tag for record attempts, so no fish case or restaurant carries it.
