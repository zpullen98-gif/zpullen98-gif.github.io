The field guide

Look closely. Learn the difference.

The illustration shows representative forms. Producer, age and handling affect texture and flavor; protected names identify particular products.

  1. 1

    Parmigiano Reggiano

    A hard, matured cheese with a granular structure that breaks into irregular pieces.

    Character
    Texture becomes more granular with age.
    Milk
    Cow's milk.

    Remember Its pale interior may contain small crystals; they are not blue mold.

    Practise in the deck: Parmigiano Reggiano Read the Lexicon: Parmigiano Reggiano
  2. 2

    Cheddar

    A firm cheese available in white and colored forms and a range of maturities.

    Character
    Flavor develops with aging.
    Milk
    Cow's milk.

    Remember An orange color does not by itself indicate greater age or stronger flavor.

  3. 3

    Brie

    A soft cheese with a white surface rind surrounding a creamy interior.

    Milk
    Cow's milk.
    Ripening
    The interior softens as the cheese ripens.

    Remember The white outer rind differs from blue veins inside a cheese.

  4. 4

    Roquefort

    A sheep's milk cheese with blue-green veins in an ivory interior.

    Milk
    Raw sheep's milk.
    Ripening
    Penicillium roqueforti develops within the cheese.

    Remember Roquefort is not an orange-colored blue cheese.

    Read the Lexicon: Roquefort
  5. 5

    Mozzarella di Bufala Campana

    A fresh stretched-curd cheese made with buffalo milk.

    Milk
    Buffalo milk.
    Method
    Curd is stretched, then shaped.

    Remember Mozzarella can also be made with cow's milk; this full name identifies the buffalo product.

  6. 6

    Comté

    A matured raw cow's milk cheese from the Jura, made with a cooked and pressed curd.

    Milk
    Raw cow's milk.
    Method
    Cooked, pressed and matured.

    Remember Cooked curd describes cheesemaking, not a ready-made hot cheese dish.

    Read the Lexicon: Comté

Recorded correction notes

  • Comté prints “Bold, tarity, blue”. Comté is a firm, nutty, aged Alpine cheese with no blue veining, so the style line should read something like nutty, firm, complex.Comté is a pressed cooked hard cheese, the plate's own Cheese Categories panel files it under Hard cheeses, the wedge pictured is a plain firm cheese, and the line simply repeats the Roquefort card with tangy misprinted as tarity.
  • Époisses prints “Smoky, rich, pungent”. Époisses is a washed rind cheese and is never smoked, so smoky should be replaced with a word like meaty or salty.Époisses de Bourgogne is washed with brine and marc de Bourgogne, and the app's own deck describes the washed rind family as brine or booze washed and stinky, never smoked.
  • Parmigiano Reggiano prints “Pictured as a bright orange crumbly block”. Parmigiano Reggiano has a pale straw yellow paste with a crystalline grain, and the orange block pictured looks like colored cheddar.Parmigiano Reggiano is never colored with annatto, so its paste is straw colored and the picture would mislead a server at the counter.
  • Blue Shropshire prints “Blue Shropshire, pictured with a white paste”. The cheese is called Shropshire Blue, and its paste is orange from annatto with blue veins rather than the white paste shown.Shropshire Blue is the name used by every maker, and it is an annatto tinted Stilton style blue, so both the reversed name and the white paste picture would teach a server to misname and misidentify it.

Cheese categories

  • Soft cheeses: Creamy, mild, spreadable. Brie, Camembert, Goat Cheese
  • Semi-soft cheeses: Smooth, rich, flexible. Havarti, Gouda, Taleggio
  • Hard cheeses: Firm, aged, complex. Parmigiano Reggiano, Comté, Cheddar
  • Blue cheeses: Bold, pungent, distinctive. Roquefort, Gorgonzola, Stilton
  • Fresh cheeses: Light, delicate, clean. Mozzarella, Feta, Ricotta
