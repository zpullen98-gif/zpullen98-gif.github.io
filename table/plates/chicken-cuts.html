The field guide

Look closely. Learn the difference.

These are selected chicken portions, not a complete butchery diagram. Skin, bone and trimming vary by supplier.

  1. 1

    Breast

    The broad chest portion that supplies the principal white meat of a chicken.

    Part
    Chest muscles.
    Character
    White meat, unlike the leg portions.

    Remember A breast is not a thigh or a drumstick.

  2. 2

    Thigh

    The dark meat portion of the upper leg, between the body and the drumstick.

    Part
    Upper leg.
    Character
    Dark meat.

    Remember The thigh is above the leg joint; the drumstick is below it.

  3. 3

    Drumstick

    The lower leg portion with a narrow bone end and a rounded mass of dark meat.

    Part
    Lower leg.
    Character
    Dark meat.

    Remember A drumstick is a leg cut; a drumette is part of the wing.

  4. 4

    Wing

    The jointed upper limb, commonly divided into a drumette, flat and tip.

    Part
    Wing.
    Structure
    Three connected sections.

    Remember The small drumette belongs to the wing, not the leg.

  5. 5

    Liver

    The soft, lobed organ that produces bile and is included among chicken giblets.

    Part
    Digestive organ.
    Giblets
    Grouped with the heart and gizzard.

    Remember The liver is an organ, not a skeletal muscle cut.

  6. 6

    Gizzard

    The thick, muscular stomach that mechanically grinds a bird's food.

    Part
    Muscular stomach.
    Giblets
    Grouped with the heart and liver.

    Remember Its grinding function distinguishes it from the liver.

Recorded correction notes

  • Neck prints “Neck (Bone-in), with a photo of thick marbled slabs”. Trust the words on the card and not the picture, because a chicken neck is a slender skin-on cylinder about the width of a finger, while the photo shows chop-thick marbled pieces that look like pork neck bones.Chicken necks are long and thin with almost no marbling, and the pictured pieces have the thickness and intramuscular fat of pork neck bones.
  • Thigh prints “Thigh (Boneless or Bone-in); Bone-in Thigh; Boneless Thigh”. Read the three thigh cards as one cut sold two ways, bone-in or boneless, not as three different cuts.Bone-in and boneless thighs are the same muscle group with or without the bone, and the right-column Thigh card already names both forms before the bottom row repeats them.
  • Liver & Gizzard prints “Sauté, Braise”. Sauté the liver quickly and braise or simmer the gizzard, because the two organs need opposite treatment and the shared line does not say which method belongs to which.The liver has almost no connective tissue and turns grainy when overcooked, while the gizzard is a tough working muscle whose connective tissue only softens with long moist cooking.
