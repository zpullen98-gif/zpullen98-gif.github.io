The field guide

Look closely. Learn the difference.

A representative study selection, not a map of native or exclusive crops. Cultivar, grower, weather, storage and imports change availability; confirm the local harvest with the supplier.

  1. 1

    Okra

    A ridged, edible seed pod that can thicken dishes through its mucilage.

    Food form
    Young pod.
    Notice
    A warm-season member of the mallow family.

    Remember The pod is a fruiting structure, not a root.

    Read the Lexicon: Okra
  2. 2

    Collard Greens

    Large, broad brassica leaves grown without forming a tight cabbage head.

    Food form
    Leaves.
    Notice
    Cooking softens their robust character.

    Remember Collards remain open-leaved rather than heading like cabbage.

    Read the Lexicon: Collard Greens
  3. 3

    Sweet Potato

    A thickened storage root, often shown with orange flesh.

    Food form
    Storage root.
    Notice
    Flesh and skin colors vary by cultivar.

    Remember It differs botanically from both potato and true yam.

    Read the Lexicon: Sweet Potato
  4. 4

    Southern Pea (Black-eyed)

    A cowpea with pale seeds marked by a dark eye.

    Food form
    Cowpea seeds.
    Notice
    A warm-season legume.

    Remember Cowpeas differ from cool-season garden peas.

  5. 5

    Summer Squash (Crookneck)

    A yellow squash with a curved neck, picked before the rind hardens.

    Food form
    Immature squash fruit.
    Notice
    Young skin remains tender.

    Remember Summer and winter squash describe harvest and use, not just calendar dates.

  6. 6

    Peanut

    A legume whose seed-filled pods develop underground.

    Food form
    Seeds inside pods.
    Notice
    The familiar shell is the pod.

    Remember Despite the name, it is not a tree nut.

Recorded correction notes

  • Southern Peas prints “May to August”. Fresh Southern peas in Georgia, South Carolina and Alabama come in from about June through September.Cowpeas are sown only after the last frost and need 60 to 90 days to reach the green shell stage, so the fresh crop arrives in early summer and runs into September rather than starting in May.
  • Broccoli prints “California, Georgia, Florida”. Broccoli's Where line should name Southeastern states only, such as Georgia, Florida and North Carolina.California is on the West Coast and lies outside the Gulf South, Atlantic Coast and Appalachia that the plate's own region line and map define, while every other box names only Southeastern states.
