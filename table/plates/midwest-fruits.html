The field guide

Look closely. Learn the difference.

A representative study selection, not a map of native or exclusive crops. Cultivar, grower, weather, storage and imports change availability; confirm the local harvest with the supplier.

  1. 1

    Apple

    A crisp tree fruit with seeds held in a central core.

    Food form
    Pome fruit.
    Notice
    Cultivars differ in color, texture and cooking use.

    Remember A core differs from the single stone in a peach.

  2. 2

    Tart Cherry

    A sour red stone fruit commonly used in cherry pies.

    Food form
    Stone fruit.
    Notice
    Also called sour cherry.

    Remember It differs from the sweet cherry often served fresh.

  3. 3

    Strawberry

    A red fruit with small seed-like achenes on its outer surface.

    Food form
    Fruit of a low-growing plant.
    Notice
    Garden strawberries spread by runners.

    Remember Those outer dots are tiny fruits, each containing a seed.

  4. 4

    Blueberry

    A small blue berry bearing a crown-shaped calyx at its tip.

    Food form
    Shrub-grown berry.
    Notice
    Cultivars have different harvest windows.

    Remember Blueberries are not miniature grapes.

  5. 5

    Grape (Concord)

    A dark purple, seeded grape commonly used for juice and jelly.

    Food form
    Fruit borne in bunches on a vine.
    Notice
    Concord has a pronounced flavor.

    Remember Concord and muscadine are different grape types.

  6. 6

    Cantaloupe

    An orange-fleshed muskmelon with a netted rind and a central seed cavity.

    Food form
    Melon fruit.
    Notice
    A netted rind surrounds a central seed cavity.

    Remember Cantaloupe and watermelon are different melon crops.

    Read the Lexicon: Cantaloupe

Recorded correction notes

  • Figs prints “Figs, Season August to September”. Figs are a Mediterranean and California crop and not a Midwest harvest, so any fig on a Midwest menu is shipped in rather than picked locally.The common fig is not winter hardy across the twelve states on the plate's own map, and the app's Lexicon places the crop in Turkey, Greece, Italy, Spain, the Levant and California, with no Midwest orchard or market crop.
  • Black Raspberries prints “Season July to August”. Midwest black raspberries ripen from late June to mid July and are finished well before August, ahead of the red raspberries beside them.Black raspberries carry a single early summer crop about two weeks ahead of summer red raspberries, and Ohio, the leading black raspberry state, picks from late June to mid July.
  • Plantain prints “Plantain, Season August to October”. Plantains are a tropical cooking banana that cannot be grown in any Midwest state and have no Midwest season, so this card does not belong on a local harvest plate.Plantain is a frost tender tropical plant whose United States crop comes from Puerto Rico, Hawaii and imports, and the app's Lexicon teaches it as a vegetable staple of West Africa, the Caribbean and Latin America.
