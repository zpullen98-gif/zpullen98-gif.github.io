The field guide

Look closely. Learn the difference.

A representative study selection, not a map of native or exclusive crops. Cultivar, grower, weather, storage and imports change availability; confirm the local harvest with the supplier.

  1. 1

    Chile Pepper

    An elongated Capsicum fruit used for varying degrees of heat.

    Food form
    Pepper fruit.
    Notice
    Heat varies by cultivar and growing conditions.

    Remember The pictured Anaheim-style form does not promise a fixed heat level.

  2. 2

    Tomatillo

    A small, tart, tomato-like fruit enclosed by a papery husk.

    Food form
    Husked fruit.
    Notice
    Often used green.

    Remember It is not simply an unripe tomato.

  3. 3

    Sweet Corn

    Tender kernels arranged in rows on an ear, harvested while still juicy.

    Food form
    Immature kernels on a cob.
    Notice
    Kernels at harvest release milky juice.

    Remember Sweet corn is harvested before dry grain corn.

  4. 4

    Zucchini

    An elongated summer squash picked while its skin and seeds are tender.

    Food form
    Immature squash fruit.
    Notice
    Green, gray and golden forms exist.

    Remember It is a summer squash, even when used in a warm cooked dish.

    Read the Lexicon: Zucchini
  5. 5

    Bell Pepper

    A blocky, sweet pepper with thick walls and a hollow seed cavity.

    Food form
    Pepper fruit.
    Notice
    Mature fruit colors vary by cultivar.

    Remember Bell peppers lack the pungency of hot chile types.

  6. 6

    Winter Squash (Butternut)

    A mature, hard-rinded squash with a long neck and a rounded seed cavity.

    Food form
    Mature squash fruit.
    Notice
    Orange flesh within a tan rind.

    Remember Winter squash is harvested mature; summer squash is picked young.

Recorded correction notes

  • Pueblo Chiles prints “Mild, earthy, rich”. The Pueblo chile, the Mosco and Mirasol type grown around Pueblo, Colorado, is medium hot, hotter than a typical Hatch or Anaheim green chile, so read the profile as medium hot, earthy, meaty rather than mild.Pueblo growers and Colorado State University put the Mosco at roughly 5,000 to 20,000 Scoville, several times the heat of an Anaheim and above the Big Jim Hatch, and the growers market it as hotter than Hatch.
  • Potatoes prints “SPINACH”. The card is mislabeled and should read Potatoes, since the drawing is red and yellow potatoes and the August to November season and the hearty, earthy, versatile profile are potato facts.Spinach is a tender cool season leaf, about 91 percent water in the Lexicon, that no one calls hearty or a storage crop, while the picture plainly shows tubers and the Southwest potato harvest in Colorado's San Luis Valley runs late summer into fall.
  • Arugula prints “Mild, fresh, slightly sweet”. Arugula is peppery and nutty with a sharp mustard like bite, so read the profile as peppery, nutty, green rather than mild and sweet.The Lexicon entry Arugula teaches that it is a brassica whose chewing releases isothiocyanates, the same reaction that sharpens mustard and horseradish, and only the youngest baby leaf is mild.
