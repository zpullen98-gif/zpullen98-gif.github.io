What the plate gets wrong

The picture was generated, and these are its errors of fact, checked against the kitchen and the Lexicon. Read the plate with them.

  • Pueblo Chiles prints “Mild, earthy, rich”. The Pueblo chile, the Mosco and Mirasol type grown around Pueblo, Colorado, is medium hot, hotter than a typical Hatch or Anaheim green chile, so read the profile as medium hot, earthy, meaty rather than mild. Pueblo growers and Colorado State University put the Mosco at roughly 5,000 to 20,000 Scoville, several times the heat of an Anaheim and above the Big Jim Hatch, and the growers market it as hotter than Hatch.
  • Potatoes prints “SPINACH”. The card is mislabeled and should read Potatoes, since the drawing is red and yellow potatoes and the August to November season and the hearty, earthy, versatile profile are potato facts. Spinach is a tender cool season leaf, about 91 percent water in the Lexicon, that no one calls hearty or a storage crop, while the picture plainly shows tubers and the Southwest potato harvest in Colorado's San Luis Valley runs late summer into fall.
  • Arugula prints “Mild, fresh, slightly sweet”. Arugula is peppery and nutty with a sharp mustard like bite, so read the profile as peppery, nutty, green rather than mild and sweet. The Lexicon entry Arugula teaches that it is a brassica whose chewing releases isothiocyanates, the same reaction that sharpens mustard and horseradish, and only the youngest baby leaf is mild.
