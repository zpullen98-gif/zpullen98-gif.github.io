The field guide

Look closely. Learn the difference.

These six single ingredients are not blends. The cinnamon drawing represents the general bark form, not a certified species.

  1. 1

    Black Pepper

    Small peppercorns made from the fruit of the tropical vine Piper nigrum.

    Plant part
    Fruit.
    Character
    Pungent; used whole or ground.

    Remember Black, white and green peppercorns come from the same species but differ in harvest and processing.

  2. 2

    Cumin

    Slender, tan, ridged spice fruits used as a whole or ground seasoning.

    Plant part
    Dried fruits.
    Plant
    Cuminum cyminum.

    Remember Cumin is not coriander; their shapes and aromas differ.

    Read the Lexicon: Cumin
  3. 3

    Coriander

    The round, tan seed spice from the same plant whose leaves are called cilantro.

    Plant part
    Mature seed spice.
    Plant
    Coriandrum sativum.

    Remember Cilantro leaves and coriander spice are different parts of one plant.

  4. 4

    Cinnamon

    An aromatic spice made from tree bark, sold as rolled quills or powder.

    Plant part
    Inner bark.
    Forms
    Several Cinnamomum species supply cinnamon spices.

    Remember A cinnamon quill is rolled bark, not a hollow stem.

  5. 5

    Turmeric

    A yellow-orange spice obtained from the underground rhizomes of Curcuma longa.

    Plant part
    Rhizome, an underground stem.
    Form
    Fresh rhizome or processed ground spice.

    Remember Its rhizome is different from a seed or a true root.

    Read the Lexicon: Turmeric
  6. 6

    Cloves

    Dried, unopened flower buds with a small rounded head and a narrow stalk-like base.

    Plant part
    Flower buds.
    Plant
    Syzygium aromaticum.

    Remember A clove spice bud is unrelated to a clove of garlic.

    Read the Lexicon: Cloves

Recorded correction notes

  • Green Pepper prints “Green Pepper / Flavor: Fresh, citrusy, mild / Use: Salads, vegetables, fish”. The card shows green peppercorns, the unripe berry of the black pepper vine sold brined or freeze dried, so it should be named Green Peppercorn; in an American kitchen green pepper means a green bell pepper.The picture is a bowl of small round green berries beside black and white peppercorns, and the app's Black Pepper and the Pepper Family entry defines green pepper as the unripe berry, fresh and bright, used in steak au poivre vert.
  • Paprika prints “Flavor: Sweet, smoky, mild”. Plain paprika is sweet, fruity and mild but not smoky; only Spanish smoked paprika (pimenton de la Vera) tastes of smoke, so the card should say sweet, fruity, mild or name the smoked kind.Sweet and Hungarian paprika are air dried ground red peppers with no smoke flavor, and the app's Paprika and Pimenton entry keeps the oak smoked Spanish style separate from the bright, fruity Hungarian kind.
  • Sweet Basil prints “Sweet Basil / Flavor: Sweet, spicy, fresh / Use: Italian, pasta, salads”. Sweet basil is the same herb as the Basil card in row 3 (Ocimum basilicum, the common Genovese basil), so the plate lists one herb twice under two names; keep one basil card, or make the second card Thai basil, a different anise scented plant.Sweet basil is the standard trade and botanical name for ordinary culinary basil, both cards draw the same broad green leaf and give nearly the same uses, and the app's Thai Basil entry uses sweet basil as the plain name for Genovese basil.
  • Anise prints “Anise, drawn as eight pointed star shaped pods”. The picture is star anise, the pod of the Asian tree Illicium verum, a different plant from anise seed; name the card Star Anise, or keep the name Anise and draw the small oval seed of Pimpinella anisum.Anise seed is the small gray green Mediterranean seed of pastis, ouzo and pizzelle while star anise is the eight pointed pod of pho, red braised pork and five spice, and the app keeps them as two separate Lexicon entries that share anethole but not personality.
  • Harissa prints “Harissa, drawn as a red paste in a bowl, among the dry spices”. Harissa is a Tunisian chili paste of dried chilies pounded with garlic, salt and toasted spices and kept under olive oil, a condiment rather than a dry spice; if a dry blend is meant, call it harissa spice blend.The card draws a wet red paste, and the app's Lexicon and Floor Deck both file harissa as a Tunisian red paste of dried chilies, garlic and toasted caraway, coriander and cumin finished under oil.
