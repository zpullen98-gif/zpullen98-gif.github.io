The thermometer: measure the food, not its colourIllustrated study · 4 numbered views

Compare probe placement in a thin portion and a thick boneless portion. The measuring area must reach the place you intend to check.

The small insets are simplified placement diagrams, not anatomical sections or a scale for insertion depth. This fine-tip digital probe represents one instrument type: follow your own model’s sensing-area, calibration and care instructions. Blank displays provide no temperature reading.

One numbered view at a time. No grade or change to your learning record.

Whole illustration. Choose a view for a closer look.

  1. 1
    Know the sensing area

    Check where your thermometer measures and how far it must be inserted. A fine-tip thermocouple and a dial stem can need different depths. Check accuracy according to the manufacturer and your kitchen’s procedure.

  2. 2
    Enter a thin portion from the side

    For a thin patty, insert a suitable probe through the side so its sensing area reaches the centre. Avoid measuring against the cooking surface. The inset shows the intended internal location.

  3. 3
    Check a thick portion internally

    Place the sensing area in the thickest part, away from bone, fat and gristle. Check more than one location in large or irregular food. Read against the food’s required time-and-temperature standard; do not assume carryover will make it safe.

  4. 4
    Clean between checks

    The picture shows washing only. Wash, rinse, sanitise and air-dry the food-contact probe between uses, following the instrument and sanitiser instructions. Keep non-waterproof electronics out of the water.

Observe
Locate the entry point and the intended measuring point in each food. Notice that the display supplies no reading to memorise.
Explain
Surface browning describes the exterior. A correctly placed, accurate thermometer measures an internal location; its result must still be checked against the appropriate cooking process.
Apply
With your trainer, identify the sensing area of the actual kitchen probe. Practise side entry on a thin portion and identify the required cleaning steps before the next food.
Check your understanding

A patty is brown outside and the probe touches its upper surface. Have you established its internal cooking temperature?

Reveal the explanation

No. Use a suitable clean probe with its sensing area inside the centre, normally entering a thin patty from the side. Check the actual reading and required time-and-temperature standard; surface colour and predicted carryover are not substitutes.

The allergy handoff: listen, verify, prepare, confirmIllustrated study · 4 numbered views

Follow the information as carefully as the plate. A clear conversation must reach the kitchen and return with a verified answer.

This is a general communication example, not Brennan’s approved allergy procedure. The rice-and-broccoli plate is illustrative: its appearance cannot establish ingredients, cross-contact controls or suitability for any particular guest.

One numbered view at a time. No grade or change to your learning record.

Whole illustration. Choose a view for a closer look.

  1. 1
    Listen and record

    Ask which ingredient must be avoided, record the guest’s exact concern and repeat it back. Identify the guest’s seat. Bring the request to the designated chef or manager before promising an accommodation.

  2. 2
    Verify the whole preparation

    Check the current recipe, ingredient labels, sauces, garnishes and preparation arrangements with the responsible person. A familiar dish name or an old recipe is not enough. Explain honestly if the kitchen cannot accommodate the request.

  3. 3
    Control cross-contact

    Use the verified ingredients, cleaned and sanitised equipment and work area, and proper handwashing. The picture shows plating, not proof of those controls. If an unwanted ingredient contacts the food, remake the dish; picking it off is insufficient.

  4. 4
    Confirm at pickup and service

    Confirm the designated dish and any agreed modifications with the kitchen. Carry it separately and deliver it directly to the identified guest. If its identity or preparation is uncertain, pause and check again.

Observe
Identify who receives the request, who verifies it and who delivers the plate. Which parts of ingredient checking and cleaning remain invisible?
Explain
Allergen cross-contact can happen through ingredients, utensils, surfaces or handling. Removing a visible garnish or applying heat does not reliably remove the allergen risk.
Apply
Practise: “Thank you for telling me. I’ll check the ingredients and preparation with our chef before confirming what we can offer.” Then rehearse the pickup confirmation.
Check your understanding

The kitchen notices the unwanted garnish on an otherwise correct allergy order. Can you remove it and serve the plate?

Reveal the explanation

No. Stop the pickup, tell the responsible chef or manager, and have the dish remade using the verified ingredients and controls. Confirm the replacement’s identity before carrying it separately to the guest.

Sugar: cooled texture and caramel colourIllustrated study · 6 numbered views

Read two different tests: cooled syrup samples across the top, then caramel colours across the bottom.

The upper samples represent fully cooled cold-water tests. Never touch or taste hot syrup. The listed ranges are sea-level guides from the Exploratorium; use the recipe, an accurate thermometer and altitude adjustment. The caramel row is a colour comparison, not a temperature scale.

One numbered view at a time. No grade or change to your learning record.

Whole illustration. Choose a view for a closer look.

  1. 1
    Soft-ball test

    At roughly 235–240°F (113–116°C), a cooled syrup sample forms a soft ball that flattens readily. Its shape, rather than amber colour, identifies the test.

  2. 2
    Firm-ball test

    At roughly 245–250°F (118–121°C), the cooled sample holds a ball but remains pliable. Compare its retained shape with the softer sample.

  3. 3
    Hard-crack test

    At roughly 300–310°F (149–154°C), cooled syrup forms brittle threads that snap. This is a texture check, not the name for dark caramel.

  4. 4
    Pale caramel

    A light golden colour marks early browning in this comparison. Watch against a pale background and follow the recipe’s intended flavour.

  5. 5
    Amber caramel

    The deeper amber sample shows more browning. Caramelisation creates flavours beyond simple sweetness.

  6. 6
    Dark caramel

    The darker sample illustrates a stronger colour, not a universally better result. Control residual heat and stop before the batch develops a burnt flavour.

Observe
Separate the upper row’s cooled shapes from the lower row’s colours. A clear syrup can already have a useful confectionery structure.
Explain
Water loss changes syrup concentration and its cooled texture. Caramel browning adds a different set of changes; the two observations are not interchangeable.
Apply
Connect New Orleans Bread Pudding’s pralines and whiskey caramel with two different sugar preparations. Confirm the house formulas before describing a precise stage.
Check your understanding
