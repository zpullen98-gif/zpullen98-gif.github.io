How to use the Table

A little recall, one clear explanation, then a practical application. You can choose any level and return whenever you need a refresher.

  1. Recall. Open Today's study and try a short round before looking at the answers.
  2. Understand. Open the lesson behind a missed card. Name the ingredient, method and result in plain English.
  3. Apply. Describe one dish aloud as if speaking to a guest, or cook a suitable recipe and check its result.
Six kitchen-to-tableside study journeys

Choose one subject for a sitting. Follow the reading, connect it to the menu, then test what you can explain without looking.

Read a plate, then tell its storyTurn unfamiliar menu words into a short, useful description of flavour and texture.
  1. Identify: capers

    Learn the ingredient before describing what it contributes. Notice the distinction between capers and caperberries.

  2. Compare: the fish

    Build a vocabulary for texture, then separate the species from its cooking method and sauce.

  3. Describe: Swordfish Grenobloise

    Use the training sheet to name the fish, sauce and accompaniments. Read the checks before making a dietary promise.

  4. Practise: the whole dish

    Recall the components, explain one unfamiliar word and give the tableside description aloud.

Say it without looking: Can I describe the dish in one sentence, explain a caper in everyday English and name one detail to confirm with the kitchen?

Choose heat to suit the cutExplain why a tender cut and a working muscle need different treatment.
  1. Identify: tenderloin

    Start with the cut and its natural tenderness, before attaching a cooking method to it.

  2. Compare: braise and daube

    Learn how time, gentle heat and liquid change connective tissue; compare that goal with quick cooking.

  3. Describe: Lamb Shank en Croûte

    Separate the slow-cooked filling, pastry and jus. Keep the sheet’s ingredients distinct from general daube traditions.

  4. Practise: braising decisions

    Test the method, then explain why tenderness cannot be promised from a fixed cooking time alone.

Say it without looking: Can I explain why shank benefits from slow cooking, what en croûte means and which parts of the house preparation still need confirmation?

Follow the sauce familyConnect a named sauce to its base, texture and role on the plate.
  1. Identify: the mother sauces

    Find the brown-sauce branch and see how a family is organised before memorising individual names.

  2. Compare: espagnole

    Read its construction and follow the linked demi-glace comparison. The two names are related, but are not interchangeable.

  3. Describe: Chateau Espagnole

    Connect the sauce to the tenderloin and frascarelli in the supplied sheet. Do not turn a classic recipe into an assumed house recipe.

  4. Practise: the sauce

    Recall its base, method and common faults, then translate that knowledge into a guest-friendly description.

Say it without looking: Can I distinguish espagnole from demi-glace and describe the sauce’s role without promising an unconfirmed ingredient?

Travel from pasta shape to plateUse origin and technique to explain a dish without losing sight of its actual ingredients.
  1. Identify: fresh pasta and agnolotti

    Learn the vocabulary of dough, filling and shape, then connect it to the regional tradition.

  2. Compare: the house pasta

    Compare the general tradition with the pork, greens, cheeses and lemon pangrattato listed on the training sheet.

  3. Describe: an allergy question

    Practise checking the filling, pasta, crumb and preparation rather than treating the main ingredient as the whole dish.

  4. Practise: Agnolotti del Plin

    Say the name, explain the shape and rehearse a concise description before testing the deeper ingredient notes.

Say it without looking: Can I distinguish the regional background from this house version, explain pangrattato and pass an ingredient question to the kitchen clearly?

Answer the question behind the orderGive clear menu guidance while recognising when the kitchen must confirm an answer.
  1. Identify: the guest’s need

    Learn to clarify the request, communicate it accurately and check the complete preparation.

  2. Compare: tartare components

    Locate the raw beef, egg in the dressing, separate yolk and rye tuile. Removing a visible garnish does not describe the whole preparation.

  3. Describe: a different raw dish

    Apply the same questioning to fish, salsa seca and accompaniments. Use the lesson’s explicit checks instead of transferring assumptions from tartare.

  4. Practise: the conversation

    Rehearse what you can explain, what you must check and how you report the answer back to the guest.

Say it without looking: Can I name the complete dish, state what is known and ask a precise kitchen question without saying that an unverified modification is safe?

Finish with texture and clarityDescribe a dessert through its custard, texture and finishing elements.
  1. Identify: crème anglaise

    Understand a pouring custard and the care needed when heating it, before learning more elaborate desserts.

  2. Compare: crème pâtissière

    Compare a thicker pastry cream with anglaise. Similar names do not mean identical ingredients or methods.

  3. Describe: Chocolate Crémeux

    Return to the actual sheet for the crémeux, cocoa nib crumble, lemon olive oil and salt. Read its dietary notes and confirmation points.

  4. Practise: the dessert

    Recall each component, explain the contrasts and practise a short closing recommendation aloud.

Say it without looking: Can I explain crémeux without calling every custard the same thing, describe the contrasting textures and identify the details that require a kitchen check?

Understand progress, notes and offline use
  • Your chosen level is a study focus. It is not a restriction or a professional qualification. Use All levels where offered to explore beyond it.
  • Reading, recall and cooking are different achievements. Opening a lesson does not prove you have learnt it. Cards are self-checks; quizzes and practical records have their own progress rules, explained alongside the activity.
  • Record cooking after doing it. The Repertoire keeps your cooking history and suggests what to revisit. Use the recipe’s result checks to judge your work.
  • Your record belongs to this browser. Use Session and tools to export a backup before changing devices. A website update preserves the saved record; clearing browser storage can remove it.
  • Prepare before going offline. Let the app finish its first download. Open the house menu, cooking lessons and images you need while connected, then check them in airplane mode. Images and Cook at home lessons are cached as used and may later be evicted by the browser; videos and external sources need a connection.
